Generative recommendation has emerged as a new paradigm that predicts item identifiers from a user's interaction history~. By formulating recommendation as conditional generation, GR enables the use of generative architectures and training techniques for personalized recommendation~. Recently, GR has gained attention in industrial applications beyond academic research~. As they become part of practical pipelines, understanding and controlling their behavior becomes increasingly important. Beyond predicting likely interactions, recommendation services should support control toward both and ~. Platform operators may need to reduce exposure to particular content types, while users may seek experiences not fully reflected in their interaction histories. As illustrated in Figure~, a recommendation model without explicit control may continue to recommend racing games based on a user's interaction history . Controllability would instead let the user specify goals, , or , and adjust how strongly they influence recommendations conditioned on the interaction history . This calls for a way to connect text-defined discovery goals to internal controls in a recommendation model. Recently, sparse autoencoders have been actively used to interpret internal representations of LLMs and steer generated outputs~. SAEs decompose dense hidden states into sparsely activated features, providing units whose responses can be examined and modified~. Related studies have also applied SAEs to recommendation representations and the control of item attributes~. However, connecting text-defined discovery goals to sparse feature interventions on GR remains underexplored. To this end, we propose able enerative ecommender }, a framework for concept-guided steering in generative recommendation. The core idea is to ground text-defined play concepts in game descriptions and select the sparse features that shift recommendations toward each concept. proceeds through three stages. First, scores games against LLM-defined play concepts using their descriptions, then aggregates the scores over interaction histories. Second, contrasts SAE responses between users with high and low concept scores to identify candidates, then selects the feature that most increases the share of games with the concept on the validation split. Third, amplifies or suppresses the selected feature at inference time without updating the recommender parameters. Using industrial recommenders trained on real-world game datasets, we extensively evaluate how each intervention shifts recommendations and how it affects accuracy. Our contributions are summarized as follows: To the best of our knowledge, we propose the first framework that connects text-defined play concepts to SAE features for steering generative recommenders. We identify an effective steering feature for each concept by contrasting user histories based on concept scores and selecting by steering effect. We validate on real-world game datasets, examining steering effects on deployed generative recommenders. Our work has two limitations that suggest directions for future work. We evaluate on twelve LLM-generated play concepts grounded in game descriptions. However, evolving needs of platforms and discovery goals of users may require diverse steering criteria . Future work could extend with controls for more diverse criteria. We demonstrate that a Top-K SAE~ can support concept steering in generative recommendation as a proof-of-concept. However, recent works on SAEs have explored a broader range of architectures and training strategies~. Future work could adapt these advances to generative recommendation to investigate more flexible concept control and assess their effects on recommendation quality.
