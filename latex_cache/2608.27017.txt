We note that structured filtering conditions could in some applications systematically exclude certain groups or information sources, and that biases in the underlying embedding models may propagate into retrieval rankings; practitioners should audit filter distributions and embedding fairness when deploying program-based retrieval in sensitive domains. Real-world retrieval often composes structured constraints with semantic intents over text and images through arbitrary Boolean logic. Existing hybrid pipelines such as reciprocal rank fusion or self-querying retrievers admit only a fixed form of composition, while recent reinforcement-learning retrievers train the language model as a for a single backend, leaving the orchestration of heterogeneous retrieval paths outside its action space. We propose , which recasts the language model as a : given a natural-language query, it synthesizes an executable program in a hybrid DSL interleaving SQL operators over structured fields with vector-retrieval primitives over text and images, with SQL itself providing the logical algebra that fuses heterogeneous candidate sets. Given a natural-language query, the RL-trained policy emits a hybrid DSL program that combines an SQL filter with text and image vector-retrieval primitives. The DSL execution engine dispatches each primitive to its embedding-based backend, substitutes the returned candidate sets into the SQL through placeholder injection, and lets the SQL engine perform the final AND/OR/NOT fusion to produce ranked results. The model is trained in two stages: SFT on expert DSL examples followed by RL under a hierarchical composite reward over format, executability, and result quality.} Real-world retrieval rarely reduces to either symbolic filtering or pure semantic matching. A user may ask for , or for . Such queries share two properties: information is split across heterogeneous backends , and constraints are composed by arbitrary Boolean connectives . Solving them requires not only understanding the user wants, but deciding to find it: which conditions to push down as exact filters, which intents to pose as semantic queries, and how to logically combine the resulting candidate sets. Existing methods fall short along two axes. On , pure retrievers~ cover a single modality; fusion pipelines~ express only weighted disjunction; and self-querying retrievers~ admit simple key--value conjunctions but not disjunction, negation, or nested predicates. On , recent RL approaches such as Search-R1~ and DeepRetrieval~ train models to issue better queries to a backend but cannot compose across heterogeneous stores. Hybrid retrieval therefore requires enlarging the model's role from to , with an action space that is itself an executable program over a logically complete operator set. We propose , a framework that trains a small language model to orchestrate hybrid retrieval by synthesizing executable retrieval programs. Our key observation is that SQL's logical algebra can serve as the orchestration skeleton, while vector retrieval is incorporated as a first-class primitive whose results plug into SQL through candidate-set placeholders . This reduces orchestration to learnable program generation in a hybrid DSL while inheriting SQL's logical completeness for free. We train Qwen3-4B with GRPO~ and DAPO~ under a hierarchical four-term reward that mirrors the layered correctness requirements of executable program generation. To evaluate the framework we construct two hybrid retrieval benchmarks from public corpora: Amazon e-commerce and Enron email , with queries spanning single-leaf to nested Boolean expressions. On both benchmarks our 4B model surpasses GPT-5.5 and a comprehensive suite of retrieval, reranking, structured-query, and graph-based baselines. In summary, our contributions are: {2pt} {0pt} We reformulate hybrid retrieval as program generation in a DSL that unifies SQL operators with multimodal vector primitives, lifting the model's action space from a textual query to a logically complete executable program. The expressiveness strictly subsumes that of fixed-fusion pipelines and single-backend RL retrievers. We introduce a four-term composite reward that mirrors the layered correctness requirements of program generation, generalizing the single-signal rewards used by prior RL retrievers. We release benchmarks over Amazon products and Enron email, together with four paradigms of baselines, and demonstrate that a 4B open-source model trained with our framework consistently outperforms frontier commercial LLMs at a fraction of their inference cost. While ProRetrieval demonstrates strong results on hybrid retrieval, our study has several limitations. The DSL assumes a predefined schema; extending it to schema-free or evolving corpora is left to future work. Our query construction pipeline samples logical expressions up to two levels of nesting; deeper nesting is syntactically expressible but underrepresented in training. We validate on e-commerce and email; broader generalization to scientific literature, legal documents, and personal knowledge bases remains to be tested. Our benchmarks contain 3{,}000 products and 5{,}000 emails, respectively. While sufficient for evaluating retrieval accuracy, scaling to corpora with millions of documents may introduce additional challenges in DSL execution latency and candidate-set composition. The framework synthesizes a single program per query; extending to multi-turn interaction where the model refines its DSL based on intermediate results is a promising direction. Image retrieval quality is bounded by the text-to-image alignment of the underlying multimodal embedder. We do not report cross-seed variance due to RL training cost. ProRetrieval trains a small language model to act as a retrieval orchestrator that synthesizes executable hybrid retrieval programs. The framework, illustrated in Figure~, comprises three components: ~a that interleaves SQL operators with text and image vector-retrieval primitives ; ~a that automatically produces $ $ triples covering single-leaf to nested Boolean expressions ; and ~a that warm-starts the model with supervised fine-tuning on gold DSL and then optimizes it with reinforcement learning under a hierarchical composite reward . A program in our DSL is a JSON object with two fields: { "sql": "SELECT DISTINCT id FROM <T> WHERE <conditions>", "retrieval_list": [ {"type": "text", "field": "<f1>", "query": "<semantic phrase>"}, {"type": "image", "field": "<f2>", "query": "<visual phrase>"}, ... ] } The field is a standard SQL query over the structured fields of the underlying table; it may invoke vector retrieval through placeholders of the form or , where $k$ indexes the corresponding entry in . Each entry specifies the modality, the field on which similarity is computed, and the textual query to be embedded. Because the placeholders appear as candidate-set constraints inside arbitrary SQL clauses, the program inherits SQL's full logical algebra: , , , and nested subqueries can freely combine structured predicates with semantic ones. At execution time, each entry in is dispatched to the corresponding backend , which returns the top-$K$ candidate identifiers $_k$ . The placeholders are then substituted by the corresponding sets, transforming semantic similarity into set membership predicates . The resulting SQL is executed against the relational backend, which performs the final logical composition via its native query engine. This two-phase design decouples the embedding computation from the logical orchestration, allowing each component to be optimized independently. The DSL strictly subsumes the action spaces of prior methods: reciprocal rank fusion corresponds to a disjunction of vector predicates without structured filtering; self-querying retrievers to a single vector predicate conjoined with key--value filters; pure text-to-SQL to the empty . This hierarchy means any query expressible by a simpler paradigm is expressible in our DSL, while the converse does not hold---queries requiring cross-modal logical composition are unique to the full DSL. Executable programs satisfy layered correctness: they must be syntactically valid before they can be executed, executed before they can produce results, and produce correct results before length matters. We accordingly decompose R = S_f + S_e + S_r + S_l, with $S_f\! \!\{0,1\}$ , $S_e\! \!\{0,1\}$ , $S_r\! \! $ , and $S_l\! \! $ . We use rank-1 hit rather than a softer metric for $S_r$ because each query is anchored to a single ground-truth document, where MRR reduces to $1/$ and provides noisier gradients without additional information. Although summed with equal weight, the layered structure induces an implicit priority: a program failing $S_f$ earns none of the rest, so the gradient sharpens validity, executability, accuracy, then efficiency in that order. The length term $S_l$ penalizes unnecessarily verbose programs. We use equal weights; the layered dependency already provides implicit prioritization, and performance is insensitive to moderate weight variations . We train on the verl framework~ from Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B bases. SFT runs for 3 epochs with learning rate $5{ }10^{-5}$ and batch size 64; we select the checkpoint with the lowest validation loss. Text and image candidates for vector primitives are precomputed offline with Qwen3 text and vision embedding backbones, indexed for nearest-neighbor lookup, and shared across all baselines. Sparse and dense retrievers~ and modern multimodal embedders~, optionally followed by cross-encoder rerankers~, each operate within a single representational space. Industry pipelines combine them at the rank level via reciprocal rank fusion~, which captures only a weighted disjunction, or through self-querying retrievers~, whose filter language admits only conjunctions of simple key--value predicates. Knowledge-graph retrievers such as GraphRAG~ introduce an orthogonal structured signal and are complementary to our field-centric design. Across this line the orchestration logic is fixed by the pipeline designer and cannot adapt per query. Recent work uses RL to train language models that interact with retrieval: Search-R1~ interleaves reasoning with calls to a search engine; DeepRetrieval~ optimizes structured Boolean / SQL-like query reformulation against a single backend; s3~, R1-Searcher~, and follow-ups~ explore further variants, and SQL-R1~ applies RL to text-to-SQL. These methods improve the queries a model issues, but the action space is restricted to the type of query a single fixed backend accepts and none orchestrates multiple heterogeneous backends with arbitrary logical composition. In particular, SQL-R1 cannot express semantic similarity queries because SQL lacks vector retrieval operators; our action space ablation quantifies this gap: SQL-only achieves 0.650 Hit@1 vs.\ 0.809 for the full hybrid DSL. We compare directly with Search-R1, DeepRetrieval, and SQL-R1. DSLs have long bridged language models and symbolic computation in program synthesis~ and tool use~. Closest to our work, SUQL~ extends SQL with a operator for conversational QA, and Text2VectorSQL~ proposes a unified SQL interface with vector search predicates. Text2VectorSQL embeds vector operations as SQL UDFs, coupling retrieval and fusion in a database-native engine; our placeholder injection decouples them, enabling independent optimization and extension to image modality. The two systems target different tasks---Text2VectorSQL evaluates SQL execution accuracy on Spider/BIRD-style benchmarks, whereas we evaluate document retrieval quality ---and our SFT baseline already represents the same supervised-learning paradigm, upon which RL provides a further $+12.9$\,pp gain . Other approaches include TableQuery~ and CRUSH~, focusing on structured-only retrieval without vector primitives. All of the above rely on prompting or SFT. ProRetrieval differs by treating the DSL as a learnable policy space optimized end-to-end by RL with a hierarchical reward tailored to executable hybrid programs.
