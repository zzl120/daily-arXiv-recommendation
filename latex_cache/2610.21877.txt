Neural sparse retrievers such as SPLADE provide efficient retrieval but rely on relatively compact encoders with limited representational capacity. To bridge this gap, we propose an LLM-based sparse retrieval framework with input-adaptive sparse alignment. Specifically, we introduce a dynamic $ $-truncation strategy that selects input-specific supervision supports based on the cumulative activation mass of SPLADE representations. This strategy guides the LLM to preserve salient lexical signals while suppressing less informative activations, without relying on a fixed support budget. Subsequent retrieval fine-tuning combines contrastive learning, knowledge distillation, and sparsity regularization to improve retrieval effectiveness. Experiments on MS MARCO and six selected BEIR datasets demonstrate improvements over SPLADE++ while maintaining competitive effectiveness against LLM-based sparse retrievers. Compared with Lion-sp, our method reduces index storage by 6.4× and end-to-end query latency by over 11×, achieving a favorable effectiveness–efficiency trade-off. Sparse retrieval remains central to information retrieval , valued for its efficiency, interpretability, and compatibility with inverted indexing~. Recent neural sparse retrievers such as SPLADE~, uniCOIL~, and DeepImpact~ extend this paradigm by learning high-dimensional vocabulary representations, achieving strong effectiveness while preserving the deployment advantages of classical sparse systems. However, these approaches typically rely on relatively small encoder architectures , limiting their ability to fully exploit the semantic richness of large pre-trained language models . In parallel, query expansion techniques based on pre-trained language models and LLMs have been extensively explored to alleviate lexical mismatch and enrich retrieval representations, as systematically reviewed by Li et al~. Large decoder-based LLMs offer substantially stronger semantic modeling capabilities, but integrating them into sparse retrieval remains challenging due to structural differences between continuous hidden representations and discrete lexical signals. Direct projection often leads to unstable sparsity and inefficient representations. This raises a key question: {bigpic.pdf} Motivated by recent advances in neural sparse retrieval and the powerful semantic modeling capabilities of large language models, we investigate whether LLM-enhanced representations can be effectively integrated into sparse retrieval without sacrificing its inherent efficiency and interpretability. Drawing inspiration from the compatibility of sparse lexical systems with practical inverted indexing, we propose a lightweight alignment framework that bridges LLM semantic representations with high-dimensional sparse vocabulary spaces. Our key contributions are summarized as follows: We introduce an input-adaptive $ $-truncation mechanism that dynamically controls sparsity based on semantic distribution, which mitigates structural mismatch, suppresses long-tail noise, and preserves dominant semantic signals without task-specific fine-tuning. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method improves in-domain retrieval performance over strong BERT-based sparse baselines and maintains competitive out-of-domain effectiveness under stricter sparsity constraints. Compared with prior LLM-based sparse retrievers, our approach achieves an average sparsity of $L_{0}=182$, reducing index storage by $6.4 $ and improving query-time latency by over $11 $, showcasing a favorable effectiveness--efficiency trade-off compatible with practical inverted-index deployment. Learned sparse retrievers such as DeepImpact~, uniCOIL~, and SPLADE~ improve classical lexical matching by learning term importance within the vocabulary space. These models retain compatibility with inverted indices while achieving substantial gains over traditional methods like BM25. Dense retrieval methods leverage pre-trained language models to encode queries and documents into continuous embeddings~. Beyond representation learning, self-supervised domain adaptation through pseudo-relevance labeling has also been explored to improve dense and conversational dense retrieval across domains ~. While effective at modeling semantic similarity, they require approximate nearest neighbor search for scalability~, and sacrifice the transparency and efficiency of lexical inverted indexing. Recent work explores scaling decoder-only LLMs for sparse retrieval, demonstrating strong effectiveness and zero-shot generalization~. Another line of work studies how to align neural representations with interpretable lexical spaces. For example, MILCO~ introduces a multilingual connector that maps queries and documents into a shared English lexical space for multilingual and cross-lingual learned sparse retrieval. While these methods highlight the importance of lexical-space alignment, existing LLM-based sparse retrievers often produce highly dense activations, resulting in substantial storage and latency overhead. Another line of research explores LLM-based query expansion to enhance lexical matching and retrieval effectiveness. Li et al ~ provide a comprehensive survey of query expansion techniques in the PLM and LLM era. Recent studies further investigate prompt-based expansion and rank fusion ~, as well as automatic exemplar construction and multi-LLM expansion refinement ~. Unlike these generation-oriented approaches, our method directly learns sparse vocabulary representations through LLM-to-SPLADE alignment, without explicitly generating expansion text. Our work focuses on aligning LLM representations with the SPLADE vocabulary space while explicitly controlling sparsity through input-adaptive $ $-truncation. Our goal is to align dense representations produced by a pre-trained language model with SPLADE’s high-dimensional sparse vocabulary space, enabling efficient sparse retrieval while leveraging the semantic richness of LLM representations. In this work, E2E latency refers to the online query-time cost, consisting of two components: query encoding into a sparse representation via LLM inference, and sparse retrieval over the inverted index using conventional term matching. This two-part decomposition is crucial for understanding the practical deployability of LLM-based sparse retrievers, as the encoding cost often dominates the overall latency in real-world settings. While Lion-sp achieves strong effectiveness , it suffers from severe inefficiency due to highly dense representations , leading to substantial storage overhead and latency exceeding 1000ms per query. Such overhead renders Lion-sp impractical for most production environments, particularly those with strict latency budgets or limited GPU resources for online inference. The root cause lies in its dense activation patterns: with over a thousand non-zero terms per document, both the index footprint and the computational cost of scoring grow proportionally. Our method addresses this limitation through a two-stage training framework that combines adaptive sparse supervision with FLOPS regularization. Specifically, dynamic $ $-truncation is applied only during the alignment stage to guide the learning of compact sparse representations, without requiring additional truncation at inference time. The resulting model achieves an average document sparsity of $L_0 = 182$, reducing index storage by 6.4$ $ and E2E query latency by over 11$ $ compared with Lion-sp. The substantially fewer activated terms contribute to more efficient inverted-index retrieval, while the compact index further reduces storage requirements. These results demonstrate that our training strategy achieves a favorable effectiveness--efficiency trade-off without introducing additional inference-time sparsification. Despite adopting a larger backbone than SPLADE++ , our approach maintains comparable efficiency, with both storage and latency remaining in the same order of magnitude as SPLADE++. As shown in Figure~, our model lies on a favorable Pareto frontier, achieving higher MRR@10 than SPLADE++ while substantially reducing latency relative to Lion-sp. This positions our framework as a practically viable solution for deploying LLM-enhanced sparse retrieval in latency-sensitive, resource-constrained environments, where both storage budget and response time are critical constraints. We presented a lightweight framework for aligning LLM representations with sparse lexical retrieval through input-adaptive $ $-truncation. By selecting input-specific sparse supports from SPLADE anchor representations, the proposed dynamic truncation mechanism helps alleviate head collapse and suppress long-tail activations while preserving salient semantic signals. Experimental results on MS MARCO and BEIR show that our method achieves competitive retrieval effectiveness under substantially stricter sparsity constraints. In particular, with $L_0=182$ and an end-to-end latency of approximately 90 ms per query, our model reduces index storage by 6.4$ $ and improves query-time efficiency by over 11$ $ compared with prior LLM-based sparse retrieval, while remaining compatible with inverted indexing.
