Upstream models may filter out items preferred by downstream rankers, while independently tuned downstream fusion can offset improvements in upstream fusion. As illustrated in Figure~, the pre-ranking stage filters thousands of candidates under a tight latency budget, while the downstream ranking stage performs computationally intensive multi-objective optimization to produce the final list. Owing to their different computational budgets, feature spaces and objectives, the two stages are typically optimized independently under a stage-wise training paradigm. {Fig1.pdf} However, independent optimization induces cross-stage preference inconsistency. Pre-ranking fixes the candidate pool, so items favored by ranking may be discarded irreversibly, while ranking is optimized over a distribution it cannot correct. Pipeline quality is thus bounded by its weakest stage rather than the global optimum. Existing remedies rely on cross-stage distillation or cascade coordination: COPR , unbiased pre-ranking , and hybrid coordination transfer ranking supervision to upstream stages to reduce selection bias. However, these methods only refine pre-ranking scores while leaving the fusion process untouched. As shown in Figure~, the final pre-ranking decision is produced by a multi-task fusion module, whose ensemble formulation is often manually designed. Even with better-aligned pre-ranking scores, the resulting fused score may still differ substantially from downstream preferences. Multi-task fusion is also a core optimization component in both pre-ranking and ranking, and has been studied using hand-designed strategies, heuristic search , reinforcement learning , and end-to-end learning . However, these methods predominantly target the ranking stage, leaving pre-ranking and ranking fusion modules independently optimized and the cross-stage inconsistency unresolved. Jointly optimizing the two fusion modules, however, introduces two challenges. First, industrial recommender systems involve dozens of heterogeneous objectives, making naive aggregation of their pairwise preference losses increasingly costly and difficult to scale. Second, end-to-end optimization can amplify attribute-dependent reward bias , causing the model to over-optimize high-reward attribute groups at the expense of other objectives. These challenges motivate a unified framework that jointly optimizes fusion across stages while controlling such optimization bias. We therefore argue that fusion modules, rather than only scoring models, should be optimized jointly across cascaded stages. We propose UniRec, a fied Cross-stage ommendation Fusion model, which optimizes both stages' fusion modules under downstream-aware supervision and reformulates stage-wise optimization as end-to-end cross-stage preference optimization. UniRec consists of three complementary components. First, we develop a jointly trained cross-stage architecture. The two fusion agents partially share their input embeddings, allowing gradients to flow in both directions. An adapter network further injects the pre-ranking representation into the ranking agent as an explicit feature. To our knowledge, this is the first framework to optimize the fusion modules of two cascaded stages as a single differentiable module in the industrial recommender system. Second, we introduce a dual-axis preference alignment objective. Along the vertical axis, a cross-stage consistency term transfers downstream pairwise preferences to the upstream fusion score. Along the horizontal axis, a compact aggregation term reorganizes dozens of heterogeneous pairwise objectives into two bidirectional preference signals. Third, we propose an Attribute Group-Relative Regularization method to control attribute-dependent optimization bias. Inspired by the grouping principle of GRPO , it replaces sampled rollouts with candidates sharing the same attribute group and computes both advantages and policy normalization within each group. As a result, uniformly promoting a high-reward attribute group provides no additional optimization gain, preventing the end-to-end objective from exploiting systematic attribute-level reward differences. Offline and large-scale online experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of UniRec, which is fully deployed in production. Our contributions are as follows: We formulate end-to-end cross-stage multi-task fusion as a new optimization paradigm for cascaded recommenders, realized by a jointly trained architecture that enables cross-stage gradient flow and mutual adaptation. We design two complementary objectives for this architecture. The dual-axis preference alignment objective supervises the fusion space both across stages and across business objectives, while the attribute group-relative regularization prevents end-to-end optimization from exploiting systematic attribute-dependent reward differences. We conduct extensive offline and large-scale online A/B experiments on a production short-video system, demonstrating consistent improvements over cross-stage coordination and single-stage fusion baselines. Recent work improves pre-ranking through better models and coordination with downstream ranking. COLD is a cost-aware pre-ranking system that admits arbitrarily deep models under a latency budget. Others target cross-stage consistency: COPR aligns pre-ranking scores with the ranking model via chunk-based sampling and a $ $NDCG-weighted loss; Rethink proposes an All-Scenario hit-rate metric and a multi-objective loss optimizing the pre-ranking set rather than its ordering; HCCP couples upstream retrieval signals with downstream feedback through hybrid sampling over unexposed candidates and a margin-based InfoNCE loss . Together they show that transferring ranking supervision upstream mitigates selection bias and improves upstream representations. This line of work shares two properties. First, supervision flows one way: the ranking model acts as a frozen teacher and receives no gradient from the pre-ranking objective, so the two stages remain separate problems coupled by a signal rather than a single joint one. Second, they optimize only the pre-ranking scoring model and leave fusion unchanged. As shown in Figure~, industrial systems fuse multiple objectives via fixed formulas or independently trained weights, and this fusion module produces the final pre-ranking decision. Even with better-aligned scores, the fused output may still diverge from ranking preferences, leaving the module that actually determines candidate selection outside cross-stage optimization. Industrial recommenders formulate both pre-ranking and ranking as multi-task problems, predicting multiple engagement objectives and aggregating them through a fusion module into a unified score for candidate selection, which makes fusion design a fundamental problem. Early systems used hand-designed formulas with coefficients later searched by heuristics such as the Cross-Entropy Method and Bayesian Optimization . Subsequent work casts fusion as sequential decision-making optimized by reinforcement learning: BatchRL-MTF learns fusion weights via offline RL, RLUR optimizes retention-oriented policies, and xMTF introduces a learnable monotonic fusion cell. UNEX-RL exploits the unidirectional dependency of cascaded systems through a multi-agent information chain, but its agents output weight vectors of fixed formulas rather than learning the fusion models, and their parameters receive no cross-stage gradient. End-to-end ensemble ranking instead learns the fusion model itself: EMER adopts a transformer architecture, UMRE learns unconstrained monotonic fusion functions under Pareto-optimal multi-objective optimization , Pantheon performs personalized ensemble ranking via iterative Pareto policy optimization, and HarmonRank aligns multi-objective fusion with ranking metrics through attention. Orthogonally, a line addresses attribute-induced bias in short-video recommendation, where feedback is confounded by attributes such as video duration . These methods debias individual task signals, whereas we leave cross-attribute preference to the fusion objective and restrict the group-relative advantage to within-attribute comparisons, so that uniformly promoting a high-reward region is no longer a valid optimization direction. Despite this progress, prior work either aligns the pre-ranking scoring model through downstream supervision or improves fusion within a single stage.
