Generative recommendation makes item identifiers part of the prediction space, placing item tokenization at the interface between item representation and model supervision. Language-based approaches include the text-to-text formulation~ and the learned textual identifiers~. For structured identifiers, TIGER constructs semantic IDs through residual quantization~ of pretrained language model representations~ while SEATER combines balanced tree indexing with contrastive learning~. LC-Rec then incorporate collaborative semantics into item indexing~ and ETEGRec couples tokenizer learning with recommendation through alternating optimization~. UTGRec and UniTok further extend vocabulary learning across domains through shared tokenization~. Despite these advances, the vocabulary learning still relies on recommendation-specific data which requires additional optimization and its semantic organization remains limited by the training domains. Our work extends pretrained knowledge reuse from continuous representations to the vocabulary units, adapting an existing PLM feature dictionary for recommendation. Motivated by the ~, sparse autoencoders decompose language model activations into sparse combinations of dictionary features, exposing interpretable structure within dense representations~. Subsequent work scales these dictionaries~, improves reconstruction under sparsity constraints~ and releases pretrained SAEs for reuse through resources such as Gemma Scope~. In recommendation, SAERec uses SAE-derived intent priors to enrich sequence modeling~, but does not explore feature-level explanations of generative retrieval. In this work, we explores a practical route from mechanistic interpretability to recommendation systems, reusing a pretrained SAE feature dictionary as a shared vocabulary for GR. A widely used approach represents items as the semantic IDs , each consisting of a short sequence of discrete tokens. The mapping from items to SIDs, termed , affects the GR pipeline from two fundamental perspectives: and ~. The resulting SIDs determine which item distinctions are retained and define the token-level predictive targets used to train the recommendation model. Therefore, a central problem in tokenization is how to construct compact SIDs that preserve item distinctions and provide effective supervision for recommendation. Existing approaches commonly construct SIDs by fitting a special discrete vocabulary over item representations produced by pretrained language models ~. Inspired by the strong generalizability of unified natural language vocabularies in LMs , recent efforts have explored shared vocabularies for generalizable item tokenization~. For example, UTGRec~ learns a transferable tokenizer through multi-domain pretraining, while UniTok~ combines shared and specific experts for unified tokenization. Though these advances broaden tokenizer generalizability, they still construct vocabulary through additional fitting on the recommendation-specific data. The semantic coverage of the learned vocabulary therefore depends on the fitting data, while vocabulary construction incurs additional optimization costs. Moreover, fitting a vocabulary to recommendation-specific data does not necessarily yield SID tokens with readily interpretable semantics~. These observations raise a natural question: On the basis of recent advances in , we answer this question in the affirmative. Specifically, pretrained sparse autoencoders already provide feature dictionaries for their paired PLMs, with many features admitting human-interpretable descriptions~. For each item, the P-SAE encodes its PLM representation into a sparse activation vector, whose nonzero coordinates identify the expressed features and whose values indicate their strengths. For example, a red T-shirt might activate a feature associated with "red" more strongly than one associated with "blue". As illustrated in Figure~ , the indices of these features can serve as SID tokens, providing a natural vocabulary that can be reused without additional recommendation-specific vocabulary learning. This vocabulary also carries inspectable semantics that P-SAE features often respond to recognizable semantic patterns in their inputs, allowing the corresponding tokens to indicate which properties are expressed in an item. However, bringing this vocabulary int effective and explainable GR still requires addressing two key challenges. First, } requires preserving item distinctions in both SID construction and the supervision built on these SIDs. As illustrated in Figure~ , similar items can share dominant features while differing in their activation strengths and less active features. Representing items only by their most active feature indices can discard these distinctions, while predicting shared indices alone does not ensure that the target item outranks similar candidates. Moreover, while these features make item semantics inspectable, unleashing the potential of the vocabulary requires extending this interpretability to the retrieval. This motivates the second challenge, }, which is to trace feature contributions to items and explain why the model favors one item over another. To address these challenges, we propose }, a generative recommendation framework that dapting the retrained nterpretable vocabulary for effective and explainable generative recommendation. This adaptation spans two complementary levels: through retrained interpretable construction, and through a emantic rediction dapter . PID construction prioritizes strongly activated features while discounting those prevalent across the catalog, producing compact identifiers with accompanying activation evidence. Building on these representations, SPA incorporates feature identities and relative activation strengths into history modeling and aligns feature prediction of the target. To supervise these predictions, SPA combines sequence likelihood and strength agreement into retrieval and introduces preference alignment against competing items. API further traces feature-level contributions to retrieval, linking the semantics of each feature to the predicted evidence that favors a retrieved item. We introduce PIDs that preserve item distinctions through feature selection and relative strengths, and theoretically characterize their separability under compact token budget. We develop SPA to effective and interpretable recommendation, aligning user preferences through item-level supervision and explain rankings through feature contributions.
