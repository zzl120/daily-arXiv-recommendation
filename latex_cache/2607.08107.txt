Two-tower retrievers have become the workhorse of large-scale candidate generation in recommender systems and dense information retrieval~. The standard formulation encodes a user and a candidate item with two independent neural networks into a shared~$d$-dimensional embedding space and scores their compatibility by an inner product. Training is typically a sampled-softmax classification of the observed positive item against in-batch~ or mixed-batch~ negatives. At serving time, item embeddings are precomputed and indexed for search, yielding millisecond-scale retrieval over million-item catalogs. A single dense user embedding, however, struggles to represent users whose interests span multiple, weakly related topics: items that satisfy any of the user's interests must all sit close to a single point in the embedding space, and the resulting retrieval slates tend to over-represent the dominant interest while under-serving the rest. retrievers~ relax this bottleneck by emitting~$k$ user embeddings, one per interest, and scoring an item by the maximum inner product across the~$k$ heads. This is structurally identical to the multi-vector / late-interaction paradigm in neural information retrieval~ and yields a strictly more expressive class of user representations. One way to train multi-interest retrievers is sampled softmax: only the head with the largest inner product to the observed item receives gradient~. This routing has two shortcomings. First, it is prone to a winner-take-all dynamic in which heads that are slightly worse at initialization are selected less frequently, receive fewer gradient updates, and progressively collapse into copies of the dominant head, a ~. Second, the symmetric treatment of the~$k$ heads gives no per-user estimate of how likely each head is to explain the next item, so existing serving rules either pick the maximum-scoring head or take a union of per-head top lists under an implicit uniform prior. Neither rule reflects the heterogeneous prior mass each head should have per user. In this paper we revisit multi-interest two-tower retrieval through the lens of a generative mixture model. Emitting~$k$ user vectors and scoring by a maximum inner product originates with MaxMF~ and recurs across modern retrievers; ColBERT~ uses the same multi-vector max-similarity rule at the token level. We compare against representative instances of this family. ~ extracts interests by behavior-to-interest and trains with label-aware attention over the heads. ~ offers two extractors, ComiRec-DR and ComiRec-SA (a self-attentive pooling), both trained by hard~$ $ routing to the target and served by max-over-heads; ComiRec-SA's training and serving rules match our baseline , with their self-attentive extractor in place of our CLS Transformer; together with the serving-consistent baseline , they isolate the extractor, routing-rule, and variational-objective contributions of BACH. The closest prior in spirit is MIRN~, which routes a sequence to interests with an EM-style step; BACH differs in fitting a ~ by variational inference with a spherical recognition posterior that is sample-free and serving-consistent, rather than an EM routing heuristic, and in producing a calibrated admixture weight~$ _u$. BACH instantiates the mixture-of-softmaxes idea~ for retrieval and fits it by variational inference~, the generalization of EM~ to recognition families that need not contain the true posterior. The recognition posterior is a spherical density, von Mises-Fisher~ or power-spherical~, placing BACH alongside hyperspherical latent-variable models and prototype-based contrastive learning~, but with the heads serving simultaneously as scoring vectors and mixture means. An alternative route to interest diversity post-processes a single ranking, e.g., maximal marginal relevance~ or determinantal point processes~. BACH instead makes diversity intrinsic to the model: the per-user weights~$ _u$ allocate mass across heads, so diversity follows from the fitted mixture rather than a separate re-ranking stage. The gating tower lets us traverse the spectrum of Remark~ between two extremes: fully personalized per-user heads and a fully shared global codebook in which only~$ _u$ is personalized, the LDA-like admixture whose serving reduces to reweighting~$k$ precomputed per-topic lists. The global codebook reaches R@100~$0.297$\,/\,AUPRC~$0.053$ on MovieLens-20M , R@100~$0.102$\,/\,AUPRC~$0.015$ on Taobao , and R@100~$0.318$\,/\,AUPRC~$0.073$ on Netflix . extremes beat the MostPop floor by large margins , so both are useful, deployable operating points. The global end trades accuracy for serving: it trails the personalized heads on all three (on Netflix it falls to the single-vector baseline, and the larger catalog does not rescue it), since shared prototypes cannot match per-user encoding. In return it is (the per-head candidate lists and scores are user-independent, computed once offline, so serving reweights cached lists by~$ _u$ with no per-user nearest-neighbor queries), cheap and low-latency at scale, (a new user needs only a~$k$-simplex predicted from features, with no per-user heads to learn), (a fixed, inspectable set of global topics, with~$ _u$ a readable user profile), and (operators can override~$ _u$ to steer or constrain exposure). Intermediate points on the spectrum, a few per-user heads alongside a shared global codebook , let one balance accuracy against this precomputability; we leave their empirical study to future work. We presented , a treatment of multi-interest two-tower retrieval as a per-user mixture of softmaxes fit by variational inference. The key device is a recognition posterior over the~$k$ heads, deliberately not the model's own negative-dependent posterior, which is sample-free, distributes gradient across all heads (mitigating the routing collapse of hard~$ $ training), and is exactly the posterior of the self-normalized mixture used at serving, so the per-user weights~$ _u$ it induces are calibrated and the retrieval normalizer stays tractable. Because the concentrations~$ _r$ enter the ELBO only through the KL term, they are self-regularizing and need no prior; a shared global codebook with personalized~$ _u$ recovers an LDA-like admixture with precomputable retrieval. Empirically, on three large-scale benchmarks BACH is the strongest model at every head count, improving top-of-ranking quality over hard-routing multi-interest and single-vector baselines, with the concentration bounded and no prior, and the p-BACH and v-BACH variants essentially interchangeable. Several directions remain open: tightening the calibration of~$ _u$ (e.g., tempering the ELBO entropy term), evaluating the global-codebook admixture under its precomputable serving budget, and broader evaluation across datasets of differing scale and sparsity.
