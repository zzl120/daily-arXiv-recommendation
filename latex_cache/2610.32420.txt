Unlike traditional e-commerce where users directly search and click on specific products , emerging platforms like live-streaming e-commerce and Online-to-Offline services have shifted towards a "Container-based" display paradigm. In this setting, the user's decision process is inherently two-stage, as shown in Figure . First, selecting a container and second, purchasing specific items within that container. Consequently, the core objective of the advertising platform evolves from predicting the value of a single item to estimating the expected total GTV of a container, which is the aggregation of potential transactions across a dynamic set of items. To estimate the value of an advertisement, conventional methodologies typically employ point-wise prediction models, such as click-through rate and conversion rate estimators, combined with a deterministic pricing mechanism . However, applying this formula directly to the container scenario is non-trivial. A naive solution is to treat the container as a "super-item" with an average price or to predict the conversion probability of the most popular item. While simple, these methods overlook the internal structure of the container. They fail to model the user's complex interaction with the diverse items inside, leading to suboptimal ranking decisions where high-potential containers are undervalued because their aggregate value is not fully captured. For instance, a model might assign a high prediction score to a container based on a high click probability, prompting the merchant to pay a premium for the traffic. However, if the user eventually purchases only a low-priced item rather than the expected high-value one , the realized GTV will significantly lag behind the advertising cost, directly damaging the merchant's Return on Investment . Developing a robust GTV estimation model for containers faces several unique challenges arising from the granularity mismatch between ranking and transaction : : The observed transaction value is highly stochastic. A user might purchase a low-priced item in a premium store simply due to momentary preference, even if the room has high-value potential. Using such a single-impression GTV as the ground truth label introduces extreme variance, confusing the model during training. : Transaction data at the item level are highly sparse. Conversion signals at the container level are already much sparser than click signals, and conversions for specific items within a store are extremely sparse, making direct modeling at the item level highly challenging. : Containers are essentially dynamic sets of heterogeneous items. For instance, a store offers a mix of vouchers with varying discounts. Effectively modeling the interaction between Container-level features and Item-level features to predict the set-level value remains an open problem. To tackle these challenges, we propose a novel framework named Hierarchical GTV Estimation , which bridges the gap between container-level predicting and item-level predicting. HGE models the GTV estimation process as a hierarchical probabilistic task. Specifically, we design a Hybrid Set-Aware Encoder to capture the dynamic composition of items within a container, allowing for fine-grained feature interaction between the container context and item attributes. To mitigate label variance, we introduce a Proxy-Label Learning strategy that decomposes the post-click GTV into purchase probability, purchase quantity, and expected unit price, reducing the impact of outlier transactions. Furthermore, to address the severe label sparsity at the item level where conversion signals are extremely sparse. we propose a Residual Correction and Ensemble Fusion module. For samples with observed user behavior, RCEF aggregates item-level predictions to capture fine grained value signals. For samples lacking observed item interactions, the universal regression model is used to infer the potential container-level value.
