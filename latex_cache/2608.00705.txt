We present a triple-robustness analysis that holds a five-pipeline architecture matrix fixed and varies embedder (local e5-small vs.\ Azure text-embedding-3-small), corpus (DO-178C typed-edge requirements vs.\ Wikipedia paragraph chains via MuSiQue), and judge (paired GPT-5.4 $ $ GPT-4.1 on both corpora), over $2{ }4{,}440$ main-matrix runs, 600 cross-corpus runs, and over 5{,}000 faithfulness judgments. GraphRAG's graph walk floods the context window at precision 0.12--0.23, but the synthesizer cites selectively at precision 0.48--0.65; scoring the retrieved set as the attribution set inverts the architecture ranking, which reconciles part of the disagreement in prior reports. Answer-level citation winners are corpus- and stratum-conditional but embedder-robust: GraphRAG ties vanilla on short-hop DO-178C queries and wins every MuSiQue stratum, while agentic pipelines lead only on 3+-hop requirements queries. Faithfulness is corpus-conditional: on DO-178C it declines with hop distance (trend $p{<}0.05$ in three of four judge$ $embedder combinations); on Wikipedia chains neither judge shows a collapse. Single-judge LLM faithfulness is fragile to retrieval state: GPT-5.4's self-$ $ across embedders is $0.137$ (41\ against a same-day test--retest floor of $0.76$, and re-judging frozen inputs eleven weeks later gives $0.14$ for both judges. A learned router on dense embeddings alone reaches macro-$F_1$ $0.86$ on hop classification . We argue that RAG architecture claims should be tested at this level of robustness -- including robustness to the citation-measurement point -- before they are trusted. DO-178C-style aerospace requirements are authored as typed link graphs: each requirement parents, system-level intent, and verification artifacts. Certification authorities increasingly require auditable chains spanning two or more hops~. RAG architectures for this task have produced contradictory verdicts: Microsoft GraphRAG~ exploits structure but under-performs vector RAG on detailed queries~; Adaptive-RAG~ routes by complexity yet is hop-blind; LiSSA~ and TVR~ omit typed-graph reasoning. Prior evaluations report which architecture wins in a given setting, but not why the outcome changes when the setting changes. We address this with a analysis. Holding a five-pipeline architecture matrix fixed (vanilla, agentic, agentic+graph, GraphRAG, learned adaptive), we vary three orthogonal axes: the retrieval embedder , the corpus (DO-178C $ $ Wikipedia paragraph chains via MuSiQue), and the faithfulness judge . The design produces $2{ }4{,}440$ main-matrix runs (one full matrix per embedder), 600 cross-corpus runs plus a 200-run distractor-edge control, and over 5{,}000 faithfulness judgments. We make four contributions. GraphRAG's walk and its synthesizer pull in opposite directions: the walk fills the context at precision 0.12--0.23, the answer cites 3--5 IDs at precision 0.48--0.65 . Scoring the retrieved set as the attribution set -- a choice some comparisons make implicitly -- ranks GraphRAG last; scoring the answer's citations ranks it first or tied-first.
