Early codes select broad branches, so a routing error can exclude relevant items before fine-grained prediction begins. In multimodal recommendation, these coarse decisions must reflect collaborative preferences as well as item semantics. We introduce RoFiGR, a two-tree recommender that uses collaborative information in both SID construction and generation. It aligns cumulative L1--L2 code vectors with frozen collaborative item embeddings and explicitly encodes item distance and source level in the serialized history. During generation, a sequential teacher supervises legal branches. Fixed coarse-level weights maintain routing supervision, while a bounded adaptive residual allocates additional weight according to teacher--student demand. On Instruments, Arts, and Games, RoFiGR improves all five ranking metrics over the evaluated external baselines. Five-seed paired tests with Holm correction support the main NDCG@10 improvements. Comparisons with alternative collaborative supervision objectives and tokenizers support the proposed design. Matched task and decoding analyses distinguish the effects of training exposure, stream fusion, and candidate budgets. {a.pdf}\\ {bc.pdf} {figure1_v10.pdf} On e-commerce platforms, recommendation draws on both user interactions and product content such as text and images. Sequential models predict the next interaction by scoring candidate items~; generative recommenders instead decode an item's semantic identifier , often learned by residual quantization~. Under constrained decoding, early routing decisions determine which products remain reachable within the decoding budget. The challenge is to make these early semantic decisions reflect collaborative preferences while retaining content information for fine-grained product selection. Multimodal approaches incorporate text and image signals into identifiers and generation objectives~; RoFiGR follows MACRec's separate text and image SID trees. MACRec serializes historical text and image SIDs for a shared encoder--decoder and trains it with token-level cross-entropy. The prediction problem changes along each four-level SID: L1 starts without a predicted prefix, L2 further narrows the branch, and L3--L4 identify the item within it . Figure~ shows the level-wise differences in conditional accuracy. A uniform training objective does not explicitly account for these different roles. Collaborative information can help at two stages. During tokenization, frozen preference-aware item embeddings can align coarse content representations with behavioral similarity. During generation, a sequential teacher can compare the legal branches for a particular user history, providing information that a one-hot next-code label leaves unspecified. The first signal shapes coarse code vectors shared across users; the second guides history-conditioned branch selection. The history representation presents a related issue. After flattening multiple SIDs, token distance alone does not distinguish item boundaries and source levels. We therefore add item-distance and source-level biases to the first encoder self-attention layer. Figure~ examines these coordinates through attention patterns and prediction changes under level-specific interventions. RoFiGR combines these components in a two-tree multimodal generator. Fixed coarse-level distillation weights maintain supervision for routing, while a bounded adaptive residual directs additional weight to levels where the teacher provides a useful signal beyond the student and catalog prior. We evaluate alternative collaborative supervision objectives and tokenizers under matched task and decoding budgets, examining how coarse representations and branch supervision contribute to final ranking performance. Our contributions are: collaborative regularization of cumulative L1--L2 multimodal SID representations, evaluated through both ranking and discrete-tree diagnostics; anchored, demand-adaptive legal-branch distillation with explicit item/source coordinates in history encoding; and controlled comparisons with CF-to-token objectives, CF-aware tokenizers, and matched task and decoding budgets, supported by five-seed paired tests. Sequential recommenders predict the next interaction from historical behaviors, evolving from recurrent and convolutional encoders~ to self-attentive architectures~ enhanced with self-supervised objectives~. Multimodal variants further exploit item content to enrich preference modeling~. These models score candidate items directly. Their relative item scores can provide soft supervision for a generative recommender. TIGER~ established the two-stage paradigm that quantizes content embeddings into residual Semantic IDs and autoregressively generates the target identifier. Follow-ups improve identifier quality via collaborative, contrastive, or end-to-end tokenization~. Multimodal extensions broaden what SIDs encode~: MACRec~ aligns modalities at both tokenization and generation stages, and SynGR~ promotes cross-modal synergy during generation. RoFiGR focuses on coarse routing in this two-tree setting, combining preference-aware coarse representations with level-adaptive supervision over legal branches. Hierarchical item indexing~ and residual quantization organize item prediction into coarse-to-fine decisions. SID-MLP~ finds that the first token faces the largest candidate space and dominates prediction difficulty; later tokens perform local refinement once the prefix is fixed. How~Well~ identifies a trade-off between compositional generalization through prefix sharing and memorization of specific transitions, a strength of discriminative models~. Latte~ proves that a fixed semantic tree couples the probabilities of prefix-sharing items regardless of user context. To address such level heterogeneity, GLASS~ aggregates long-term interests for coarse tokens and retrieves local history for fine ones; Hi-SAM~ assigns cross-modal consensus to coarse levels and modality-specific details to deep ones; APAO~ reinforces vulnerable prefixes; other efforts reorganize level roles in the tokenizer~ or allocate inference computation by position~. RoFiGR uses collaborative information to shape the coarse code space and adapts branch-level supervision to the different demands of SID levels. TCA4Rec projects collaborative item scores into prefix-conditioned token distributions and optimizes a soft next-token objective. Align$^3$GR studies collaborative--semantic alignment, while MusicRec incorporates collaborative information into multimodal identifiers. Building on item-to-token score aggregation, RoFiGR couples legal-branch supervision with collaborative regularization of cumulative coarse SID vectors, anchored demand-adaptive layer allocation, and relative item/source encoding in a two-tree multimodal generator. RoFiGR combines collaborative coarse SID learning with history-conditioned branch supervision in a two-tree multimodal recommender. Coarse-code alignment shapes the candidate groups, item/source-aware attention preserves the structure of the interaction history, and anchored adaptive distillation guides early branch selection. Experiments on Instruments, Arts, and Games show consistent ranking gains, supported by tokenizer, supervision, and candidate-budget controls. The diagnostics connect these gains to greater behavioral-neighbor sharing and improved prefix survival. Together, the results identify coarse routing as a useful point for bringing collaborative preferences into generative recommendation.
