AMO preserves a recommendation core while learning appended strategies.} Large language models increasingly shape recommendations across chatbots, e-commerce, and user-generated content platforms, creating demand for Generative Engine Optimization to improve item visibility in generated responses. Existing GEO studies are mainly , optimizing visibility under general-purpose queries. However, a shared item description usually serves users with different needs and decision goals, resulting in its visibility gains being dependent on the request. To investigate how changing intent affects visibility, we formulate to evaluate shared rewrites across need specificity and decision progression. We revisit existing GEO methods and find that their visibility gains vary across user intents. More critically, learning-based prompt optimization can underperform its handwritten seed on average. Tracing these updates reveals from explicit recommendation instructions toward generic promotional tactics. To address this drift, we propose , which preserves recommendation instructions in a fixed core while learning appended strategies from citation feedback. Across four domains and ten intent states, AMO improves average citation-rank gain by 35\ Ablations show that learned additions improve on the fixed core alone and that AMO outperforms full-prompt optimization initialized from that core. It also achieves the highest gains in an agentic recommendation pipeline and, after separate adaptation, on Doubao, a commercially deployed, closed-source online assistant. We find that strategies effective for broad requests can lose visibility when an item closely matches the stated need, and that unrestricted prompt optimization can erode the advantage of an effective recommendation seed. To address this challenge, we propose AMO, which keeps recommendation instructions fixed while learning complementary rewriting strategies from citation feedback. AMO achieves the strongest aggregate visibility gains among the evaluated methods, with ablations showing that learned additions improve on the fixed core. As generated responses become a new source of organic traffic, brand owners increasingly need ways to improve their visibility in these answers. Generative Engine Optimization studies how to rewrite item content so that generative engines are more likely to use it when answering relevant user queries~. Prompt injections and strategically optimized text can promote target items in conversational search and generative engines~. These approaches exploit content as an instruction or ranking-control signal. Our white-hat setting instead requires natural, factual descriptions faithful to the original item, and studies whether recommendation evidence remains useful across intents. White-hat GEO seeks to improve a document's visibility while keeping its content natural and faithful to the source. LLM-based white-hat SEO studies quality-preserving rewrites for search rankings~, while related work considers content fidelity in ad retrieval and answer inclusion~. For generative engines, GEO~ and C-SEO Bench~ examine how rewriting affects source visibility in generated answers. To guide rewriting, methods use inferred search goals to identify what the content should emphasize~, or learn reusable rules reflecting engine preferences~. MetaOpt further uses visibility feedback to optimize the rewriting prompt~. By updating the instructions used for subsequent rewrites, prompt optimization learns a reusable strategy from engine responses. Our work complements these approaches by evaluating shared rewrites across recommendation needs and decision stages. AMO builds on feedback-driven prompt optimization while retaining recommendation instructions in a fixed core.
