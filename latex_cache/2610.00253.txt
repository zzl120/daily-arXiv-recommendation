The collection harness cut many answers short: 83.1\ Large language models now answer questions that used to go to a search engine: what to buy, which supplier to shortlist, what a company's reputation is~. Generative engine optimisation treats those answers as a surface that content can be shaped for and measured on~. This paper starts from that premise and tests the assumption beneath any per-system measurement of it: that an answer can be attributed to the system that produced it, and that what the measurement observes is a property of that system. The query domain and the collection harness are the two competing sources this corpus lets us examine. The test uses one corpus at two units of observation, and the two units give different answers. The first unit is the single stored response. A character $n$-gram classifier identifies which of five deployed endpoints produced an answer with 97.84\ The second unit is the model-by-domain-by-condition aggregate, the unit an AI-visibility report summarises. Twelve behavioural features of brand volume, concentration, sentiment and source behaviour separate four systems at 66.53\ The discriminative information about a deployed endpoint that survives a change of query domain in this corpus sits in the surface form of its answers. The brand behaviour that practitioners summarise, and that the earlier version of this work treated as a system signature, varies with the query domain, and the design cannot say how much of it belongs to the system. We state four questions. Given a single stored response to a brand-related prompt, how accurately can the deployed endpoint that produced it be identified, and how well calibrated is that identification? What carries the response-level signal: which brands the answer names, or how the answer is laid out? How much of the signal survives a change of query domain, at each of the two units of observation? What do aggregated behavioural profiles add, and where do they fail? The contributions are four.
