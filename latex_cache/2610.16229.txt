Multi-modal Recommendation, Graph Convolutional Networks, Wavelet Decomposition, Modality Fusion In the dynamic realm of e-commerce platforms, recommendation systems play an indispensable role in assisting users discover items of interest. To meet such demands, many systems rely on collaborative filtering techniques, which leverage user-item historical interactions to capture users’ patterns of interest. However, due to the rapid increase in multimedia content~ comprising images, textual data, and videos, many traditional collaborative filtering frameworks face the new challenge of incorporating diverse modalities effectively. This has become a core focus within the research domain of multi-modal recommender systems . In the existing literature, conventional MRSs focus on modality fusion as the principal means of modeling user preferences across modalities. Pioneering works, including VBPR~ and Deepstyle~, explore visual modality fusion by projecting visual features into a lower-dimensional space and subsequently integrating them with item-identity embeddings via concatenation and element-wise summation. Since user-item interactions can be structured intrinsically as a bipartite graph, researchers have increasingly adopted graph neural networks~ to encode the high-order behavioral and multi-modal semantic information into both the user and item encoding process. For instance, MMGCN~ projects the heterogeneous modality inputs into multiple user-item views and characterises user interests via the concatenation of the learned modality embeddings. As an extension, GRCN~ uses multi-modal signals to refine the user–item view by suppressing erroneous links arising from behavioral interactions. To validate the benefits of latent structures between items for item representation learning, LATTICE~ first constructs a modality-specific graph that reflects different semantic viewpoints of the item. An attention mechanism is then used to determine the relative importance of each modality within the fusion process. More recently, FREEDOM~ demonstrated that trainable latent graphs are not critical and, therefore, are frozen prior to training, improving the overall computational and memory efficiency of the model. The LGMRec~ was subsequently proposed to explore the use of hypergraphs for discerning users' global interests while complementing local graph-based embeddings, thereby generating a more robust representation of user preferences. Although existing studies have demonstrated some success in exploiting multiple modalities, these modality signals are integrated indiscriminately, allowing redundant cross-modal signals to permeate the item representation learning process. This coarse-grained integration causes excessive informational redundancy, resulting in inconsistent performance across modalities a limitation that becomes even more pronounced when additional channels are incorporated. To illustrate the above, we adopt the real-world Tiktok dataset~, consisting of three distinct modalities for our experiments. We performed experiments under varying input conditions and evaluated three recent state-of-the-art fusion based models: MMGCN~, FREEDOM~, and LGMRec~ for comparison. Leveraging well-established principles in signal processing~, we draw inspiration from the concept of wavelet transformation, which has been shown to be effective in modality denoising~. Through wavelet transformation, modality signals are decomposed into multiple levels of granularities, with each set of wavelet coefficients capturing progressively finer or coarser details of the modality features. This decomposition enables high-level multi-resolution analysis~, thereby facilitating both temporal and spatial localization that are critical for effective processing of sequential and spatial modalities. Moreover, the frequency-time localization property of wavelet transformation facilitates the suppression of dominant high-frequency components typically associated with noise~, thus allowing the extraction of most essential features through strategically guided attenuation. Motivated by the ability of multi-resolution analysis to facilitate robust feature extraction, we propose a new multi-scale modality wavelet recommender system designed to extract essential features and effectively integrate diverse modalities of varying types. In essence, MMWave comprises three key components: 1) wavelet modality mixer, 2) structure-aware multi-modal graph learning, and 3) adaptive modality regulator. To ensure that essential modality features are extracted, MMWave performs wavelet-based modality decomposition by breaking down each modality into sub-bands of features comprising both temporal and frequency characteristics. Since noise contamination often occurs in the form of high-frequency components~, the proposed model capitalizes on the single low-frequency component within the localized time-frequency representation for modality signal attenuation. In this regard, we introduce an efficient yet effective parametric filter to modulate the essential signals that approximate the original modality features. The uni-modal features are then reconstructed only with the modulated modality signals, ensuring that the most essential and representative features are captured. To account for both collaborative and semantically affiliated information, we design a new structure-aware multi-modal graph learning module to encode high-order behavioral and structural characteristics pertaining to both users and items. Finally, to mitigate MIE and achieve adaptive integration, we formulate a new adaptive modality regulator module that derives fused representations from the modulated uni-modal signals before using them to assign reciprocal weights across modalities. This process allows the model to capture complementary information with greater precision. Experiments on four real-world datasets show the efficacy of the proposed model. The exploitation of multiple modalities~ has fueled the advancement of MRSs, which enables a deeper understanding of users' fine-grained modality preferences. Conventionally, pre-trained neural networks such as VGG-16~ for visual input and the Sentence Transformer~ for textual input have been utilized for the extraction of multi-modal features. These features serve as auxiliary information to MRSs. For instance, the classical VBPR~ unimodal approach makes use of visual modality features by first concatenating with its item ID embeddings before applying a multi-layer perceptron to project the embeddings into the latent space for representation learning. To model users' varying degrees of preferences, MAML~ adopts a two-layer, attentional feedforward network on the visual and textual representations of the items. Following a similar line of work, VECF~ models intricate user preferences by leveraging pre-segmented image patches and an attention mechanism to identify important regions of the image. Since user-item interactions can be inherently represented as a bipartite graph~, recent works began leveraging graph neural networks to capture high-order complex relationships between users and items. For instance, LATTICE~ accounts for essential item-item latent connections by first constructing similarity graphs that are distinct to each modality. The fusion of these modal-specific graphs is then guided by importance scores these scores are used to combine the graphs prior to applying graph convolution for generating the final fused representation. Similar to redundancies in adaptive filtering~, redundancies that exist in graph learning have also been reported in~, which highlights the inefficiency of learning graphs during training. In this aspect, FREEDOM~ freezes the graph structure while effectively capturing important relationships between the modalities of item-item. More recently, LGMRec~ adopts hypergraph learning by treating attributes as hyperedges to capture global structural dependencies between items and attributes, thereby modeling users’ global interests. CMDL~ captures modality-specific and shared information through modality disentangled learning, using contrastive regularization to separate modality-specific and invariant representations. While majority of the works have shown promising results with dual modalities , their performance deteriorates significantly as more modalities are incorporated, exposing the modality inhibitance effect and underscoring the complexity of effective modality integration. In this section, we distinguish the modality inhibitance effect from modality imbalance~, a widely recognized issue in multi-modal learning, by first presenting their conceptual definitions, followed by their corresponding mathematical formulations. Modality imbalance occurs when a dominant and faster-converging modality overshadows its weaker counterparts. In this work, we described the modality inhibitance effect, which hinders the performance of existing fusion-based models in multi-modal recommendation systems. To address the identified issue, we propose the MMWave multi-modal recommender, which emphasizes two key aspects: the extraction of essential uni-modal features and the integration across semantically distinct modalities. In particular, we introduce an efficient yet effective wavelet-based approach to extract essential and informative modality signals for attenuation. To capture high-order behavioral and semantically affiliated modality signals, we then design a structure-aware multi-modal graph learning module that encapsulates structural patterns into modality representations. Finally, we propose a new adaptive modality regulator module that effectively integrates varying modalities through a designed cross-weighting schema, enabling the model to capture complementary signals with higher precision.
