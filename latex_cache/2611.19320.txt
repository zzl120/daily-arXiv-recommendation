Sequential recommendation~ has long been a critical domain within recommendation systems. In general, these models recommend the next item by capturing the underlying intents within temporal sequences of user interactions. Due to the sequential nature, several recent studies~ focus on the generative paradigm. Within this space, a significant focus~ has emerged on the use of Semantic Identifiers . This usually includes two steps of development, and . First, SIDs are generated by encoding item semantics with hierarchical coding, such as RQ-VAE~ or RK-Means~. Subsequently, the chronological items interaction is mapped into a sequence of item SIDs and fed into the generative recommender. The next item SID is then generated via the autoregressive process. Based on this architecture, existing efforts focus on integrating and aligning collaborative information into language models~, or maintaining more effective SID representations~ to better align with recommenders. Despite their effectiveness, these models often rely on multiple alignment tasks, complex architectural designs, or extensive hyperparameter tuning. These issues could stem from the original design of the learning framework for generative models. First, the retrieval task involves substantial ambiguity when models are learned to generate the next item directly from the entire item corpus. In other words, models during training would only be asked to memorize fixed patterns. In particular, these models could suffer from out-of-distribution patterns, such as unseen compositions, as these patterns are rarely observed during training. Moreover, recent studies~ point out that the collaborative signals are beneficial to be added into the constructions of SIDs or model architectures, thereby bridging the gap between semantics and behavioral interaction patterns. However, it is arguable that the collaborative information can be naturally captured when training generative retrieval models on sequential data. From this aspect, retrieval models are expected to retain sufficient collaborative knowledge, without the need of introducing external models and extensive hyperparameter tuning. These observations motivate us to revisit the design of the sequential training objective itself. In particular, we note that the current approach only defines the objective to align sequences with ground-truth target items, while leaving negative compositions unspecified. This idea is not well-aligned with Bayesian personalized ranking~ ~, which emphasizes the importance of relative rankings between positive and negative samples. To mitigate these challenges, we propose to add a simple multiple-choice auxiliary task to the learning objective as a natural and effective remedy. Specifically, the multiple-choice auxiliary task can explicitly present a subset of candidate items to encourage models to learn both positive and negative signals. This additional task can be seamlessly fit into the original sequential models, such as TIGER~, even without relying on pretrained language weights. The whole framework remains simple and elegant without introducing any complicated training strategy. In this paper, we propose to integrate the multiple-choice task into generative sequential recommendation. Extensive experiments validate the effectiveness and investigate the underlying principles about how this auxiliary task addresses the aforementioned limitations. Our contributions can be summarized as follows: We provide a simple manner to integrate the multiple-choice auxiliary task into generative recommendation models, along with theoretical analysis of its advantages. We show that the multiple-choice auxiliary task can successfully mitigate the limitations and achieve better recommendation performance. We conduct a series of in-depth analysis, including the exploration of the underlying factors behind the performance, the relative importance of the main sequential task and the auxiliary task, and the impacts of different candidate sampling strategies. In the generative retrieval process, the training distribution of the main task is highly sparse, where many item compositions rarely or never co-occur. Consequently, the model struggles to capture the underlying behaviors of unseen compositions. For instance, users interested in guitars are more likely to purchase relevant accessories rather than items related to orchestra. This lack of joint observations for compositions between guitars and orchestra could lead to weak generalization on such unseen combinations during testing. The situation can potentially be even worse, when items share similar semantic information but exhibit different behavioral patterns. The ill-defined representation function $g$ may induce unintended correlations. Since the training distribution lacks the composition for these unrelated items, predefined semantics force them to share a similar representation, leading to incorrect recommendations. The multiple-choice task then augments the training distribution by providing candidate items, and asking the model to pick the correct one. Through this process, the model starts to capture the compositions between items driven by different intents, which facilitates the learning of a more well-defined representation function to generalize from the training distribution to the test distribution. In particular, since $i_{u_t} c_{u_t}$, the auxiliary task forces the model to memorize the positive signal between history $s_{u_t}$ and target $i_{u_t}$. This is crucial because the training labels could become part of the input sequence. While the previous sequential task constructions often overlook these positive compositions, the multiple-choice task can also effectively bridge the gap. In this paper, we integrate the multiple-choice task as an auxiliary into generative recommendation models to alleviate the lack of comparative signals. Through analysis from the perspectives of information bottleneck and compositional generalization, we argue that the multiple-choice auxiliary task can improve the recommendation model by encouraging a more robust representation function and promoting richer compositional patterns. We conduct extensive experiments on four real-world datasets and demonstrate that our simple and elegant learning framework achieves competitive performance against mainstream strong baselines. Our work identifies the fundamental challenges of generative retrieval with SIDs, which provides an alternative direction for improving generative recommenders.
