The rotation experiment shows a larger Dual advantage as mismatch increases, while the rank--sample-size grids show that more training data can reverse a low-sample preference for Shared. Geometry choice should therefore account for both directional mismatch and available training data. Our exact risk boundary assumes local, isotropic Gaussian operator noise. Extending risk estimation to anisotropic noise and directly to ranking metrics would make geometry selection more closely reflect retrieval performance.
