Multimodal Representation Learning Next-POI Recommendation Contrastive Learning Large Language Models With the growing availability of multimodal data, modern recommender systems increasingly leverage cross-modal representation learning in data-driven applications~. In scenarios such as POI recommendation, item-related information is typically derived from {heterogeneous data sources}, including textual descriptions, visual content, and structured attributes. Effectively integrating {multimodal tourism-related information from heterogeneous data sources} into a unified and informative representation remains a fundamental challenge~. Recent advances in large language models provide a promising direction for modeling multimodal tourism-related information by transforming information from heterogeneous data sources into a shared semantic representation space~. However, {heterogeneous data sources may contain semantically diverse, redundant, inconsistent, or only partially informative signals that cannot be fully resolved through semantic unification alone and can introduce noise in multimodal data, thereby degrading the quality of the learned representations.} Despite these challenges, multimodal representation learning is typically tightly coupled with downstream tasks, where the learned representations are directly optimized based on task-specific performance signals~. While this design can improve task-specific performance, it introduces several limitations. First, the learned representations become {tied to a particular downstream architecture and its optimization process}, limiting their generality and transferability across different tasks or domains~. Second, {joint optimization of multimodal fusion and downstream recommendation models requires representation learning to be repeated when the downstream architecture changes, increasing} computational cost and reducing scalability~. More importantly, {supervision from downstream objectives} biases multimodal fusion toward task-optimal solutions rather than intrinsic cross-modal relationships, limiting cross-modal interaction modeling~. {These challenges become particularly evident in multimodal POI recommendation, where multimodal tourism-related information collected from heterogeneous data sources must be integrated with sequential user interactions. Such heterogeneous information characteristics motivate the need for a more flexible and decoupled representation learning framework.} As an important task within POI recommendation, next-POI recommendation aims to predict the next location users will visit based on their historical interaction sequence~. Compared to general POI recommendation, next-POI recommendation focuses on modeling short-term sequential patterns and temporal dynamics~. In this setting, multimodal POI information provides complementary signals for capturing contextual characteristics and user preferences~, and its effective integration in sequential recommendation has attracted increasing attention in recent work~. To address these limitations, we propose LM-based nified ultimodal nformation epresentation , a decoupled multimodal representation learning framework. {Unlike existing approaches that tightly couple representation learning with downstream models, LUMIR learns representations independently of downstream model architectures and their optimization objectives.} {Leveraging the prior knowledge and representation capabilities of LLMs, LUMIR learns unified representations from multimodal tourism-related information collected from heterogeneous data sources . On top of this unified representation space, LUMIR models cross-modal interactions while reducing the influence of redundant information and incorporating sequential signals as structural guidance for representation learning.} {Through stochastic modality augmentation and information compression, the framework is designed to reduce its dependence on noisy or partially informative modality-specific signals without requiring explicit data-quality annotations.} The learned representations are evaluated on POI recommendation as a downstream task to assess their effectiveness in addressing key challenges in multimodal recommender systems. To the best of our knowledge, this work is the first to investigate {a multimodal representation learning framework decoupled from downstream architectures and their optimization } in the context of next-POI recommendation. The key contributions are summarized as follows: We propose LUMIR, a decoupled multimodal representation learning framework that separates representation learning from {the architecture and optimization of} downstream recommendation tasks. This design facilitates a modular approach to multimodal modeling. We propose an LLM-based representation unification framework {for multimodal tourism-related information collected from heterogeneous data sources,} in which the encoder is trainable and jointly optimized with the multimodal fusion process, enabling adaptive unified representations tailored to cross-modal interactions. We interpret multimodal representation learning under the information bottleneck framework and propose a unified objective function that jointly integrates modality compression and sequence-aware alignment, enabling the learned representations to capture both cross-modal interactions and sequential structure. We conduct experiments on a multimodal POI dataset under the next-POI recommendation setting, showing that the representations improve performance compared to multimodal baselines. This section reviews recent work on task-dependent multimodal representation learning, LLM-based representation unification, and next-POI recommendation. Multimodal representation learning has been extensively studied to learn unified representations from {heterogeneous data sources}, where information is distributed across textual, visual, and structured modalities~. Recent advances therefore move beyond simple alignment to model cross-modal interactions by capturing redundant, unique, and synergistic information across modalities~. Despite differences in application domains and model architectures, these approaches generally learn multimodal representations {through joint optimization with downstream models}, where the fusion process is tightly coupled with downstream objectives. For instance, prior work has evaluated multimodal representations directly based on recommendation performance~, learned joint vision-language representations through task-driven supervision~, or optimized multimodal models for downstream tasks such as classification and question answering~. {Such designs produce representations that are tightly optimized together with a particular downstream architecture, limiting their modular reuse across different recommendation models.} Recent advances in LLMs provide a promising direction for multimodal representation learning by enabling multimodal information {collected from heterogeneous data sources to be represented within a shared semantic embedding space~.} Such semantic unification helps reduce modality heterogeneity while providing a common foundation for subsequent multimodal representation learning. Recent studies leverage LLMs as a semantic bridge across application domains. For example, LLM-based semantic enhancement frameworks have been applied to unify textual and visual signals for POI representation learning in the recommendation domain~. In vision-language tasks, models such as BLIP-2 connect visual encoders with LLMs, enabling images to be represented and processed within a language-centric semantic space~. Similarly, models such as LLaVA align visual and language features via instruction tuning, enabling unified multimodal understanding~. Building on this prior work, our work uses LLMs to {semantically unify multimodal tourism-related information collected from heterogeneous data sources before downstream representation learning.} Next-POI recommendation is a key task in tourism recommendation, which aims to predict the next location a user will visit based on historical trajectory~. POI data is inherently multimodal, with rich information sources associated with each location. Recent work has explored incorporating additional contextual and multimodal information, such as geographical features~, semantic information~, and visual content~. With the rapid development of LLMs, their capabilities have increasingly been incorporated into POI recommendation to improve recommendation performance. For example, LLMs have been leveraged to enrich multimodal POI representations by transforming textual and visual information into structured representations~. In addition, methods such as SeCor align LLM-generated representations with collaborative signals~. {These works highlight the potential of LLM-based representations for integrating multimodal information, motivating our use of LLM-based encoders within a decoupled representation learning framework.} {Despite these advances, existing approaches generally couple multimodal representation learning with downstream recommendation objectives, making the learned representations {dependent on a particular model configuration.} Such {end-to-end optimization} may dominate the fusion process and limit the modeling of intrinsic relationships among heterogeneous information sources. This section evaluates LUMIR framework from two perspectives. The multimodal fusion process in LUMIR is analyzed to understand how meaningful structures emerge during representation learning. The learned embeddings are assessed through their performance on downstream next-POI recommendation tasks. Due to the absence of direct supervision from downstream recommendation tasks, the quality of the learned representations in the multimodal fusion stage emerges from the training dynamics of the representation learning process itself. Different from existing unsupervised embedding methods that rely on fixed iterative propagation~, LUMIR involves learnable multimodal fusion with complex training dynamics, making the training process a critical factor in determining representation quality. As a result, insufficient training may lead to under-expressive representations that fail to capture meaningful multimodal structures, while excessive training may overfit dominant interaction patterns, biasing representations toward popular POIs and under-representing long-tail items~. Therefore, two aspects are examined: how the learning process improves the structural properties of the embeddings; and how these changes affect downstream recommendation performance. Representations obtained from direct concatenation of untrained modality features are used as an initial status and compared with those learned at different epochs . The range of epochs is selected to capture the evolution of representations at different training stages within a practical computational budget. The reasons for these differences across backbones are as follows. This discrepancy can be attributed to the interaction between the alignment objective and the inductive biases of different sequential models. BERT4Rec adopts a bidirectional self-attention mechanism, where each position is conditioned on both past and future context~. In this setting, the sequence-aware alignment objective is naturally compatible, as it encourages representations to capture global relational dependencies within the sequence. In contrast, SASRec and GRU4Rec follow an autoregressive paradigm, where predictions are strictly conditioned on past interactions~. Under such unidirectional modeling, the contribution of the alignment objective becomes less dominant, as these models primarily rely on directional sequential dependencies. Nevertheless, the learned representations remain effective, and the modality compression objective alone already provides strong performance gains.
