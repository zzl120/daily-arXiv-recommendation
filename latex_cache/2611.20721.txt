Generative retrieval takes a different route . Every document receives a short identifier made of discrete tokens, and a sequence model reads the query and generates the identifier of a relevant document token by token~. When the identifiers are derived from document content, they are called semantic IDs ~. SIDs are now part of industrial search and recommendation systems~ and are also used as learnable discrete features~. An SID is the address at which the generator has to find a document . Documents that share an SID can only be retrieved together, and the prefixes of the SIDs form the coarse-to-fine space that the generator walks through. Whatever query--document relevance the upstream embedding has learned, the part that quantization discards is no longer available to the generator, which sees documents only through their SIDs. What the SIDs preserve therefore bounds what generative retrieval can recall. We first reproduced the main families of SID construction on the same search corpus: residual $k$-means , RQ-VAE~, a hybrid of residual $k$-means and finite scalar quantization ~, and post-hoc capacity balancing of the last level. Each family optimizes a criterion defined on documents alone: geometric distortion, reconstruction error, fit to a lattice, or bucket capacity. Search, however, depends on relations between queries and documents. Quantization always discards information, and what matters is where the loss falls. If it falls on the directions that a query uses to separate relevant from irrelevant documents, retrieval degrades even when the distortion is small: two reconstructions with the same per-document error can preserve or swap the order of a query's top candidates . An SID tokenizer for search should therefore be built and judged by query--document retrieval, which raises two questions: in which space to quantize, so that the loss avoids the directions that queries rely on, and how to keep each query's ranking of its candidates once documents are quantized. Recent search systems have begun to bring query--item signals into SID construction through contrastive objectives on the representation before quantization~. DSI~ showed that a sequence-to-sequence model can map queries directly to document identifiers, NCI~ improved the decoder, and GenRet~ learned the tokenization. TIGER~ built semantic IDs with RQ-VAE and generated them for sequential recommendation. RIPOR~ argued that document identifiers should be built from query--document relevance rather than from document content alone: it quantizes document representations from a relevance-trained encoder and scales generative retrieval to millions of passages with prefix-oriented ranking optimization. Industrial systems now generate SIDs for recommendation, search and advertising~. CQ-SID~ builds semantic cluster IDs for e-commerce search from category and query--item signals, WeGR~ unifies behavior-aware tokenization and preference alignment for generative retrieval in search, and QARM V2~ uses SIDs as learnable features for user sequence modeling. Our work shares RIPOR's premise but concerns the tokenizer alone, under a fixed upstream retrieval embedding and a separately trained generator, and is complementary to improvements of the generator. Most SID tokenizers are residual quantizers~: each level quantizes what the previous levels left over, using $k$-means~, learned codebooks as in VQ-VAE and RQ-VAE~, or fixed lattices as in FSQ~. R3-VAE~ removes a learned reference direction and rates codewords by their cosine with the residual; CARE-SID uses this rating residual quantization, in the top-1 form of the public R3-VAE implementation, as its kernel. PRQ-KMeans~ brings projection residuals to residual $k$-means, and RQ-Kmeans+ in GPR~ initializes the codebooks of an RQ-VAE with RQ-KMeans and adds a residual connection to its encoder so that the encoder output stays close to its input early in training. LC-Rec~ assigns conflict-free indices through uniform semantic mapping, and LETTER, CoST and SimCIT add collaborative or contrastive objectives to item tokenization~. Closest to our setting, search systems inject query--item signals into the representation before quantization: CQ-SID applies a bidirectional query--item InfoNCE loss to a trainable encoder, WeGR adds query-to-item and item-to-item contrastive objectives, and UniSearch trains its video encoder with a residual query--video contrastive loss jointly with the generator. CARE-SID shares the idea of aligning documents with queries before quantization and differs in how the alignment is attached to an existing retrieval stack: it keeps the upstream retrieval embedding frozen and learns only a residual alignment whose zero-initialized output makes training start exactly from the warm-started quantizer , and an explicit anchor loss keeps the aligned space close to the original embedding, which also remains the reconstruction target. Our design analysis found this anchoring necessary to avoid bucket merging . We also measure when such alignment pays off: its gain is large for an embedding that has not been trained on search data and narrower for a search-tuned one . Product quantization and its optimized variant minimize distortion for compressed nearest-neighbor search~. JPQ~ trains the query encoder and the PQ centroids jointly with a ranking loss, RepCONC~ learns discrete representations with constrained clustering, and MoPQ~ argues that quantization should target query--document matching rather than reconstruction and optimizes the matching probability of queries against reconstructed documents. A mismatch is silent, because dimensions, data fields and loss curves all look normal, so before training and evaluation we compute the mean cosine between the embeddings and their reconstructions by these codebooks and abort if it is below 0.70. The reconstruction loss of CARE-SID is computed document by document: each document is compared only with its own reconstruction. Retrieval, however, depends on the relative order of several candidates for the same query. Two documents that each move only slightly can swap a decisive pair, and two reconstructions with the same per-document error can affect the ranking very differently . We also tried to constrain the discrete codes directly, for example by asking relevant documents of a query to share prefixes; this easily over-merges buckets, and in one extreme setting the number of first-level codewords in use dropped from 16,305 to 9,069 while retrieval became worse. Offline, every video's document embedding is encoded into a three-digit SID with Algorithm~, and a mapping from SIDs to document IDs is maintained; new videos are encoded incrementally with the same weights. Encoding needs only the alignment network, the reference vector and the three codebooks; the reconstruction head is not served. Online, the GR model reads the query and the SID sequence of the user's previously clicked videos and generates the SID of a target video digit by digit, P = _{ =1}^{3}P , where $H$ is the user history and each level uses its own code tokens. Decoding is constrained by a prefix tree of valid SIDs, and generated SIDs are mapped back to videos; when an SID holds several videos, they are taken in order of relevance within the bucket. CARE-LR keeps the CARE-SID encoding interface and leaves the first digit of every video unchanged, since everything that digit depends on is frozen. Because it updates the second- and third-level codebooks, some videos receive new second or third digits, so deploying it requires refreshing the SID mapping together with the training labels and user histories of the GR model. An SID tokenizer bounds what generative retrieval can recall, yet common SID constructions optimize criteria defined on document embeddings alone and leave uncontrolled where the quantization loss falls. We built and judged SID tokenizers by query--document retrieval instead, while still encoding every document from its own embedding. CARE-SID quantizes in a retrieval-aligned space: it places a zero-initialized residual alignment network, trained with anchored contrastive learning, in front of the rating residual quantizer of R3-VAE, improves reconstruction-view Recall@10 by 2.48 points over RQ-KMeans, and is deployed in the generative retrieval stage of an industrial video search system. CARE-LR distills the candidate distributions of the original embedding into the SID reconstructions; this listwise distillation accounts for most of its further gain of 0.86 points, for a total of 3.34 points over RQ-KMeans. The contrastive alignment pays off most when the embedding has not been trained on search data, and the choice of embedding moves SID recall far more than the choice of quantizer.
