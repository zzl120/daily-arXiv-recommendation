Because local search operates over millions of POIs under strict latency and cost constraints, modern systems decompose the problem into retrieval and reranking stages: retrieval narrows the search space, while reranking refines the final ordering over a smaller but still potentially large candidate set. Even at the reranking stage, the pool may contain hundreds or thousands of POIs, making expensive listwise or LLM-based reranking over all candidates impractical for real-time serving. A deployable reranker must therefore improve semantic and numerical reasoning while preserving bounded inference cost. In this paper we focus on the reranking stage. Our starting point is a commercial local search pipeline that, like many systems in the industry, relied for years on query-type classification, type-specific retrievers, and an XGBoost~ reranker operating on hand-crafted features such as BM25-style~ textual scores, Jaccard/Levenshtein distances, geographic proximity, and engagement signals, together with heuristic chain rankers and blending rules. While efficient, this design suffers from two limitations that we found to be dominant drivers of quality loss. POI relevance is not purely textual and not purely numerical. It depends on interactions among query semantics, POI semantics, user or target location, POI location, and numerical quality attributes such as distance, rating, and review count. Consider the query A very close candidate whose evidence suggests a mixed Spanish--Italian restaurant may be less desirable than a slightly farther POI whose name, categories, menu, and descriptions strongly indicate an authentic Italian restaurant. However, this preference is not absolute: if the authentic Italian restaurant is only a few meters farther away, semantic match should likely dominate; if it is more than a kilometer farther away, the closer mixed-cuisine restaurant may be preferable, especially when the query emphasizes proximity or walking distance. Thus, distance cannot be used as a trivial reranking feature independently of lexical semantics. Its contribution depends on query intent, POI evidence, and the magnitude of the geographic difference. We refer to this setting as : POI ranking where geographic proximity and numerical quality signals are interpreted jointly with query intent and POI semantics, rather than as independent ranking features. The same issue appears across common local-search intents. For distance should dominate once candidates satisfy the pharmacy intent. For ratings and review evidence should matter more, but only among candidates that semantically match the cuisine. For entity match and operational constraints may dominate rating or popularity. Transformer-based cross-encoders~ excel at semantic matching between query and POI text, but raw numerical values such as , , or are poor semantic objects for attention. Conversely, gradient-boosted trees~ exploit structured numerical features precisely, but provide only shallow semantic matching. A common solution is to compute a neural semantic score and combine it with numerical feature scores through a weighted sum. However, this late scalar fusion cannot let the semantic encoder itself condition query--POI matching on coarse spatial and quality concepts, and it cannot easily express interactions such as distance mattering more for or queries, ratings mattering more for queries, or review count mattering more when reliability is implied. We present H2CE , a serving-constrained POI reranker for query-conditioned semantic--numeric interaction. H2CE combines two complementary mechanisms. First, its represents numerical attributes in two forms: bucketized natural-language descriptors inside the cross-encoder input, and exact scalar values processed by dedicated numerical MLPs. The semantic flow converts distance, rating, and review count into disambiguated descriptors such as and allowing transformer self-attention to condition semantic matching on coarse spatial and quality concepts. The numerical flow preserves fine-grained scalar precision lost by bucketization. H2CE then fuses the cross-encoder CLS embedding and numerical embeddings through latent-space aggregation, using a learned non-linear classifier rather than a scalar weighted sum. This enables interactions such as preferring a slightly farther POI when semantic match and review evidence are stronger, or preferring the closer POI when candidates are semantically and qualitatively similar. Second, H2CE uses a two-stage pointwise-plus-pairwise architecture. Stage~1 applies the heterogeneous encoder pointwise to all retrieved candidates, producing a scalable coarse ranking and selecting the top~$K$. Stage~2 performs direct head-to-head comparison among those top-$K$ candidates using the same heterogeneous representation adapted to pairwise inputs. This makes relative feature tradeoffs explicit: the pairwise model can compare the semantic evidence, distance, rating, and review count of two competing POIs directly, and Copeland aggregation converts these preferences into the final top-$K$ ordering. Restricting pairwise reranking to $K{=}10$ keeps inference tractable, reducing the comparison cost from full $O $ reranking to $O )$ ordered non-self comparisons. We report NDCG~ and Rank-Deviation Quality ~. RDQ is designed for rankings where item quality is defined by relative preferences among candidates rather than by absolute category labels. Given a query $q$ and $N$ retrieved candidate POIs $=\{d_1, ,d_N\}$, the system first encodes each query--POI pair with a Heterogeneous Feature Encoder . The HFE has a semantic flow, where the query, POI textual fields, and bucketized numerical descriptors are processed by a cross-encoder, and a numerical flow, where exact distance, rating, and review-count values are processed by dedicated MLPs. A latent aggregation layer fuses the cross-encoder CLS embedding with the numerical embeddings, producing a relevance representation that can express query-conditioned interactions among lexical semantics, geospatial proximity, and quality signals. The resulting representation is used in two stages. Stage~1 applies the HFE pointwise to all $N$ candidates, producing a scalable coarse ranking and selecting the top-$K$ candidates. Stage~2 applies a pairwise version of the same heterogeneous representation to all $K $ ordered non-self pairs among the top-$K$ candidates. Its outputs form a pairwise scoring matrix that is aggregated with Copeland voting to produce the final top-$K$ ordering. The final ranked list concatenates the pairwise-reranked top-$K$ candidates with the remaining candidates in Stage~1 order. The HFE encodes each query--POI pair through two parallel flows designed to address the semantic--geo/numerical interaction problem. The semantic flow exposes coarse spatial and quality concepts to the cross-encoder, allowing query terms and POI text to attend to descriptors such as , , , or . The numerical flow preserves the exact scalar values needed to distinguish small from large distance gaps, or marginal from substantial differences in rating and review count. Together, the two flows allow the model to learn that the importance of distance, rating, and review evidence depends on the query intent and on the semantic evidence of each POI. Raw numerical attributes $_i= $ represent distance, rating, and review count. Distance $z_i$ is computed with respect to the user location or the resolved target location expressed in the query; rating $r_i$ is the average star rating; and review count $c_i$ measures the amount of customer feedback available for the POI. These values are processed through feature-specific converters: an Online Distance Converter normalizes raw distance, a Rating Converter processes star ratings on the 0--5 scale, and a Review Count Converter handles review volume. The converted values are then fed to three dedicated MLPs. For the pointwise stage, each MLP receives a single scalar value for one candidate. Our primary metric is Rank-Deviation Quality , a position-weighted correctness metric a query may have many valid answers and quality depends on their relative ordering.
