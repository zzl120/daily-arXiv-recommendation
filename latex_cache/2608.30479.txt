A central challenge in GR is transforming diverse item attributes into Semantic IDs ~, since the SID is the only channel through which item information reaches the generative model: whatever it fails to preserve is permanently invisible at decoding time. This bottleneck is acute in Location-Based Services ~, where retrieval outcomes hinge on fine-grained POI attributes that mainstream LLMs cannot perceive precisely: they fragment coordinates into digit sub-tokens~, are insensitive to the scale of numerical attributes~, and infer structure only from surface text~. The central question for LBS generative retrieval is therefore not how to SIDs, but how to make them . Existing POI-oriented SID methods pursue this goal but intervene too late. They append discrete spatial tokens such as Geohash or S2 Cell IDs as geographic prefixes~, inject geographic offsets during codebook quantization~, or fine-tune the embedding model with co-visit contrastive signals~. All of them operate on representations whose precision is already lost, since it vanishes the moment the tokenizer splits a coordinate into digit fragments; no downstream grid, rotation, or clustering can recover a value that was never continuous. Moreover, they address geography alone, leaving numerical attributes and structural information to plain text, and pay for their gains with longer identifiers that increase autoregressive decoding cost. Three challenges must therefore be resolved before the identifier is formed. In LBS retrieval a difference of merely $0.1^ $, roughly $10$\,km, already carries substantial business impact, yet LLMs mishandle coordinates in three ways ). Their numeric encoding is discontinuous~, so numerically close coordinates may land in distant embedding regions. Discrete grid encodings such as GeoHash~ and S2~Cell~, which some methods directly adopt as SID prefixes~, impose hard cell boundaries where two geographically adjacent POIs can be assigned entirely unrelated codes simply because a boundary falls between them. And coordinates are angular: the same longitude difference spans about $111$\,km at the equator but far less at high latitudes. POIs also carry ratings, average prices, favorite counts and so on, whose scales differ by orders of magnitude, so an identical gap means entirely different things ): $0.5$ in rating separates a well-regarded POI from a mediocre one, whereas $0.5$ in visit count is indistinguishable from noise~. Prior work~ serializes them as plain text without type awareness, forcing one shared numeric subspace to absorb all of them. Each POI carries a system-annotated two-level tag such as ``Shopping/Consumer Electronics'', and sits inside a geographic unit such as a business district~, neither of which is recoverable from surface text. Tags may agree at the coarse level yet diverge at the fine level: ``Apple Store'' and ``Apple Market'' are textually almost identical and share the coarse tag ``Shopping'', yet belong to ``Consumer Electronics'' and ``Super Market'' ). A business district likewise hosts many heterogeneous shops that proximity cannot separate, and stronger geographic modeling only aggravates this: the more tightly co-located POIs are grouped, the less distinguishable they become. {0.2cm} {-0.4cm} {pic/poi_intro_figure.pdf} To address these challenges, we propose HF-SID, which restores fidelity at the representation stage, before any information is committed to a discrete code. For C1, we encode each coordinate as a single continuous unit through a dedicated numerical encoder~ and transform spherical coordinates into a 3D Cartesian form, which replaces hard grid boundaries with a continuous space in which Euclidean distance is monotonic in true geographic distance; Geo-CPT then consolidates this geometry inside the LLM through pairwise-distance and nearest-pair reasoning samples. For C2, we give each attribute type an independent Type Embedding that projects it into its own subspace, and Num-CPT trains the model on cross-attribute comparison samples so that a magnitude is always read relative to the attribute it belongs to. For C3, Structure-based Contrastive Learning treats two POIs as positives only when both tag levels match exactly, making coarse-level-only matches hard negatives. We apply it solely to the residual before the last quantization layer, since the first two layers have by then collapsed co-located POIs together while the fidelity they established remains untouched. As these mechanisms enrich the representation rather than append tokens to the identifier, HF-SID achieves this with a 3-token SID, adding no decoding step over the plain quantization baseline. We introduce a unified numerical representation that encodes coordinates and dynamic attributes as single continuous units, combining a 3D Cartesian transformation with a numerical encoder and per-type type embeddings, and align it with the LLM through two-stage CPT, namely Geo-CPT for spatial reasoning and Num-CPT for scale-aware attribute comparison. Its central design choice is the Semantic ID , a short discrete code that must be both generatable by the LLM and semantically faithful to the item. SIDs are commonly obtained by Residual-Quantized Variational AutoEncoders or hierarchical K-Means over item embeddings~, yielding a coarse-to-fine codebook sequence whose prefixes are shared by similar items, which lets constrained beam search traverse a billion-scale item space and underpins industrial deployments~. A direct consequence is that whatever an item embedding fails to encode is irreversibly discarded once quantization is applied, so embedding fidelity, rather than codebook design, is the decisive factor. POI scenarios break down precisely here: text-driven SIDs assign nearly identical codes to semantically similar but spatially distant POIs, while adjacent POIs of different categories share no prefix at all, causing geographical hallucination during generation. To inject attribute awareness into POI-oriented SIDs, existing work follows three routes. prepends quantized location codes to the semantic code, using S2 Cell encodings as hierarchical geographic prefixes~, Geohash-based Geographic IDs under trie-constrained decoding~, or hierarchical geographic tokenization with residual quantization~. instead perturbs embeddings, through the Geographic Position Embedding of GenPOI~ and the geo-centroid Geo-RoPE frame of Pro-GEO~. reshapes the embedding space with log-mined supervision, contrasting spatiotemporal co-visit pairs with EM-style~ SID refinement~, or adding a diversity loss to RQ-VAE~ against semantic collapse~. All of them act after coordinates have already been serialized as text, and all treat geography as the only structured signal worth modeling. Numerical attributes fare no better: sub-word tokenization destroys numeric continuity and leaves LLMs unreliable at magnitude and comparison~, and although dedicated encoders that embed a scalar as a single continuous unit largely restore this ability~, they have been evaluated on reasoning tasks rather than identifier construction. Closest to us, ReSID~ and HiD-VAE~ quantize structured recommendation fields and multi-granularity semantics, yet both target general recommendation and neither confronts the heterogeneous attribute scales and spatial hierarchy of POIs. Given a POI $p$ with its textual description, HF-SID encodes it into a Semantic ID $_p$, guided by a single principle: restore geographic, numerical, and structural fidelity at the representation level, before quantization commits the POI to a discrete code. Averaged over the three scenarios, PV\_CVR rises by $6.74\ In this paper, we present HF-SID, a high-fidelity Semantic ID framework for generative POI retrieval in LBS. HF-SID converts coordinates into a continuous 3D Cartesian form and encodes every numerical value as a single unit through a dedicated Numerical Encoder, fuses these embeddings into the LLM with a Type Embedding that gives each attribute type its own subspace, and aligns the model via Geo-CPT and Num-CPT. A Structure-based Contrastive Learning objective, applied only to the residual before the last quantization layer, then separates co-located POIs that share a coarse tag but differ at the fine level. All mechanisms act at the embedding level and add no token overhead. Deployed on the AMap platform, HF-SID consistently improves both offline SID quality and online business metrics over the previous system, and we release AMap-S, a large-scale real-world POI benchmark.
