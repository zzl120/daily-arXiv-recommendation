As shown in Figure~ , simply concatenating or averaging item embeddings derived from different foundation models yields little or no improvement over using a single foundation model. From this observation, we hypothesize that effectively leveraging multiple foundation models requires a more sophisticated fusion strategy that preserves model-specific representations while adaptively determining their contributions to recommendation. In light of this challenge, we propose , a method for user-adaptive score-level fusion of multiple foundation models for recommendation. Its design is motivated by our empirical analysis, which suggests that the users who benefit vary across foundation models. Here, we say that a foundation model benefits a user if the user’s test item appears in the Top-$K$ recommendations generated by a recommender system using item representations from that foundation model. As shown in Figure~ , the sets of users benefiting from different foundation models overlap but are not identical, with an average Jaccard similarity of 0.48. This observation motivates modeling the contribution of each foundation model on a per-user basis, rather than applying the same fusion strategy to all users. Building on this finding, adopts a user-adaptive score-level fusion approach. Rather than directly combining heterogeneous embeddings from different foundation models into a single item representation shared across users, preserves a separate recommendation view for each model and performs fusion only after obtaining their recommendation scores. This design allows foundation models with different architectures, modalities, and representation spaces to contribute without requiring direct alignment of their embeddings or risking the loss of model-specific information. Specifically, for each user–item pair, first constructs a separate recommendation view for each foundation model by using its item representations as input to the recommender-system backbone. It then independently computes a recommendation score from each view and combines these scores through a user-conditioned attention module, which assigns different importance to each foundation model according to the target user. This module assigns user-specific weights to the recommendation scores and computes their weighted average, thereby increasing or decreasing the contribution of each foundation model to the final recommendation. Moreover, we introduce a regularization technique that helps effectively leverage multiple foundation models by preventing the fusion weights from collapsing onto a single model.
