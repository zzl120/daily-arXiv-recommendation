Recommender Systems; Generative Recommendation; Controllable Recommendation; Diversity in Recommendation Generative recommendation has emerged as a new paradigm by reformulating item retrieval as sequence generation over discrete semantic identifiers~. By leveraging advanced generative models, this paradigm enables richer contextual modeling of user behavior than traditional ranking-based approaches~. However, deploying generative recommenders in practice requires not only accurate next-item generation but also the ability to adapt to changing and often conflicting objectives on the fly~. For example, as shown in Figure~, a user planning a vacation might initially demand highly relevant results when searching for . After browsing multiple options, the user's goal might shift to exploring unique local experiences, immediately prioritizing novelty and diversity. From a platform's perspective, during a limited-time sale like Black Friday, the initial strategy might be to maximize conversion rates. But midway through, the platform might need to quickly adjust its strategy, encouraging the exploration of underrepresented semantic niches or diverse item types to avoid filter bubbles. These rapid objective shifts make static training or repeated fine-tuning impractical, motivating eal-time ontrollable enerative ecommendation: guiding generation at inference time with adjustable control signals. {Figure1_Introduction.pdf} Despite extensive research, existing methods struggle to meet this need. Traditional controllable methods, such as MIND~ and ComiRec~, introduce control through multi-interest modeling but rely on explicit retrieval steps, which do not fit the sequence generation paradigm. Within generative recommendation, controllability has mainly been introduced in three ways: Training-time control methods embed control signals into model parameters~. However, changing goals requires re-training or fine-tuning, which is impractical for real-time adjustments. Post-hoc methods ~ only filter items that were already generated. They cannot create new, diverse items if the generator didn't produce them initially. Inference-time control: most models use autoregressive decoding, generating tokens strictly from left to right. This creates : early token choices constrain all future steps~. If we try to force control signals mid-generation, it often breaks the semantic structure, producing invalid IDs that do not map to real items~. To address these limitations, we propose , a real-time controllable generative recommendation framework that enables flexible inference-time control while preserving recommendation accuracy and output validity. Specifically, we propose Hierarchical Logit Guidance, which directly modulates the logits of coarse-grained semantic tokens according to the desired control signal during inference. This lightweight, training-free intervention enables efficient and immediate adjustment of the generation behavior without requiring any modification to the model parameters. However, aggressive logit manipulation may introduce semantic mismatches or predictions that deviate from valid item identifiers. To counteract these risks, ReGen incorporates Confidence-Aware Refinement with Topology-Constrained Tree. This component iteratively identifies low-confidence tokens through a mask predictor, re-predicts them in subsequent refinement steps, and enforces structural validity by restricting all updates to semantically consistent paths within the discrete item topology, ensuring that every generated identifier corresponds to a real, existing item. While ReGen can theoretically adapt to various control objectives by formulating specific control signals, we focus on diversity control as a representative and challenging case study in this paper to evaluate its effectiveness. Our contributions are summarized as follows: We propose , a real-time controllable framework based on parallel iterative refinement. It enables global semantic planning and breaks the limitations of autoregressive models. We introduce Hierarchical Logit Guidance, applying control signals to high-level semantic tokens during inference, enabling flexible control without parameter updates. We develop Confidence-Aware Refinement with a Topology-Constrained Tree and integrate it into beam-based inference. This mechanism repairs unreliable tokens, preserves valid Semantic ID paths, and produces a compact set of controllable recommendation candidates. Extensive experiments show that ReGen achieves a favorable accuracy--diversity trade-off under moderate guidance, and ablation studies verify the importance of guidance, refinement, and topology-constrained inference. Generative recommendation reformulates recommendation as sequence generation, typically using Transformer-based models to predict semantic item ID sequences~. Various Semantic ID construction methods have been explored, including vector quantization with RQ-VAE~, structure-aware hierarchical clustering~, and direct encoding of collaborative signals~. These approaches enable coherent and personalized recommendations by integrating semantic and behavioral information. Despite this progress, mainstream generative recommendation models struggle to simultaneously achieve high accuracy, effective diversity control, and efficient inference. In particular, diversity control remains challenging under autoregressive generation. Methods such as TIGER~ and DLCRec~ suffer from irreversibility in left-to-right decoding, where prefix perturbations often lead to invalid item IDs and degraded recommendation accuracy. These limitations highlight the need for a new generative framework that enables efficient and reliable controllability without sacrificing recommendation quality. Controllable recommender systems aim to enhance user autonomy and overcome the limitations of traditional systems that overemphasize accuracy while neglecting flexibility and diversity. Early multi-interest models, such as MIND~ and ComiRec~, implicitly or explicitly capture diverse user preferences through dynamic routing and controllable aggregation. User-centric methods, including causal UCRS~, introduce counterfactual reasoning to balance accuracy and diversity. Recent LLM-based frameworks provide finer control. For instance, DLCRec~ adopts data-augmented subtask decomposition~ and employs supervised learning and RLHF for improved re-ranking and category balance. Meanwhile, Paragon~ generates conditional parameters for adaptive multi-task learning. However, LLM-based approaches often suffer from high latency and retraining costs~. Conversely, efficiency-oriented methods often compromise real-time adaptability, underscoring the need for a unified framework that achieves controllability and efficiency simultaneously without significant training overhead. In this subsection, we propose Confidence-Aware Refinement to improve recommendation quality after diversity control. While Hierarchical Logit Guidance enforces diversity by changing coarse-grained tokens, it can also create a mismatch between the modified global semantics and the remaining fine-grained tokens, which hurts coherence and relevance. To address this issue, we use token confidence to identify unreliable positions and iteratively re-predict them, while keeping the guided coarse tokens fixed. We first construct discrete semantic identifiers for items through a two-stage encoding-and-quantization pipeline. Specifically, we use Sentence-T5~ to encode the title and textual metadata of each item into a dense semantic representation. The resulting embeddings are then discretized by an RQ-VAE, which contains $L=4$ residual codebooks. Each codebook consists of $C=256$ code vectors with dimensionality $D=32$, yielding a four-level semantic ID for each item. The RQ-VAE is optimized for 2,000 epochs using AdamW~ with a learning rate of $10^{-3}$ and a batch size of 2,048. The mask predictor in ReGen is instantiated as a bidirectional Transformer encoder following a masked semantic ID modeling objective. The encoder uses a token embedding dimension of 256 and 8 attention heads in each layer. To accommodate different dataset scales and complexities, we use a 4-layer encoder for the Scientific and Instrument datasets, and a 6-layer encoder for the Video Game dataset. All model parameters are randomly initialized and trained from scratch. This work focuses on diversity control as a representative case of inference-time controllable generative recommendation. Although the proposed logit guidance can in principle be instantiated with other control signals, such as fairness, exposure balancing, or freshness, we leave a systematic evaluation of these objectives to future work. In addition, ReGen relies on the quality of the underlying Semantic ID tokenizer and its hierarchical organization. If the learned codebooks are noisy or poorly aligned with item semantics, coarse-level guidance may become less effective. Future work will study tokenizer-agnostic control mechanisms. Furthermore, beyond category coverage, richer notions of controllability such as novelty, serendipity, calibration, fairness, and exposure balancing require objective-specific control functions and evaluation protocols, which we also leave for future work. In this work, we propose ReGen, a framework for real-time controllable generative recommendation with training-free inference-time control. Our method employs parallel iterative denoising to enable flexible control without parameter updates, thereby addressing the rigidity and instability of existing generative recommenders when discrete control signals are introduced. ReGen combines logit-space guidance for coarse-grained semantic control with confidence-aware refinement to iteratively repair low-confidence tokens and preserve relevance and validity. Extensive experiments show that ReGen achieves a favorable accuracy–diversity trade-off and converges efficiently with only a few refinement steps.
