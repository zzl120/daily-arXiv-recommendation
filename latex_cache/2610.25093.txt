We introduce SRBQ, combining subspace decomposition, residual quantization, and occupancy-aware assignment to construct balanced token--subspace--item units for history reconstruction. We align masking with the SRBQ hierarchy, prioritize informative historical units through predictive entropy, and use curriculum learning to connect history reconstruction with future-path generation. Extensive experiments on four datasets demonstrate consistent gains and separately verify balanced reconstruction targets, the benefit of reusing quantizer-defined groups, and dependence on user history. Sequential recommenders model ordered user behavior using Markov chains~, recurrent or convolutional networks~, graph-based models~, and self-attention~. Generative recommendation instead formulates recommendation as identifier generation~. Language-model-based methods such as P5~ adopt text-to-text interfaces with prompting or efficient tuning~, while semantic-ID methods represent items as discrete tuples to enable exact catalog-level generation~. Recent work advances identifier-based recommendation through representations, behavior, and decoding: GNPR-SID constructs semantic and collaborative identifiers for next-POI generation~, EAGER jointly generates behavioral and semantic tokens~, and RPG scales semantic-ID length through parallel decoding~. IMBGen further combines residual and parallel quantization for multi-behavior generation~, whereas LCKD-SR studies efficient knowledge transfer from LLMs to sequential recommenders~. Complementary directions include masked diffusion~, cascaded ranking~, test-time reranking~, and ~. Despite these advances, most methods focus on generation or inference, while supervision for modeling observed interaction histories remains comparatively underexplored. History self-supervision reconstructs masked items~, pretrains multiple sequence views~, or contrasts augmented histories~, but is mainly developed for discriminative recommendation. MHL~ extends history reconstruction to causal semantic-ID generation. SAHL aligns history supervision with SRBQ's token-, subspace-, and item-level hierarchy. Item identifiers define the output space and the semantic relationships exposed to the recommender. Atomic IDs contain no explicit content similarity, whereas modality encoders map item descriptions and attributes into dense representations~; Sentence-T5~, for example, places semantically related items close in the embedding space. Vector quantization converts these dense representations into discrete codes suitable for generation. Product quantization~ independently encodes low-dimensional subvectors, while OPQ~ learns a global rotation to reduce quantization distortion under a fixed code budget. Residual quantization instead successively encodes the information left unexplained by previous codewords, yielding a coarse-to-fine representation~. RQ-VAE demonstrates that stacked residual codes support both reconstruction and autoregressive generation, and TIGER adopts this formulation for recommendation~. Subsequent studies explore transferable quantized representations~, compact indexing for large catalogs~, and learnable or task-aware code assignments~. More recent methods further refine semantic-ID construction. DIGER~ exposes code assignments to recommendation gradients; ReSID~ jointly considers predictability, discrimination, and balance; DOS~ combines collaborative alignment with orthogonal residual quantization; CapsID~ introduces variable-length identifiers; and AsymRec~ constructs multi-faceted hierarchies. Tlow~ transforms Sentence-T5 embeddings before partitioned quantization and aligns token embeddings with codebook geometry. These methods increasingly emphasize code distribution, utilization, and hierarchy. In this work, we revisit generative recommendation from the perspective of history learning and propose SAHL to coordinate semantic-ID construction with historical supervision. SRBQ constructs a balanced hierarchy at the token, subspace, and item levels, which is directly reused to define multi-granularity reconstruction units along user histories. Entropy-guided target selection and curriculum optimization further encourage the model to capture persistent preferences while progressively aligning training with next-item inference. Extensive experiments demonstrate consistent improvements over strong generative recommendation baselines and confirm the complementary benefits of structured semantic IDs and explicit history supervision. Further analyses show that the gains stem from structure-aligned reconstruction and user-specific historical context rather than arbitrary token grouping. These findings indicate that semantic IDs can serve not only as generation targets, but also as structured interfaces for learning from historical behavior. Future work may explore adaptive tokenization, recommendation-aware assignment, and tighter joint optimization between semantic-ID construction and downstream recommendation.
