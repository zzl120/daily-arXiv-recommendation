With the rapid advancement of Large Language Models , the landscape of text embedding models is evolving from traditional encoder-only architectures, such as Contriever~, Sentence-BERT~, BGE~ to LLM-based embedding models including NV-Embed~, Llama2vec~, and Qwen3-Embedding~. Despite their impressive performance, most of them treat LLMs primarily as static text encoders, overlooking their powerful chain-of-thought~ reasoning capabilities. Recent works such as Search-R3~ and LREM~ have attempted to harness the generative power of LLMs by explicitly generating reasoning chains or query expansions to augment query semantics, then produce a reasoning-augmented query embedding for retrieval. Despite these advancements, the current dense retrieval paradigm remains , ignoring the rich context provided by user history. This paradigm is particularly limited in local-lifestyle e-commerce scenarios. While personalized search methods~ such as UIA~ and ZAM~ utilize user history for personalized retrieval, they typically rely on implicit user sequence modeling and embedding interactions~, lacking the reasoning capability to explicitly deduce latent intent. To overcome this, recent generative approaches like PBR~ and CoPS~ have attempted to leverage LLMs to explicitly rewrite queries or generate pseudo-labels based on user history. Dense retrieval has evolved from BERT-based dual-encoder architectures, such as DPR~, Sentence-BERT~, and BGE~, to Large Language Model based embedding models. State-of-the-art models like Llama2vec~ and NV-Embed~ leverage the vast semantic knowledge of LLMs to generate high-quality text embeddings using contrastive learning. However, these methods typically utilize LLMs as text encoders, ignoring their inherent generative and reasoning capabilities. Recently, inspired by the Chain-of-Thought capability~ of LLMs, researchers have begun to integrate reasoning into dense retrieval. O1-Embedder~ generates thoughts for the query before retrieval. Search-R3~ and LREM~ further advance this by employing Reinforcement Learning to encourage the model to generate explicit reasoning steps before producing embedding, thereby integrating the thinking process into representation learning. These methods demonstrate that explicit reasoning can significantly clarify ambiguous queries. However, they remain predominantly , focusing on reasoning about the query's surface form . In the context of e-commerce, where intent is often deeply personalized, purely query-centric reasoning fails to address the information scarcity of broad and long-tail queries. Our work differs by extending the reasoning scope from the query alone to the user-query pair, enabling the model to bridge the intent gap through user-centric deduction. Personalized search requires integrating user historical behaviors to disambiguate intent. Prior methods like UIA~ employ a cross-attention mechanism to dynamically aggregate user history representations based on the current query. UEPPR~ unifies short-term behaviors and long-term preferences into a Query-User Tower, utilizing lightweight Transformers for in-session attention. OneSearch~ introduces an end-to-end generative retrieval framework that incorporates short-term behaviors into prompts and compresses long-term preferences into RQ-OPQ vectors, directly generating item IDs under relevance constraints. However, these approaches rely on implicit modeling of user behavior sequences, lacking explicit reasoning capabilities for complex intent understanding. With the development of LLMs, recent works have explored generative query enhancement for personalization~. CoPS~ integrates LLMs with a memory mechanism for personalized search experiences. PBR~ leverages LLMs to generate pseudo-queries and reasoning chains based on user history. However, these methods typically operate in a : the LLM acts as an external re-writer, optimized separately from the downstream retriever. This separation prevents the reasoning process from being directly aligned with the final retrieval objective. In contrast, our work unifies the reasoning and retrieval processes into an end-to-end framework, where the reasoning process is explicitly optimized to maximize downstream retrieval utility. Reinforcement Learning has become a critical component in LLM training pipelines, enhancing model controllability and performance~. For instance, Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback ~, typically employs Proximal Policy Optimization ~ to align models with human preferences. PPO optimizes a policy model using scalar signals from a reward model while constraining deviations via a reference model and estimating future value with a critic network. Despite its success in models like InstructGPT~, PPO suffers from high computational complexity and training instability due to its multi-model interaction. To address this, Direct Preference Optimization ~ simplifies alignment by modeling preference learning as a classification problem, eliminating the need for an explicit reward model. While efficient, DPO may struggle with complex reasoning tasks where intermediate steps are crucial. Recently, Group Relative Policy Optimization ~, introduced in DeepSeekMath, offers a compelling alternative.
