There are three primary problems with current ICD prediction methods: They are unable to comprehend multimodal patient data because they rely on either structured EHR data or unstructured clinical notes. They also struggle with scalability to a larger amount of ICD codes , as traditional classifiers need dense output layers and often do not generalize well to long tail rare diseases. They lack transparency for clinical use. To address these challenges, this research proposes a two-stage framework that first retrieves ICD codes using a multimodal dual-encoder retrieval model, where structured and unstructured patient data are integrated through gated fusion. The second stage refines the top-k retrieved candidates with an LLM-based re-ranker that provides ranked codes with clinically relevant explanations. Our experiments show that the proposed approach improves Micro-F1 and Precision over a multimodal dual-fusion classifier baseline. These improvements demonstrate that combining a gated multimodal retrieval system with LLM-based re-ranking is a practical alternative to dense multi-label classification for automated ICD coding. Automated ICD coding, Healthcare, Multimodal Fusion, Dual-encoder retrieval, ANN , LLM-based re-ranking, Clinical NLP Healthcare systems around the world use a standardized coding system to classify and document patient conditions. The International Statistical Classification of Diseases , created by the World Health Organization , serves as the global standard for documenting patient conditions, facilitating medical research, and the development of predictive models Today, medical codes are still largely assigned manually by medical professionals. This leads to scalability issues due to an increase in multimodal data in Electronic Health Records , ranging from structured clinical lab reports to unstructured clinical notes captured during a patient's visit. Combined with a poor doctor-to-patient ratio in some developing countries ranging from 1:1456 to 1:10000 , which is lower than the recommended ratio of 1:1000 by WHO , this creates a pressing need for automated ICD coding to reduce manual workload and support clinical decision making. Retrieval systems based on dual-encoder architectures are well known to efficiently match queries with items at scale. This design is widely deployed in recommendation platforms such as YouTube , because it decouples query computation from item indexing, enabling the model to perform faster candidate generation over millions of items using an approximate nearest neighbor search . ICD coding faces a similar scalability constraint where each patient admission needs to be compared against a large code catalog, making multi-label scoring expensive as the label space grows. Motivated by this setting, we adopted the dual-encoder retrieval framework to encode patient admissions and ICD code data into a shared embedding space to efficiently output top-k candidate codes. Our methodology integrates two complementary modalities to create a multimodal patient profile: Structured lab reports and unstructured clinical notes are provided to an encoder with gated fusion to combine embeddings from both modalities and form a unified patient embedding. In parallel, the ICD encoder embeds code metadata into the same space. The dual-encoder retrieval model learns to bring clinically appropriate ICD codes close to their corresponding patient representation in the embedding space. The retrieval output consists of top-k candidate codes with similarity scores indicating relative relevance. The subsequent step is a re-ranker to further refine the ICD codes ranking. We use an LLM to rescore the retrieved top-k candidate codes by providing both patient and ICD code data as input. The explanation-aware re-ranking improves the ranking quality by using LLM's semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities, while simultaneously providing a brief natural language explanation to support human review. The primary objective of this work is to assist healthcare professionals in their screening process. Empirical results on benchmark datasets such as MIMIC-III demonstrated a consistent improvement in performance relative to existing baselines. The main contribution is a scalable multimodal retrieve-and-rerank approach for ICD coding, where patient admissions and ICD-code metadata are aligned in a shared embedding space and retrieved codes are further refined using an explanation-aware re-ranker. This section provides the advancements in ICD code prediction. Automated ICD code prediction has been a point of concern in the medical industry due to the high complexity of codes and the high cost of manual coding. Automated assignment of ICD codes has been researched using deep neural architectures over clinical notes as input. Mullenbach et al. proposed Convolutional Attention for Multi-Label Classification , it applies a CNN encoder with label specific attention, where each ICD code is associated with an attention vector to focus on relevant portions of the patient discharge summary. Trained on the MIMIC-III dataset, it achieves a Micro-F1 score of 54\ A multimodal framework for automated ICD coding uses unstructured clinical notes, semi-structured diagnosis descriptions, and structured tabular EHR data. This architecture integrates a Text-CNN classifier for free-text notes, a diagnosis-ranking network using character level CNN + BiLSTM for semi-structured text, and a decision-tree model for structured lab data. These models are combined using an ensemble learning approach that improves classification performance. It achieves a Micro-F1 score of 76.33\ A prominent method to address scalability is the multi-stage retrieve-and-re-rank model , which considers the task as a medical code recommendation problem rather than a conventional multi-label classification. The initial retrieval stage uses hybrid keyword and semantic search to narrow down ICD codes, followed by a re-ranker with contrastive learning to improve the prediction accuracy. On MIMIC-III dataset, it achieves a Micro-F1 of 60\ The paper by Yan et al. provides a survey on automated ICD coding, marking the change from rule based systems to deep learning architectures. The author highlights the large scale of codes, class imbalance, multimodal EHR integration and the need for explainability in new architectures for more clinical relevance. Across previous studies, text only and multimodal classifiers suffer from scalability limitations due to dense output layers and limited support for long tail codes. Retrieval based and label semantic methods improved scalability and ranking metrics by leveraging ICD code descriptions and contrastive learning, but they are limited to text only and offered limited explainability. Our framework unifies these strengths by learning a multimodal patient representation through gated fusion of unstructured clinical notes and structured EHR data, retrieving codes through a dual-encoder architecture aligned in a shared semantic space, refining predictions with an LLM based re-ranker that also provides natural language justification. This combination directly addresses scalability, multimodal integration, and interpretability. This section covers the proposed architecture for the multimodal ICD code retrieval, followed by LLM re-ranker to refine the ordering of the retrieved candidates. {image_trainingigng.png} Fig.~ illustrates the training architecture of the proposed dual-encoder retrieval model. The patient tower constructs a multimodal patient representation from two complementary sources: structured EHR features and unstructured clinical notes. The structured features are processed through a Multi-Layer Perceptron and projected into 768-dimensional embedding space. In parallel, the unstructured clinical notes are encoded into a 768-dimensional embedding $z_{}$ using Clinical-Longformer . Both modality-specific embeddings go through L2 normalization to stabilize training and ensure that dot product/cosine similarity corresponds to angular distance. The two normalized vectors are combined using a sigmoid gated fusion layer that learns per dimension mixture weights. This produces the final patient embedding $e_{} ^{768}$. The ICD tower encodes ICD code metadata into the same 768-dimensional ICD embedding $e_{}$ using BioClinicalBERT to enable similarity based retrieval. We extract the token and pass it through a lightweight linear projection layer followed by L2 normalization. This gives the final code embedding $e_{} ^{768}$ During training, both towers learn with a contrastive retrieval objective that allows clinically correct patient–code pairs to be embedded together, while incorrect pairs are pushed apart. Detailed components of this architecture are described in the following subsections. The Patient Tower generates a unified multimodal embedding by integrating information from two parallel sub-encoders: a text sub-encoder for unstructured clinical narratives and a structured sub-encoder for EHR data. A single encoder was avoided because it either forces numerical data to be serialized as weak tokens or causes text to dominate numeric features during optimization. Separate sub-encoders preserve modality specific biases and allow controlled fusion. The text sub-encoder processes unstructured clinical narratives with a domain-specialized transformer . Clinical notes are long, so we segmented them into chunks, each chunk is encoded and mean-pooled. Then we aggregate chunk embeddings via attention to produce a single encounter vector $h_{}$. The linear projection projects the encounter vector into the shared embedding space and then we apply normalization for stable training. z_{}= ^{d} The structured sub-encoder is a lightweight feed-forward network designed to process numerical and categorical signals . The resulting $e_{} ^{d}$ is optimized for dual-encoder retrieval against ICD embeddings. The ICD Code Tower encodes each ICD code into a dense semantic vector in the same vector space as the patient tower to enable scalable similarity based retrieval. Each ICD code is represented using available code metadata such as description, chapter/category and hierarchy path to inject taxonomy structure. These features are provided to a biomedical text encoder to generate a dense vector representation. Next, we apply a lightweight projection layer to align the embedding with the vector space followed by L2 normalization. The ICD tower is optimized jointly with the patient tower using contrastive retrieval defined in the next section, which aligns clinically correct patient-code pairs and separates mismatches in the same vector space. After training, ICD embeddings are computed and stored in a vector index for efficient ANN retrieval during inference. The Patient Tower and ICD Code Tower are jointly optimized using contrastive retrieval objective so that relevant patient-code pairs are embedded close in the shared semantic space. After training, we freeze the ICD code tower and run an offline job over the full ICD catalog to generate dense embedding for each ICD code. Each embedding is L2-normalized and stored in a parquet embedding store. We then build an ANN index using Facebook AI similarity search . The FAISS index is stored as a serving artifact and it can be reloaded without recomputing code embeddings. During inference, for each patient admission, we run the patient tower to generate a normalized patient embedding. After that, the embedding is used to query the FAISS index to obtain top-$K$ codes and their similar scores. Finally, the retrieved code list is passed to the LLM re-ranking module for further refinement. The LLM-based re-ranking component reorders the top-$K$ ICD codes returned by the retriever model and simultaneously provides human-readable explanation. While the retriever efficiently generates a candidate set, it may assign similar scores to semantically close but clinically different codes. To resolve this, we use a second stage to reason over the patient and ICD code context to make finer distinctions. We use claude 3.7 sonnet in a few-shot setting to reassess the clinical relevance of each candidate. For a given patient $i$, we first construct a textual summary $p_i$ that concatenates important elements from structured EHR features and unstructured clinical notes . We then present $p_i$ together with candidate ICD code metadata $c_j$ in a natural language prompt, the prompt includes a brief patient summary, the ICD code description, and available category or hierarchy information. We ask the LLM to judge how well the code matches the case and to briefly justify the decision. The LLM outputs a JSON output, for each patient-code pair, it provides a scalar relevance score $r_{i,j}$ and a short explanation $e_{i,j}$. \{ \}_{j=1}^{k} \;=\; f_{}\! , where $f_{} $ denotes the LLM’s relevance estimate induced by the prompt. In practice, $r_{i,j}$ can be derived from the LLM’s ranking of candidates or from explicit relevance scores returned in the JSON output. These scores are used to reorder the top-$K$ codes produced by the retriever. In explanations, the LLM highlights key phrases or findings in the patient summary that support its decision. We evaluate our approach using the MIMIC-III v1.4 critical care dataset , which contains EHRs of 53,423 ICU related hospital admissions from 2001-2012. Each sample corresponds to a single hospital admission with ICD-9 codes. To avoid leakage across multiple records for the same patient, the dataset was split at patient level into 70\ Each patient admission from the MIMIC-III dataset has a set of ICD-9 codes without an inherent ranking or score. The proposed retrieval model outputs a ranked list of ICD codes with similarity score for each admission. Therefore, we evaluated performance using metrics that capture both retrieval and ranking quality. Across experiments, we report Precision@K on the retrieved ranked list. Additionally, we report Micro-F1@K score using top-k decoding rule for the classification style comparison across methods, where the top-k retrieved ICD codes are treated as positives. For classifier baselines, ICD codes are ranked by predicted probability and top-k codes are used to calculate Precision@k and Micro-F1@k The LLM-based re-ranking is evaluated using NDCG@8 on a human annotated subset with graded relevance labels provided by clinical annotators, since the MIMIC-III does not provide ground truth rankings. Human labels are collected using a 3 level rubric where 2 is correct match, 1 is partially related, 0 is irrelevant. Two humans labelled a stratified subset of N=2000 samples to measure agreement, and remaining cases are single annotated. BioClinicalBERT:} BioClinicalBERT is a transformer-based language model pretrained on large-scale biomedical and clinical texts. In this baseline, we used unstructured clinical notes textual data as input. We tokenized and concatenated the discharge summaries, medications, previous prescriptions and symptom descriptions using BioClinicalBERT, followed by a fully connected multi-label classification head applied to the contextual embeddings. The model is fine-tuned using binary cross-entropy loss over all ICD codes, with a learning rate of $2e^{-5}$, Adam optimizer, and early stopping based on validation Micro-F1. This baseline extracts semantic features from clinical text but had limited information about structured lab and demographic features. {3. Dual Fusion Encoder:} The dual fusion model is multimodal, it consisted of two separate encoders, one for structured data encoder which processed lab reports and demographic information using a feed forward neural network with multiple hidden layers and ReLU activation function. The other unstructured data encoder was a clinical-longformer model to extract generate embeddings for unstructured features. The output of the structured and unstructured encoders are concatenated and passed through a fusion layer followed by a multi-label classification head. The model is trained using binary cross-entropy loss function, using Adam Optimizer and early stopping based on validation Micro-F1. This baseline leverages both modalities, but remains a conventional classifier that produces logits for each ICD code with limited scalability. Proposed Dual-Encoder Retriever + LLM Re-ranker:} Our proposed framework performs ICD code retrieval followed by LLM re-ranking. The patient tower outputs multimodal embedding by fusing two sub-encoders: clinical-longformer for unstructured clinical notes and MLP encoding structured EHR features. Both modality vectors are projected into a shared 768 dimensional normalized embedding space. The ICD tower encodes code metadata using BioClinicalBERT. We train both towers jointly using multi-positive InfoNCE loss function with AdamW optimizer and a batch size of 256. Each admission can have multiple positive ICD code labels, so we constructed negatives using in-batch negatives and hard negatives . We then freeze the ICD tower to precompute embeddings and store them in a parquet backed embedding table. During inference, we build a FAISS ANN index and retrieve top-k candidates. Across MIMIC-III, the proposed approach improves Precision@k and Micro-F1 over multimodal baseline, and LLM re-ranking further improves the NDCG@k on a human annotated dataset. While the LLM re-ranking component showed gains in ranking quality, we need further large scale clinical validation. These results suggest that retrieval based approach offers a scalable alternative to dense multi-label classification. Further validation is needed before deployment in real clinical coding workflows. Although the retriever system improved performance on the MIMIC-III dataset, the framework is limited to a single center ICU population and has yet to be validated against larger ICD-10 label space. Additionally, while the LLM improved accuracy, its high computation cost, additional latency, and potential for clinical hallucination present challenges for real-time deployment. Therefore, it should be treated as an optional second stage for ambiguous cases rather than as a replacement for the low-latency retriever. Future work will focus on scaling the system by utilizing knowledge distillation to create efficient student re-ranker, and doing rigorous clinical validation to ensure faithfulness of the generated explanation.
