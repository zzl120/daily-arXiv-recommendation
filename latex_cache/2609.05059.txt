Auditors, brand trackers, and researchers sample LLM answers to commercial questions and report what they see. Every such report carries an implicit sampling claim about how much of what the model would say the collected runs have seen. Prior work established that identical prompts can yield different answers run to run , and that repetition inside a single prompt changes model behaviour . What has not been measured is the accumulation structure of repeated answers: how fast the set of distinct brands, and the set of distinct cited domains, grows as runs accumulate. We treat runs as sampling units and brands as species, and apply the classical accumulation toolkit : the exact rarefaction curve $A $, the expected number of distinct items after $k$ of the $n$ collected runs, averaged over run subsets; the final rarefaction increment $A -A =Q_1/n$, where $Q_1$ counts items seen in exactly one run; and the Chao2 richness estimate, a lower bound, in its bias-corrected form when $Q_2=0$. Rarefaction interpolates within the collected sample; $Q_1=0$ means every observed item recurred, never that no unobserved item exists. Two studies follow: a first pass on existing corpora whose result the second, larger study revises. This note measures set growth; mean stability is a different measurement with its own budgets. Leviathan et al.\ show repetition inside one prompt improves accuracy; we measure repetition across calls. Citation instability in generative search is documented on single runs ; the accumulation view quantifies how much of the citation record a sampling budget has observed. The estimators are the species-accumulation classics . Whether repeated queries exhaust an LLM's brand recommendations is a property of the deployment, and retrieval is the switch. Grounded in web search, the answer set is short and a handful of repeats sees it whole. Ungrounded, five engines from five vendors were still producing never-seen organizations at the fifteenth identical run, with repertoires three to six times the grounded engine's. Cited-domain accumulation, measured wherever citations exist, had not closed at any horizon tested. For anyone buying or selling repeated-query audits the budget question therefore splits three ways: grounded brand lists are cheap to finish; ungrounded brand lists and citation maps are open-ended at practical budgets; and none of these budgets settles recommendation frequencies, which need their own design .
