Instead, it explains a simple design intuition: A feature observation $o_t$ is useful w.r.t. model $ P_{ }$ only if it adds information beyond the current context . Consider the conditional mutual information at the current context by definition: &I_{ } \\ & = E [ D_{}\! ( P_ \, \|\, P_ ) ]. If this quantity is zero, the nonnegativity of the KL divergence implies that P_ = P_ . Hence, $o_t$ provides no additional predictive information for the next SID token at the current context . The contrapositive proves the theorem. Although conditional mutual information is nonnegative in expectation, the predictive gain of a realized observation can vary across contexts. This consideration is particularly important in industrial search and recommendation systems, where numerous features are available to characterize complex and evolving user preferences, yet generative retrieval models can incorporate only limited additional information during decoding because of efficiency and scalability constraints. Feature injection must therefore be selective, retaining observations that improve prediction at the current prefix while avoiding those that provide no gain or reduce the probability of the correct next SID token. Generative Retrieval casts retrieval as autoregressive identifier generation . Following TIGER~, items are commonly represented by hierarchical Semantic IDs learned through residual quantization. This paradigm has also been successfully adopted in large-scale industrial scenarios . Deploying GR in industrial systems, however, exposes a fundamental mismatch between request-conditioned SID generation and the feature-intensive nature of such systems~. Large-scale retrieval depends heavily on heterogeneous signals, such as user profiles, behavioral histories, and freshness cues~. Yet existing GR methods scale poorly with the number of features , typically appending all signals to the input or injecting them according to a predetermined scheme, making it difficult to jointly maintain retrieval quality and serving efficiency~. Specifically, encoding all features indiscriminately inflates serving cost and introduces irrelevant or conflicting evidence, as feature utility is inherently request-dependent: a feature indispensable for one request may be pure noise for another~. This raises the question of . Meanwhile, injecting features at a fixed scheme cannot adapt to the decoding state, but residual quantization induces a hierarchical SID space where different depths resolve distinctions at different granularities. Therefore, a feature highly informative at one level may be irrelevant at others. This raises the question of . Addressing both questions is crucial for efficient and scalable GR deployment in industrial search, yet effective feature injection remains largely overlooked. To this end, we propose gentic enerative etrieval }, which reformulates generative retrieval as an interleaved process of SID generation and feature acquisition. encapsulates heterogeneous features as tools, each uniquely identified by a tool token. Conditioned on the current decoding state, the model decides whether to emit a tool token and, if so, which tool to invoke. The acquired feature values are then represented as observation tokens that reshape the probability distribution over subsequent SID tokens, steering generation toward feature-consistent paths. As features are introduced only when the decoding state warrants them, reduces feature noise and naturally aligns feature granularity with the coarse-to-fine progression of SID generation. Through this adaptive tool use, shifts generative retrieval from passive SID generation to agentic decision-making over both items and features. Distinct from traditional discriminative retrieval~, generative retrieval reformulates retrieval as conditional sequence generation over discrete identifiers~. Early work focused on document retrieval, where DSI stores query--document associations in model parameters~, NCI introduces structured identifiers and prefix-aware decoding~, and SEAL uses document-derived textual access paths~. These studies established that the identifier space is learnable and that errors at different decoding positions have different retrieval consequences~. Generative recommendation extends this idea to multi-token item semantic identifiers . TIGER quantizes item representations with RQ-VAE and generates hierarchical SIDs~, making coarse-to-fine constrained decoding a standard formulation. Subsequent work shows that SID quality depends on semantic consistency, discrimination, codebook utilization, and tree balance~, while autoregressive SID trees may also restrict ranking expressiveness~. builds on this SID-based generative retrieval paradigm, but shifts the focus from how identifiers are constructed to how external evidence should interact with SID generation. Real-world GR systems rely on heterogeneous auxiliary information, including user profiles, behavioral histories, scenario context, freshness signals, and intent features. Existing methods usually incorporate such information before decoding or through a fixed injection pattern~. DiffusionGS performs query-conditioned denoising over candidate items~; DualGR separates long- and short-term user interests~; GLASS retrieves user histories conditioned on coarse SIDs~; and OneReason partitions behavioral histories into temporal streams~. These methods show that request-aware evidence selection is important, and they move beyond globally encoding all context indiscriminately. However, their injection mechanisms are still largely predetermined: evidence is selected before generation, kept fixed after a coarse decision, or injected at predefined network locations. They do not formulate feature acquisition itself as an explicit decoding-time action. differs by expanding the generative action space with tool tokens, allowing the model to decide whether a feature is needed, which feature to acquire, and where in the SID hierarchy the acquired evidence should affect subsequent generation. Industrial systems pursue GR for unification, cross-domain reuse, and serving efficiency. UniSearch jointly trains generators and encoders for video and live search~; OneSearch develops task-aware SID construction for e-commerce~; IntSR shares models across search and recommendation~; OneRec unifies retrieval and ranking~; and OneMall extends GR across multiple scenarios~. Complementary work improves SID construction, collision control, and long-term maintenance~, and generative interfaces have also been extended to reranking and query recommendation~. Beyond maximum-likelihood training, preference-oriented methods align GR with ranking quality or business value: UniSearch uses ranking signals~, while related methods apply preference optimization to e-commerce~, adapt DPO to hierarchical SIDs~, use listwise optimization~, generative flow networks~, token-level signals~, iterative preference construction~, page-wise supervision~, and list-level value optimization~. Efficiency methods accelerate decoding through draft verification~, process reward models~, and load-aware computation~. These efforts optimize different components of large-scale GR pipelines, whereas targets a complementary bottleneck: avoiding noisy and expensive static feature injection by acquiring useful features on demand during decoding. Recent work introduces intermediate reasoning to improve intent understanding in search and recommendation. OneSearch-V2 constructs keyword-based chains of thought~; OneRec-Think interleaves textual reasoning with generation~; SCoTER transfers structured reasoning from LLMs~; DiffuReason combines latent thinking with diffusion refinement~; and OneReason organizes recommendation around perception and cognition~. Agentic recommendation further allows models to actively acquire external evidence. KP-Agent combines LLM agents with domain tools~, and ChainRec learns to route and terminate tool chains~. However, general-purpose LLM planning typically introduces sequential inference, external orchestration, and tool-chain latency, which are difficult to accommodate in latency-constrained retrieval. Unlike these agentic recommenders, treats feature access as a lightweight action inside native constrained SID decoding. Tool invocation is not a separate planning loop, but an interleaved part of beam search that directly reshapes the probability distribution over subsequent SID tokens.
