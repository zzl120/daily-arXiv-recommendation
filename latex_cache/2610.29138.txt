Prior work incorporates visual content into CTR prediction and recommendation through visual encoders and task-aware representation learning . Fine-grained vision--language modeling provides complementary approaches: FashionBERT jointly models image patches and text , FILIP uses token-level late interaction , and MM-Rec models relations between image regions and text for news recommendation . These studies establish the value of content representations and fine-grained interactions across different tasks. Industrial CTR deployment requires balancing content granularity with the computational cost of feature interaction. For , the relevant design question is how to retain separately accessible content details when constructing inputs to a highly efficient shared CTR predictor. Visual ID and residual-quantization semantic IDs encode content representations as discrete codes for downstream prediction . COURIER also examines cluster IDs as a downstream interface for pretrained visual representations . More recent approaches explore content--collaborative alignment for cold-start prediction and prototype-composed semantic identifiers . These approaches motivate the use of discrete content interfaces, while the organization and granularity of the codes remain important design choices. To retain explicit spatial structure, uses spatially indexed, multi-scale codes from VAR's reconstruction-trained tokenizer , together with field-specific textual tokens. Its emphasis is on retaining structured content units whose task-trainable embeddings can interact with conventional features during relevance and CTR learning. Prior work combines relevance and behavioral supervision through representation pretraining, coupled prediction modules, and joint objectives. SEMINAR learns reusable multimodal representations with multiple pretraining objectives, including query--item relevance . PRECTR pretrains a relevance module before joint training with a CTR base model ; PRECTR-V2 further develops a lightweight text encoder using relevance supervision and LLM distillation . Joint relevance--engagement ranking instead optimizes the two tasks together and combines their outputs for ranking . emphasizes the scope of relevance pretraining and transfer: relevance supervision trains the shared SID embeddings, content-interaction layers, and prediction backbone, which are then transferred and fine-tuned for personalized CTR prediction. This connects a fine-grained multimodal input interface with relevance-to-CTR transfer of the shared predictor. While demonstrates significant improvements in multimodal CTR prediction and creative discrimination, it presents several limitations that suggest valuable directions for future research: Currently, the multi-scale visual tokenizer is pretrained for image reconstruction and kept frozen during relevance pretraining . While this discrete interface effectively bridges modalities, it is not strictly optimized for search semantics. Future work could explore jointly fine-tuning the visual tokenizer alongside the relevance pretraining task, enabling an end-to-end semantic tokenization process that could yield a higher upper bound for cross-modal matching. We have validated the effectiveness of the pretrained semantic prior primarily through personalized CTR post-training . However, whether this progressive pretraining paradigm can seamlessly generalize to other downstream search tasks remains to be fully explored. Integrating the fine-grained SID representation with Large Language Models to build a more comprehensive, universal foundational model for search systems is an exciting open direction. Replacing a single global image embedding with 30 multi-scale tokens inevitably increases the width of the concatenated feature vector and the computational cost of the initial bottom-layer projection. However, because the heavy Stage I token extraction is executed entirely offline, online inference only incurs the cost of extra discrete ID lookups and this widened initial projection, before the representation is mapped into fixed-capacity latent slots. In practice, this additional computational overhead is negligible; our online deployment confirmed no significant increase in serving latency during the late-stage ranking phase. Nonetheless, extending this fine-grained tokenization framework to extreme low-latency early-stage retrieval systems remains an area for future optimization. In this paper, we introduced , a three-stage progressive learning framework that integrates multimodal content into ID-based CTR prediction. By discretizing multimodal content into fine-grained Semantic IDs, the framework seamlessly unifies content tokenization, semantic relevance pretraining, and personalized post-training within a shared ID-based architecture. This decoupled design enables the predictor to establish a robust relevance-informed semantic prior before adapting to implicit, personalized user behaviors. Offline evaluations on the industrial Taobao dataset and the public MMCTR benchmark, alongside completed online A/B testing, demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach. Specifically, not only improves standard product-level CTR prediction but also exhibits strong capability in within-product creative discrimination.
