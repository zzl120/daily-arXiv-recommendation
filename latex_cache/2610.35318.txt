Existing architectures typically process sequential and non-sequential features with separate modules, limiting bidirectional information exchange. Directly applying homogeneous self-attention to all semantic tokens can also be inefficient when sequential and non-sequential signals exhibit different effective-ranks . Meanwhile, shared representations can overlook scenario-specific patterns and induce negative transfer between scenarios. In this paper, we propose , a unified framework for multi-scenario recommendation that jointly models eterogeneous feature nteractions via effective- scaling. HiRank integrates gated sequential attention with heterogeneous feature interaction within a unified backbone, enabling bidirectional fusion between sequential and non-sequential representations. HiRank further uses scenario-aware aggregation to compress heterogeneous low-order tokens into a compact representation. Distinct learned projections transform the full compact representation into scenario-aware composite tokens with complementary global feature views and more uniform information density, enabling more effective high-order self-attention. Extensive experiments on industrial-scale datasets demonstrate favorable performance scaling with parameter count and consistent improvements over competitive baselines. In production e-commerce systems, these models often need to jointly estimate multiple conversion-related targets, including purchase conversion rate and add-to-cart rate. To estimate these targets, rankers integrate heterogeneous signals from user behavior histories and candidate item features. Prior research on recommendation ranking has largely progressed along two parallel lines: behavioral sequence modeling and feature interaction. The former focuses on extracting interest signals relevant to the candidate item from users' interaction histories. Representative advances include target-aware attention in DIN~, GRU-based interest evolution in DIEN~, and efficient long-sequence compression in LONGER~. The latter focuses on learning dependencies among high-dimensional features across multiple fields, including user attributes, item attributes, and contextual signals. Research in this direction has evolved from second-order interactions in factorization machines ~ and explicit higher-order feature crosses in DCNv2~ to stacked FM blocks in Wukong~ and multi-head token mixing in RankMixer~. Despite progress in both directions, industrial ranking systems often connect these components through an pipeline. A sequence encoder first compresses a user's history into a fixed-dimensional representation, which a downstream interaction module then combines with non-sequential features. When this summary is the sole behavioral input to subsequent modules, they cannot directly access event-level sequence states to retrieve additional evidence within the same forward pass. In parallel, the success of large foundation models~ has motivated efforts to scale the capacity of recommendation models. RankMixer~ and OneTrans~ exemplify recent architectural advances in this direction. RankMixer combines efficient token mixing with token-specific transformations to scale heterogeneous feature interaction, while OneTrans unifies behavioral sequence modeling and feature interaction within a shared Transformer backbone. Both studies report favorable scaling behavior, with performance gains demonstrated through offline evaluations on production data and online A/B tests. In CVR prediction, the relevance of behavioral signals depends on the candidate item, while their relative importance may vary across traffic sources and user segments. Given the sparsity of purchase conversion feedback, Taobao's homepage CVR model is trained on user-item samples with purchase feedback collected across the entire platform. This training setup motivates a scenario-aware CVR model that learns transferable patterns from platform-wide data while adapting to the characteristics of the target recommendation scenario. In this paper, we propose , a unified framework for multi-scenario recommendation that couples modeling of the main user behavior sequence with long-term memory retrieval and heterogeneous feature interaction. Inspired by the HSTU family~, HiRank maintains explicit main-sequence states for gated sequential modeling, retains selected long-term behavioral sequences as separate memories, and organizes non-sequential features and pooled behavioral summaries into semantic tokens. Its design considers representation diversity at two stages: the variation of retrieval queries across users and the diversity of composite tokens used for subsequent feature interaction. For long-term memory retrieval, queries constructed from relatively static profile features may concentrate in a limited set of representation directions, restricting their effective dimensionality across users. To preserve behavior-dependent variation, HiRank initializes interest queries by attending to the main sequence and uses the resulting representations to retrieve evidence from the corresponding long-term memories. A complementary candidate-conditioned pathway reads the main sequence through shared gated-attention projections and accesses the same memories from the candidate's perspective. These pathways provide complementary behavioral evidence for joint modeling with the main-sequence states and semantic feature tokens. HiRank integrates these representations through head-wise token mixing and token-specific feed-forward transformations. When multiple blocks are stacked, the interaction outputs update both the main-sequence states and the queries used for subsequent long-term retrieval. This feedback allows the evolving feature context to guide further sequence modeling and memory access, coupling behavioral evidence extraction with heterogeneous feature interaction throughout the backbone. To adapt the fused representations to different recommendation scenarios, HiRank uses scene-aware queries to aggregate the final token bank into a compact representation. Following the composite-projection principle of Hiformer~, distinct learned projections transform this complete representation into multiple composite tokens for further attention-based interaction. Each composite token has access to the full aggregated context while learning a distinct projection of it. This construction aims to promote complementary representation directions and a more balanced singular-value spectrum across composite tokens, providing diverse inputs for subsequent interaction. The refined shared representation is then consumed by task-specific prediction towers for multiple conversion-related objectives. Our contributions are fourfold: We propose HiRank, which integrates explicit main-sequence states, query-based access to long-term memories, and semantically grouped feature tokens within a shared interaction backbone. We derive interest queries from the main sequence to preserve behavior-dependent variation across users, and complement them with candidate-conditioned queries for long-term memory retrieval. When blocks are stacked, the interaction outputs update both the main-sequence states and retrieval queries, allowing the evolving feature context to guide subsequent behavioral evidence extraction. We combine scene-aware aggregation with composite projections of the complete aggregated representation to construct multiple composite tokens. From an effective-rank perspective, this design targets diverse representation directions and a balanced spectrum for subsequent attention-based interaction. We conducted extensive experiments on Taobao's production dataset and deployed the proposed method in Taobao's recommendation system, which serves hundreds of millions of daily active users. The results demonstrate statistically significant increases in both the number of orders per user and gross merchandise value per user. Unified ranking architectures differ in which representations remain explicit and how information flows between input types. Feature-interaction architectures~ provide expressive operators for heterogeneous tokens. Sequence-centric designs such as HSTU~ and ULTRA-HSTU~ emphasize event-level processing, with the latter exploring semi-local attention and separate transducers for different behavioral streams. Joint modeling further requires an architecture that coordinates these representations throughout the backbone. One approach preserves separate computational streams. InterFormer~ maintains sequence and feature-interaction streams, exchanging selected summaries bidirectionally while retaining token-level states within each stream. OneTrans~ instead organizes both input types in a common causal token stream, sharing transformations across behavioral tokens and assigning token-specific parameters to heterogeneous feature tokens. Its pyramid structure progressively reduces the sequential query positions processed by deeper layers. Query-mediated architectures concentrate interaction in a compact set of evolving representations. HyFormer~ retains behavioral sequences separately and alternates cross-attention decoding with global-query mixing. MixFormer~ couples feature mixing with query-conditioned sequence aggregation, aligning feature heads with attention heads and constructing sequence key--value states through layer-specific feed-forward transformations. These designs permit repeated access to behavioral evidence as the interaction context evolves. HiRank adopts a hierarchical organization in which an evolving main-sequence state and source-specific long-term memories play distinct roles within the shared interaction backbone. Alongside backbone design, multi-scenario recommendation studies how a shared model adapts to heterogeneous recommendation contexts. It is closely related to multi-domain learning, which balances shared patterns with domain-specific characteristics across traffic distributions. Multi-task learning addresses a complementary dimension by sharing information across prediction objectives. Foundational architectures include MMoE~, which learns task-specific combinations of shared experts, and PLE~, which progressively separates shared and task-specific representations to mitigate negative transfer. For multi-domain CTR prediction, STAR~ combines shared central parameters with domain-specific parameters to capture both commonalities and distinctions across domains. Subsequent methods refine adaptation and information sharing across domains and tasks. PEPNet~ uses personalized priors to gate embeddings and hidden representations for multi-domain and multi-task adaptation. M3oE~ models common, domain-aspect, and task-aspect preferences through multiple expert modules and two-level fusion. More recently, SAF~ combines sample-reweighted decorrelation with adaptive feature and expert gating to address spurious correlations in multi-domain multi-task recommendation. The Multi-Distribution Learner ~ represents features, scenarios, and tasks as tokens and lets scenario and task tokens query feature representations through domain-aware attention. Combined with feature-token interaction and scenario--task fusion, this design supports joint multi-scenario and multi-task prediction. Unlike language tokens represented in a shared vocabulary space, recommendation features are highly heterogeneous in both semantics and statistical properties. Unified Transformer architectures such as OneTrans~ demonstrate the potential of jointly modeling behavior sequences and feature interactions, yet their ablations also show that retaining all tokens at every layer can increase computation without consistent performance gains. From a representational perspective, single-head attention aggregation satisfies $ $ and therefore cannot itself increase the algebraic rank of the value representations. Moreover, pure softmax-attention stacks exhibit a bias toward token uniformity in the absence of residual connections and feed-forward transformations~. RankMixer~ offers a complementary mechanism through efficient cross-token channel regrouping and concatenation. These considerations motivate a unified framework that combines attention with token mixing, balancing adaptive dependency modeling with efficient information exchange across heterogeneous features. Industrial recommenders also maintain multiple long-term behavior histories. Incorporating all such histories into the main sequence of a unified backbone can exceed practical computation and serving-latency budgets. Alongside candidate-aware retrieval, we derive user-interest queries from the main behavior sequence to provide a complementary interest perspective. This choice exploits the fine-grained behavioral context preserved in the main sequence, mitigating the information compression that may arise from aggregated profile statistics and providing a richer basis for interest retrieval. Finally, High-Rank Attention can be viewed as a mechanism for scenario-specific representation learning, conceptually related to the sharing and specialization principles of PLE~. It generates multiple composite projections from a common scenario-aware compressed representation, allowing attention to operate on learned latent views that already integrate heterogeneous information. Heterogeneous attention then models higher-order interactions among these views. We propose HiRank, a unified framework for multi-scenario recommendation that combines multi-behavior sequence modeling with heterogeneous feature interaction. Its backbone iteratively updates main-sequence states and complementary long-term retrieval queries, allowing feature context to guide subsequent behavior modeling. Scenario-aware aggregation and composite projections further construct complementary latent views for high-order interaction. Ablation studies support the complementary roles of the key components, while effective-rank diagnostics characterize representation diversity and scaling experiments show gains from longer sequences and increased model capacity. A user–item decoupled variant enables reuse of user-side computation across candidates within a request. Our method has been successfully deployed on Taobao’s homepage feeds, where both offline evaluations and online A/B tests demonstrate improvements over the production baseline.
