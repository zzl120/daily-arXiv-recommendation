Recent advances in sequential recommendation enhance transformer-based models with latent reasoning to refine user representations by autoregressively generating intermediate reasoning vectors. However, exploring the optimal reasoning depth for diverse user histories remains challenging: existing methods typically employ a fixed number of reasoning steps for all users. This ``one-size-fits-all'' strategy often leads to suboptimal representation learning due to over-thinking in simple histories or under-thinking in complex ones. To address this issue, we propose DESRec, a deliberate latent reasoning framework that performs personalized latent reasoning to learn more informative reasoning states and enhance user modeling for sequential recommendation. Specifically, it designs a reasoning step selection module that identifies the optimal reasoning step for each user sequence during the training phase. Through a self-distillation mechanism, it compresses this most informative state into the final reasoning step, which serves as the final user representation for item ranking during inference. Moreover, it introduces a progressive preference extraction mechanism for personalized reasoning, which dynamically segments a user’s history into a variable number of periods and summarizes each into a latent representing the user's preference for that period. It will be used as the input of the subsequent reasoning. The number of milestones varies with the users' history, enabling a personalized reasoning over users’ evolving interests. As a key research direction, sequential recommendation mainly aims to capture users’ behavioral patterns by modeling their historical interactions. Typical methods such as SASRec~ and BERT4Rec~ mainly utilize transformer architectures to model user preferences. These models represent user preferences using the last hidden state of the sequence and perform recommendations by matching them with candidate items. However, due to limitations in computational capacity and depth, such methods often cannot conduct fine-grained analysis over user sequences. As a result, they struggle to capture complex user interests, particularly for long-tail users and items. {image/intro-6sample-new.pdf} Recently, researchers~ have explored test-time computation to improve model performance. Chain-of-Thought~ first emerged as a prominent technique that enhances LLMs’ reasoning by decomposing complex tasks and thinking step-by-step. Nevertheless, existing large reasoning models often adopt explicit reasoning, where reasoning steps are generated as explicit tokens~. Unlike NLP tasks, where intermediate tokens are meaningful, recommenders focus on discrete IDs, which are difficult to supervise or explain in reasoning. Moreover, mapping continuous embeddings to discrete IDs will introduce semantic loss~. Therefore, recent work~ proposed latent reasoning in continuous embedding spaces without relying on explicit tokens. This paradigm is more suitable for recommendation and enables better supervision of the reasoning process. For example, ReaRec~ autoregressively generates latent reasoning vectors as intermediate steps and performs recommendation by computing similarity between the final vector and candidates. It effectively improves the model's computation capacity and facilitates deeper analysis of user preferences. While latent reasoning can effectively extend the computational capacity of models, there remain several limitations. Current approaches typically for all users. However, users' preferences vary significantly across histories, and such a one-size-fits-all reasoning process fails to capture these differences. It may lead to over-reasoning for users with simple histories and introduce redundant signals; while for complex histories, it will result in under-reasoning and incomplete preference understanding. Therefore, it is crucial to adjust the reasoning process to identify the optimal reasoning state and depth under different conditions. As shown in Figure~, the optimal reasoning steps vary considerably across users. For instance, in the Baby \& Products dataset, users exhibit highly diverse optimal reasoning depths. A natural method is to dynamically select the reasoning depth at inference time. However, such test-time adaptation usually requires extra deployment-side judgments and computational cost, which complicates serving and may be unstable. Therefore, the critical challenge lies in how to exploit the heterogeneity of optimal reasoning depths during training and learn more informative reasoning representations, while keeping inference simple with a fixed reasoning length. {image/1modelERL_2datasets_best_step_distribution_barplot_binned.pdf} To address these challenges, we believe that models should carefully analyze the evolving user interests reflected in histories and learn to under different conditions for better user preference modeling and reasoning. Therefore, we propose DESRec, a deliberate latent reasoning framework for sequential recommendation. It analyzes the evolving user interests in histories and performs personalized latent reasoning that preserves the most informative reasoning state to enhance user preference modeling. Specifically, we first design a reasoning step selection module that identifies the optimal reasoning depth for each user during training by selecting the step with the highest recommendation score. A self-distillation mechanism is then applied to preserve and compress critical information from the optimal reasoning step into the final-step representation, which serves as the final user representation for item ranking during inference. Moreover, we introduce a progressive preference extraction module to model users' evolving interests and support more personalized reasoning. It dynamically segments each user's interaction history into different periods and summarizes each into a latent milestone. The milestones are progressively integrated into the reasoning process, enabling the model to focus on distinct preference phases for personalized user modeling and better reasoning state learning. By jointly modeling user preference evolution and exploiting optimal reasoning state and depth, these components construct a deliberate and personalized latent reasoning framework, leading to more accurate recommendations. In summary, our main contributions are as follows: we propose DESRec, a deliberate latent reasoning framework for sequential recommendation that enhances user preference modeling and learns more informative reasoning states by performing personalized latent reasoning. We design a reasoning step selection module that identifies each user's optimal reasoning step during training and compresses its information into the final-step representation via self-distillation, enabling effective recommendation with the final step at inference. We introduce a progressive preference extraction module that summarizes users' histories into a variable number of milestones and guides personalized reasoning to capture evolving preferences. Sequential recommendation mainly aims to model user preferences from interaction sequences for next-item prediction. Early methods~ primarily rely on Markov chains to estimate item-item transitions using matrix factorization. With the rise of deep learning, various architectures have been explored for sequence modeling, including CNN~, RNN~, and GNN~. For instance, Caser~ employs CNNs to capture sequential patterns and model short- and long-term preferences. Later, the advent of attention mechanisms and transformer architectures brought significant advances to sequential recommendation~. For example, S3-Rec~ designs multiple self-supervised tasks to improve the representation learning of recommenders. To alleviate cold-start issues, recent methods~ attempted to incorporate additional signals such as text content to enrich item representations. For example, UniSRec~ leverages multi-domain data and item text embeddings to train the sequential model. However, most existing models cannot reason over user behavior sequences, limiting their ability to capture complex preferences. In this work, we introduce deliberate multi-step latent reasoning into sequential recommendation for better modeling of user preferences. Reasoning is an essential capability to analyze complex problems and draw accurate conclusions. Early studies~ introduced Chain-of-Thought to encourage models to decompose problems into intermediate reasoning steps. Recent work on test-time computation~ has led to reasoning models such as OpenAI-o1~ and DeepSeek-R1~, which perform test-time reasoning by incorporating long COT prompting and RL strategies such as GRPO, achieving better performance on challenging problems. Despite these strengths, these methods still suffer from the difficulties of supervising intermediate steps, inefficiency from long outputs, and semantic loss when mapping embeddings to discrete tokens. To address these issues, researchers~ have explored latent reasoning, where models perform multi-step reasoning in the latent space without generating explicit tokens. For instance, CODI~ compresses natural language CoT into latent spaces via self-distillation for efficient reasoning. In recommendation, ReaRec~ applies latent reasoning to sequential recommenders by training transformer-based models to autoregressively generate latent reasoning vectors. However, it does not explicitly exploit the varying reasoning requirements across users. {image/model.pdf} In this section, we introduce DESRec, a deliberate latent reasoning framework designed to enhance sequential recommendation through personalized latent reasoning. In this work, we proposed DESRec, a deliberate latent reasoning framework that enhances sequential recommendation and user preference modeling by introducing personalized latent reasoning. Unlike prior methods that adopt a one-size-fits-all reasoning process for all users, DESRec introduces an optimal reasoning step selection and self-distillation module that identifies the optimal reasoning depth for each user during training and preserves the most informative reasoning states into the final reasoning step for more accurate recommendation at inference. Additionally, DESRec incorporates a progressive preference extraction mechanism that summarizes user preferences into milestones over different periods and progressively reasons over them. It facilitates modeling the evolving user preferences, especially in complex behavior sequences. With these two components, DESRec achieves consistent improvements under any preset reasoning steps at inference.
