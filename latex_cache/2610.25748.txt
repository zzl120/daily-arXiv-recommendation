Compared to text-based and static image advertisements, video enables richer narrative expression and an enhanced sense of immersion, fostering deeper emotional resonance and stronger user interaction~. Consequently, video advertising has emerged as one of the fastest-growing segments within the digital advertising landscape. Over the past several decades, advertising optimization research has been primarily concentrated on targeting, allocation, and bidding mechanisms~. As these techniques approach a performance plateau, the next frontier of efficacy improvement has shifted toward the creative itself. Creative optimization grounded in user characteristics and contextual information is increasingly recognized as the pivotal determinant of advertising effectiveness~. Nevertheless, video creatives are costly artifacts produced by specialized teams, characterized by limited output and extended production cycles, which collectively constrain both the quantity and diversity of deployable creatives~. Under these constraints, the industry has converged on a retrieval-based paradigm: advertisers typically commission professional personnel to pre-produce a limited pool of candidate video creatives or modular assets targeting specific products; at serving time, the most suitable variant is retrieved to match user preferences and contextual information~. Despite its practical utility, this paradigm suffers from two fundamental limitations. First, static inventory constraints: the static and finite nature of the creative repository renders it inadequate to accommodate rapidly evolving user preferences, thereby compromising user resonance and overall advertising effectiveness. Second, feedback latency: user feedback signals can only inform subsequent production cycles and offer no mechanism for real-time optimization of creatives currently in deployment, introducing a significant latency between performance signals and creative production. To address these limitations, we propose MAPS, a multi-agent personalized script-driven video advertising system. MAPS consists of four collaborative agents. The Profile Agent constructs structured user profiles from historical interaction data and contextual information, including the video currently being consumed by the user, and continuously refines these profiles as new behavioral observations accumulate. The Scripter Agent generates personalized advertisement video scripts conditioned on user preferences, guided by target product information such as core selling points and available creative assets. The Director Agent synthesizes personalized candidate creatives from the generated scripts and target product information, ensuring that the outputs simultaneously align with individual user preferences and faithfully convey target product information. The Reflector Agent attributes online user interaction signals to profile-level and script-level causes and converts them into minimal revisions routed back to the Profile and Scripter Agents for iterative refinement. To train the Scripter Agent, we construct a reinforcement learning framework based on the Group Relative Policy Optimization ~ algorithm and design a multi-dimensional reward function that ensures structural output regularity while steering the generated scripts to align advertisement narratives with target product information and individual user preferences. Offline experiments on two representative tasks demonstrate that MAPS captures user preferences with high fidelity and generates high-quality, personalized, and native video creatives. Online A/B experiments in a specific scenario in the Kuaishou advertising system further demonstrate that MAPS delivers significant improvements in click-through rate , conversion rate , and average revenue per user over the generative baseline at industrial scale. In this paper, we introduce MAPS, a multi-agent personalized script-driven video advertising system for advertisement creative generation. By coordinating the Profile, Scripter, Director, and Reflector Agents, MAPS transforms personalized video advertising from static retrieval over a limited creative inventory into dynamic generation within a continuous creative space. We apply reinforcement learning to script generation, enabling the production of structured scripts that integrate product information into narratives aligned with user preferences. The evolution of video advertising can be characterized along two critical dimensions: personalization and nativeness~. Traditional advertising formats, such as billboards and broadcast television commercials, occupy the lower end of both dimensions, relying on static creatives and mass-reach distribution. Digital advertising, encompassing programmatic display, search, and retargeting, primarily advances personalization through audience targeting, yet typically achieves only limited integration between creatives and contextual content. Native advertising formats, including product placement, branded content marketing, and influencer marketing, attain a high degree of nativeness through narrative expression and seamless contextual integration, but remain constrained in their capacity for individualized personalization. Scaling creative-level personalization has proven particularly challenging due to prohibitive production costs and labor-intensive creative workflows~. Although dynamic creative optimization partially alleviates this tension, the substantial overhead of video production continues to impede the simultaneous realization of high personalization and high nativeness. Its primary responsibility is to construct structured user profiles from heterogeneous historical interaction data and real-time contextual signals, and to continuously refine these profiles as new behavioral observations accumulate, thereby providing high-quality user preference priors for the Scripter Agent's subsequent personalized script generation. The inputs to the Profile Agent encompass two categories of information. The first category comprises user historical interaction data, including viewing history, play duration, likes, comments, collections, shares, and follows. These multi-dimensional behavioral signals collectively characterize the user's historical content consumption patterns. The second category consists of real-time contextual information, specifically the video content currently being consumed by the user, which serves as an immediate contextual anchor for preference inference. Both categories of information are jointly fed into the Profile Agent, which employs an MLLM as its backbone to drive the profile construction process. Based on the above inputs, the Profile Agent performs deep inference over user content consumption preferences and produces a structured user profile across four dimensions: : the content domains and themes preferred by the user, such as gaming, travel, music, and fashion; : the content expression styles favored by the user, such as humorous, informative, emotionally resonant, or narratively immersive; : latent needs revealed through the user's content consumption behaviors, such as quality-of-life improvement, self-expression, and entertainment; : behavioral preferences exhibited in consumption decisions, such as price-sensitivity, quality-orientation, or trend-following. The resulting multi-dimensional profile is serialized into structured text and passed to the Scripter Agent as the user preference conditioning for script generation. The user profile is not a static artifact but evolves as behavioral observations continue to accumulate. Following each advertisement impression, the Reflector Agent attributes online user feedback to profile-level causes and routes the resulting revisions to the Profile Agent, which incorporates them into its update procedure to drive iterative refinement of the user profile. This dynamic update mechanism enables the system to responsively capture shifts in user preferences, thereby supporting closed-loop creative evolution across successive impressions over extended temporal horizons and facilitating progressive narrative development. The Scripter Agent is the core creative generation module of the MAPS framework, responsible for transforming target product information into a structurally complete, personalized advertisement video script conditioned on individual user characteristics. It receives two streams of input: the structured user profile output by the Profile Agent, which encapsulates the user's interest preferences, aesthetic inclinations, and consumption motivations; and the detailed attribute information of the target product, including product appearance, core selling points, and brand recognition elements. Conditioned on these two input streams, the Scripter Agent produces a fine-grained personalized advertisement script that fully specifies the script content, voiceover, on-screen text, visual descriptions, and pacing for each scene, and passes the resulting script to the Director Agent for subsequent video production. In addition, the Scripter Agent receives script-level revisions routed from the Reflector Agent and incorporates them to iteratively refine the script. Although general-purpose MLLMs possess a certain baseline capability for script generation, crafting scripts for personalized short-form advertisement videos demands more specialized competencies: naturally weaving product information into narrative contexts aligned with user preferences, adapting tone and expressive style to individual user characteristics, and constructing emotionally resonant call-to-action sequences that match user decision-making tendencies. To acquire these competencies, we adopt a two-stage training paradigm: a supervised fine-tuning stage to establish the foundational capability for structured script generation, followed by a reinforcement learning stage to align model outputs with personalized quality objectives. The SFT stage aims to teach the model the fundamental syntax and structural conventions of advertisement scripts within the MAPS framework. We formulate the task as a sequence-to-sequence generation problem: the input $x$ consists of the structured user profile and target product attribute information, while the output $y$ is the structured text of the target advertisement script. Its inputs come from two sources: the structured advertisement script output by the Scripter Agent, which fully specifies the script content, voiceover, visual descriptions, and pacing for each scene; and the available visual assets of the target product, including product images and brand logos, which serve as visual grounding constraints to ensure that the generated content faithfully reflects the product's visual characteristics and brand identity. The Reflector Agent serves as the feedback-driven refinement module of the MAPS framework, closing the loop between deployed creatives and upstream generation. It operates on online user interaction signals collected after each advertisement impression, such as play duration, likes, comments, and purchases. It attributes these signals to their underlying causes, converts the attribution results into minimal revision directives, and routes them back to the Profile Agent and the Scripter Agent for iterative refinement. Given the online interaction signals associated with a deployed creative, the Reflector Agent performs attribution to localize the source of suboptimal performance, distinguishing between two complementary categories of causes: : inaccuracies in user modeling, such as misjudged topical interests, mismatched presentation styles, or incorrectly inferred decision-making tendencies, which indicate that the user profile fails to faithfully capture the user's actual preferences. : deficiencies in the generated script, such as a weak narrative hook, misaligned tone or pacing, unclear CTA, or suboptimal presentation of product selling points, which indicate that the script itself does not effectively engage the target user. Based on the attribution results, the Reflector Agent produces minimal revision directives that can be executed by upstream agents at low latency and bounded cost, avoiding full regeneration of the creative. Profile-level directives are routed back to the Profile Agent to update the structured user profile, whereas script-level directives are routed back to the Scripter Agent to iteratively refine the advertisement script. Through this targeted feedback loop, MAPS continuously adapts its creatives to evolving user preferences across successive impressions, enabling closed-loop creative evolution without re-running the entire generation pipeline from scratch.
