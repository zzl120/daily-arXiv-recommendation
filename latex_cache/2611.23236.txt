In recent years, large language models have leveraged their rich semantic understanding to provide high-quality embeddings for item side information~. This approach effectively alleviates data sparsity and cold-start issues in recommendation. However, these embeddings may leak sensitive user information~. Embedding inversion attacks can reconstruct the original text from public embeddings with high accuracy, and the risk increases when data is sent to uncontrolled third-party LLM APIs~. {picture/1.png} Faced with this input-side threat, existing defenses struggle. Post‑hoc recommendation protections such as differential privacy in recommender systems~ and federated learning for recommendation~ assume a trustworthy collector and can hardly prevent leakage once data leaves the device. Traditional synonym‑substitution obfuscation stays at the word level~, treating items as isolated tokens and easily shattering semantics, thereby destroying semantic relationships between items anchored by side information. To simultaneously protect privacy and maintain utility, input-side protection should achieve two semantic-level goals. The first goal is to so that embedding inversion attacks cannot exploit the invertible mapping between embeddings and their source texts~. If obfuscated embeddings remain too close to the original, attackers can still approximate the text. Therefore, embeddings must move away from the original to break this invertible connection. The second goal is to that connect cold and popular items through shared attributes~. Maintaining these relative relationships preserves the semantic skeleton needed for recommendation without requiring global semantic fidelity. Traditional discrete token-level substitution cannot finely control the magnitude or direction of changes in continuous embedding space. Such perturbations disrupt local semantic relations and tightly couple deviation strength with order preservation, making joint optimization impossible. The pipeline of LLM allows fine-grained manipulation of embeddings before decoding~, generating new words while decoupling deviation strength from relational structure. However, recent LLM-based text privacy methods, e.g., EmojiPrompt~, LLM-Anonymization~, and IncogniText~, all follow semantic fidelity, hiding private attributes while preserving literal meaning. This misaligns with recommender systems, which need relational fidelity, i.e., preserving the relative semantic skeleton across items rather than literal text. Under relational fidelity, obfuscated content can be arbitrary as long as a usable item similarity structure persists, turning the task from constrained masking into active topology reconstruction. LLMs naturally suit this, yet existing methods were never designed for it. To realize , we propose a with three sequential stages: prototype-aware graph construction, prototype variant exploration, and anchor-oriented graph expansion. We first identify high-influence prototypes and build a static semantic neighbor graph to capture their structural impact. The LLM then explores suitable obfuscated variants for these prototypes, which serve as stable structural anchors. Finally, the framework propagates these anchored mappings through the static graph to cover the remaining vocabulary. This process is optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization ~ with three complementary rewards: i) maximizes embedding distance to enforce , ii) preserves local similarity order to maintain the semantic distribution necessary for recommendation, and iii) constrains generation within category-specific semantic manifolds to prevent degenerate shortcuts. By jointly optimizing these rewards, the framework balances privacy and utility, protecting sensitive information while preserving the relative semantic structure crucial for recommendation. Our contributions are as follows: We formulate input-side obfuscation for LLM-enhanced recommendation as a topology-preserving privacy problem, introducing to defend against embedding inversion while preserving relative item semantics. We propose a that scales topology-aware obfuscation from high-influence prototype words to the full vocabulary. We design three GRPO rewards, i.e., individual dissimilarity, structural consistency, and semantic constraint, to jointly enforce semantic shift and preserve item relations. Under aligned CosSim, experiments on two benchmarks show that our method achieves the highest recommendation utility at comparable defense strength. Large language models have reshaped recommender systems, either as semantic enhancers enriching item metadata, user profiles, and interaction graphs~, or as direct recommenders via language modeling, prompting, instruction tuning, or parameter-efficient fine-tuning, covering frameworks like P5~, M6-Rec~, TALLRec~, LLaRA~ and ReLLa~, and ranking or explanation models~. However, encoding side information and interactions into continuous embeddings raises privacy concerns. User clicks can be inferred from exposure , and LLM recommenders are vulnerable to inversion attacks , underscoring the need for input-level privacy protection . Embedding inversion attacks aim to reconstruct original text or sensitive attributes from exposed embeddings, posing significant privacy risks in NLP. Early studies demonstrated that embedding models leak rich semantic information~. Subsequent work further recovered full sentences using generative decoders~ and inverted dense embeddings with high fidelity through iterative optimization and beam search~. Recent attacks further expose practical vulnerabilities under cross-model~, black-box~, multilingual~, and zero-shot settings~, motivating input-side protection for recommendation embeddings. Text obfuscation protects privacy by perturbing raw text while preserving downstream utility. Existing approaches include word- or character-level substitution and adversarial perturbation~, homograph or typo-based replacement, and context-aware embedding perturbation~, as well as differential privacy-based sanitization such as SanText/SanText+~ and local DP token mapping~. Other methods exploit controlled paraphrasing, masking, or hybrid perturbations to hide sensitive attributes. Recent work explores LLM-based privacy protection, including prompt obfuscation~, LLM-based anonymization~, conditional attribute randomization~, utility-aware rewriting~, and privacy-preserving LLM inference~. However, these studies mostly target general NLP text, user-generated content, or LLM prompts, leaving privacy-preserving obfuscation for item-side textual metadata in recommender systems largely underexplored. Future work may extend the framework to other modalities and decentralized deployment. *{Limitations} Our framework currently treats side-information words with a unified obfuscation strategy, without explicitly assigning higher protection priority to more sensitive or rare words over generic ones. Future work may incorporate sensitivity-aware weighting to better allocate privacy budgets across words. Additionally, PGPO introduces extra computational cost due to LLM-based variant generation, which may be mitigated in future work through offline caching, batch generation, lightweight policy distillation, or retrieval-based reuse of high-quality obfuscation mappings. Moreover, our current study focuses primarily on textual side information, leaving the extension of relational fidelity to multimodal inputs such as images, audio, video, and knowledge graphs for future work.
