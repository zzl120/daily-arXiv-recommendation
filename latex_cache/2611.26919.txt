 Retrieval-augmented generation grounds language models in organization-specific knowledge by retrieving from a dense vector index . Such an index lives in the latent space of the embedding model that built it: queries must be embedded by that same model, or inner-product search no longer returns meaningful neighbours. In practice, every query must therefore go through the indexing model, whether that is an API with per-call cost and network latency or a large self-hosted model with its own serving footprint. Using a different encoder for queries normally requires re-embedding the entire corpus. That can be expensive, and in many deployments it is not possible at all: the index may be managed by a third party, the raw documents may no longer be accessible, or the corpus may be ingested continuously. We study : a query is encoded by one model, projected into the latent space of a different model, and retrieved directly against that model's existing index. Either model may be open-weight or served through an API. Prior work has shown that mappings between embedding spaces are feasible, from unsupervised alignment to embedding conversion that serves a smaller model online while retaining a larger model offline and effective point-wise distillation . We build on these insights and focus on the setting that arises at the very start of every deployment: the . At cold start, three constraints hold at once. First, there are no query logs and no relevance labels. Second, the target index already exists, but every anchor document must also be embedded by the source model, in API calls or in compute, so the training budget is measured in anchors. Third, a projection will not be perfect, so some queries must still be answered by the target model, and the queries that matter most are rarely the common ones. Real traffic is dominated by easy queries, while the comparatively rare complex queries are where retrieval quality is most visible to users and where a projection is most likely to fail. In summary, we make the following contributions: We formalize : label-free projection of queries from one model into the existing index of another under an anchor budget, without re-indexing. We show that budget-aware anchor selection via Cosine FPS is an important and loss-independent design choice, whose benefit grows with corpus redundancy. We introduce a Manifold Alignment Loss that couples directional alignment with orthogonal regularization, preventing representation collapse in the data-scarce regime. We design an approximate escalation router that targets the rare, hard queries that define perceived quality, at negligible local cost. To our knowledge, ERA is the first end-to-end system that combines label-free cold-start alignment, budget-aware anchor selection, and hard-query escalation for zero-reindex cross-model retrieval. ERA builds directly on Embedding-Converter and LEAF and focuses on the regime. Both prior methods sample reference texts uniformly at random, which is natural when paired embeddings are plentiful. At cold start, however, every anchor must be embedded by the source model, and real corpora are highly redundant: in a corpus of 1{,}000 documents where 900 are near-duplicates of one topic and 100 span distinct topics, uniform sampling of 100 anchors spends roughly 90 of them on the redundant cluster and covers only about 10 distinct regions of the target space. ERA instead selects anchors with Cosine Farthest Point Sampling so that the budget covers distinct regions first---a design choice that, on clustered corpora, improves both Embedding-Converter and LEAF as well as ERA under the same anchor budget. Learned embedding spaces are known to be anisotropic, with vectors concentrating in a narrow cone on the hypersphere . Contrastive and metric learning address this by encouraging uniformity across the sphere, including Global Orthogonal Regularization and related objectives . When a projection is learned from only a small anchor set, point-wise objectives can collapse toward a low-variance subspace; ERA pairs directional alignment with orthogonal regularization to preserve angular spread in this regime. We introduced ERA , an end-to-end system for cold-start cross-model latent retrieval that enables queries encoded by one model to directly search an existing vector index built by another, without re-embedding the corpus and without human relevance labels. By selecting anchor documents via Cosine Farthest Point Sampling, training a lightweight adapter with a Manifold Alignment Loss that prevents representation collapse in the data-scarce regime, and pairing the adapter with a deterministic router that escalates the rare, complex queries that govern perceived quality, ERA matches or outperforms point-wise $ _2$ and embedding-conversion baselines across five BEIR benchmarks while adding only ${ }1.2$\,ms of adapter and router compute beyond the local encoder. *{Limitations} Our study highlights four practical boundaries that motivate future work: When $d_S < d_T$, an asymmetric adapter cannot recover semantic distinctions that the source encoder never captured; as confirmed by our reverse-direction negative control , ERA faithfully reproduces the target manifold's geometry up to its native capacity ceiling. On heavy-tailed corpora with disconnected outlier documents , greedy Cosine FPS can spend part of a small anchor budget on peripheral outliers rather than the dense semantic core. Exploring quantile-based farthest point sampling (e.g., selecting the $q$-th percentile farthest point) is a promising direction. While the lightweight $0.6$B router reliably isolates large-gap failure queries on structured domains , it behaves closer to uniform load balancing on short, noisy financial queries , and static validation quantiles can drift under test-time distribution shifts.
