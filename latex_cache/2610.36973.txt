For instance, in e-commerce scenarios, click-through rate captures initial user engagement with recommended items, whereas conversion rate reflects actual purchase conversions that drive transactional value~. However, these objectives do not always align: optimizing one in isolation far from guarantees a commensurate improvement in the other~. Consequently, effectively coordinating these distinct objectives to improve joint recommendation performance remains a critical challenge for GR. Over the past decade, extensive efforts have been devoted to multi-objective recommendation. Specifically, existing techniques coordinate multiple objectives either by directly balancing multi-objective losses and gradients~, or by transferring and fusing model capabilities via item rankings~, hidden representations~, and prediction scores~. However, being designed for traditional recommenders that score whole items, these methods operate only at the coarse level. When adapted to GR, even if multiple objectives are aligned at the item level, the lack of fine-grained supervision prevents the model from coordinating these objectives at intermediate decoding steps, which can still cause retrieval failures and lead to suboptimal joint recommendation performance~. To overcome the limitations of traditional item-wise methods in GR, we consider introducing ~, a paradigm that has achieved remarkable success in merging specialized experts in foundation models. Following the standard multi-teacher distillation framework~, MOPD trains separate teacher models dedicated to individual objectives and distills their capabilities into a single student model. Specifically, it samples trajectories on-policy from the student and minimizes the average reverse Kullback-Leibler divergence from the student to each teacher's next-token probability distribution at every decoding step~. This mechanism makes MOPD particularly well-suited for GR due to two distinct advantages: , which optimizes within the student's own generation space and is equivalent to directly maximizing the KL-regularized joint recommendation objective ; and , which goes beyond coarse item-wise signals to provide fine-grained supervision at each token, naturally fitting the multi-token decoding process of GR.
