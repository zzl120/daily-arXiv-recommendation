Existing methods can infer latent states from logged interactions, yet the statistical meaning of the latent environment and its effect on preference remain underdetermined. We formulate this task as conditionally identifiable risk-aware recommendation and propose Conditionally Identifiable Latent-Environment Recommendation . CILER uses a user-conditioned exponential family to model the latent environment and a feature-indexed polynomial to specify how it changes preference. It predicts by marginalizing item probabilities over the inferred environment distribution. Under sufficient variation, correct specification, and decoder regularity, CILER identifies the environment-sensitive representation up to the stated equivalence class. We further bound excess deployment log-risk by environment-inference error. Controlled studies test the observable consequences of sufficient variation and model specification. A change in the latent environment can alter preference over item categories even when the user and candidate items remain the same, a pattern illustrated by the temporal shift on Meituan in Fig.~~. In such settings, a naive strategy is to learn stable preference by enforcing invariance across training environments~. This strategy reduces sensitivity to observed environment shifts, and it also discards environment-sensitive preference that remains relevant to deployment ranking. Generalizing in OOD recommendation requires separating the two kinds of preference. Recent research infers latent environments from logged interactions and separates stable from environment-sensitive preference without environment labels~. Reconstruction and disentanglement objectives can fit the observed interactions, yet they do not fix the statistical meaning of the latent environment. Observationally equivalent parameterizations can assign the same interaction variation differently between the stable and environment-sensitive components. They can also imply different environment distributions and preference mechanisms~. Additional sparsity or disentanglement penalties narrow the solution empirically, but they do not resolve this ambiguity. CI-RR seeks a predictor that minimizes deployment risk under an identifiability constraint . Our work relates to out-of-distribution recommendation, environment inference, and identifiable latent representation learning. These areas define the prediction setting, the unobserved source of shift, and the structural conditions used by CILER. Out-of-distribution recommenders aim to preserve ranking performance when interaction distributions change. Early methods learn invariant preferences or separate stable preference from environment-sensitive preference~. Recent methods address specified shifts through distributionally robust objectives, popularity-aware modeling, causal graph learning, or test-time adaptation~. Recent recommendation studies further show that discarding variant preference can reduce generalization, while sequential models construct auxiliary environments to learn stable preference under shift~. Cross-domain methods transfer invariances from labeled domains or adapt with deployment interactions~. These methods improve OOD ranking, yet their objectives do not determine the distribution of an unobserved environment or its effect on preference. CILER constrains both components and marginalizes item probabilities over the inferred environment distribution at deployment. Invariant learning typically assumes observed environment labels, and its guarantees depend on the supplied partition~. When labels are unavailable, EIIL and related methods infer discrete partitions that reveal invariance violations~. TIVA estimates a partition from observed attributes~. Recent methods infer task-specific partitions from temporal structure or feature decorrelation~. Continuously indexed adaptation replaces discrete domains with a domain index~. These methods use the inferred environment to train an invariant predictor. CILER learns a conditional latent-environment distribution and marginalizes item probabilities over this distribution at deployment. Latent representations are not identifiable from observations alone without additional structure~. Nonlinear ICA introduces observed auxiliary variables that modulate exponential-family latent distributions~. Recent causal representation results identify latent variables from unknown or soft interventions across multiple environments~. Score-based methods recover latent causal models from changes in density scores~. General-environment and weight-variant models use sufficient mechanism variation across observed conditions~. Polynomial causal models identify nonlinear mechanisms when their coefficients vary sufficiently across conditions~. CILER brings these conditions to OOD recommendation through a user-conditioned exponential-family environment and a feature-indexed polynomial preference mechanism. In this work, we presented Conditionally Identifiable Latent-Environment Recommendation for OOD recommendation under latent environment shifts. CILER restricts the latent-environment distribution and the environment-sensitive preference mechanism, then marginalizes item probabilities over the inferred environment distribution at deployment. Theoretically, we established conditional identifiability of the environment-sensitive representation under the stated conditions and bounded excess deployment log-risk by environment-inference error. Empirically, CILER ranks first on all twelve OOD ranking metrics across three datasets while retaining competitive IID accuracy. Controlled studies and capacity-matched ablations further connect recovery and ranking performance to the stated conditions and the two restrictions.
