Multi-task learning over heterogeneous data is fundamental to modern recommendation, while generative models are emerging as the backbone of next-generation recommenders. However, the integration of multi-task learning into the generative paradigm remains largely unexplored. Existing multi-task recommenders, in both discriminative and generative paradigms, extract task-relevant features from a single task-agnostic representation and wire tasks into a predefined conversion funnel. We show that this scheme is inherently prone to a threefold collapse. , where task-specific signals are injected late and diluted in the shared latent space. , where task dependencies are either implicitly absorbed by the backbone or statically fixed by predefined funnels. , where tasks depend on features at different scales and shift across training stages. We propose , a multi-task generative recommender with three components, each alleviating one collapse. injects task identity into computation stream early and separates the shared context stream from the task-specific stream, alleviating signal dilution. replaces the predefined funnel with explicit cross-task interaction, letting each task condition on the realized outcomes of its predecessors with learned, input-adaptive strength. lets each task gather multi-scale information across different layers at different training stages. By sharing information across tasks, MTL reduces serving cost and improves robustness under bias, sparsity, and cold-start settings~. Existing work mainly follows three directions. The first focuses on allocating capacity across tasks, evolving from shared-bottom~ to MMoE~ and PLE~ to mitigate negative transfer. The second investigates the relation among tasks, such as ESMM~ and AITM~, which leverage dependencies among user actions to reduce sparsity and selection bias. The third operates at the optimization level. GradNorm~ rebalances per-task loss scales, PCGrad~ projects away the conflicting component between task gradients. CAGrad~ and Nash-MTL~ solve for an update direction that every task accepts. Tailored for high-cardinality, non-stationary streaming data~, GR leverages Transformer-based sequence models to reformulate recommendation as a direct sequence generation task. By unifying historical behaviors, feedback, context, and candidates into a single tokenized sequence, GR collapses the fragmented cascade into one backbone and enables end-to-end model scaling~. Although GR is becoming the backbone of a new generation of recommender systems, how to meet the multi-objective demands inherent to industrial settings remains an open problem. GR is trained under a single-objective, next-item prediction paradigm ~ and does not natively support the joint optimization of multiple heterogeneous objectives. To bridge this mismatch, existing multi-task generative recommenders fuse task modeling into the shared sequence representation, either conditioning tasks at output-side heads~ or injecting task tokens that are encoded jointly with behavior tokens~. Either way, task signals become entangled with the shared representation. Multi-task learning with the generative paradigm still faces three collapses. Task-specific signals are injected late and diluted in the shared space. Context tokens and task tokens are functionally heterogeneous. Encoded as a continuous stream, context tokens must characterize user behavior across diverse scenarios, yielding a high-dimensional representation coupled with user intention. Task tokens, in contrast, are grounded on task-specific semantic manifolds~. Task tokens require distinct, task-oriented emphases. Task dependencies are either implicitly absorbed by the backbone or statically fixed by predefined funnels~. Interactions entangled inside the shared stream can only be curbed by cross-task gradient isolation that discards useful cross-task signal~. Yet some multi-task dependencies are complex and cannot be specified a priori~. Different layers of a deep backbone encode context at different granularities. From fine-grained local patterns to abstract long-range intent, heterogeneous tasks rely on different depths at different training stages~. Taking only the last layer or a fixed per-layer scalar gate~ flattens this hierarchy into one representation shared by all tasks. Early shared-bottom networks and Cross-Stitch~ share a single trunk and branch into per-task towers. Expert-based designs refine this sharing. MMoE~ routes soft-selected experts per task, and PLE~ explicitly separates shared and task-specific experts through progressive layered extraction to curb negative transfer. A parallel line targets multi-domain/multi-scenario settings. STAR~ multiplies a shared centered network with domain-specific weights in a star topology, while M2M~ and APG~ generate customized network parameters from scenario or instance context. Recent mixture-of-experts variants such as STEM~ and HoME~ push specialization further via task-specific embeddings, stop-gradient gating, and hierarchical expert grouping. Orthogonally, an optimization-centric line attributes multi-task interference to conflicting gradients on shared parameters and manipulates the update direction, e.g., PCGrad~, GradNorm~, CAGrad~ and Nash-MTL~. Generative recommendation reformulates recommendation as sequential transduction over user behaviors, with backbones such as HSTU~, TIGER~, and OneRec~ scaling attention-based next-item generation. Multi-task modeling in GR, however, remains largely inherited from the classical recipe. Tasks are still read out of one shared sequence representation. IntTravel~ strengthens an HSTU backbone for travel recommendation but encodes the behavior sequence without task tokens, branching only after encoding. OneTrans~ unifies sequential and non-sequential features into a single token stream with mixed shared/token-specific parameterization, yet it likewise carries no task token and follows the unified single-stream paradigm. HGenPush~ and Onerank~ takes a first step toward task-aware encoding by inserting task tokens into the sequence, but these tokens share one stream and one parameter set with the context tokens, task-specific signals are still diluted in the shared space.
