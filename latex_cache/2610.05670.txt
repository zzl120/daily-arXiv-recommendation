Generative recommendation~ has emerged as a transformative paradigm for recommender systems in today's generative AI era, reformulating the recommendation problem from conventional embedding-based matching to token generation~. Compared to traditional methods, GR offers superior generalization that subsumes retrieval and ranking into a single generation process. Despite its remarkable success and rapid adoption by major platforms serving hundreds of millions of users , existing GR methods are predominantly optimized for recommendation accuracy while neglecting a critical yet underexplored societal dimension: the of the generated recommendations. As illustrated in Figure~, existing GR methods may accurately capture user preference yet recommend uncredible content to users. This oversight leads to serious consequences, including eroded user trust, reputational risks to platforms, and even social instability . {Figures/toy_example2.png} There are some recent efforts on developing credible content recommendation methods . For example, some works disentangle news content into veracity embeddings and event embeddings, and utilize only event embeddings to compute user-item matching scores, thereby mitigating the influence of uncredible content. Cai et al. employ a diffusion model to improve recommendation credibility. However, these methods all operate within the paradigm, where recommendations are made by computing similarity scores between user and item embeddings over a large candidate pool. As such, they are inherently incompatible with the emerging GR paradigm, which forgoes embedding matching entirely and instead directly generates the semantic IDs~ of target items. In this paper, we focus on how to improve the credibility of GR. However, building a credible GR model is non-trivial, primarily due to the following two challenges : Existing methods tokenize items by encoding their metadata through pre-trained language models and subsequently quantizing the resulting embeddings into discrete tokens via vector quantization . However, these models are pre-trained with objectives such as masked language modeling or next-token prediction, which capture topical and syntactic patterns but are inherently agnostic to content credibility~. As a result, credible and uncredible content that share similar topics are mapped to nearby regions in the embedding space. This credibility entanglement causes topically similar but factually divergent items to be assigned similar/nearby tokens, making them difficult to discriminate during the subsequent generation process and potentially leading to the recommendation of uncredible content. A straightforward solution to mitigate uncredible content is to directly remove uncredible items from training data and retrain the generation model. However, this naive removal strategy inevitably degrades recommendation accuracy, as the valuable information in the uncredible items that reflect users' preference is also removed. For instance, a user who frequently engages with uncredible health news articles still reveals a strong interest in wellness topics, which should be leveraged to capture the user's preference and support accurate generation. The empirical evidence is provided in Appendix . Therefore, there is a fundamental trade-off between recommendation accuracy and credibility in the generation stage. To address these challenges, in this paper, we propose a novel dible enerative ecommendation model, . Specifically, to overcome , we design a novel credibility-aware tokenizer that introduces credibility regularization to enforce intra-class compactness and inter-class separation between credible and uncredible items. This compels the codebook to disentangle credibility signals at the discrete token level and thereby eliminating entanglement between factually divergent items. To address , we propose an accuracy-preserving and credibility-oriented generator grounded in discrete diffusion. Exploiting the fact that discrete diffusion training is conditioned on masked tokens, we design an asymmetric masking probability reduction strategy that selectively reduces the masking probabilities of tokens associated with uncredible content identified in the tokenization stage, while leaving all other tokens encoding valuable user preference signals at their original masking probabilities. We instantiate this strategy through two variants: a weighting strategy that performs absolute erasure of uncredible token contributions, and a weighting strategy that applies distance-aware down-weighting based on proximity to cluster centroids. These designs gracefully suppress uncredible generation while preserving the topical preference signals, achieving a balance between recommendation accuracy and credibility. Overall, our contributions are summarized as: We propose a novel credible generative content recommendation model, , which is the first GR model that considers the credibility of the generated recommendations. We design a credibility-aware tokenizer and a novel accuracy-preserving and credibility-oriented generator, which work collaboratively to improve the recommendation credibility while preserving high recommendation accuracy. Extensive experiments on three real-world datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed model in generating credible and accurate content recommendations. Credible content recommendation follows the task of sequential recommendation . A recommendation model is deemed more credible when its generated recommendation lists contain fewer such items. To address , we propose an accuracy-preserving and credibility-oriented generator based on discrete diffusion. Specifically, we design an to suppress the contributions of the last tokens of uncredible items during generation, while preserving valuable content-level topical signals. Our design is motivated by the hierarchical nature of our credibility-aware tokenizer: the earlier levels capture dominant semantic patterns such as topics and writing styles, while the later levels progressively encode finer-grained details through residual refinement~. Since rather than coarse topical divergence , such credibility-discriminative signals are more likely to be preserved at the last few token levels after the shared topical information has been absorbed by the preceding levels. Conversely, reducing the masking probabilities of earlier levels would suppress tokens that encode valuable user preference signals, thereby degrading recommendation accuracy. All datasets used in our experiments are derived from publicly available sources and contain no personally identifiable information. Existing GR methods generally follow a two-stage design: tokenization stage that represents each item with discrete tokens, and generation stage that generates a sequence of tokens to represent the recommended items . From the perspective of tokenization, existing methods can be broadly categorized into clustering-based and vector quantization-based approaches. Clustering-based methods organize items into hierarchical groups and represent each item with the cluster ID . Vector quantization-based methods learn a shared codebook of discrete tokens and represent each item with the corresponding tokens in the codebook . For example, TIGER~ pioneered this direction by utilizing RQ-VAE to quantize item content embeddings into hierarchical semantic IDs. Subsequent work has sought to enrich the tokenization process: Some works ~ integrates collaborative signals from pre-trained recommendation models into vector quantization through contrastive alignment, and LC-Rec~ aligns a large language model with semantic IDs via multi-task learning to improve the model's understanding of discrete tokens. SETRec learns order-agnostic identifiers to improve the performance of GR through LLMs. Nevertheless, these methods follow a two-stage paradigm where the tokenizer and the downstream recommender are optimized separately, leading to objective misalignment between the tokenizer and generator. To bridge this gap, recent efforts have explored end-to-end joint optimization: ETEGRec~ proposes alternating optimization between the tokenizer and recommender with alignment losses, while DIGER~ introduces differentiable semantic IDs via Gumbel-Softmax to enable direct gradient flow from the recommender back to the tokenizer. From the perspective of generation, existing GR models can be categorized into autoregressive models and non-autoregressive models. Most existing methods adopt AR generation, which generate tokens sequentially from left to right . At industrial scale, HSTU~ introduces hierarchical sequential transducers with significant scalability, and OneRec~ unifies retrieval and ranking within a single generative model with preference alignment. However, the AR paradigm suffers from two inherent limitations: the sequential decoding process is time-consuming, as each token must wait for all preceding tokens to be generated, and errors made at earlier generation steps propagate irreversibly to subsequent tokens, resulting in error accumulation~. To address these limitations, NAR models have been proposed . Inspired by discrete diffusion language models and diffusion models in RSs , LLaDA-Rec~ introduces a parallel generation paradigm, where all tokens are generated simultaneously conditioned on bidirectional context. This design enables richer inter-token dependency modeling by attending to both preceding and following tokens. Although GR has shown promising results, existing methods generally focus on improving recommendation accuracy while ignoring the credibility of the recommended content. To fill this gap, in this paper, we propose a novel GR model that explicitly incorporates content credibility into both the tokenization and generation stages. RSs play a crucial role in helping users quickly locate content they are interested in . However, RSs have been widely recognized as significant contributors to the spreading of uncredible content, including fake news, rumor, and misinformation, across modern web platforms . The majority of prior work in this area has centered on two directions: examining whether standard recommendation algorithms amplify uncredible content, and developing mitigation strategies. The majority of prior work has centered on examining whether standard recommendation algorithms amplify uncredible content . For instance, Pathak et al.~ conduct a systematic evaluation showing that collaborative filtering algorithms disproportionately recommend misinformation to users who have previously engaged with such content, creating self-reinforcing filter bubbles. While these studies provide valuable diagnostic insights, they remain at the analysis level and do not propose concrete algorithmic solutions. In contrast, a smaller body of work moves beyond analysis to develop novel mitigation strategies . Wang et al.~ propose veracity-aware news recommendation frameworks that explicitly disentangle items' veracity embeddings from event embeddings and solely utilize event embeddings to reduce the exposure of fake news. Sallami et al.~ introduce FANAR, a trust-based adaptation of collaborative filtering that excludes untrustworthy neighbors from the recommendation process. More recently, Cai et al.~ explores to steer diffusion models toward credible content recommendation through two designs: a disentangled diffusion model and the progressively enhancement of credible subspace projection. Although these mitigation methods have demonstrated effectiveness, they are fundamentally built upon discriminative modeling paradigms that rely on computing embedding similarities between user and item embeddings to make recommendations. As such, they are inherently incompatible with the emerging GR paradigm, which formulates the recommendation task as a sequence generation problem over discrete tokens rather than a matching problem over continuous embeddings. To bridge this gap, in this paper, we propose a novel credibility-aware GR model.
