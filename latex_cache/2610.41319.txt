Modern map applications naturally encompass two distinct yet coupled functional domains: the spatial mobility domain, which records high-frequency utility actions such as destination searches, route requests, and navigation, and the local-service domain, which captures service exploration and consumption across diverse commercial categories. Because users rely on map platforms primarily as mobility tools, explicit service transactions are often sparse, whereas mobility trajectories carry rich contextual cues about companion lifestyle needs. Conventional next-POI prediction focuses on forecasting a user's immediate spatial destination within the mobility domain~, yet it largely overlooks the derivative service demands triggered by these trips. For instance, planning a route to a family park often entails subsequent target-domain needs for child-friendly dining or nearby parking. Bridging these two domains requires jointly interpreting heterogeneous map histories alongside user profiles to uncover latent consumption intents. This motivates cross-domain POI recommendation from mobility behaviors to local services, which leverages abundant mobility evidence to proactively recommend relevant everyday services beyond explicitly stated user queries, as illustrated in Figure~. {figures/teaser_final.pdf} Large language models combine broad world knowledge with reasoning capabilities, offering a strong semantic foundation for linking observed mobility behaviors to implicit service needs. Expressing these latent connections in natural language provides an interpretable bridge between source-domain mobility evidence and target-domain service preferences. Recent studies have explored explicit intent modeling and query-based recommendation from various perspectives. Specifically, PIGEON~ predicts open-set living needs to retrieve local services via embeddings, AIR~ extracts atomic intents to transfer preferences across content and e-commerce domains, and EAGER~ aligns supervised query generation with single-domain click preferences through reinforcement learning. Although these works demonstrate the promise of explicit intent representations and behavioral optimization, they primarily generate isolated or unranked intents from homogeneous item interactions. Consequently, a critical challenge remains in how to infer a prioritized set of complementary service intents from noisy, heterogeneous map logs and jointly optimize their list-wise target-domain utility through subsequent multi-service feedback. Addressing this cross-domain task entails three fundamental challenges. First, contextualized multi-intent reasoning requires jointly interpreting heterogeneous mobility actions and user profiles in context. The model must distinguish user-specific service demands from generic commonsense associations, derive complementary expansion intents alongside primary needs, and establish their relative priorities. Second, behavioral supervision across domains is inherently indirect because interaction logs record only external actions rather than underlying motivations. Since multiple plausible query sets can correspond to the same mobility activity, merely imitating static reference queries via supervised learning fails to directly optimize alignment with subsequent target-domain interactions. Third, ranked query generation involves a trade-off between individual query relevance and overall list coverage. Optimizing solely for relevance often yields repetitive queries targeting a single dominant service, whereas pursuing broad coverage risks introducing weak queries. Because a single response-level reward conflates these factors across the entire output, fine-grained credit assignment is required to disentangle individual query quality from the collective utility of the complete list. To overcome these challenges, we propose MILES-Rec, a two-stage framework that bridges the mobility domain with local-service discovery through prioritized natural-language queries. To support contextualized multi-intent reasoning, we construct progressive reasoning demonstrations that guide the model through situation understanding, intent expansion, and query prioritization via supervised fine-tuning. To learn effectively from indirect behavioral supervision, we introduce intent-aligned Group Relative Policy Optimization, refining the supervised policy with feedback from subsequent target-domain interactions. The behavioral reward jointly measures query-to-service relevance and one-to-one ranked service coverage while penalizing redundancy and excessive length. Furthermore, we design a two-level credit assignment mechanism to coordinate individual relevance with list coverage. A global list-level advantage guides the entire response including reasoning and structure, whereas a local query-specific advantage is selectively applied to the corresponding query-text tokens. At inference time, MILES-Rec generates prioritized service queries from user profiles and map histories to retrieve relevant local-service POIs. We further construct MILES-Bench from real-world user profiles and heterogeneous interaction logs on a large-scale mapping platform. It provides 19,006 verified reasoning demonstrations for supervised fine-tuning, 3,000 interaction-grounded samples for reinforcement learning, and two user-disjoint test sets covering rich and sparse histories across ten cities. To assess both intermediate intent reasoning and downstream retrieval, we establish a unified evaluation protocol spanning POI Retrieval Utility over a two-million-POI pool, Service Intent Alignment, and Semantic Demand Alignment. Together, these dimensions evaluate retrieved POI utility alongside the category relevance, constraint compatibility, and service coverage of the generated natural-language intent queries. We construct MILES-Bench from real-world map logs covering both rich and sparse histories, paired with a three-dimensional evaluation protocol linking POI retrieval utility with service and semantic alignment.
