Corresponding authors.} {fig/intro.png} Driven by the scaling of model capacity and data, LLMs have progressed from generation to knowledge-intensive reasoning and agentic decision making~. This progress has motivated researchers to transfer these capabilities to recommender systems by adapting language-model architectures, or even language models themselves, to improve existing recommendation systems~. Recent generative recommendation models, exemplified by the family~, have demonstrated that Transformer-based architectures can scale recommendation modeling to support industrial-scale end-to-end deployment. However, they mostly make predictions through implicit representation computing, making their decision processes difficult to interpret or control~. Although several studies introduce latent reasoning mechanisms into recommendation~, their reasoning still operates in a non-semantic latent space, making it difficult to determine whether the learned representations encode coherent, task-relevant reasoning. A more intuitive approach is to directly use LLMs as the backbone of foundation recommendation models and adapt them through recommendation-specific training~. Recent work, particularly OneReason~, demonstrates the success of the {Think-then-Answer} paradigm for FRMs, in which the model reasons over user preferences and context before generating recommendations. However, recommendation reasoning cannot simply inherit generic CoT patterns~, as user interests are diverse and evolving, requiring structured reasoning from preference induction to intent inference and decision making. Despite their success, explicit CoT-based FRMs face two key limitations. As shown in Figure~ , explicit CoT often generates lengthy and redundant reasoning even for simple requests with clear intents. This increases inference latency and reduces throughput, while bringing only marginal recommendation gains. As shown in Figure~ , a fixed, single-path explicit-CoT strategy can overemphasize dominant historical preferences while overlooking short-term, context-dependent interests. If even a minor error or hallucination occurs during reasoning, it may propagate to the final output and result in a mismatched recommendation. To address these limitations, we propose , a latent-reasoning framework for FRMs. Rather than autoregressively emitting a rationale for every recommendation decision, inserts three special learnable latent tokens between the user context and the target SID, enabling a interface with constant reasoning-token overhead. Specifically, introduces MV-ACoT, which constructs complementary teacher supervision from multiple perspectives and prompts the teacher model to adapt its reasoning depth to the complexity of the observed behavioral signals. The latent token alignment procedure consists of three stages. It first learns contextual representations at the latent-token positions through rationale prediction and then connects the same latent interface to next-item prediction. Finally, curriculum-based post-training supports both direct and latent-reasoning modes for downstream recommendation. Because the acceleration provided by alone cannot meet the latency requirements of synchronous inference at full traffic scale, we further deploy a production serving architecture that prioritizes valuable asynchronous updates under limited GPU capacity while keeping foundation-model inference off the online request path. We conduct experiments on an industrial Kuaishou recommendation dataset and the public Kuaishou LLM-Rec benchmark. Our primary model is built on Qwen3.5-0.8B} and outperforms all compared methods across the reported metrics. With the rise of LLMs, early LLM-based approaches directly prompted LLMs to make recommendation decisions~. Although these methods leverage the world knowledge of LLMs, they remain limited in modeling collaborative signals. Recent studies have advanced recommendation systems toward foundation recommendation models along two complementary directions. The first develops recommendation-native backbones: TIGER represents items using semantic IDs~, HSTU formulates large-scale recommendation as sequence transduction~, and OneRec unifies retrieval and ranking in an end-to-end generative architecture~. The second adapts pretrained LLMs to recommendation data. LC-Rec aligns linguistic knowledge with collaborative semantics~, while the RecGPT family develops transferable LLM-based recommenders through recommendation-domain pre-training~. Despite their success, these models mainly focus on behavior modeling and item generation, providing limited supervision for inferring latent user needs and making recommendation decisions. Recent work has begun to equip recommendation models with reasoning capabilities. Latent reasoning methods reduce inference costs by avoiding lengthy natural-language rationales. ReaRec recursively refines user representations in the hidden space~, OnePiece introduces reasoning modules into industrial ranking models~, OneSearch-V2 distills LLM reasoning into compact internal states~, and REG4Rec explores multiple latent reasoning paths through self-reflection~. In parallel, explicit reasoning methods expose intermediate reasoning processes in natural language. GOT4Rec~, Reason4Rec~, and ThinkRec~ construct user preference analyses or multi-path reasoning trajectories through supervised fine-tuning. RecOne~, OneRec-Think~, and OpenOneRec~ further combine CoT supervision with reinforcement learning to improve recommendation decisions. OneReason~ reports that its thinking mode does not consistently outperform non-thinking inference. PauseRec~ further identifies weakened verbalization, text--SID representation mismatch, and rationale sensitivity as limitations of explicit reasoning training, and replaces rationales with implicit pause tokens. Although these methods advance reasoning-based recommendation, explicit reasoning is costly and sensitive to the interface between natural language and item IDs, whereas latent reasoning often lacks structured recommendation supervision. Following the production serving architecture of OneReason~, we deploy the FRM as a dedicated retrieval channel rather than replacing the existing retrieval pipeline. Its candidates are merged with those from the incumbent retrieval pipeline and compete in the same fine-ranking stage, allowing to complement the established recommendation system. We extend this architecture with behavior-triggered real-time inference. Streaming interactions undergo value-aware admission, with update priorities and refresh intervals determined by user value and behavior strength. The scheduler deduplicates requests, preserves the latest context, prioritizes high-value updates, and enforces a strict egress QPS cap. For each admitted update, an asynchronous gateway constructs $P_u;H_u;_u;$, invokes the disaggregated gRPC inference service, maps generated SIDs to ranked items, and caches the results in Redis. The online system retrieves these cached candidates and retains the previous result when an update is delayed or unavailable. Consequently, recent behavior can promptly influence ranking while model inference remains outside the latency-critical request path and GPU load remains bounded.
