We propose a list-wise ranking-uncertainty metric that captures the model's confidence in its predictions, which then guides our resource allocation strategy through a self-supervised learning mechanism. Traditionally, model uncertainty is measured via point-wise estimation methods, such as Bayesian Neural Networks~, MC Dropout~, and Deep Ensembles~, which calculate the variance of isolated prediction scores. However, dropout methods require multiple forward passes and ensemble approaches necessitate maintaining multiple models, both incurring inference latency and storage burdens prohibitive for real-time service. We therefore adopt a variational Bayesian layer~ as the prediction head, which maintains weights as $W $ and yields both a predictive mean $ ^{||}$ and prediction variance $^2 ^{||}$ for all items in a single forward pass. Nevertheless, point-wise estimation alone is insufficient, since sequential recommendation is fundamentally a ranking problem where the relative order of items matters more than absolute scores. We therefore lift uncertainty from individual scores to the : rather than measuring how uncertain any single score is, we measure how stable the entire ordering remains under the predictive distribution. Concretely, we treat each pairwise comparison within the Top-$K$ as a probabilistic event and define the overall ranking uncertainty as the aggregated negative log-likelihood of these events, so that ambiguous orderings contribute more uncertainty than well-separated ones. This construction reduces to deriving the pairwise outranking probability $p_{i j}$ under the model's own predictive distribution, which the variational Bayesian layer makes available in closed form. We adopt a diagonal parameterization of $ _W$ across output dimensions~, so that the predictive components for different items are conditionally independent given the input. The linear projection $z = Wx$ then produces jointly Gaussian logits with class-wise independent components: $z_i $, and the pairwise difference $z_i - z_j $. Two questions need to be addressed: first, why model uncertainty can serve as a signal of instance complexity; second, whether our list-wise construction preserves the probabilistic semantics that makes this signal meaningful. The use of predictive uncertainty as a signal of instance complexity is well-established in machine learning, with roots in curriculum learning~ and uncertainty quantification~. This usage rests on calibration~: when a model's confidence aligns with its empirical accuracy, higher predictive uncertainty signals harder instances. To ground $_{}$ on a calibrated source of uncertainty, we adopt the variational Bayesian last layer ~ as the prediction head. Trained with a variational evidence lower bound, VBLL achieves lower expected calibration error than deterministic baselines across regression and classification tasks~. Its predictive variance $ ^2$ quantifies epistemic uncertainty~: a larger value reflects insufficient evidence for a confident prediction and therefore serves as a proxy for instance complexity. The calibration argument above concerns the predictive distribution over a single outcome, while sequential recommendation operates on ordered lists. $_{}$ bridges this gap through two probabilistic constructions. The pairwise outranking probability $p_{i j}$ is the closed-form solution of the Bayesian Thurstone model~ under Gaussian distribution. Aggregating these probabilities through the negative log-likelihood induces a coherent measure of list-wise ranking confidence, approaching its minimum when all top-K pairs are confidently separated. The resulting $_{}$ is therefore a functional of the predictive distribution~, reflecting the structural stability of the predicted ordering as a list-wise complexity signal. We empirically verify this argument in Section~. Guided by self-supervised list-wise ranking uncertainty as a proactive resource signal, RACE performs multi-granularity slimming across parameter and data spaces via hypernetwork-style channel customization and importance-aware progressive filtering, with low overhead from intrinsic feature reuse. Extensive experiments show that RACE balances recommendation accuracy and inference efficiency across diverse resource-constrained scenarios. Beyond on-device compression, this label-free self-assessment generalizes to broader sequential recommendation tasks such as performance estimation and model refresh scheduling. In future work, we plan to extend this paradigm to generative recommenders, where ranking uncertainty could guide adaptive decoding depth and selective beam expansion. RACE does not require additional data collection beyond the interaction histories used by the underlying recommender and does not alter its recommendation objective.
