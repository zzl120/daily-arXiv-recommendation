 In this paper, we revisited long-sequence recommendation from the perspective of target-aware global interest modeling. We identified that existing methods struggled to simultaneously achieve target-aware modeling, global interest modeling, and real-world deployability. We hope SITA will inspire future research on scalable, target-aware, and deployable long-sequence recommendation. As long-sequence modeling enables the identification of user interests that are most relevant to a candidate item from long behavior sequences, it has become a fundamental component of modern recommender systems. However, as user histories continue to grow in both length and complexity, efficiently modeling candidate-relevant interests from long behavior sequences remains a fundamental challenge for large-scale recommendation . Compression-based methods have emerged as a promising paradigm for long-sequence modeling due to their favorable balance between recommendation effectiveness and inference efficiency, as exemplified by recent methods such as C-Former, VISTA, and DV365. Instead of performing candidate-specific interaction over the original behavior sequence , they first compress the entire user history into a compact set of reusable interest tokens, which can be shared across different candidate items during inference. Such a paradigm not only preserves the information contained in the complete behavior sequence, but also significantly reduces the computational cost of candidate-aware prediction, instead of relying on behavior retrieval or filtering . As a result, compression-based methods have become particularly attractive for large-scale recommender systems. Despite their effectiveness, existing compression-based methods face a fundamental trade-off between scalable interest storage and candidate-specific personalization . As illustrated in Figure~, this trade-off can be understood from three perspectives. Figure~ represents the ideal scenario, where candidate-specific user interests are explicitly maintained for every user--item pair, enabling strong personalization. However, since modern recommender systems typically involve hundreds of millions of users and billions of candidate items, such a design incurs prohibitive storage and maintenance costs. Figure~ illustrates the strategy adopted by existing methods. Instead of maintaining candidate-specific interests, they store a reusable set of user-level interest tokens that are shared across all candidate items. While this design achieves scalable storage, it forces all candidate items to reuse the same compressed interests, thereby substantially limiting personalization capability. Figure~ illustrates our target design, which preserves the scalability advantages of user-level storage while enabling different candidate items to select different subsets of reusable interest tokens. Achieving this goal requires the stored interest tokens to be semantically diverse and well structured, so that they can support flexible candidate-specific activation. Overall, achieving this goal poses two fundamental challenges for compression-based long-sequence modeling: How can candidate-specific interest modeling be achieved without sacrificing the scalability advantages of user-level storage? It then progressively compresses user behavior sequences into the interest tokens through a stack of Transformer-style compression blocks. During this process, intra-group refinement and inter-group communication are jointly performed to enhance the semantic specialization of each interest group while enabling information exchange across different groups. Finally, each candidate item selects a subset of reusable interest tokens according to its SID, producing the final candidate-specific interest representation. By combining structured reusable interest tokens with SID-guided selective activation, SITA enables candidate-specific interest modeling under the user-level storage constraint without sacrificing scalability. The selected codewords from all codebooks are concatenated and fed into an MLP decoder to reconstruct the original multimodal representation. The reconstruction loss is computed using the mean squared error between the reconstructed and original representations. Following the standard vector quantization formulation, the training objective also includes the codebook and commitment losses. Beyond these standard objectives, BPQ further introduces a usage-balance regularization to prevent codeword collapse. Long-sequence modeling aims to capture informative user interests from extensive historical interactions for accurate prediction over target items~. As user behavior histories continue to grow, numerous methods have been proposed to model increasingly long behavior sequences, gradually evolving into two representative paradigms: retrieval-based methods and compression-based methods~. first retrieve the historical interactions most relevant to the target item from the original behavior sequence and then perform user interest modeling over the retrieved subsequence. SIM~ first introduced this paradigm and achieved promising performance. ETA~ and MIRRN~ further improved retrieval efficiency through hash-based indexing and Hamming-distance. TWIN~ and DARE~ addressed the inconsistency issues in retrieval-based modeling. More recent works, including LIC~, MUSE~, and RAL-CDNet~, further enhanced retrieval-based modeling by incorporating temporal information, cross-domain knowledge, and multimodal information, respectively. These methods improve retrieval quality and efficiency from different perspectives while following the same retrieval-based paradigm. first compress the complete behavior sequence into a compact set of shared interest representations and then perform downstream interest modeling over the compressed representations. Early works, such as MIMN~ and HPMN~, employed memory networks for sequence compression, while ENCODE~ and TWIN V2~ adopted clustering-based approaches. More recent methods, including C-Former~ and VISTA~, perform target-aware interaction over compressed interests. Other approaches, such as Trinity~, CHIME~, and DMQN~, further improve compression quality through histogram-based representations and semantic codebooks. These methods mainly differ in how compressed interests are constructed while sharing the same compression-based paradigm. However, their shared compressed representations limit target-aware interest modeling. Our work revisits this paradigm to achieve target-aware global interest modeling under real-world deployment constraints. Item identifiers are the foundation of modern recommender systems and have recently evolved from random discrete IDs to semantic identifiers , which encode semantic similarity among items in compact discrete representations~. Existing SID methods are primarily built upon residual quantization, including RQ-KMeans~, which performs residual clustering, and RQ-VAE~, which learns semantic identifiers through end-to-end neural quantization with reconstruction and commitment objectives.
