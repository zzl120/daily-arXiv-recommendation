We study whole-page optimization, which selects and orders the items on a page jointly to maximize the score of the page. The number of possible pages grows combinatorially, so good pages must be generated rather than enumerated. Some generative re-ranking methods use the same page-level training signal for every choice in a sampled page. Others imitate target pages built before training, which remain largely fixed as the generator improves. We address both limitations with one local page-level objective, which repeatedly moves the current generator toward pages with higher score. This objective splits exactly into one training target per position, and each target compares candidates at the same partial page by the scores of the complete pages they lead to. Every position therefore learns to choose the item that is better for the whole page, rather than the item that looks best on its own. We call this procedure whole-page policy improvement. On two public datasets, KuaiRand-1K and RL4RS-SeqSlate, which cover multi-objective item selection and constrained ordering, and on an industrial dataset of logged pages, our method outperforms the evaluated generative re-ranking baselines. On a synthetic benchmark with a known optimal page, it also comes closest to the optimum, showing that its improvements come from better whole-page decisions. Web platforms such as content feeds, e-commerce sites and video services serve recommendations as pages of items that users browse together. The contribution of a recommendation item depends on the page around it. An early selection affects both the candidates that remain feasible and the context of later recommendations. A recommendation system must therefore select and order items jointly, while balancing engagement objectives and satisfying page constraints. Whole-page optimization seeks to maximize page score through this joint selection and ordering decision~. The space of feasible pages grows combinatorially, so pages cannot be enumerated, and evaluator search at serving time requires many queries for every request. A common approach is to train a generator against a page evaluator and use the learned generator for serving~. We take the evaluator as given and focus on generator training. Generative re-ranking methods either learn from scores of sampled pages, which give one page-level signal to every choice on the page, or fit target lists built by an evaluator, which remain largely fixed as the generator improves~. The training challenge is to compare next-item alternatives at the same partial page by the complete pages they lead to, and to keep these comparisons current as the generator changes. We propose , a training procedure that alternates search guided by the current generator with learning from complete-page scores. We turn those scores into per-position targets, so each choice is trained according to the whole page it leads to. The updated generator guides the next round, allowing both the search and the targets to adapt as training progresses. We derive this procedure from a local page-score objective and prove improvement for exact updates and convergence to the optimal page score for repeated exact full updates. At serving time, the trained generator produces pages without evaluator search. {figures/method_overview.pdf} $, which constructs the next round's data and targets.} Our method outperforms generative recommendation baselines and representative RL algorithms at every length setting of two public datasets and on an industrial dataset of logged pages.
