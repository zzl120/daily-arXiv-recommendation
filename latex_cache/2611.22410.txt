 In this work, we propose VerRec, a novel reinforcement learning framework with verbalized feedback for generative recommendation. VerRec augments scalar rewards with personalized feedback generated by an LLM-based user simulator. Experiments on three Amazon review datasets with two backbones demonstrate that VerRec improves recommendation performance. Further analyses highlight the complementary roles of personalized verbalized feedback and joint optimization in guiding exploration toward more diverse candidates with higher rewards. In future work, we will develop more personalized and accurate user simulators to improve feedback quality. Moreover, incorporating real-world users' verbalized feedback into this framework is a promising direction for guiding candidate refinement and aligning the policy with users’ evolving preferences. Finally, we will also investigate more efficient feedback generation to reduce RL training overhead. In this section, we present the experimental results and corresponding analysis to answer the following research questions . How does the recommendation performance of VerRec compare to other baselines? How do different feedback strategies compare in simulation quality and downstream recommendation performance? How does verbalized feedback affect candidate exploration during RL training? How do the key components of VerRec affect its overall performance? Following prior works~, we conduct experiments on three categories of the Amazon review dataset.}: Cell Phones and Accessories , Baby, and Book. Each dataset contains users' interaction histories and rich textual metadata associated with the items. We apply 5-core filtering to ensure that every user and item has at least five interactions. Each user's interactions are chronologically ordered and segmented using a sliding window with a maximum sequence length of 10. To simulate realistic recommendation scenarios, we adopt a temporal split and partition the resulting sequences into training, validation, and test sets at a ratio of 8:1:1, reserving the most recent interactions for validation and testing. For evaluation metrics, we adopt the full-item ranking setting, where each model is required to rank all candidate items rather than a sampled subset. We evaluate the effectiveness of top-$K$ recommendations using Hit Ratio and Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain , with $K \{5,10,20\}$. We compare VerRec with three groups of methods: traditional recommenders, LLM-based recommenders without reasoning, and reasoning-enhanced LLM-based recommenders. Generative recommendation has emerged as a paradigm for directly generating items from user interaction histories. Early work explores recommendation through text generation, using pretrained language models to process user and item information and generate recommendation outputs~. Subsequent work introduces compact semantic IDs to represent items and formulates next-item prediction as SID generation~. Recent work further improves generative recommendation through multi-task supervised fine-tuning that aligns language semantics with collaborative signals~. Beyond supervised learning, recent studies apply reinforcement learning to align generative recommendation with recommendation objectives, either by directly optimizing item generation~ or by jointly optimizing reasoning and item prediction~. These approaches use scalar rewards to evaluate recommendation outcomes and guide policy updates. Our work introduces personalized verbalized feedback to explicitly guide candidate exploration. Verbalized feedback provides actionable guidance beyond scalar rewards by identifying errors and suggesting improvements. Existing work has primarily explored such feedback in general-purpose tasks. One line of work uses such feedback at inference time, revising outputs through iterative self-critique or retaining verbal reflections to guide subsequent attempts without updating model parameters~. External tools can further ground this process by providing feedback for output verification and correction~. Another line incorporates such feedback into model training, either by optimizing critique generators to improve a fixed model's revisions~ or by constructing synthetic preference data for offline optimization while constraining changes in unrelated contexts~. Recent studies combine textual critiques with scalar rewards in online RL, learning from initial responses and their refinements to guide exploration and using successful refinements as off-policy guidance~. Our work investigates explicit guidance in RL for generative recommendation and develops a recommendation-specific framework for guidance acquisition and utilization. {Figures/overall.pdf} User simulators provide controllable environments for training and evaluating recommender systems without costly online interactions. Traditional platforms model user choices or responses through handcrafted or learned behavioral models~. Recent LLM-based simulators enrich this process with profiles, memory, and reasoning. One line of work directly initializes generative user agents with interaction histories and user profiles, relying on the in-context understanding ability of LLMs to simulate user preferences and behaviors~. Another line fine-tunes LLM-based simulators on user feedback or behavioral trajectories, enabling them to better understand user preferences and align simulated behaviors with real users~. Other studies evaluate whether LLMs can faithfully express preferences and provide consistent feedback in conversational recommendation~. Unlike prior simulators that mainly serve as interactive environments or evaluation tools, we employ a user simulator to synthesize personalized verbalized feedback from the user's historical preferences. {Figures/example.pdf} Generative recommendation reformulates personalized recommendation as autoregressive generation of the next item conditioned on a user's interaction history~. A common training pipeline combines supervised fine-tuning with subsequent reinforcement learning ~. Although SFT trains the recommendation policy by fitting to the interactions, its supervision is largely restricted to observed behavior, causing poor generalization and diversity~. RL then complements SFT by sampling items from the current policy and optimizing the policy with recommendation-oriented rewards~. Exploring interactions that are absent from the training data, RL extends supervision beyond observed behavior and thereby improves the generalization ability, making it an important step in GR training~. RL for GR follows an iterative process. Conditioned on a user's interaction history, the policy samples candidate items and receives scalar rewards based on recommendation objectives. It then updates its parameters to increase the likelihood of candidates with higher rewards. Prior work has applied this learning process to improve recommendation accuracy or user engagement through item generation~, and to optimize reasoning trajectories together with item predictions based on recommendation outcomes~. In essence, these methods rely on scalar reward to judge the policy’s behaviors and update the policy to favor those that can lead to higher rewards. However, the reliance on scalar rewards will lead to the }: a low reward can only indicate an unsuccessful recommendation but fail to reveal the underlying reason and optimization direction. As an example shown in Figure~, we consider a user whose interaction history contains fantasy and adventure books. Policy A recommends finance books that miss the user's interests, whereas Policy B recommends books the user has already read and offers insufficient diversity. Both recommendations may receive the same low reward, but require different corrections: the former should move toward the user's preferred genres, while the latter should avoid previously consumed books within those genres. The reward alone does not indicate which correction is needed, leaving the policy to discover better candidates through repeated trial and error. Based on the above insights, we aim to investigate } in RL, which can identify the cause of failure and direct exploration toward suitable candidates. However, incorporating explicit guidance into RL for GR presents two key challenges: The guidance should identify why a candidate matches or conflicts with a user's preferences and indicate how it can be improved. Simulated verbalized feedback offers a potential source of such guidance by expressing preference assessments and refinement suggestions in natural language~. The challenge is to obtain reliable, personalized feedback based on the user's historical preferences and the recommended candidate, so that it provides concrete directions for refinement. The obtained feedback should inform policy updates so that the policy can internalize the suggested improvements. However, verbalized feedback is discrete and cannot directly guide policy updates through backpropagation. The challenge is to incorporate the information in textual guidance into rewards, enabling the policy to learn from useful suggestions and improve subsequent recommendations. Building on these insights, we propose VerRec, a reinforcement learning framework with verbalized feedback for generative recommendation. At its core, VerRec consists of two tightly coupled components. 1) : For each original candidate generated by the policy, an LLM-based user simulator generates personalized VF based on the user's historical preferences and the candidate. This VF explains how the candidate matches or conflicts with the user's preferences. 2) : For each original candidate, the policy combines the candidate, its corresponding VF, and the user's history to construct a refinement prompt and generate a refined candidate. The original and refined candidates are then combined into an expanded group and jointly optimized with GRPO using scalar rewards. This process allows VF to guide exploration while the reward function penalizes unsuccessful original candidates. To evaluate the effectiveness of VerRec, we conduct comprehensive experiments on three real-world datasets. The results demonstrate that VerRec effectively uses verbalized feedback to guide exploration and improve recommendation performance. Further analyses show that verbalized guidance encourages exploration of more diverse candidates and enables the policy to attain higher rewards as training progresses. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows: We formulate personalized verbalized feedback as an explicit exploration signal that guides the policy in reinforcement learning for generative recommendation. We propose VerRec, a novel RL framework that integrates personalized feedback simulation, feedback-conditioned candidate refinement, and joint GRPO optimization over original and refined candidates. We evaluate VerRec on three Amazon review datasets with two generative recommendation backbones.
