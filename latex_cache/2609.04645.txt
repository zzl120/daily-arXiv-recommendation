Multimodal recommendations have emerged as an indispensable component of modern recommender systems, enabling personalized item suggestions by capturing users' preferences over rich visual and textual content. Existing approaches typically extract multimodal information into dense embeddings or sparse IDs via lookup tables , relying on modality-specific encoders that process each signal in isolation. Many efforts have been devoted to enhancing multimodal representations for recommendation by leveraging the cross-modal understanding capabilities of Vision-Language Models , which have demonstrated remarkable proficiency in tasks such as image captioning and visual question answering , suggesting a promising paradigm for jointly modeling visual and textual features within a unified architecture. In parallel, Chain-of-Thought reasoning has become a cornerstone technique for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models, enabling them to decompose complex tasks into interpretable intermediate steps. Recent studies have extended CoT from LLMs to VLMs. However, in contrast to LLMs where longer reasoning chains yield consistent gains, VLMs frequently struggle with long-context reasoning due to the progressive attenuation of both visual and textual signals as the generated sequence lengthens --- a phenomenon we term . As recently demonstrated by and in the context of visual representations, attention to visual tokens decays substantially as the reasoning chain grows; we observe the same effect extends to textual tokens and is particularly acute in recommendation settings, where VLMs must ultimately produce dense embeddings for downstream models, leaving no opportunity for the auto-regressive process to refresh the fading multimodal signals . Recent work on latent reasoning offers a compelling alternative: instead of decoding intermediate thoughts into discrete tokens, the model reasons in continuous hidden space, bypassing the lossy argmax bottleneck. However, existing latent reasoning methods target text-generation tasks and have not been explored for recommendation, where the output is a dense embedding rather than a token sequence, and where visual and textual signals --- not vision alone --- must be preserved. {figures/overview.pdf} . interleaves latent tokens with multi-step CoT reasoning; latent groups are aligned patch-wise with a frozen vision encoder~ , while CoT text is supervised by $_{}$~ . projects $^{ }$ via a Bridge MLP into the same backbone for contrastive training~ ; intermediate features are anchored to the stop-gradient CoT hidden states $^{ }_{}$~ . } Building on these advances, we propose } , which reformulates multimodal recommendation as multi-stage latent reasoning within a single VLM. operates as a two-stage architecture with complementary alignment mechanisms designed to counteract cross-modal dilution at each stage.
