However, traditional NLP-based systems often struggle with maintaining contextual accuracy and generating relevant responses in dynamic financial environments. To address these challenges, Retrieval-Augmented Generation models have emerged as a powerful solution, combining retrieval-based methods with generative capabilities to enhance response quality. In this study, we present a Retrieval-Augmented Generation system designed to generate responses related to stock market information. The system integrates retrieval-based and generative approaches to enhance the accuracy and relevance of financial text generation. To evaluate its effectiveness, we employ the ROUGE metric, specifically ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L, to measure lexical and structural similarity between generated and reference texts. Experimental results on a dataset of 100 stock market-related queries demonstrate high ROUGE scores, indicating strong alignment with reference documents. Additionally, we assess latency to evaluate the system’s responsiveness, achieving an average response time of 1.16 seconds. These findings confirm that our RAG model is capable of providing accurate and timely stock market insights, making it a promising approach for financial information retrieval. Their ability to understand and generate human-like text has led to significant advancements in tasks such as machine translation, sentiment analysis, question answering, and text summarization. As a result, LLMs have been widely adopted across multiple domains, including education , healthcare , finance , code writing , cybersecurity , the labor market , and legal aspects , where they enhance automation, improve decision-making, and enable more natural human-computer interactions. One of the most promising approaches in this domain is Retrieval-Augmented Generation , which combines the strengths of retrieval-based and generative models to enhance the accuracy and reliability of generated content. Despite the impressive capabilities of existing LLMs, their development is still constrained by several challenges. One of the most significant issues is the hallucination problem , where models generate responses that are fluent and coherent but factually inaccurate. Another major limitation is the difficulty in updating their knowledge. Since LLMs store information within their internal memory , keeping them up to date requires retraining or fine-tuning with new data—an expensive and resource-intensive process. Additionally, general-purpose LLMs often lack expertise in specialized domains , making them less effective for domain-specific tasks. Developing LLMs tailored to a particular field requires extensive effort in dataset collection and model training, further increasing the complexity of implementation. This makes the deployment of systems for specific tasks costly in terms of both time and resources. Furthermore, collecting and processing training data for each specialized domain can present challenges such as a lack of high-quality data, security requirements, and the complexity of fine-tuning the model to fit the specific context. Their research highlighted the importance of cross-company event correlation in financial forecasting, demonstrating how news about one company could impact the stock prices of others within the same industry. Similarly, Sidra and Jaydip explored multiple machine learning and deep learning techniques for weekly stock market predictions, integrating sentiment analysis from social media with historical stock prices. Their findings showed that public sentiment plays a crucial role in improving stock movement predictions. Another notable study proposed an LSTM-based forecasting model that incorporated sentiment analysis to establish correlations between stock values and news sentiment. The results demonstrated that using NLP for feature extraction and LSTM for time-series training significantly improved prediction accuracy, reducing the mean square error to 0.062 . Additionally, "Smart Trader," an AI-powered chatbot designed for the Saudi Tadawul market, showcased the practical applications of NLP and machine learning in stock trading. By leveraging real-time market data and user preferences, the chatbot provided personalized investment recommendations, filling a gap in the market for AI-driven financial advisory services . Retrieval-Augmented Generation is a hybrid approach that enhances language models by integrating external knowledge retrieval mechanisms. Instead of relying solely on parametric memory, RAG retrieves relevant documents or facts from external sources and conditions its responses based on retrieved content. This method is particularly useful for knowledge-intensive NLP tasks such as open-domain question answering and fact-based text generation . By dynamically incorporating relevant information at inference time, RAG models can mitigate issues like hallucination and outdated knowledge, which are common challenges in traditional language models. The architecture of RAG consists of two main components: a retriever module and a generator module. The retriever module searches for relevant external knowledge, while the generator module synthesizes a response based on the retrieved content. The retriever module usually comprises three components: an encoder for encoding inputs into embeddings, an efficient indexing system that supports approximate nearest neighbor search, and a datastore for storing external knowledge in the form of key-value pairs . Beside, Retrieval-augmented generators and default generators are the two primary types of generator modules. Default generators encompass most pre-trained or fine-tuned large language models, including Mistral models , Gemini-series models , and GPT-series models . In contrast, RA generators integrate retrieval mechanisms to enhance response generation, incorporating modules like Enc-Dec and RETRO . These generators refine their outputs by leveraging retrieved knowledge, allowing them to generate more contextually informed responses or predictions. Recent studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of RAG in improving factual accuracy and reducing misinformation in AI-generated text. For example, RAG models have been used to enhance chatbot responses by retrieving real-time information from external databases . Additionally, advances in retrieval mechanisms, such as dense passage retrieval and contrastive learning, have further improved the quality of retrieved content, leading to more coherent and contextually relevant responses. Retrieval-Augmented Generation has been increasingly applied in financial domains to enhance decision-making by integrating real-time knowledge retrieval with language model capabilities. In market sentiment analysis, fine-tuned models like FinBERT within a RAG pipeline have been used to analyze financial news and predict stock price movements, though sentiment alone shows limited predictive power . Similarly, RAG improves financial analysis by addressing challenges such as lack of interpretability in ML models and hallucinations in LLMs. The AlphaFin dataset and Stock-Chain framework have demonstrated the effectiveness of integrating retrieval mechanisms for financial reasoning tasks . Beyond sentiment analysis, RAG plays a crucial role in multi-modal financial analytics. The FinTMMBench benchmark evaluates temporal-aware RAG systems that process diverse financial data sources, such as stock prices, news, and technical charts, enabling more comprehensive trend analysis and event detection . Additionally, in financial risk assessment, RAG enhances LLM performance in audit report analysis by improving faithfulness and context relevance. Experiments show that models like LLaMa3.1 and Gemini-1.5-flash significantly improve financial risk evaluations by extracting detailed insights from reports . These applications highlight the potential of RAG in financial markets, bridging the gap between real-time information retrieval and AI-generated financial insights. Stop words are commonly used words in a language that carry minimal semantic meaning, such as conjunctions, prepositions, and auxiliary verbs. Removing stop words is a crucial step in text preprocessing as it helps reduce dimensionality, improve computational efficiency, and enhance the relevance of text-based features for downstream natural language processing tasks. Fig illustrates the frequency of removed stop words in the documents. In this study, we implemented a stop word removal process specifically for Vietnamese text. Given the unique characteristics of the Vietnamese language, where words are often multi-syllabic and require tokenization before processing, we utilized the Underthesea library for word segmentation. The preprocessing pipeline consists of several key steps. First, the input text was normalized by stripping leading and trailing whitespaces. Additionally, all URLs and special characters were removed using regular expressions to eliminate noise in the data. To ensure uniformity, all text was converted to lowercase, which helps in minimizing variations due to capitalization. Next, since Vietnamese is not naturally space-separated like English, we applied word\_tokenize from the Underthesea library to segment text into meaningful tokens. Following tokenization, we curated a list of common Vietnamese stop words and removed them from the tokenized text. This step reduces redundancy and ensures that only semantically meaningful words are retained for further analysis. After performing these preprocessing steps, the cleaned text was stored for subsequent stages of the information retrieval and retrieval-augmented generation pipeline. The removal of stop words significantly enhances the quality of text embeddings by focusing on content-bearing words while discarding non-informative tokens. This refinement is particularly beneficial for improving the performance of semantic similarity searches and document retrieval tasks. This section presents the architecture of the proposed Retrieval-Augmented Generation system, which enhances large language model responses by integrating an external knowledge retrieval mechanism. The system consists of three main components: text embedding, information retrieval, and response generation. As mentioned above, the documents consist of stock market news articles crawled from VnExpress, one of Vietnam’s most popular online newspapers. VnExpress provides up-to-date financial news, market analysis, and expert opinions, making it a valuable data source for financial insights. {embedding.jpg} Specifically, we employ the LaBSE model from the SentenceTransformers library. This model is chosen for its multilingual capabilities, allowing it to effectively encode text in various languages. Notably, LaBSE provides strong support for the Vietnamese language, ensuring high-quality semantic representations of Vietnamese financial news. Documents are first preprocessed and tokenized using Jieba , a widely used text segmentation tool primarily designed for Chinese. Despite its origin, jieba has been adapted for Vietnamese and is used here to segment sentences into meaningful tokens before embedding. Proper tokenization is crucial for accurately representing text semantics. The embeddings are then computed and stored in a FAISS database, which enables efficient nearest-neighbor retrieval. The generated embeddings form a high-dimensional vector space that facilitates rapid similarity comparisons. Once all textual data has been transformed into vector representations, they are systematically stored in the FAISS DB store for efficient retrieval during the next processing stages. Given a user query, the retrieval process aims to fetch the most relevant documents from the FAISS database. The retrieval mechanism follows a hybrid approach that integrates FAISS-based vector similarity search with BM25 ranking. This combination leverages both semantic similarity and term frequency-based relevance to enhance the accuracy of retrieved documents. To perform vector-based retrieval, the input query is first embedded using the LaBSE model, ensuring a dense numerical representation in the same vector space as the stored documents. This query embedding is then compared against the stored document embeddings using FAISS. FAISS efficiently retrieves the top-k nearest neighbors by measuring the L2 distance between the query and stored vectors. After retrieving an initial set of candidate documents from FAISS, a BM25 ranking step is applied to further refine the results. BM25 is a TF-IDF-based retrieval model that scores documents based on their word frequency, document length normalization, and inverse document frequency . This step ensures that retrieved documents are not only semantically similar but also contextually relevant based on keyword occurrences. The BM25 ranking model is particularly effective in handling keyword-based relevance, especially for queries where specific terms are important. By applying BM25 after FAISS retrieval, we re-rank the initially retrieved documents to prioritize those that best match the user’s intent. This hybrid approach ensures that the final set of documents includes both semantically related and keyword-relevant results, optimizing the retrieval process for accuracy and contextual relevance. The final stage of the system focuses on generating a response based on the retrieved documents. We utilize the Gemini model, specifically Gemini-1.5-Flash , due to its optimized performance for real-time applications, lower latency, and ability to process long-context inputs efficiently. This choice ensures that the model can effectively integrate retrieved knowledge while maintaining responsiveness. To generate a well-informed response, a structured prompt is carefully crafted by incorporating the retrieved documents as contextual information. The prompt is formatted to include a clear instruction, the user’s query, and the most relevant retrieved texts, ensuring that the model leverages external knowledge effectively. Once the structured prompt is prepared, Gemini-1.5-Flash processes it and generates a response that aligns factually with the retrieved information. The model does this by grounding its output in the provided texts, minimizing hallucinations, and maintaining consistency with real-world stocket market informations. By leveraging this retrieval-augmented approach, the system significantly enhances the quality of responses, ensuring that they are not only coherent and contextually aware but also well-supported by factual knowledge. To assess the performance of our Retrieval-Augmented Generation system in generating responses related to stock market information, we employ ROUGE Score as the primary evaluation metric. ROUGE is a widely used metric for evaluating text summarization and generation tasks by comparing the overlap of n-grams, word sequences, and longest common subsequences between the generated text and reference answers.
