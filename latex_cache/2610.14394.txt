This scale and constant evolution bring major stability challenges. While standard fixes like gradient clipping , adaptive optimizers, learning rate schedules , and normalization layers are widely used, they often prove insufficient. Their effectiveness can be hit-or-miss, especially when faced with the and pressures from common in continuous training on these platforms. These factors do not just make existing tools less reliable; they can actively trigger a domino effect of instability. For instance, simple gradient clipping might stop gradients from exploding but could also unintentionally flatten important learning signals, slowing down the model's ability to learn or adapt to new data. The sophisticated deep learning models used today, often with attention mechanisms , combined with ever-changing input data, need more than generic fixes; they need an approach that understands the sequence of failure. Much of the existing research offers solutions to training instability as isolated fixes. However, there is a missing piece: a deep dive into the common triggers and the specific chain reaction---from radients to eights to oss ---that leads to model divergence in these demanding, large-scale recommender systems. We often see instability starting with issues like data shifts or scaling efforts, which then set off this G-W-L cascade. Furthermore, the field lacks a clear framework that compares different solutions by mapping them to the specific stage of this cascade they address. This paper aims to fill these gaps. Our key contributions are: We conduct an empirical analysis of training dynamics in large-scale SFV recommender models. We identify common triggers and detail the sequential links within the instability cascade, which we formalize as the G-W-L cascade. Based on our analysis of the G-W-L cascade, we propose a clear way to categorize stability techniques. This framework maps solutions to the specific stage of the cascade they target: controlling gradients, stabilizing weights, or shaping the loss function. We present results from a large-scale empirical study on an industrial SFV recommender model. This study compares a variety of optimization and architectural changes, demonstrating a robust training approach that greatly improves both stability and the model's final performance by strategically addressing different parts of the G-W-L cascade. Such resilience is critical for deploying and maintaining robust, large-scale models in dynamic industrial environments, effectively mitigating a common and costly failure mode. This paper investigated training instability in large-scale industrial recommender systems through the lens of a Gradient $ $ Weight $ $ Loss explosion cascade. Our contributions are threefold: characterizing this cascade, proposing a structured mitigation framework targeting its components, and providing an extensive comparative evaluation of these strategies on a real-world Short-Form Video recommender model. Our experiments demonstrate that interventions specifically designed to control gradient magnitudes , internal model values/weights , and loss characteristics significantly influence training dynamics and model performance.
