Its effectiveness depends on identifying relevant documents, covering essential information, and generating coherent responses with reliable citations~. These requirements become particularly challenging for complex narrative queries involving multiple information needs. Effective retrieval alone cannot guarantee that relevant evidence is preserved, accurately synthesized, and appropriately attributed during answer generation. The TREC 2026 RAG track provides a large-scale setting for investigating these challenges using ClimbMix-400B, a corpus containing approximately 553 million documents~. The track evaluates evidence retrieval and end-to-end answer generation, emphasizing factual coverage and citation support~. Existing approaches improve individual stages through query expansion, rank fusion, language-model reranking, and relevance assessment~. Integrating these techniques into an effective RAG pipeline requires careful coordination between evidence selection and answer construction. To address these challenges, we develop two fully automatic systems for the TREC 2026 RAG track. The retrieval system, , combines multi-query BM25, reciprocal rank fusion, ensemble relevance assessment, listwise reranking, and adaptive retrieval depth. The generation system, , performs evidence-linked claim extraction, citation-grounded answer construction, and source-support verification. Figure~ illustrates the proposed architecture. We assess both pipelines through component-level experiments and official TREC evaluations to examine the relationship between evidence selection and answer quality. The Ragnar\"ok framework introduced reusable baselines and standardized interfaces for evaluating RAG systems~. BERGEN demonstrated the value of controlled comparisons across retrievers, rerankers, and language models~. The TREC 2025 RAG track expanded evaluation through retrieval, generation, and relevance-assessment tasks~. Prior research establishes a methodological foundation for evaluating retrieval quality and examining how language models utilize retrieved evidence~. RAGFlip examines query-level performance regressions in retriever upgrades, highlighting reliability considerations that aggregate retrieval metrics may obscure~. Advances in evidence acquisition have incorporated language-model-assisted query reformulation and relevance assessment. Query\-2doc demonstrated that generated pseudo-documents can improve lexical retrieval without additional retriever training~. Large-scale studies of LLM-based relevance judgments have reported strong correlations with human-derived system rankings~. Such approaches support diversified lexical retrieval and automated relevance assessment. Their application also raises questions about judgment consistency, relevance thresholds, and the selection of evidence across multiple information needs. Answer evaluation extends beyond document relevance to factual completeness and source attribution. Nugget-based methods assess the coverage of essential facts, while citation-support evaluations measure whether attributed documents substantiate generated statements~. VeriScore evaluates the factual accuracy of verifiable claims across long-form generation tasks~. ARES and RAGChecker introduce fine-grained evaluation methods for assessing component quality and identifying failures across retrieval and generation stages~. Building on prior research on deterministic evidence grounding and multi-pass alignment~, our work investigates how evidence is retained during document selection, claim extraction, sentence assembly, and citation filtering. The analysis focuses on the relationship between retrieved evidence, factual coverage, and citation support in long-form answer generation. The 2026 guidelines reward three outcomes: pairwise answer quality, nugget coverage, and citation support~. The official evaluation supports the effectiveness of diversified lexical retrieval and LLM-based relevance assessment. The retrieval system attained a set precision of 0.455, a set recall of 0.118, a manual nugget gain of 0.910, and an automatic nugget gain of 0.687, with 57.5 documents submitted. The performance highlights the value of multi-query BM25, rank fusion, and ensemble relevance judgments for large-scale evidence discovery. The approach also eliminates the need for a custom dense index over ClimbMix, reducing indexing requirements and supporting deployment in resource-constrained environments. The generation pipeline encountered challenges in converting retrieved evidence into coherent and comprehensive answers. Claim extraction emphasized factual atomicity and source attribution, while greedy sentence selection prioritized lexical novelty. The absence of explicit discourse planning limited information organization across narrative facets. Citation filtering introduced an additional tradeoff. Removing unsupported references increased locally assessed citation precision but left 24.3\ The evaluation indicates that retrieval effectiveness and generation quality require distinct optimization strategies. Organizing extracted claims by narrative facet could improve answer coherence and factual coverage. Citation verification could incorporate sentence revision to preserve source attribution. The limited gains from listwise reranking also suggest opportunities to allocate computational resources toward structured answer planning and evidence verification. Development experiments used 22 topics for both parameter selection and evaluation, potentially introducing optimistic estimates. Model-generated relevance judgments may introduce evaluator bias. BM25 retrieval may overlook relevant documents with limited lexical overlap. Generation evaluation covered selected runs across two assessment batches. Citation verification also relied on a single LLM judge, making support assessments sensitive to model calibration. We presented two fully automatic systems for the TREC 2026 RAG track, integrating large-scale lexical retrieval with claim-first answer generation. The retrieval pipeline combined multi-query BM25, rank fusion, ensemble relevance assessment, and adaptive depth selection. Official retrieval results included a set precision of 0.455 and a manual nugget gain of 0.910, with 57.5 documents submitted. The generation evaluation identified opportunities to improve evidence preservation, answer coherence, and citation support. Future work will focus on facet-aware answer planning and support-aware claim revision. Implementation details, experimental configurations, and additional analyses are provided in Appendix~.
