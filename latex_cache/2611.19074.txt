 Industrial recommendation faces a resolution--complexity dilemma: dense interactions make detailed modeling of long behavior histories expensive, while compact history summaries restrict the evidence available to prediction. A unified backbone must preserve rich behavioral details within strict serving budgets. We introduce , a unified architecture that explicitly decouples from . Its group-wise token layout jointly encodes behavior segments with non-sequential context, expands interaction ranges via hierarchical group merging, and coordinates distributed groups through and modules. Through this paradigm, computation is concentrated on structured interactions while preserving fine-grained evidence, ensuring that every encoded event remains directly accessible to the module. Research on these inputs has historically developed along two complementary branches: sequence modeling extracts user interests from past actions~, while feature-interaction models learn crosses among non-sequential fields~. Conventional systems often encode the history first and then feed its summary into a feature-interaction module. Recent unified recommendation Transformers bring these two branches into a shared backbone, allowing sequential and non-sequential representations to interact seamlessly throughout the model~. Existing unified models explore various ways to organize this interaction. OneTrans places behavior and non-sequential tokens in a shared causal stream~. HyFormer and MixFormer alternate history aggregation through feature-derived queries with query or feature mixing~. EST and Kunlun combine feature interaction with sparse behavior propagation and hierarchical sequence summarization, respectively~. Our earlier ~ unifies field, behavior, and target tokens, using Bottom-Full-Top-Sliding attention and nonlinear multiplicative interactions to improve representation learning. Together, these methods have significantly advanced the design of interaction operators and the information flow between sequential and non-sequential inputs. Long histories, however, make it difficult to give every behavior rich contextual modeling within a practical compute budget. For example, several nearby actions may together indicate an intent that is unclear from any single action. Dense self-attention can model these relationships, but incurs a quadratic cost in sequence length~; TokenFormer's shallow full-attention layers retain this cost even though its upper layers use narrower windows. To reduce computation, OneTrans progressively shrinks the set of behavior queries, leaving fewer event representations for deeper layers. HyFormer and MixFormer concentrate joint field--sequence interaction in compact query states, so subsequent feature interactions operate on aggregated history representations. These strategies are efficient, but leave a vital design goal unmet: Meeting this goal requires deciding precisely where detailed interactions are needed and how information travels between them. Building on TokenFormer's unified modeling, we introduce . Instead of compressing history into a compact summary, our approach is built on a direct premise: we preserve an individual token for every retained behavior throughout encoding. Each behavior interacts locally with neighboring events and field context within its group, while a small set of communication tokens bridges the groups. This ensures that the final prediction can still attend directly to every encoded event—a property we term . Our serves as the organizing principle that coordinates these local groups and their communication paths. Specifically, we partition the behavior sequence into contiguous segments and assign each segment a subset of non-sequential tokens and learned . The updates the tokens within each group, so each behavior representation incorporates neighboring events and field context. As depth increases, combines adjacent groups. For instance, merging two groups of four behavior tokens creates a group with eight behavior tokens, each enjoying a wider interaction scope. Merging therefore broadens direct interaction without ever pooling or discarding individual behaviors. Meanwhile, two compact communication paths connect these local computations at every layer. mixes field representations updated by different local histories and redistributes the resulting context to the groups. exchanges information among the group relay tokens and a small set of learned . The relay tokens carry this shared information back into local encoding, while the readout tokens accumulate information for prediction. Finally, uses the readout tokens as queries for candidate-aware cross-attention over the complete encoded token set. The model thus preserves individual behavior tokens for prediction while strictly controlling attention cost through group sizes and compact communication paths.
