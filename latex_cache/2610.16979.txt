As illustrated in Figure~, modern industrial search systems typically organize candidate generation, pre-ranking, and ranking as a multi-stage cascade. Such systems maintain multiple retrieval channels and pass their merged candidate set through downstream ranking stages, increasing both serving complexity and computational cost. Generative retrieval offers an alternative paradigm by representing each item as a sequence of discrete tokens, commonly referred to as a Semantic ID , and directly generating candidate SIDs conditioned on the query and user context~. By replacing independent candidate scoring with autoregressive generation, GR provides a unified interface for modeling relevance, semantics, and user preference. Realizing this potential in large-scale industrial search system, however, depends on learning behaviorally informative SIDs and aligning the policy model with observed user feedback in a robust and transferable manner. {pics/we_gr_overview.pdf} The first challenge lies in constructing SIDs that preserve collaborative semantics while maintaining effective codebook utilization. Existing quantization methods mainly follow two paradigms. RQ-VAE-based methods support end-to-end learning and allow task-specific supervision to directly shape SID construction, but discrete assignment and successive residual decomposition can produce imbalanced codeword usage and codebook collapse~. RQ-Kmeans-based methods provide more explicit control over cluster assignments and can incorporate balancing strategies or alternative quantizers~; however, collaborative and business-specific signals are usually encoded only in upstream item representations and may be attenuated or distorted during multi-level residual quantization. Consequently, existing paradigms offer complementary strengths but do not fully reconcile collaborative-information preservation with stable codebook utilization, limiting the representational capacity of the resulting SID space. The second challenge lies in aligning the generative policy with user preferences. Supervised fine-tuning maximizes the likelihood of observed positive SIDs but does not explicitly distinguish the relative value of generated candidates. On-policy reinforcement learning facilitates preference optimization by leveraging reward signals from user interactions~. Group Relative Policy Optimization is a widely used optimizer that learns from the relative advantages of multiple sampled trajectories. However, when using outcome-based rewards from clicks and orders, GRPO requires generated SIDs to exactly match positive items, resulting in severe reward sparsity and insufficient learning signals~. To alleviate reward sparsity, existing approaches either expand rollout budgets via guided decoding ~ or introduce auxiliary rankers for denser rewards~. Larger rollout budgets incur substantial computation, while beam search introduces a mismatch between selected trajectories and the policy's sampling distribution. On the other hand, auxiliary rankers—trained strictly on candidates from upstream retrieval stages—provide unreliable scores for medium- and long-tail items explored by the generative policy. Furthermore, practical ranking scores often rely on hand-crafted heuristics tailored to specific scenarios, limiting cross-scenario generalizability and requiring costly adaptation. Thus, denser rewards do not necessarily yield better preference alignment and may instead inject misleading supervision. To address these challenges, we present , a unified generative retrieval framework for industrial search systems. WeGR connects behavior-aware SID construction with reward-model-free preference optimization through two components. First, the incorporates query-to-item and item-to-item collaborative supervision into continuous representation learning. It further combines first-layer codebook refresh with residual-fidelity and collision regularization, mitigating persistent codeword underutilization while preserving semantic structure across quantization layers. Second, leverages the hierarchical structure of SIDs for coarse-to-fine GRPO optimization. By progressively expanding the horizons for both reward evaluation and policy optimization, DHPO allows sampled trajectories to receive behavioral rewards through prefix matching during early training, before gradually transitioning to exact item-level preference optimization. This enables more effective use of click- and order-based outcome rewards without auxiliary reward models. We further introduce Multi-Positive Supervised Fine-Tuning to learn from multiple positive items and provide stable learning signals when group-relative rewards become uninformative. Generative retrieval formulates retrieval as autoregressive generation, directly predicting target identifiers conditioned on queries or user histories~. For item retrieval, each item is represented as a Semantic ID , a sequence of discrete tokens encoding its semantic information. Such SIDs are constructed through two paradigms: RQ-VAE-based learnable quantization~ and RQ-Kmeans-based residual clustering~. ~ construct SIDs by successively clustering item embeddings and residuals, with explicit assignment control. OneRec~ uses balanced clustering, while OneSearch~ combines selective cluster balancing with optimized product quantization of the remaining residual~. These designs separate quantization from behavior-aware representation learning. In OneSearch, collaborative signals enter upstream contrastive embeddings, but subsequent clustering does not directly optimize behavioral objectives. Preserving these signals in SIDs thus relies on quantization fidelity, not direct behavioral supervision of code assignment. learn representations and codebooks via reconstruction and quantization, allowing task supervision. LC-Rec~ combines learned indices with collaborative-semantic alignment tasks, while LETTER~ directly regularizes the tokenizer via collaborative contrastive alignment and code-assignment diversity. ETEGRec~ couples tokenization and recommendation via sequence--item and preference--semantic alignment. These approaches differ in whether alignment targets the recommender, the tokenizer, or their joint training, rather than merely in the information used. Yet task alignment cannot ensure effective codebook utilization: hard assignments can undertrain rare codewords, causing codebook collapse~~. DIGER~ enables recommendation-guided differentiable assignment with Gumbel-noise exploration to reduce code concentration; R3-VAE~ stabilizes quantization via reference-vector guidance and scoring-based assignment. CoCo-VAE jointly addresses behavioral alignment and codebook underutilization within the RQ-VAE framework. It combines query-to-item and item-to-item supervision in continuous representations with codebook maintenance and regularization to preserve collaborative semantics, quantization fidelity, and codebook utilization. Preference alignment in GR aims to capture user preferences and downstream utility, such as relevance, engagement, and conversion, beyond generation likelihood. Existing approaches achieve this through either inference-time guidance or training-time optimization. Given predicted semantic-code values, GPR's Value-Guided Trie-based Beam Search~ allocates beam widths under trie constraints without updating the generative policy. Training-based approaches~ incorporate utility into supervised or preference objectives. Exponential reward-weighted SFT~ reweights logged interactions by observed rewards, while VARG~ combines value-aware supervision with local ordinal learning over value-ordered codes. DPO~ learns relative candidate preferences; RankGR~ extends comparisons to lists, and RAD-DPO~ addresses shared-prefix conflicts and noisy implicit feedback. Reinforcement learning aligns generation with retrieval utility through relevance-feedback training~, PPO~, or GRPO~. However, exact-match behavioral rewards require the complete SID of a clicked or ordered item, leaving partially correct prefixes unrewarded~. Limited rollouts can yield all-zero reward groups and no reward-driven policy gradient. With advantages centered within each group, increasing sample count alone cannot recover a learning signal if all sampled rewards remain identical. Existing methods alleviate reward sparsity by modifying sampling or reward design~. Larger rollout budgets increase computational costs, while HCGRec~ uses target-prefix hints to recover rewarding trajectories but introduces additional training complexity and a potential training-inference mismatch. VARG~ provides denser feedback through relevance and ranker-derived rewards, yet its reliance on auxiliary scoring systems may limit cross-scenario generalizability. Hierarchy-aware methods alleviate reward sparsity through finer-grained credit assignment. OneSearch-V2~ combines behavioral rewards with estimated relevance and CTR, using position-wise advantages and prefix-dependent gating. HRPO~ estimates prefix utilities from user-group statistics to derive token-level rewards. However, both methods rely on additional utility estimation or reward design, potentially introducing noisy supervision and limiting generalizability across search scenarios. DHPO alleviates reward sparsity through curriculum learning by progressively expanding reward evaluation and policy optimization horizons from prefix-level to item-level matching. This enables effective behavioral supervision while ensuring accurate credit assignment, without relying on auxiliary reward models. In this section, we present WeGR, a unified generative retrieval framework for e-commerce search. WeGR consists of two complementary components. First, CoCo-VAE incorporates query-to-item and item-to-item collaborative signals into SID construction while mitigating codebook collapse through complementary regularization. Building on these SIDs, DHPO progressively expands reward evaluation and policy optimization horizons, enabling coarse-to-fine preference learning under sparse outcome rewards without relying on auxiliary reward models. The overall framework is illustrated in Figure~. {pics/WeGR.png} To preserve collaborative information while maintaining effective codebook utilization, we propose CoCo-VAE, a collaborative-aware residual quantization framework. Specifically, CoCo-VAE incorporates query-to-item and item-to-item collaborative signals to guide representation learning, while combining Codebook Refresh with Fidelity Anti-collapse Regularization to mitigate codebook collapse during hierarchical quantization. We introduce Q2I and I2I collaborative signals to address the lack of co-occurrence information in multimodal embeddings~. Specifically, an auxiliary contrastive loss is employed to capture collaborative relationships and guide SID construction. Unlike existing methods that impose constraints directly within the discrete codebook space, which can cause codebook collapse by forcing semantically dissimilar embeddings to share the same SID, we apply such constraints exclusively to the continuous representations of the encoder with a detached projection head. This design decouples collaborative representation learning from codebook optimization, allowing behavioral signals to enrich item representations without disrupting codebook assignments, thereby preserving representation diversity, quantization fidelity, and codebook stability. In residual quantization, some codewords may become ``dead'' due to insufficient usage during training, limiting effective codebook capacity. Incorporating Q2I and I2I collaborative signals may further concentrate item assignments on a few popular codewords, exacerbating codebook imbalance and collapse. To address this issue, we introduce Fidelity Anti-collapse Regularization from two complementary perspectives. At the sample level, we enable each sample to update multiple semantically relevant codewords instead of only its nearest one, expanding gradient coverage. At the codeword level, we regularize codeword assignments across samples by penalizing excessive collisions, preventing a large number of samples from concentrating on a few codewords.
