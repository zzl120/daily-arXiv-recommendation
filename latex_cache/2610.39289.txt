Recently, Large Language Model -powered recommender agents have emerged as a promising approach to personalized recommendation by leveraging pretrained knowledge and reasoning capabilities to interpret user interactions . These recommender agents can capture user preferences and behavioral contexts from past experiences and utilize the accumulated knowledge to inform subsequent recommendation decisions. However, such personalization often requires personal information, including user profiles, interaction histories, and preferences, raising privacy concerns when this information is processed by centralized servers or external LLM services. These concerns are further underscored by privacy regulations, such as GDPR~ and CCPA~, which impose restrictions on the collection, processing, and sharing of personal information. A naive remedy is to deploy recommender agents independently on local devices to keep personal information private. However, this approach limits their ability to leverage diverse behavioral patterns and decision-making experiences observed from other users. Therefore, a collaborative recommendation framework that keeps personal information local while enabling recommender agents to leverage knowledge derived from one another's experiences is needed. From this perspective, Federated Recommendation is proposed as a promising approach to enabling collaboration across local devices while keeping user data private . Existing FedRec methods have enabled such collaboration through numerical aggregation of model parameters~ or collaborative signals~. However, FedRec clients typically operate on resource-constrained personal devices, and repeatedly updating the parameters of LLM-based agents can incur substantial computational and memory overhead. Meanwhile, recent recommender agents can accumulate and update derived from user experiences without modifying model parameters. This capability suggests that recommender agents can adapt not only through parameter updates but also by explicitly maintaining and utilizing experience-derived knowledge. By extending this approach, we envision a new paradigm for FedRec in which agents share natural-language knowledge rather than model parameters, enabling collaborative learning without repeated LLM fine-tuning . Unlike model parameters, recommendation knowledge expressed in natural language lacks predefined shared identifiers for alignment. We therefore need to define a consistently structured of knowledge sharing that makes explicit what each contribution describes. Recommendation knowledge describes how users with particular interaction patterns proceed to their next choices. However, similar patterns may lead to different decisions, such as continuing familiar interests or exploring new directions. A common representation alone therefore does not determine which contributions should be combined. Integration must account for both the preceding interaction patterns and the subsequent decision directions, consolidating related reasoning while preserving explanations for distinct choices. To address these challenges, we propose , a structured reasoning federation framework for Federated Recommender Agents. FedRecAgent keeps both client-side and server-side LLM parameters fixed, enabling personalization and collaboration through updates to experience-derived natural-language knowledge. Specifically, each client manages shareable knowledge units and private memories from local experiences. Each shareable unit follows a Condition--Transition--Reasoning structure: Condition describes the behavioral context, Transition captures the observed decision transition, and Reasoning explains the corresponding choice. These units abstract away specific item identities and user-specific preference details. Meanwhile, each local agent maintains two complementary private memories on its device: , capturing user preferences, and , capturing choice tendencies across behavioral contexts. The server organizes uploaded units by condition and transition and integrates their reasoning into a hierarchical global knowledge bank. During recommendation, each client uses its private memories to retrieve and select shared reasoning from the bank for personalized ranking. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: We formulate a new research problem, termed , in which LLM-powered recommender agents collaboratively leverage knowledge acquired from other agents while keeping private user information local. To address this problem, we propose , a framework that enables federated collaboration through the sharing and integration of natural-language knowledge rather than model parameters. In FedRecAgent, we design private dual memories to accumulate personalized knowledge and formulate C--T--R as a consistent unit for knowledge sharing that abstracts away private user and item details. We further establish condition--transition alignment as the criterion for hierarchical reasoning integration. We evaluate FedRecAgent across three recommendation domains under both dense and sparse settings, comparing it with representative federated and LLM-based recommendation methods.
