Generative recommendation has recently introduced a different paradigm. Items are encoded as hierarchical semantic identifiers through residual vector quantization~, and an encoder--decoder recommender autoregressively generates the identifier of the next item~. Subsequent improvements to codebooks and training objectives~ have further integrated item representation learning and ranked retrieval within a unified language-modeling framework. Most advances in this paradigm focus on the tokenizer. LETTER improves codebook quality through contrastive learning~; LC-Rec, SEED, and Co-design jointly quantize language representations and collaborative signals~; ETEGRec aligns the tokenizer and recommender through knowledge distillation~; and DIGER uses a Gumbel--Softmax relaxation to learn differentiable SIDs~. A recent line of work instead optimizes beam-search behavior in the recommender. APAO identifies vulnerable prefixes from model states and adaptively emphasizes them~, while BEAR introduces a beam-aware ranking constraint that pushes positive code tokens into the Top-$B$ set~. Both methods primarily preserve the single positive target path. How corpus-level, history-conditioned transition distributions can provide coverage supervision for multiple plausible prefixes remains underexplored. Our analysis of decoding paths reveals the severity of early pruning . P5~ and M6-Rec~ formulate recommendation as a language task. TIGER~ established the current generative retrieval paradigm by combining RQ-VAE SIDs with autoregressive generation. TAAL operates on the training objective and decoding of the recommender within this paradigm. LETTER~ improves codebook learning through multitask contrastive objectives. LC-Rec~ and SEED~ integrate language and collaborative signals into item quantization, while Co-design~ jointly optimizes collaborative and semantic quantization. ETEGRec~ aligns the tokenizer and recommender through distillation, and DIGER~ introduces differentiable semantic identifiers with Gumbel relaxation. These methods mainly improve offline SID quantization or tokenizer learning. TAAL instead operates after SIDs are fixed, modifying recommender training and candidate calibration. We use LETTER-TIGER as the backbone, so the two directions are complementary. Generative retrieval commonly uses trie-constrained beam search~, and STATIC~ accelerates this process on hardware. APAO~ detects vulnerable prefixes from the model's own prediction states and applies adaptive optimization. BEAR~ uses a beam-aware ranking regularizer so that positive code tokens satisfy the condition for entering the Top-$B$ candidates. Search-error analysis in machine translation provides a related perspective~. APAO and BEAR primarily supervise the positive target path and its model-internal ranking state. TAAL estimates a history-conditioned $ $ transition distribution from the training corpus, covers multiple plausible prefixes during training, and removes global marginal frequency with PMI at inference. Its focus is the source and dual use of external collaborative transition information rather than a redefinition of within-beam constraints on the positive path. Transition and co-occurrence structure are classical recommendation signals, from item-based collaborative filtering~ to factorized personalized Markov chains~. Recent work also injects collaborative cues into SID generation through personalized natural language at inference~. TAAL introduces no additional language model; it uses a statistical transition table to construct both the training soft target and the inference calibration score.
