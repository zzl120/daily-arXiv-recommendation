We presented a group-wise LLM augmentation framework for sequential recommendation and evaluated it on ML-32M and a large proprietary dataset by separating items into cold-start , extreme long-tail , regular long-tail , and popular tiers . Across candidate selection, augmentation ratio, cross-group splits, and LLM-side design choices, the results show that augmentation is group dependent rather than governed by a single strategy. Embedding-based candidate selection is more reliable than random selection when semantic retrieval is available, and the underrepresented groups differ in how much augmentation they can absorb and in how they trade off with overall NDCG@20. The LLM ablations also show that augmentation quality depends not only on model scale but also on prompting and insertion choices, with clear effects on GN@20, coverage, cost, and guardrail performance. Just as importantly, the best single-group settings do not carry over directly to the joint case, where cross-group augmentation remains a constrained multi-objective problem. A natural next step is to make that joint calibration explicit and test whether the same patterns hold across other recommenders and datasets. Collaborative filtering models learn latent item representations from user-item interactions, but perform poorly when items have inherently sparse interaction data . Popularity bias creates a feedback loop that systematically suppresses exposure for items with insufficient co-occurrence signals for both of long-tail and cold-start content . We build on this by introducing a percentile-based grouping strategy over the interaction distribution, augmenting user histories with LLM-generated interactions drawn from group-specific candidate pools, and analyzing resulting trade-off between overall and group-specific ranking quality. Our contributions are summarized as follows: We propose a group-wise targeted augmentation strategy that classifies items by interaction frequency to tailor the augmenting approach for each group, adapting to the dataset's popoularity distribution. We characterize the trade-off between group-specific and overall metrics by estimating the Pareto frontier and examining their correlated regions, demonstrating that each group induces a distinct trade-off curve determined by its representation in the historical data. This highlights that a single strategy does not generalize across groups and that one-size-fits-all approaches are suboptimal. We demonstrate that individually optimal per-group strategies do not compose into a globally optimal solution, confirming cross-group augmentation as a multi-objective optimization problem requiring joint calibration. For each target group $G_k \{G_0, G_1, G_2\}$, the pipeline randomly samples a proportion of users and augments each with a single synthetic item via LLM, selected from among $c=10$ candidates drawn from $G_k$. The augmentation per user is motivated by previous literature , where a single synthetic interaction per user minimizes the risk of shifting the users' preference distribution while still providing a meaningful signal to the base recommender. Candidate selection is performed via cosine similarity between Linq-Embed-Mistral item embeddings and mean-pooled users' representation on , determined by the encoder ablation in Table . On , where item metadata is insufficient for meaningful embedding construction, random sampling is used to select candidates for augmentation. The LLM selects from a closed candidate pool represented in the prompt , eliminating out-of-catalog selections. The selected item is inserted at $S_{n-2}$, in the user's interaction sequence $S_u= $ immediately preceding the validation and test items. SASRec is retrained from scratch on the augmented interaction matrix. All metrics are averaged over three independent runs. The prompt templates and implementation details are provided in Appendix . We formulate evaluation as a constrained optimization, group-specific NDCG@20 and group coverage GCov@100 that measures each group's share of top-100 recommendations per user, are the objectives to maximize, subject to overall NDCG@20 degradation remaining within an acceptable threshold. Given the vast search space, optimizing all variables concurrently results in a combinatorial explosion. Therefore, we fixed specific variables such as the quantity and positioning of the augmented data, following prior work and prioritized reporting the most impactful axes of variation. To systematically navigate this search space, we utilized a coordinate descent optimization strategy: sequentially fixing hyperparameters to isolate and optimize individual variables step-by-step. This greedy optimization pipeline guarantees practical reproducibility for any dataset and model.
