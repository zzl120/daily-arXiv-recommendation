We study this distinction in UAE GovTrust, a controlled English-Arabic retrieval diagnostic comprising 22 authored service summaries and 50 scenarios with 110 query variants. Twelve methods separate lexical and dense ranking, rank fusion, scope filtering, a multilingual cross-encoder, and direct service lookup. On 72 matched answerable queries with bilingual evidence, role-filtered reciprocal rank fusion retrieves a relevant first result for 69 queries (95.8\ Information retrieval; scope filtering; multilingual retrieval; Arabic; public services; RAG evaluation; behavioral testing A public-service question may mention where a person lives, where a vehicle is registered, where a property is located, and which authority issued a document. These are different relations. Retrieving a passage about a city named in the question is therefore insufficient: the system must determine which relation controls the requested service. For example, in the catalogue studied here, a question about a toll gate in Abu Dhabi should retrieve the Abu Dhabi toll-system card even when the vehicle is registered in Dubai. Reversing those two locations changes the relevant card without changing the set of location names. This problem matters to retrieval-augmented generation because a generator cannot reliably ground an answer in evidence that an upstream filter has discarded. It is also relevant to enterprise service catalogues, policy portals, and operational assistants in which organizational units or service territories overlap. Those applications motivate the study; they are not deployment settings evaluated in the experiments. The present work isolates the retrieval and routing stages so their errors can be observed without a generator or an automated answer judge obscuring the source of failure. UAE GovTrust is presented as a controlled diagnostic, not as an expert-validated benchmark of UAE law. Its corpus consists of short, authored service summaries, its questions are synthetic, and its labels describe relevance within that catalogue. An initial development experiment suggested a large benefit from scope filtering. A subsequent technical audit reproduced the original arithmetic but exposed failures in the rule-based router. This report incorporates that criticism through stronger comparisons, an explicit post-audit stress set, and a separation of routing errors from errors in ranking eligible evidence. The contribution has three parts. First, it provides a reproducible bilingual diagnostic in which the service-controlling location differs from an incidental location. Second, it disentangles scope selection, ranking, sparse-branch eligibility, and service lookup through twelve comparison methods. Third, it documents how the benefit of a correct hard filter can coexist with severe failures of the mechanism that chooses that filter. The resulting evidence supports a limited engineering conclusion: scope inference must be evaluated independently of the retrieval gains obtained when the scope is correct. No claim is made to the first UAE regulatory RAG system, a new general filtering algorithm, or production readiness. Regulatory retrieval and grounded legal question answering already have substantial prior work. RIRAG introduced ObliQA using Abu Dhabi Global Market regulatory material, and the RIRAG-2025 shared task developed that research setting . Version- and scope-aware question answering over normative documents has also been studied in a deployed system . These studies rule out treating a jurisdiction or version filter itself as a new contribution. UAE GovTrust instead examines a small, explicitly controlled ambiguity: several locations are present, but they occupy different service-related roles. It does not reproduce the document scale, domain validation, or end-to-end evaluation of those systems. Multilingual and bilingual evaluation are likewise established. bLLeQA examines grounded legal question answering in French and Dutch ; MIRAGE-Bench evaluates multilingual RAG systems ; and RAGBench distinguishes retrieval-related and answer-related quality dimensions . Alrajeh studies Arabic-English citation fabrication, false premises, and unanswerable questions for Saudi data-protection law and the GDPR using manually verified references . Wang and Suresh audit the institutional frameworks assumed by multilingual chatbots when jurisdiction is unspecified . The present questions usually specify competing locations rather than omitting location altogether, and the outputs assessed here are ranked cards and gate decisions rather than legal citations or generated explanations. The diagnostic design builds on behavioral testing. CheckList distinguishes targeted capabilities and invariance tests from aggregate accuracy . FollowIR evaluates whether retrieval models follow relevance instructions rather than merely match their words . NevIR tests ranking distinctions that depend on negation . Accordingly, the role swaps, irrelevant-fact additions, and negation cases in this report are a domain-specific application of existing behavioral evaluation principles. They are not a new general testing paradigm. The Arabic text is intended as Modern Standard Arabic; it is not an Emirati-dialect benchmark, unlike the dialect coverage investigated by EDRAC . The retrieval comparisons use BM25 , multilingual E5 embeddings , reciprocal rank fusion , and a compact multilingual cross-encoder associated with mMARCO training . The chosen cross-encoder is a practical open-model comparison, not an assertion of the strongest available reranker. A direct service lookup is included because a 22-card catalogue may be solvable largely through service classification and scope selection. This control tests the need for retrieval complexity within the actual experimental setting. The frozen catalogue contains 22 English-Arabic service cards linked to 21 distinct official-source URLs. Services cover business licensing and incorporation, tenancy and utilities, rental disputes, tolls and parking, buses, visa-validity channels, identity documents, VAT registration, work permits, and national digital identity. Applicability labels use five values: federal , Abu Dhabi , Dubai , Abu Dhabi Global Market , and Dubai International Financial Centre . These are catalogue-routing labels. They do not form a complete legal ontology. In particular, the publisher level and the service applicability label are separate fields: a federal publisher can describe a channel relevant to a particular issuing location. The cards are AI-assisted factual summaries, not copies of official documents. Their source register records access status, observation date, publisher level, and unresolved temporal and redistribution questions. At construction, thirteen cards were based on inspected page bodies and nine on indexed excerpts. The revision inspected page bodies supporting three additional cards: Dubai tenancy registration, the Dubai Rental Disputes Center overview, and Federal Tax Authority VAT registration. Six cards remain supported only by indexed excerpts. The experimental text was kept frozen during this recheck. Inspecting a source page does not independently validate the Arabic wording, all legal exceptions, or the candidate relevance labels. The original set has 50 scenarios: 36 answerable, eight requiring clarification, and six requiring abstention under the catalogue policy. Thirty answerable scenarios require one card and six require two. Every scenario has English and Arabic variants; ten also have a mixed-language variant. The primary retrieval analysis uses the 72 English-Arabic variants of the 36 answerable scenarios. The 100 matched variants of all scenarios assess gate decisions. The additional ten mixed-language variants are exploratory and are not pooled into the primary estimate. Fourteen paired families change the relevant local scope, while four families hold a federal service invariant under a location change. Questions, relevance labels, and routing patterns were developed within the same workflow. They were not independently authored, and no untouched test split exists. The Arabic variants and domain labels remain candidates pending qualified independent review. All references to a correct scope or relevant card in the results therefore mean agreement with this explicit catalogue annotation, not an independent finding about UAE law or administrative eligibility. Preprocessing lowercases text, removes Arabic diacritics and tatweel, and normalizes selected alif and ya forms. E5 uses intfloat/multilingual-e5-small with query and passage prefixes, attention-masked mean pooling, L2 normalization, and exact dot-product ranking. Inputs are truncated to 512 tokens; vectors have 384 dimensions. RRF sums 1/ over lexical and dense branches, with branch depth 100. All 22 cards fit within that depth. Ties are broken by the existing ascending document-ID order. The original RRF implementation admits zero-score BM25 cards, so an uninformative lexical branch can still contribute ranks determined partly by document IDs. PositiveRRF removes cards with BM25 scores of zero from that branch before assigning ranks. The dense branch remains unchanged. Scope filtering precedes branch ranking in routed variants. This detail is particularly relevant when Arabic questions are matched to English-only evidence, where sparse lexical overlap can be limited. MentionRRF filters by every recognized location mentioned in the query, retaining FED. RoleRRF instead calls the original deterministic infer function to select a service-controlling role and scope before RRF. OracleRRF substitutes the annotated action and allowed scopes; it is an upper-bound control for scope selection with that ranker, not a deployable method. RoleDense and RolePositiveRRF use the same unchanged router with dense ranking or positive-score fusion. The router was not patched after the audit or tuned to the new stress outcomes. Its exact ordering of global checks, regular expressions, and clause matching is released in the code. CrossEncoder uses cross-encoder/mmarco-mMiniLMv2-L12-H384-v1. It jointly encodes each query-card pair and ranks the scalar logits without an activation. All 22 cards are scored, avoiding a first-stage recall bottleneck in this small corpus. RoleCrossEncoder uses the same logits but restricts the candidate set to the original router output. Accuracy calculation shares full-corpus pair logits across these two variants; timed routed requests score only eligible cards. Pair batches contain at most eight examples, with longest-first truncation at 512 tokens. Neither neural model is fine-tuned on UAE GovTrust. DirectLookup combines the unchanged router with bilingual keyword patterns for the thirteen service types, then returns cards matching both the selected scope and detected service, in document-ID order. It does not encode or search card text. Its evidence-language invariance is therefore a design property, not evidence of cross-lingual document retrieval. The service patterns are a transparent catalogue-specific comparison and share the development bias of the routing rules. They can fail to identify a service even when the scope is correct. The nine previously disclosed audit probes were first reproduced as development examples: two retained the expected route and seven failed. They were not treated as unseen evaluation. A separate, frozen post-audit diagnostic set then covered six services: business licensing, tenancy registration, tolls, parking, buses, and visa validity. Each service has two location assignments and two languages, giving 24 unchanged controls. Seven transformations per control produce 168 perturbations: paraphrasing the controlling clause; adding an irrelevant Sharjah reference, a 2021 phone-purchase date, or an unknown old phone number; placing the distracting location after the controlling clause; negating an alternative location; and correcting an earlier location. Labels are inherited from the target service and location in the original catalogue. Negation and correction cases explicitly identify the final intended location, but their expected labels have not received independent linguistic or domain review. The transformations were authored after the audit with knowledge of the router. They deliberately exercise known weaknesses, were frozen before the new comparative run, and are neither representative user traffic nor a held-out robustness benchmark. Their purpose is to expose mechanisms and compare behavior on disclosed cases, not estimate a deployment failure rate. Hit@1 is one when the first retrieved card belongs to G , including either relevant card in a two-card question. Recall@5 is the fraction of G in the first five results. nDCG@10 uses binary relevance with logarithmic discount and normalization by the ideal ordering. Empty rankings score zero on these retrieval measures. Wrong-scope first hits count returned first cards whose scope lies outside the annotated allowed set; an empty ranking is not counted as a wrong-scope return and is reported separately. This distinction prevents a system that refuses every query from appearing reliable merely because it returns no inapplicable cards. Routing agreement requires the correct action and, for answerable cases, an exact match to the annotated allowed-scope set. Filter survival measures whether all relevant cards remain eligible. Error decomposition distinguishes incorrect gate actions, answering with an incorrect scope, and a wrong first result despite correct routing. A paired local-target measure additionally requires both members of a scope-changing family to retrieve the relevant local card first; a shared federal card does not satisfy that criterion. Translations and perturbations are not treated as independent samples. The original RoleRRF-minus-RRF Hit@1 difference is accompanied by the preserved exploratory percentile bootstrap over eighteen paired scenario families: 10,000 resamples with seed 20261021. Its interval describes variation over the authored families and is not a population confidence statement. The new comparisons and stress outcomes are descriptive; no confirmatory significance claim, multiplicity-adjusted hypothesis test, or population generalization is made. Freezing a post-audit set improves traceability but does not make it independently held out. The revision used a newly installed Python environment on the same host as the original study. Execution used an AMD EPYC 9V74 80-Core Processor CPU, one inference thread, float32 weights, and no quantization. Both models were downloaded from pinned revisions with recorded SHA-256 hashes. Fresh E5 embeddings were generated for both evidence-language conditions. Index construction and model loading were separated from request measurements. The model and package manifests, environment record, and complete dependency lock are included with the reproducibility material. Latency was measured on the 72 original matched answerable queries with bilingual cards. Each method received three warm-up queries followed by three seeded, randomized passes, yielding 216 measurements per method and 2,592 in total. Timed requests included routing where used, tokenization, fresh query encoding or cross-encoder scoring, sorting, and output construction. There was no query-vector cache, network request, generator, concurrency test, or cold-start measurement. The median and empirical 95th percentile describe this single-host, 22-card workload; they are not enterprise service-level guarantees. An occurrence of Sharjah triggers abstention before a role is resolved, even when it is an unrelated past residence. The central distinction is between having correct scope metadata and correctly inferring that metadata from a question. The original development templates make the second problem look solved, allowing a hard filter to expose its ranking benefit. The post-audit examples show that the assumption fails when irrelevant dates and locations, altered syntax, negation, or corrections enter the input. Reporting only oracle-filter performance, only development-template accuracy, or only wrong-scope returned results would conceal part of that failure. A useful evaluation must count non-answer errors and evidence exclusion alongside ranking errors. The direct-lookup comparison also changes the architectural interpretation. A small catalogue with one or two cards per service-scope combination can often be handled by identifying a service and selecting its scope. Its strong score does not establish an alternative production solution, because its keyword detector shares the same development constraints and its router shares the stress failures. It does show that the catalogue does not justify a claim that a complex hybrid architecture is necessary. Retrieval research on full policies would need substantially more documents, overlapping services, chunk boundaries, duplicated and superseded provisions, and relevance judgments beyond catalogue membership. The evidence-language experiment demonstrates a separate failure that persists even when routing is correct. A multilingual dense model can retrieve across languages, but a hybrid implementation can dilute that ability if its sparse branch promotes zero-overlap documents. The positive-score ablation is deliberately simple and reproducible. It should not be generalized into a claim that a particular fusion constant, weighting scheme, or sparse cutoff is optimal; none was tuned or compared systematically. Similar care is needed when interpreting the cross-encoder result: a single compact checkpoint and this synthetic catalogue do not represent the full range of reranking methods. Several validity limits are fundamental. The corpus, translations, prompts, parser, and labels were co-developed with AI assistance. Independent Arabic and public-service experts have not validated them. The stress set is post hoc and intentionally adversarial to known rules. Sample counts reflect many dependent translations and transformations, with only six services in the stress design. Neither a large number of perturbations nor a narrow computational confidence interval compensates for that limited variety. Model pretraining exposure to public service descriptions is unknown. No independent test set, human relevance annotation study, or inter-annotator agreement estimate is available. The material is also a service catalogue rather than a collection of official source passages. Six cards still lack an inspected full page body, and observing a current webpage does not establish an authentic historical version or a legally complete description. No experiment here evaluates document-authority verification, temporal change detection, generated-answer faithfulness, citation correctness, or legal eligibility. The hypothetical historical queries only test refusal when the frozen catalogue lacks a requested version. They cannot support claims about handling real policy changes. The report also does not establish safety, fairness, or production effectiveness for a government service. A next-stage study should separate construction from evaluation. Qualified Arabic and domain reviewers should first check the source-backed labels and document exceptions. A different authoring group should then create unseen questions, preserving families across splits and withholding negation, correction, and irrelevant-fact formulations from parser development. Learned or schema-constrained language-model role extraction, query rewriting, and clarification strategies should be compared against the disclosed rules. Only after retrieval and routing are validated should a fixed generator be added and scored for supported claims, completeness, and citation applicability. These are proposed extensions; no such experiments are claimed in this report. UAE GovTrust shows both the benefit and the fragility of scope filtering in a controlled bilingual public-service catalogue. With the original templates, role-filtered fusion improves first-result retrieval, but a simple lookup and filtered dense retrieval achieve similar results. Removing zero-score lexical candidates materially improves the English-only evidence condition. Under disclosed post-audit perturbations, the unchanged router frequently blocks retrieval or selects an inapplicable scope, reversing the practical benefit of filtering. The supported contribution is a reproducible diagnostic and error decomposition, not an expert-validated UAE benchmark or an end-to-end RAG system.
