As a result, POI recommendation has emerged as a key research task~ based on LBSN data, aiming to understand users’ behavioral preferences~ from their historical trajectories and provide location-based services~. A notable variant of this task is next POI recommendation, which aims to predict the next location a user is likely to visit based on the spatial-temporal context of their check-in history and a given temporal cue~, assisting users in trip planning~ and in discovering locations of potential interest~. Recently, large language models have demonstrated remarkable progress in contextual understanding~, marking a significant breakthrough in tackling complex real-world tasks. This has motivated the use of LLM-based generative modeling as a novel paradigm for next POI recommendation, where the model directly generates the ID of the next POI at the target time based on user-provided prompts. Compared to traditional POI recommendation methods, this approach avoids the semantic loss that occurs when heterogeneous textual data are converted into numerical IDs. It effectively leverages the general knowledge of LLMs to capture contextual dependencies in user check-in trajectories. Moreover, semantic POI ID representations have been proposed to initialize IDs with similar meanings for geographically or functionally related locations, further enhancing the LLM's ability to understand and generalize POI semantics. Recent methods~ based on LLMs primarily focus on fine-tuning pre-trained models but overlook the benefits of expanding computational capacity through test-time scaling, enhancing the next POI generation via reasoning. For instance, GNPR fine-tunes the base model with pre-trained SIDs to enforce semantic alignment between the model and the ID space. However, this decoupled training strategy leads to an incomplete understanding of SID information, particularly under sparse POI recommendation scenarios. As a result, current LLM-based approaches struggle to accurately incorporate SID signals during generation, yielding suboptimal performance. This insufficient comprehension of semantic IDs is further reflected in the model’s poor generalization on out-of-domain datasets and its limited capability to distinguish SID categories. These limitations highlight the importance of designing effective reasoning models to improve both SID understanding and sequential modeling within LLMs. However, developing a generation-based POI recommendation framework with integrated reasoning is nontrivial due to several challenges: Traditional reasoning models rely on RL-based policy optimization, which assumes that the base model has sufficient predictive capability to sample correct answers. Moreover, overfitting during the supervised fine-tuning stage can restrict exploration in subsequent RL training and decouple memorization from reasoning, ultimately resulting in suboptimal learning . Existing policy optimization methods treat all prompts equally, neglecting varying prediction difficulties, which limits exploration of more challenging prompts. In POI recommendation scenarios, predicting targets that deviate from a user’s habitual temporal patterns or commonly visited regions presents greater difficulty. While Dr.GRPO removes the variance term in advantage estimation to alleviate signal amplification caused by estimation bias, it still overlooks another form of bias: the tendency of the model to produce similar intra-group advantage distributions across samples of varying difficulty. For example, a hard instance with rewards and an easy one with both yield the same advantage under mean-centered advantage estimation. This leads to uniform updates across tasks of different difficulty, which undermines fine-grained learning and limits the ability to assign appropriate update magnitudes based on model competence. {fig/introduction.pdf} To address the aforementioned challenges, we propose Think2Go as shown in Figure~, a unified framework that combines SFT and RL-based reasoning to unlock LLM’s test-time reasoning capacity, thereby improving its comprehension of semantic identifiers and modeling of sequential user behaviors. To tackle the first challenge, we introduce a unified training paradigm that integrates both SFT and RL-based policy optimization. The model generates structured outputs in the form of , where the token serves as a bridge between the reasoning and SFT phase. The final token is used for computing the SFT loss, while the and segments comprise the reasoning trajectory, which is optimized via policy gradients. This unified structure facilitates seamless learning between memorization and exploration. To address the second challenge, we propose an epistemic uncertainty advantage calibration strategy for prompt-based learning. Specifically, we use kernel density estimation to assess the spatial-temporal similarity between the current query and the user’s historical behavior. This similarity quantifies epistemic uncertainty and adaptively modulates the advantage signal to better explore challenging instances. For the third challenge, we introduce difficulty-aware advantage weighting, which regularizes the reward of each sample by its maximum value to reflect prediction difficulty. This allows the model to adaptively adjust the scale of policy updates. As a result, the combined effect of prompt and model-aware calibrations serves as an implicit curriculum learning mechanism, delivering fine-grained, instance-specific gradients that enable more effective and adaptive policy optimization. Existing literature on next POI recommendation primarily focuses on modeling users' historical check-in sequences to capture the spatial-temporal transition patterns~ between POIs, making it a typical sequential recommendation task~. Early studies~ widely adopted Markov Chain methods~, leveraging personalized transition matrices to model user preferences and mobility patterns. However, these approaches fall short in capturing users' complex interests and implicit behavioral signals compared to deep learning-based models. With the rise of deep learning, research has gradually shifted towards models based on Recurrent Neural Networks ~ and attention mechanisms~. For instance, DeepMove~ integrates attention and gating mechanisms to jointly learn users’ long-term periodic and short-term preferences. STGN~ enhances LSTM architectures by incorporating spatial-temporal gates into the gating units, enabling the modeling of long- and short-term spatial-temporal preferences. STAN~ and STiSAN~ further introduce attention mechanisms to capture both continuous and non-continuous spatial-temporal contextual intervals in the modeling process. In addition, Graph Neural Networks have been leveraged to model high-level POI transition patterns by utilizing the user-POI graph structure~. For example, Graph-Flashback~ constructs a powerful weighted user-POI knowledge graph to initialize POI embeddings, while STHGCN~ employs hypergraphs to capture trajectory-level collaborative signals. Recently, LLMs with extensive commonsense knowledge have shown strong potential in recommendation tasks, leading to a rise in LLM-based approaches for next POI recommendation~. LLM-Mob~ enhances the modeling of historical trajectory context through in-context learning. NextLocLLM~ attempts to predict spatial coordinates directly to improve spatial reasoning within LLMs. LLM4POI~ reformulates next POI recommendation as a question-answering task and fine-tunes the model using QLoRA-based supervised learning. Refine-POI~ applies Group-based Rule-guided Policy Optimization ~ with recommendation-driven reward design to generate top-k recommendation lists. However, they have not explored reasoning-enhanced models to boost generalization. While Refine-POI leverages the GRPO framework for reasoning-based prediction, it overlooks the optimization of the advantage function, which limits performance improvements. Recent advancements in generative Artificial Intelligence , particularly with models like OpenAI-o1~ and Deepseek-R1~, have extended the scope of research from large language models to large reasoning models . This shift is predicated on the application of the Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback training framework, coupled with test-time scaling and an emphasis on deep thinking capabilities. The core innovation lies in explicitly leveraging a long chain-of-thought mechanism~. Given the sampled trajectories, the next step is to assign appropriate rewards to each one. Conventionally, reward signals include format rewards and evaluation-based rewards , which encourage the model to generate outputs with correct answer formatting and ultimately improve task performance. However, the inherent sparsity of such rewards severely restricts effective optimization. This is particularly problematic in the initial training phase, where the model often fails to generate outputs in the prescribed format and much less the correct answer. To address this challenge, we design a progressive reward mechanism that first encourages the model to generate well-formatted responses and then to produce semantically correct answers. For format rewards, we use a two-level scheme: if the output contains a partial format , a reward of 0.5 is assigned; if the output satisfies the full structure and exceeds a length threshold of 300 tokens, a full reward of 1.0 is given. Benefiting from SFT, the model gradually learns to produce partially correct formats before mastering the complete structure. The length constraint is crucial for preventing reward hacking, where the model exploits formatting alone to obtain undeserved rewards. For evaluation-based rewards, we adopt a fine-grained scoring strategy based on decomposed semantic ID units. Each correctly predicted unit receives a reward of ${L}$, where $L$ denotes the total number of semantic units. This approach provides graded feedback for partially correct answers and encourages the model to incrementally refine its output toward complete correctness. Dr.GRPO removes the regularization term to avoid difficulty bias caused by excessively small group-wise standard deviations when the input prompts are either too hard or too easy, and retains only the centered outcome reward as the advantage. However, this leads to a problem in multi-reward settings where predictions of varying quality receive identical advantages if their rewards follow similar group-wise distributions. As a result, it weakens the model's ability to distinguish between better and worse strategies, reduces the directional specificity of gradient updates, and increases the risk of converging to suboptimal local minima. For example, consider a group size of 3, where the reward values are for an easy instance and for a hard one. After mean-centering, both groups yield the same advantage distribution: . As a result, the optimization process becomes insensitive to task difficulty, weakening the learning incentives for challenging samples and ultimately constraining the effectiveness of policy optimization and the upper bound of model performance. We introduce a unified fine-tuning and reinforcement learning–based reasoning model to enhance the model’s reasoning ability, where the policy is optimized by calibrating the advantage based on both the epistemic uncertainty of the data and the difficulty agnostic bias in the model's advantage estimation. Building upon this design, we propose Think2Go, an advantage-calibrated reasoning model for time-sensitive next POI recommendation. Think2Go employs kernel density estimation to quantify epistemic uncertainty by measuring the spatial-temporal proximity between the current query and the user’s historical activity patterns. Moreover, difficulty-aware calibration enables reward-sensitive advantage estimation by scaling rewards relative to the maximum, thus mitigating bias from uniform update strength. This allows for more fine-grained and adaptive control over the policy updates, ensuring that samples of varying difficulty contribute appropriately to the learning process.
