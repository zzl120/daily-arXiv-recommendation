Large-scale e-commerce platforms serve millions of users and manage vast, continuously evolving item catalogs, making personalized recommendation essential for efficient product discovery and user engagement. Sequential recommendation plays a crucial role in these platforms by predicting users' future interests from their chronological interaction histories. Conventional sequential recommenders, such as GRU4Rec, SASRec, and BERT4Rec, employ recurrent or Transformer-based architectures to model user behavior . Despite their effectiveness, these methods typically rely on scoring items from a predefined catalog, which poses challenges for efficient retrieval as the item space and recommendation scenarios continue to expand. Generative recommendation offers an alternative by representing items as sequences of discrete semantic tokens and casting retrieval as conditional sequence generation. TIGER~ established this paradigm by constructing semantic IDs with residual quantization and generating the next item autoregressively. Subsequent work has explored improved item tokenization and recommendation architectures, including incorporating collaborative signals into item representations , as well as modeling the structure of semantic tokens . This paradigm provides a unified way to represent items and generate recommendations, and has shown promise for large-scale retrieval. Despite recent progress, applying generative recommendation to large-scale e-commerce still faces several challenges: A SID is not merely a flat token sequence: its levels form a hierarchy, where later codes are generated from the earlier ones. Treating SIDs as ordinary one-dimensional sequences may prevent the model from explicitly learning these dependencies. Standard next-token prediction supervises a single subsequent item for each user context, whereas practical recommendation often needs to retrieve multiple relevant items. This gap can limit the diversity of training signals and weaken the alignment between the training objective and list-based retrieval. Click-only supervision overlooks information in exposure logs. Items that are shown to users but not clicked can provide informative negative feedback, yet this signal is not directly captured by standard NTP. Reinforcement learning can align generated recommendations with user feedback. However, optimizing candidate-level rewards alone does not explicitly encourage a better relative ordering of candidates within a recommendation list. We propose , a generative recommender designed for large-scale e-commerce that addresses these challenges through coordinated designs in item tokenization, model architecture, and learning objectives. RedMall constructs hierarchical SIDs by applying residual vector quantization to multimodal item representations refined with chronological behavior supervision. To expose the structure of these SIDs to the recommender, we introduce a lightweight SID encoder that fuses embeddings across SID levels and maps the fused representation back to level-specific features. The generation model further combines a sparse mixture-of-experts decoder, multi-target prediction , and dual gated attention. MTP supports session-wise recommendation, while dual gated attention adaptively modulates attention outputs and enables bidirectional modeling of the observed interaction history. To use behavioral feedback beyond clicks, we introduce an exposure-aware loss that treats exposed but unclicked items as negative feedback during training. We further combine Group Relative Policy Optimization with an auxiliary LambdaRank objective . The reward combines real-world user feedback with ranking model predictions to guide preference alignment, while the auxiliary LambdaRank loss explicitly optimizes the relative ordering of generated items. Together, these objectives improve recommendation relevance and list-wise ranking quality. We evaluate RedMall on four public benchmarks---Amazon Beauty, Amazon Toys, Amazon Food, and MovieLens-1M---and conduct online A/B experiments in industrial e-commerce recommendation and advertising scenarios. RedMall outperforms the evaluated baselines across all reported Recall and NDCG metrics on the public datasets. In online experiments, it improves advertiser value by 2.24\ The contributions of this work are summarized as follows: We present RedMall, a generative recommender for large-scale e-commerce that integrates multimodal, behavior-aware SID construction with generative retrieval. We introduce a lightweight SID encoder to explicitly model dependencies across SID levels, and combine it with MoE, MTP, and dual gated attention to strengthen representation learning and multi-item recommendation. We propose an exposure-aware training objective and a preference-alignment framework that combines GRPO with LambdaRank to incorporate negative exposure feedback and improve the relative ordering of generated candidates. We conduct experiments on four public datasets and online A/B tests in e-commerce and advertising, demonstrating consistent improvements over the evaluated baselines and gains in business metrics. Generative recommendation has shown considerable potential in industrial recommendation systems. However, the performance of existing generative retrieval methods is limited by several challenges. Semantic IDs are treated as one-dimensional sequences, overlooking the hierarchical dependencies among different layers. Next-token prediction used in existing methods is unable to explicitly optimize session-wise recommendation. Although reinforcement learning has been applied to align generated recommendations with user preferences, few existing methods explicitly optimize the ranking of positive items in session-wise recommendation. To address the above issues, we propose RedMall, a generative recommender designed for large-scale e-commerce. Firstly, RedMall constructs hierarchical SIDs through residual quantization over multimodal representations. Secondly, RedMall introduces a lightweight SID encoder to explicitly model layer-wise dependencies in SIDs. Moreover, RedMall incorporates sparse mixture-of-experts, dual gated attention, and multi-target prediction to scale model size, enhance user interest representation, and optimize session-wise recommendation. We further design an exposure-aware loss that treats exposed but unclicked items as negative feedback. To better align user preferences and list-wise ranking objectives, we combine Group Relative Policy Optimization with an auxiliary LambdaRank loss, using both real user feedback and item ranking to guide optimization. Experiments on four public datasets---Amazon Beauty, Amazon Toys, Amazon Food, and MovieLens-1M---show that RedMall outperforms the evaluated baselines across all reported Recall and NDCG metrics. Generative retrieval methods, such as TIGER and OneRec, formulate recommendation as autoregressive next-token prediction . However, in modern recommendation scenarios, a user's recent interaction history can support multiple relevant recommendations, whereas supervision based on a single subsequent item provides a limited training signal. We therefore introduce multi-target prediction , extending single-item supervision to multiple target items under the same user context. Compared with single-target NTP training, MTP provides richer supervision that may capture a broader range of user interests and reduce reliance on a single observed interaction. It also better aligns training with session-wise recommendation, where multiple relevant items must be retrieved for each user context. In deploying RedMall to large-scale e-commerce systems, we address two practical challenges: intra-SID item ranking and inference acceleration. Since multiple items may share the same semantic ID , directly retrieving all items associated with each generated SID can reduce recommendation diversity and exhaust the limited retrieval quota, preventing sufficient coverage of distinct SIDs. To address this issue, we introduce a posterior-based ranking strategy to select representative items within each SID. Specifically, we assign each item a ranking score based on two historical performance metrics: Gross Merchandise Volume per Mille and Orders per Mille : score = 0.8 + 0.2 , where per mille means per 1,000 impressions. Items sharing the same SID are ranked by their scores, and only the top-$K$ items are retained, with $K=40$ in our production deployment. This strategy improves retrieval quota utilization and enables broader coverage of distinct SIDs. To support large-scale online serving, we employ several inference optimizations. First, we introduce KV caching to accelerate autoregressive decoding during beam search by reusing previously computed attention states. Furthermore, we adopt batched inference and leverage multithreading and multiprocessing to accelerate data processing and model inference. Our production serving system is deployed on 24 NVIDIA L20 GPUs and processes approximately 120 million user inference requests per day. With these optimizations, the serving system achieves a throughput of up to 420 queries per second per GPU. In this paper, we presented RedMall, a generative recommender designed for large-scale e-commerce. RedMall co-designs SID tokenization, SID encoding, architecture, and model training. SID tokenization provides accurate e-commerce representations and end-to-end SID generation. The SID encoder explicitly models dependencies across SID layers, while MoE, dual gated attention, and multi-target prediction scale model capacity, strengthen sequence modeling, and support session-wise recommendation, respectively. We further introduced the Exposure-Aware Loss to incorporate exposed but unclicked items as negative feedback, and combined GRPO with a LambdaRank loss to align user preferences and improve the ranking of positive items. Large-scale online A/B tests show that RedMall delivers substantial gains in both e-commerce recommendation and e-commerce advertising. These results demonstrate the potential of generative modeling for large-scale e-commerce. In future work, we plan to explore how to integrate RedMall with the reasoning capabilities of large language models to support accurate user-intent reasoning. Sequential recommendation aims to predict users' future interactions based on their historical behavior sequences. Early neural approaches employ various architectures to capture temporal patterns and sequential dependencies, including GRU4Rec~, SASRec~, and BERT4Rec~. Recently, advances in large language models have inspired two major research directions: representation-oriented and generation-oriented approaches. The former leverages the powerful representation capabilities of LLMs to enhance item embeddings~, or employs LLMs to model user behavior sequences and predict future item representations~. In contrast, generation-oriented approaches reformulate recommendation as an autoregressive generation task, directly generating item identifiers or semantic tokens conditioned on user interaction histories. This paradigm reduces the reliance on conventional candidate scoring and has motivated recent advances in generative recommendation. Unlike traditional recommendation frameworks, generative recommendation represents items as discrete token sequences and formulates recommendation as a sequence generation problem. Existing methods typically consist of two key components: item tokenization and generative modeling. TIGER~ pioneered Semantic ID -based generative recommendation by employing RQ-VAE~ for item tokenization and T5~ for autoregressive item generation. Subsequent studies have improved item representations and tokenization strategies. LETTER~ and CoST~ incorporate collaborative signals and contrastive learning to enhance semantic representations, while CoFiRec introduces item-aware positional encoding to improve feature modeling. Recent industrial advances further demonstrate the scalability and effectiveness of generative recommendation. OneRec~ establishes an end-to-end generative recommendation framework for large-scale industrial applications, unifying retrieval and ranking through session-wise generation and preference alignment. OneRec-V2~ improves computational efficiency through a lazy decoder-only architecture and incorporates reinforcement learning with real-world user feedback for preference alignment. OneRec-Think~ further explores explicit reasoning by integrating semantic alignment, reasoning, and personalized recommendation. Despite these advances, most SID-based generative recommenders treat SIDs as flattened token sequences, overlooking their inherent hierarchical dependencies. Tree-Transformer~ addresses this limitation by introducing tree-aware absolute positional encoding and topology-aware relative positional encoding to capture SID structural information. However, these specialized positional encodings may compromise the original sequential positional information. To overcome this limitation, we propose a lightweight SID encoder that explicitly captures hierarchical dependencies within SIDs while preserving the original sequential positional encoding.
