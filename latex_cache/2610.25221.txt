Recent advances increasingly leverage large language models to bridge domains through global item semantics. However, this paradigm is inherently limited by two issues: I. semantic domain isolation, where item semantics remain domain-specific and hinder transferable modeling, and II. semantic-behavior inconsistency, where local semantic relations are more consistent with behavioral transitions, whereas such consistency often breaks down globally across domains. To this end, we propose PerCDR, an LLM-enhanced CDSR framework that shifts the focus from global semantic bridging to persona-guided behavioral modeling. Specifically, PerCDR leverages LLM-inferred personas to globally guide transferable cross-domain behavioral patterns, while exploiting local semantic relations to enhance intra-domain behavioral modeling, followed by domain preference adaptation for recommendation refinement. Sequential Recommendation aims to predict a user’s next interaction based on historical behavior sequences and has become a fundamental paradigm for personalized recommendation~. However, the inherent sparsity of user-item interactions often results in insufficient behavioral modeling, thereby limiting model performance. To alleviate this issue, Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation exploits user behaviors from auxiliary domains to improve user modeling in the target domain, emerging as a promising solution~. Classic CDSR methods primarily rely on collaborative signals from user-item interactions for cross-domain transfer. For instance, C$^2$DSR~ captures cross-domain item relations through interaction co-occurrences, and Tri-CDR~ enhances user modeling with mixed-domain behavioral sequences. However, such signals are often too scarce to establish reliable relations across heterogeneous domains. Consequently, some studies have incorporated textual information into CDSR, using item semantics to establish cross-domain connections. Early efforts, such as UniSRec~ and CTT~, leverage pre-trained language models to enrich item representations and uncover transferable knowledge across domains. More recently, the emergence of Large Language Models has further enhanced semantic learning, inspiring approaches such as LLMCDSR~, LLM4CDSR~, and SemaCDR~. Despite their effectiveness, these methods share a common paradigm, as illustrated in Figure~ , which globally aligns item semantics to facilitate joint behavioral modeling across domains. However, such a paradigm suffers from two key issues:\\ . As illustrated in Figure~ , items in the Kitchen domain are primarily described by functional attributes, , “storage” and “accessories”, whereas items in the Food domain are characterized by product categories, , “coffee” and “tea”. Such isolated domain-specific semantics make it difficult for global alignment to learn transferable behavioral patterns across domains. \\ . As shown in Figure~ , a user who purchases a spoon subsequently interacts with a semantically related item within the same domain, , a whisk, but also purchases a semantically distant item in another domain, , peanut oil. This observation suggests that semantic proximity is more consistent with behavioral transitions within a domain than across domains. {figs/introduction.pdf} Taken together, these observations suggest that the conventional perspective, which relates domains through global item semantics, is inherently suboptimal. Instead, CDSR should adopt a perspective that relates behaviors across domains according to the underlying behavioral rationale of users. Motivated by this insight, we propose a CDSR paradigm, as illustrated in Figure~ . Specifically, we leverage LLMs to infer transferable user personas from mixed-domain interaction histories, capturing high-level user characteristics such as habits, intents, and behavioral tendencies. These user personas then provide global guidance for establishing cross-domain behavioral relations, rather than globally aligning item semantics. Meanwhile, local semantic structures are exploited as associations to strengthen intra-domain behavioral modeling. By leveraging global persona guidance and local semantic association, our framework performs joint modeling to learn transferable behavioral patterns across domains. Furthermore, we introduce a domain preference adaptation strategy that refines the joint predictions with domain-specific predictions for more accurate recommendations. To sum up, our contributions are as follows: We propose PerCDR, a novel persona-guided CDSR framework that moves beyond the conventional semantic bridging paradigm. It consists of two stages: Joint Behavioral Modeling and Domain Preference Adaptation. In the Joint Behavioral Modeling stage, LLM-inferred user personas provide global guidance for cross-domain behavioral modeling, while local semantic associations enhance intra-domain behavioral modeling, enabling unified behavioral patterns to be learned across domains. In the Domain Preference Adaptation stage, joint predictions are refined with domain-specific ones to better adapt to domain preferences and improve performance. Extensive experiments demonstrate the superiority of PerCDR over state-of-the-art methods, and further analyses validate the effectiveness of the proposed model. Early studies primarily establish cross-domain transfer through collaborative signals. Representative methods include $ $-Net~, which learns shared user preferences via cross-domain transfer modules, C$^2$DSR~, which models cross-domain item co-occurrences using graph neural networks, and Tri-CDR~, which captures global interests from mixed-domain behavioral sequences through contrastive learning. To further facilitate cross-domain transfer, recent studies have introduced textual side information into CDSR. Representative methods such as UniSRec~ and CTT~ leverage pre-trained language models to enrich item representations for transferability, while HGTL~ further incorporates category semantics to strengthen structural modeling. These studies demonstrate the effectiveness of textual information in CDSR and motivate the subsequent exploration of richer semantic knowledge with LLMs. Large Language Models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in semantic understanding and reasoning, driving rapid advances in SR~. The second employs LLMs to enhance SR models by generating semantic representations or auxiliary supervision, , SAID~, LLMEmb~, LLM-ESR~, and CLLMRec~. Recent studies have further incorporated LLMs into CDSR. Representative methods either generate semantically related pseudo items, , LLMCDSR~ and LLM-EDT~, or enhance cross-domain semantic representations, , LLM4CDSR~ and SemaCDR~. Despite their differences, these methods consistently employ LLMs for item-centric semantic bridging.
