In this work, we study a novel problem of structured knowledge retrieval under rule-based constraints. We introduce CoRE, a structured knowledge retrieval framework that jointly learns rule routing and rule-constraining retrieval without explicit query–rule supervision. CoRE treats rule selection as a latent decision and employs alternating-based bi-level optimization to jointly specialize rule-constraining retrievers and optimize the routing agent through retrieval feedback. Experiments demonstrate that CoRE consistently outperforms existing baselines, while ablation studies confirm the complementary importance of routing-agent optimization and rule-constraining retriever adaptation. Analysis of the optimization dynamics further reveals how routing and retrieval progressively co-adapt through iterative feedback. Our work lies at the intersection of structured knowledge retrieval and constrained agentic reasoning, while additionally addressing the joint adaptation of agent policies and reasoning-constrained retrieval tools. Accordingly, we review related work from three perspectives: Structured knowledge retrieval, Constrained agentic reasoning for retrieval, and Co-adaptation of agents and tools. Early structured retrieval methods primarily improve how textual and relational signals are combined. MFAR~ adaptively aggregates retrieval scores across structured fields, while KAR~ augments retrieval with LLM-generated query expansions and relation filtering. More recent methods introduce richer structural reasoning. MoR~ generates a planning graph and combines structural traversal with textual matching, whereas HybGRAG~ and AvaTaR~ use LLM feedback to refine retrieval strategies or tool use. These methods increasingly adapt how structure is used, but the structural strategy itself is typically generated or optimized separately from the retriever. Our method instead treats rule selection as latent and jointly learns which structural rule to use and how to retrieve under that rule from downstream retrieval feedback. A complementary line of work moves structural reasoning from retrieval design into the LLM policy itself. RoG~ first generates relation paths as explicit reasoning plans and then retrieves along those paths. ToG~ makes this process interactive by selecting relations and entities during graph exploration. RoE~ and GraphDancer~ further learn such exploration policies through supervised and reinforcement learning. This progression shifts from predefined retrieval structure to learned traversal decisions, but also requires the LLM to perform low-level graph exploration. Our method separates the two levels: a routing agent selects a schema-valid reasoning rule, while a specialized retriever executes that rule and ranks the reachable evidence. The router is trained directly from retrieval outcomes rather than gold reasoning paths or query--rule labels. Recent self-improving agents~ go beyond optimizing a single policy by adapting interacting components in a feedback loop~. CoEvolve~ couples agent learning with an evolving training distribution, WebEvolver~ co-adapts a web agent and world model, and Tool-R0~ jointly improves a task generator and tool-using solver. Our work brings this co-adaptation principle to structured retrieval.
