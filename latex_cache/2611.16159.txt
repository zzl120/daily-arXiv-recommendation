Generative Recommendation, User Behavior Trajectory Prediction {motivation.pdf} Recommender systems play an important role in helping users discover relevant items from large catalogs~. Personalized recommendation mainly needs to learn what users like from their past interactions. Since these interactions unfold over time, sequential recommendation has been developed to learn from historical user–item interactions and predict the next item a user is likely to interact with, yielding remarkable success in both academia and industry~. Recent advances in large language models ~ have popularized generative recommendation , shifting recommendation from scoring items to generating their identifiers. A prominent approach represents items as Semantic IDs and autoregressively generates their constituent tokens conditioned on the user's interaction history~. This formulation brings user preference modeling and item prediction into a unified sequence generation framework. Despite these advances, most sequential and generative recommendation methods remain focused on next item prediction~. While effective for anticipating an immediate interaction, this objective provides only a limited view of how user behavior unfolds over time. Understanding and predicting a user's subsequent course of actions is important for anticipating related needs and enabling more proactive recommendation. For example, a user may purchase running shoes, followed by sports socks and a water bottle, as illustrated in Fig.~ . Predicting only the next purchase captures one part of this progression, whereas forecasting the sequence reveals how related behaviors follow one another. User Behavior Trajectory Prediction aims to capture this broader progression by forecasting an ordered sequence of future interactions from a user's history~. Unlike a next item ranked list, whose items are alternative candidates for a single future interaction, a predicted trajectory contains behaviors at successive future positions. Recent work has begun to explore this direction. LPDO~ employs diffusion with listwise preference optimization to model future trajectories. MaskGR~ explores multi-item recommendation through masked diffusion over SIDs. EchoRec~ combines supervision from future interactions with cycle-consistent preference alignment to enhance generative recommendation. GCB~ extends SID-based GR to trajectory prediction, autoregressively generating the semantic tokens of successive items so that earlier generated behaviors provide context for later predictions. However, extending SID-based generation from a single item to a behavior trajectory also exposes limitations in decoding, information propagation, and learning. Token-autoregressive decoding intertwines within-item SID generation with across-item behavior transitions. Passing selected items forward without explicit predictive uncertainty feedback leaves useful information about alternative behaviors behind. Moreover, teacher-forced likelihood training learns to continue observed behavior prefixes, whereas inference requires the model to continue its own predictions. We identify the following three key limitations. Serial decoding uses the same autoregressive process to construct each item's semantic code and move between successive items~. This requires multiple dependent decoding steps per behavior and leaves transitions between complete interactions implicit in the token sequence. Autoregressive methods typically pass selected items to later predictions~, although these intermediate behaviors have not yet been observed. In Fig.~ , the behavior at $t+2$ should be evaluated by its current accuracy, the collaborative transition from $t+1$, and its contribution to predicting $t+3$. Yet likelihood supervision under observed prefixes does not directly evaluate these generated transitions or assign later prediction rewards to earlier choices. To address these limitations, we propose , a behavior-aware generative recommendation framework that aligns generation, feedback, and learning with successive user behaviors. To resolve the granularity mismatch, we build a on OPQ tokenization and parallel SID prediction. At each step, parallel SID predictions define a distribution over items, and a recurrent behavioral state advances once per item interaction. Joint SID and item-level supervision learns semantic representations and ordered behavior dependencies. To retain predictive uncertainty during trajectory generation, we introduce . This mechanism combines the selected item's representation with a candidate summary weighted by both predicted probabilities and similarity to the selected item. The resulting feedback embedding updates the behavioral state, allowing subsequent predictions to use both the selected behavior and information about its plausible alternatives. To bridge the training–inference gap in trajectory generation, we introduce . After supervised initialization, the model generates trajectories conditioned on its own predictions. Current candidates are evaluated using observed future targets and directed collaborative transition signals, while later credit assigns subsequent prediction quality to earlier choices. These signals jointly optimize the SID predictor and behavior-state transitions, enabling behavior-level dependencies to be reflected throughout the generation process. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: We integrate parallel SID prediction with recurrent behavior state updates, trained under joint SID and item-level supervision. We pass the selected item and a probability-weighted summary of related candidates to the next step, allowing subsequent predictions to use information about current alternatives. We combine current candidate evaluation with later credit assignment on model-generated prefixes, learning how each prediction follows preceding behaviors and supports later forecasts. We conduct extensive experiments on four public datasets and demonstrate strong predictive performance against competitive baselines. Generative recommendation produces item identifiers conditioned on user history. Semantic IDs represent an item with several discrete codes, allowing different items to share semantic components~. TIGER~ constructs hierarchical SIDs through residual quantization and autoregressively generates the codes of the next item. Subsequent work improves how these identifiers incorporate recommendation signals. LC-Rec~ aligns language and collaborative semantics through learned item indices and alignment tasks, while LETTER~ incorporates semantic, collaborative, and code-diversity regularization into item tokenization. RPG~ addresses the cost of sequential code generation by constructing SIDs with optimized product quantization and predicting their code positions in parallel. Other studies introduce collaborative guidance, interaction timing, and future behavior supervision into SID-based recommendation. TCBSA~ projects item scores from a discriminative recommender onto SID prefixes to guide beam selection at test time, keeping both models frozen. ChronoSID~ introduces time-gap supervision into item representation learning and interleaves historical time-gap tokens with SIDs to account for elapsed time between interactions. EchoRec~ connects prediction branches across future positions and uses cycle-consistent preference alignment to learn from multiple future interactions. GCB~ applies SID generation to predict behavior trajectories. Building on these developments, we study how SID-based generative recommendation can guide the transition between predicted behaviors and support learning from the trajectories. User behavior trajectory prediction forecasts several successive interactions conditioned on an observed history~. Each output position corresponds to a future interaction, making both positional prediction accuracy and dependencies between successive behaviors relevant to the task. LPDO~ develops a diffusion-based approach and incorporates listwise preference supervision into the prediction of future interaction sequences. MaskGR~ also explores multi-item recommendation through masked diffusion over SIDs. EchoRec~ combines supervision from future interactions with cycle-consistent preference alignment to enhance generative recommendation. GCB~ models future interactions as a concatenated SID sequence and trains its autoregressive generator with teacher-forced token likelihood, using preceding item tokens as context for subsequent predictions. develops a unified behavior-level formulation for trajectory generation, feedback, and learning. It separates parallel SID prediction within an item from state updates between interactions. Each update receives the selected item together with a probability-weighted summary of its alternatives, allowing the current prediction distribution to inform subsequent predictions. Beyond supervised learning from observed trajectories, we optimize trajectories generated from the model's own previous predictions. Multi-item recommendation includes several tasks with different output units. Next-basket methods predict the products associated with the next shopping event. ReCANet~ explicitly models users' repeat-purchase patterns, while CASE~ models item-specific repurchase cycles in calendar time and uses set attention to identify items likely to be purchased again. Although these methods use temporal information from historical purchases, their target is a set of items within the next basket, without a prescribed interaction order among those items. Session-wise recommendation and list- or page-level reranking organize multiple items into a recommendation output. OneRec~ generates session-wise video lists for a user request and refines them through preference alignment. APCR~ uses LLM-based pairwise comparisons against a reference item and position-aware reranking to refine recommendation lists. PAR~ performs page-level reranking by considering interactions within and across lists, together with the spatial relationships among displayed items. These methods aim to improve the quality of a recommended list or page. UBTP defines its output as a sequence of future user interactions. Each output position predicts the item at a specific future interaction. These interactions need not form a single basket or served session. The prediction target therefore differs from basket membership or page placement: UBTP forecasts which items the user will interact with and in what temporal order. In , intermediate items are predictions made before the corresponding interactions are observed, and they provide context for subsequent forecasts. We accordingly learn how each prediction follows the preceding behavior and how it supports accurate predictions of later interactions. We compare with 11 baseline methods and two additional variants, SASRec-M and RPG-M, covering conventional sequential recommendation, diffusion-based recommendation, generative recommendation, and user behavior trajectory prediction. FPMC~ combines matrix factorization with first-order Markov transitions. SASRec~ uses self-attention to model interaction histories, while STOSA~ incorporates stochastic representations of item transitions. DCRec~ augments sequential representations with contrastive learning. Among diffusion-based methods, DiffuRec~ reconstructs target-item representations through history-conditioned reverse diffusion, and PreferDiff~ incorporates personalized ranking signals into diffusion training. LPDO~ formulates UBTP as diffusion-based trajectory generation with listwise preference supervision. For generative recommendation, TIGER~ represents items with residual-quantized semantic IDs and autoregressively generates the next item's SID. RPG~ uses unordered semantic IDs to support parallel token prediction within an item. EchoRec~ uses future behaviors as supervision and aligns holistic preference representations with the decoding representations. GCB~ represents items with discrete semantic IDs and autoregressively generates a sequence of future behaviors. FPMC, STOSA, DCRec, DiffuRec, PreferDiff, SASRec, RPG, TIGER, and EchoRec retain their model-specific training objectives and are adapted to the $K$-step UBTP task. By coupling behavioral state updates with item-anchored probability feedback, the framework carries information from selected items and predictive distributions across future positions. Relation-aware learning on model-generated trajectories combines position accuracy and directed collaborative transition rewards with credit assigned to earlier choices for later prediction quality. Experiments on four public datasets show improvements in position-wise ranking and trajectory coverage, with ablation studies supporting the contributions of the proposed components.
