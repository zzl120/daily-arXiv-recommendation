state-of-the art efficiency-effectiveness trade-offs for neural retrieval by enabling document representations to be pre-computed and efficiently indexed. Yet, their relatively simple interaction mechanisms, typically MaxSim, can limit their effectiveness compared to cross-encoders. MICE, a recently introduced re-ranking architecture, aims to combine cross-encoder-like effectiveness through expressive transformer-based scoring with reduced inference-time computation through document-side pre-computation. However, no practical way to store these document representations was explored. We address this limitation here and introduce , the first end-to-end retrieval pipeline with MICE, based on PLAID to index the contextualized document token representations. We conduct a controlled comparison of MICE, ColBERT and cross-encoders by training comparable models using the Ettin Family under matched experimental conditions. Our results show that defines a new class of retrieval architecture with an efficiency–effectiveness trade-off of its own: it matches late-interaction retrieval throughput while retaining the effectiveness of cross-encoders. Multi-stage ranking pipelines balance retrieval efficiency and effectiveness by combining an first-stage retriever with a more but less efficient re-ranker . Recent neural retrieval models offer more favorable efficiency--effectiveness trade-offs. Late-interaction models such as ColBERT pre-compute document token representations and rely on specialized indexes such as PLAID for efficient storage, candidate generation, and retrieval. This makes them well-suited as end-to-end retrieval systems. However, they still tend to be less effective than cross-encoders . This leaves a gap between the expressive but costly scoring of cross-encoders and the efficient but more constrained interactions of late-interaction models. Several architectures have explored intermediate designs between independently encoded retrieval models and fully joint cross-encoders. Mid-fusion approaches such as PreTTR and MORES process queries and documents separately in early layers and jointly in later ones. Starting from a cross-encoder, MICE follows this direction and aims to improve efficiency while largely retaining effectiveness by ~removing superfluous interactions from the costly self-attention mechanism , and ~enabling the pre-computation of document representations. It is composed of two parts: the , where document and query representations are contextualized independently, and the , where frozen document representations interact with the query to detect matching signals. By doing so, it preserves richer interactions than MaxSim while reducing inference-time computation further than mid-fusion approaches by limiting those interactions. More recently, the ``Fast But Not Late'' KaLM-Reranker similarly pre-computes document-side representations before introducing query--document interactions at scoring time, but within an encoder-decoder architecture , thus less suited for the IR task than an encoder-only . MICE's pre-computed document representations make indexed retrieval possible in principle. The original work~, however, only studies re-ranking, and analyze efficiency only theoretically, leaving open how to store and search its fine-grained representations over a full collection. These challenges have been explored for late-interaction systems, through vector compression as in ColBERTv2 , or product quantization . PLAID combines compressed multi-vector storage with centroid-based candidate generation and pruning. As its retrieval procedure is tightly coupled to MaxSim, it is unclear whether the same indexing can be transferred to MICE. We explore this issue and introduce by adapting MICE to use PLAID, enabling efficient storage of its fine-grained document representations. We also revisit MICE training with recent training strategies and successfully learn ModernBERT-based MICE models. Together, these contributions establish as a new class of retrieval architecture that offers a more favorable efficiency–effectiveness trade-off than late-interaction scorers. Specifically, we ask the following questions: Can we build an efficient MICE-based end-to-end retriever? Can MICE benefit from ModernBERT and match the effectiveness of comparable cross-encoder and late-interaction models? Our code, configurations and all checkpoints are released at { \ }. addresses two limitations of MICE . First, we turn theoretical gains from document pre-computation into reality by introducing a low-dimensional bottleneck that maps MICE's contextualized document representations to a representational space compatible with PLAID. We show that this does not impact the effectiveness of the interaction layers. Second, we develop a transfer strategy that starts from an already trained cross-encoder and progressively adapts it to the MICE architecture. This allows ModernBERT-based MICE models to retain the effectiveness of their cross-encoder counterparts. Even if PLAID targets MaxSim-based models such as ColBERT , not attention-based interaction as in MICE , we can use its residual format for compressing and storing documents representations. To limit the size of the vectors to store, we insert a bottleneck on the contextualized document representations: a linear projection to dimension 128 , followed by LayerNorm for normalization . The indexed 128-d vectors are then decompressed and a linear up-projection restores the original model dimension for the interaction layers, without significative drop in effectiveness . While representations are stored using the ColBERTv2 residual format , we bypass PLAID's inverted-index retrieval pipeline and only decompress candidate representations for the interaction layers. thus still relies on a distinct first-stage retriever, DistilBERT-SPLADEv3 , to retrieve the top passages for re-ranking.
