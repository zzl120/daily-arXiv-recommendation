Search agents, enterprise search, retrieval-augmented generation, conversational context, evidence delivery, abstention An employee asks an internal search assistant to check a refund rule after the assistant gave the wrong answer. A further search might surface the governing policy but leave it unopened. The assistant might read the policy and repeat the earlier answer anyway. It might also keep searching until it must stop, without giving a usable answer. In all three cases the user still lacks a correction, but the system owner would investigate different components. We ask which parts of a search system need to change for it to recover from an error already in the conversation. We use BrowseComp-Plus , a fixed public corpus with document-level evidence judgments. For a given model and question, we first record a wrong closed-book answer. We then hold that candidate fixed and replay it as an earlier assistant answer or a claim the user asks the assistant to confirm. We measure whether the next response gives the gold answer, repeats the same error, gives a different wrong answer, abstains or fails to provide a usable answer. The design controls evidence at two points. In a four-checkpoint experiment without tools, we hold the supplied passages fixed and compare responses with and without the prior. Varying the passage set also lets us compare the prior's persistence when the gold answer appears in a benchmark source. In a separate experiment with two search agents, we place a gold page first in the results or put the decisive passage directly into context. We record document exposure, passage delivery, and whether the agent stops and answers. The rank-placement arm tests whether listing a source is enough to make its contents available. The delivery arms test what happens after bypassing source discovery and opening. In the paired depth comparison under a wrong hint, returning 20 results per search instead of five increased gold-document recall by 8.6 percentage points. Accuracy changed by an estimated 0.5 points (95\ selected search tasks, yet one agent still often searched to its limit without answering. With search disabled and the exact same answer-bearing passages supplied in both conditions, a confident prior cost one checkpoint 16.1 accuracy points. These are distinct ways an error can survive changes to a search system. The contribution is a set of paired tests for telling them apart when the error originates in the model itself. Sycophancy studies measure a model's tendency to agree with a user . Work on knowledge conflicts varies disagreement between retrieved passages and parametric knowledge, introduces plausible misleading passages, or probes how models resolve a conflict . Search-augmented systems also face contradictions between sources . Here the wrong candidate comes from the model's earlier answer and appears in dialogue. The contrast is how a model responds to the candidate when we vary source evidence separately, including supplying the same passages with and without the conversational claim. Supplying a decisive passage directly changed accuracy in both agents. These interventions help distinguish a ranking or discovery problem from a document access or passage-selection problem, without inferring from the trace how much the model relied on any given source. The supplied-evidence experiment isolates sensitivity to the dialogue given fixed passages. Nemotron often repeated the prior. Under user insistence, gpt-oss lost more correct answers to abstention than to repetition. The search intervention identifies a distinct failure: Nemotron sometimes exhausted its search budget without returning an answer after receiving a decisive passage. Evaluations should report correction, abstention, and non-completion separately, using all episodes as the denominator. We propose an evaluation protocol for internal corpora based on these interventions. The public benchmark measures the diagnostic stages. Internal evaluations also require labels for which documents and versions the caller can access. For each question, establish the authorized supporting passages and contrast runs with and without an earlier wrong answer. Rerun the pair with controlled search-result placement and direct passage delivery. Keep the index snapshot, permissions, tools, decoding settings and answer parser fixed across the compared checkpoints. Candidates generated independently by each model are useful for stress testing its own prior errors. A common candidate is needed to compare responses to the same claim. A trace should record queries, ranked document identifiers, opened versions, the exact passages inserted in context, termination reason and the final answer. With those records, a team can use the following measurements to decide which component to investigate: Measure recall of authorized supporting documents and inspect query generation, indexing or ranking when they are absent from the results. Measure how often listed sources are opened and how often the relevant sections survive extraction, chunk selection and the context budget. On cases with an adequate passage in context, compare accuracy, wrong-prior adoption and abstention with and without the earlier claim. Record explicit abstention, round-limit and token-limit exits, unparsable responses and tool errors. Evaluate any stopping or fallback policy against both answer quality and search cost. A system can correctly omit a passage under the caller's access rights. The retrieval denominator therefore comprises the annotated sources the caller is authorized to access. The complete evidence arm requires the answer string in a relevance-annotated source. It does not establish that every question has a complete multi-hop proof in the assembled windows. Neutral or partial windows may support an indirect inference. The paired correct-baseline analysis establishes that a particular checkpoint answered correctly once with the same evidence and no hint. Screening depends on model-specific errors, and the four checkpoints differ in their eligible question sets and candidate answers. With one sample per cell, our intervals describe variation across questions and do not separately estimate decoding variance. The checkpoints, quantization and serving settings limit generalization to model families. The insisting-user condition changes speaker, wording and the request for confirmation. Within-question pairing controls question and passage differences. The reported estimates do not account for possible run-order effects. Supplied-evidence outcomes use model judges and a normalized string-match sensitivity analysis. The abstention split is an exploratory rule based on recognizable wording and can miss unusual phrasings. A refusal to confirm the candidate can count as an abstention in the insisting condition. Adoption conditional on commitment is descriptive, since the hint also changes the probability of committing. Qwen3.5 had the highest failure share without evidence , mostly due to the output cap without a parsable answer. The observed search traces link adoption to whether particular pages were retrieved or opened. They do not identify what the model inferred from a preview or another source. The intervention uses small, selected question sets and machine-reviewed passages. Its scoring and sampling configuration differs from the observed runs. Direct delivery controls which passage is in context and changes both the placement and content of evidence relative to the ranking arm. It cannot establish what evidence the model relied on. Neither evidence sufficiency nor answer labels received independent human validation in this study. The corpus is a fixed public benchmark, so it provides no direct evidence about internal permissions or conflicting policy versions. Recovery from an earlier error is an end-to-end property of a search system. An evaluation can test whether the supporting source was retrieved, whether the passage reached the model, and whether the model returned a corrected answer.
