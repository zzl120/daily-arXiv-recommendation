Finding such judgments manually from an extensive collection of judgments is time consuming and dependent on how fa miliar the reviewer is with earlier cases. Prior case retrieval is hence a practical information retrieval problem that is not easily amenable to solution through mere keyword matching. This task has several challenges. First, Indian judgments tend to be lengthy, repetitive and contain different kinds of content in one document like facts of the case, parties’ arguments, statute, prior cases cited, reasoning and the decision itself. A prior case that shares the same statute with the query can be legally irrelevant. An other prior case can express the same legal idea but in a completely different way. In this research we aim to study how various retrieval and reranking techniques could be integrated for improving the prior case retrieval in the context of Indian Supreme Court decisions. This study will explore how lexical, semantic, and rhetorical role based evidence can contribute to the task of ranking relevant cases. In the field of legal text processing numerous papers have been presented in top tier journals, conferences and workshops in NLP and artificial intelligence. Before the application of neural networks,there were lexical matching approaches in classical NLP to solve information retrieval tasks. Previous works in Prior Case Retrieval as in used feature based models such as SVM . formalized the Probabilistic Relevance Framework , which ranks documents by estimating their probability of relevance to a query. From this framework, they derive BM25, a highly effective term weighting model, along with extensions such as BM25F that incorporate document structure and multiple fields. The work also examines relevance feedback, parameter optimization, and contrasts PRF with alternative retrieval models, establishing BM25 as a robust baseline in information retrieval. The FIRE 2019 AILA track also focused on building a framework for computing different methods to retrieve relevant prior cases. Participants at COLIEE 2021 have shown that BM25 algorithm is a strong baseline for the retrieval task and majority of the participants have used BM25, XG-boost combined with other techniques like TF-IDF, Large Language models or transformer methods like BERT. TLIRat COLIEE 2021 adopted a two stage legal case retrieval framework combining lexical retrieval with neural reranking. In the first stage, BM25/TF–IDF was used to retrieve a high recall candidate set. In the second stage, the candidates were reranked using BERT-PLI , which models the relevance between query and candidate cases by capturing entailment style semantic relationships, leading to improved discrimination of legally relevant precedents. Prior case retrieval was addressed by explicitly extracting evidential sentences from court judgments that capture legally salient facts and reasoning. Instead of matching full documents, the approach ranks candidate cases based on the similarity between evidence extracted from the query case and evidence from prior cases, improving retrieval precision. We call the relationship between the set of facts of the query case and the Ratio of a particular prior case "Facts to Ratio support." It should not be interpreted as textual entailment in the traditional natural language inference way. On the contrary, this is the statement of whether the principle/reasoning in the Ratio provides any support for the set of facts in the query case. Query side input reflects the conditions behind the conflict, while candidate side input is the legal rationale or doctrine that has been considered previously in the case. The hypothesis suggests that the more a candidate case’s Ratio Of The Decision explains the conditions reflected in the query, the higher the reranking score should be for that candidate case. The role supervision dataset for Phase 3 was created to help detect which rhetorical role comparison really signifies relevance, as opposed to a rhetorical role comparison which is only a surface level look alike. The role supervision dataset consists of two types of query-candidate pairs. This includes 807 query judgments, with 5,184 positive pairs and 5,181 hard negative pairs. Positive pairs were retrieved directly from official relevance judgments, where a positive pair is a query and its candidate, which are part of the official relevance judgment, while hard negatives were retrieved by first searching for candidates using role based BM25, followed by picking the top ranking candidates that are not included in the official relevance judgment. It involves four sequential steps, including role aware candidate retrieval, sliding window legal entailment reranking, signed role aware ranking, and validation frozen rank fusion. Role aware candidate retrieval aims at obtaining the high recall set of candidates by means of utilizing several BM25 channels. Semantic consistency between Facts of the query and Ratio of the Decision of the candidate is ensured in the second step. Contribution of separate rhetorical roles is captured at the third step as signed contribution. The fourth step implies integration of the outputs of the two previous steps into a unified ranking. Stage one involves role based candidate retrieval over the entire corpus of cases using eleven BM25 channels. These channels include full text and rhetorically motivated rhetorical role interactions. The results from all the channels are then combined using rank fusion to generate a diverse pool of 600 candidates. During the construction of the final pipeline, there are eleven retrieval channels generated using the sparse BM25 model for the whole corpus which has been tagged with roles. The first retrieval channel will be generated using the concatenation of the whole text, and the remaining retrieval channels will be created based on the rhetorical relations that arise due to legal considerations: Fact–Fact, Fact–Ratio, and Fact–RulingByPresentCourt, Argument–Argument and Argument–Ratio, Precedent–Precedent and Precedent–Ratio, Ratio–Ratio, Statute–Statute and Statute–Ratio. Each channel retains its top 600 candidates. Rather than directly adding BM25 scores across channels, the final pipeline applies rank based fusion, since BM25 score ranges can differ substantially across role specific vocabularies. For a candidate c in channel j, the fusion score is computed as shown in Equation SRRF = _{j} {K + r_j }, where $r_j $ denotes the rank of candidate c in channel j, w j is the corresponding channel weight, and k=60 is used during first stage selection. This stage therefore combines full text lexical retrieval with role specific lexical evidence, producing a unified candidate pool for the subsequent semantic and role aware ranking stages. This appendix defines the full set of metrics used consistently across every phase of this study. For a query $q$ with ranked list $ _q$, reciprocal rank is defined as RR = {r_q} where $r_q$ is the rank position of the first officially relevant candidate in $ _q$. Mean Reciprocal Rank is the average of $RR $ over all queries: MRR = {|Q|} _{q Q} {r_q} MRR is sensitive only to the position of the first relevant result and is used throughout this study as a quick indicator of whether a system surfaces at least one correct candidate early. For a cutoff $K$, precision and recall at $K$ are computed per query as P@K = R_q|}{K}, R@K = R_q|}{|R_q|} where $ _q^{ }$ denotes the top $K$ candidates in the ranked list and $R_q$ is the officially relevant set for query $q$. These are reported either per query or macro averaged across queries, and are used primarily to characterize candidate pool recall at intermediate pipeline stages . Average Precision for a single query averages precision at each rank position where a relevant candidate appears: AP = {|R_q|} _{k \,:\, c_k R_q} P@k MAP is the mean of $AP $ over all queries. Unlike MRR, MAP accounts for the position of every relevant candidate in the ranking, not just the first. For binary relevance, Discounted Cumulative Gain at rank $K$ is DCG@K = _{i=1}^{K} { _2 } where $rel_i \{0,1\}$ indicates whether the candidate at rank $i$ is officially relevant. nDCG@K normalizes this by the ideal DCG obtainable at that cutoff, $IDCG@K $, the DCG of the best possible ordering: nDCG@K = {IDCG@K } nDCG rewards relevant candidates appearing earlier in the ranking more heavily than those appearing later, and is reported as a mean across queries. These are the primary metrics used for all official evaluation in this study, computed by the frozen evaluator described in the main text. Unlike the per query metrics above, micro averaged metrics aggregate hits and denominators across the entire query set before computing a ratio, rather than averaging per query ratios: P_{ }@K = | _q^{ } R_q|}{ _{q} | _q^{ }|}, R_{ }@K = | _q^{ } R_q|}{ _{q} |R_q|}, F1_{ }@K = @K R_{ }@K}{P_{ }@K + R_{ }@K} Because these are computed as ratios of pooled sums rather than as an average of per query scores, queries with more officially relevant candidates contribute proportionally more to the final metric. This micro aggregated Precision/Recall/F1 at the frozen cutoff is treated as the single official measure of system quality throughout this study; all other metrics are reported as supporting context. Used only in internal group ranking evaluation of intermediate neural rankers, not as an official metric. In addition to the ranking metrics above, two intermediate components --- a role combination classifier and an early cross-encoder --- are evaluated at the level of binary pair classification rather than per query ranking, using standard ROC-AUC, binary F1, and Average Precision . These metrics characterize how well a component separates relevant from non relevant pairs in isolation and are not directly comparable to the per query ranking metrics above; they are reported only where they originate, and are never substituted for official Micro F1 in any final comparison. Where both are reported, macro averaged metrics and micro averaged metrics are explicitly distinguished, since the two can diverge substantially --- a query with a large number of officially relevant candidates influences a micro metric far more than a macro metric. All results labeled in this study use the micro aggregated convention, matching the evaluator used for the final reported result.
