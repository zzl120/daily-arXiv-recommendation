The proposed dual-granularity raw feature construction relies on generative models to produce coarse-grained textual and visual references. Although these generated features are used only as auxiliary item-side information, they may inherit biases or inaccuracies from the underlying generators. We therefore keep the original item features as the fine-grained source, use generated information only as a complementary view, and empirically examine whether the improvement comes from dual-granularity modeling rather than replacing original information with generated content. DIGCN comprises three core components: a dual-granularity raw feature construction module that leverages the multimodal understanding capabilities of MLLMs to generate coarse-grained raw features, which are combined with the original fine-grained raw features to form dual-granularity representations; an intermediate fusion graph convolutional network equipped with dynamic cross-modality weight correction, which performs layer-wise modality fusion during the message-passing process to capture inter-modality features while preserving modality-specific features; and an item-item semantic graph that enhances item representations through neighbor aggregation based on pre-computed feature similarities. With the growing diversity of information types on social media, multimodal information has been increasingly utilized to address the data sparsity problem in recommender systems . Early work, such as VBPR , incorporated visual content into matrix factorization to alleviate sparsity issues. Subsequent works extended this approach by integrating both visual and textual modalities, further enriching item representations and improving recommendation performance. Leveraging graph-based architectures, MMGCN pioneered the use of GCNs to extract modality-specific features from user-item interactions. Later models, such as DualGNN and LATTICE , introduced user-user and item-item graphs to better capture shared patterns in user preferences and item relationships. Building on these advancements, FREEDOM improved stability by freezing the item semantic graph and reducing noise in the user-item bipartite graph, while LOBSTER introduced global factors to capture cross-modal shared features. Despite these advancements, multimodal recommender systems still face two major challenges: low-quality representation learning and modality fusion . For representation learning, current approaches focus on multi-granularity representations. Specifically, MambaRec constructs multi-scale representations to capture fine-grained semantic patterns, while HPMRec employs hypercomplex embeddings with multi-components to enhance the diversity of multimodal features. For modality fusion, contrastive learning has become a common strategy for aligning multimodal representations. Specifically, MMSSL and MICRO utilize contrastive learning to align multimodal signals with collaborative information, improving performance without extensive labeled data. BM3 further explores inter-modal relationships to enhance recommendation accuracy, and MENTOR introduces a multi-level self-supervised task to align modalities without compromising user-item interactions. However, we argue that learning multi-granularity representations remains challenging due to inherent data sparsity, and alignment-only modality fusion may over-compress valuable modality-specific information. Therefore, we suggest to and propose DIGCN, which leverages large language models to construct dual-granularity raw features, avoiding extra training difficulty, and introduces an intermediate fusion GCN to achieve high-quality modality fusion. The proliferation of online information has created an overload of content, making effective recommender systems critical to navigating this complexity . Traditional recommender systems predominantly infer user preference through historical interaction data . However, their performance is fundamentally limited by the data sparsity problem, as inferring comprehensive user and item profiles from sparse interactions remains a significant challenge. To mitigate this limitation, recent works have increasingly turned to multimodal recommender systems, leveraging rich auxiliary data from explicitly multimodal information to enrich representations and alleviate data sparsity problem . Building upon multimodal data, existing multimodal recommender systems nevertheless face two persistent bottlenecks : low-quality representation learning and modality fusion. , current solutions to multi-granularity representation learning struggle to acquire sufficiently nuanced and discriminative features, primarily due to the intrinsic data sparsity in recommendation scenarios, which provides inadequate supervisory signal for training such complex representations . , current fusion strategies often rely on contrastive learning to align representations across modalities . While promoting consistency, alignment-only objectives may over-compress modality-specific information by minimizing inter-modal distance, thereby weakening unique, discriminative features that are crucial for recommendation. These two limitations, ineffective representation learning and information loss during fusion, work together to slow down further progress of multimodal recommendations. We therefore rethink these two limitations from a foundational perspective. First, if multi-granularity representation learning increases training difficulty, could we to construct multi-granularity raw features before training phase, thereby obtaining the desired representations directly? The powerful multimodal understanding capabilities of Large Language Models offer a feasible and potent means to realize this goal . Second, given that alignment-only multimodal objectives may weaken modality-specific information, could we to capture cross-modality signals before obtaining final representations via late fusion, most advanced works adopt late fusion, integrating modalities after GCN.}? We use the message-passing stages in Graph Convolution Networks to realize this goal. To this end, we propose a ual-granularity raw feature empowered ntermediate fusion raph onvolutional etwork for multimodal recommendation. Specifically, DIGCN leverages the powerful multimodal understanding capabilities of LLMs to construct dual-granularity raw features based on original multimodal information. Compared to multi-granularity representation learning, our dual-granularity raw feature component offers better interpretability while reducing the extra training difficulty. Moreover, DIGCN introduces an intermediate fusion graph convolutional network equipped with dynamic cross-modality weight correction, designed to capture cross-modality signals and achieve effective modality fusion. Compared to relying only on contrastive alignment, our intermediate fusion graph convolutional network preserves modality-specific information while fusing multiple modalities during propagation. Notably, DIGCN demonstrates high extensibility, capable of further benefiting from additional contrastive learning-based alignment tasks. Comprehensive experiments on four real-world datasets validate that DIGCN consistently outperforms a variety of strong baselines. Additional analyses confirm its superiority under varying levels of data sparsity and cold-start scenarios.
