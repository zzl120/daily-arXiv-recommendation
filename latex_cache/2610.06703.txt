In this direction, we present , a two-phase cross-domain recommendation framework that personalizes music suggestions emotionally aligns them with the book being read. In the first phase, transformer-based sentiment embeddings are constructed from user reviews and mapped across domains via a Conditional Generative Adversarial Network, whose mask-conditioned generator handles missing sentiment components and injects stochasticity for richer preference transfer. A compact rating neural network then fuses sentiment-specific interaction scores with a collaborative filtering prior to predict music ratings. In the second phase, large language models classify each book into a valence--arousal emotional quadrant, and candidate tracks are filtered to match that quadrant. Experiments on both the English Amazon and Chinese Douban datasets show that SAGA-CDR achieves the best rating prediction accuracy on Amazon and the lowest RMSE on Douban , with ranking performance competitive with the strongest sentiment-aware baseline, even in cross-lingual settings. Cross-Domain Recommendation, Generative Adversarial Networks, Large Language Models, Sentiment Analysis, Emotion-Aware Recommendation Recommender systems operating within a single domain cannot leverage preferences expressed across different content types. Cross-domain recommendation addresses this shortcoming by transferring knowledge from a source domain, where user activity is abundant, to a target domain where data are sparse. Most CDR methods rely on structural signals such as shared latent factors or user-item interaction patterns, ignoring the rich emotional information embedded in user-generated text. In the books \& music case, emotional alignment is particularly well-motivated. Experimental evidence shows that emotionally congruent background music significantly enhances reading immersion and perceived text quality~. A RS for this scenario must therefore satisfy two simultaneous objectives: ~match individual user taste and ~align with the emotional tone of the book being read. In order to address these issues, we propose , a two-phase recommendation pipeline, whose main contributions are: A formulation of emotion-guided book-to-music recommendation as a two-phase task, combining cross-domain preference transfer with valence–arousal congruence between the book and the recommended track. A cross-domain mapping in which the deterministic bridge used by prior sentiment-aware CDR methods is replaced by a conditional GAN with a presence mask over sentiment partitions and noise injection, addressing missing partitions and one-to-many transfer mechanisms. An affective filtering stage in which LLMs assign books to VA quadrants, with the choice of LLM informed by a human study on cases where the models disagree. An empirical evaluation on two public benchmarks, including cross-lingual transfer from sparse English data to dense Chinese data, with post-hoc SHAP and ICE analysis. Finally, the paper concludes in Section~. Traditional CDR methods often focus on structural signals, learning a bridge function between independently trained embedding spaces. EMCDR trains separate Matrix Factorization models per domain and fits a shared multi-layered perceptron to translate source user embeddings to the target space, providing a cold-start profile for unseen users. PTUPCDR extends this approach with a meta-network that generates a personalised bridge function for each user, conditioned on a characteristic embedding derived from their transferable interests in the source-domain interaction history. More recently, HEAD leverages hyperbolic representations to model hierarchical user--item interactions while preserving power-law structures across domains. Meanwhile, CFAA aligns BERT-based cross-domain user and item embeddings through Optimal Transport and graph-based attribution matching to mitigate domain discrepancy. However, these methods operate purely on interaction data and metadata, overlooking the rich affective signals embedded in review text. While some models, like RC-DFM , inject raw review text into Stacked Denoising Autoencoders to bridge latent spaces, sentiment is not extracted explicitly. To enrich representations beyond interaction patterns, a second line of work incorporates sentiment or emotion extracted from user reviews. CDR-SAFM~ partitions reviews into three polarity classes using a rating-based heuristic, applies Latent Dirichlet Allocation to each class, and trains independent MLPs to map sentiment topic vectors across domains. STN-CDRS~ addresses data sparsity by converting unrated text reviews into pseudo-ratings using sentiment analysis from a recurrent neural network. CRAS~ uses VADER to discard negative reviews, retain only positive and neutral reviews for topic extraction, and train an improved CycleGAN to translate user preference matrices between domains. UTAM~ combines TextBlob and BERT for aspect-level sentiment, clustering users thematically and matching source-domain clusters to target-domain counterparts. Moving to richer affect representations, AAPAM~ extracts seven-dimensional emotion profiles via a Tweet Affective Classifier, identifying cross-domain user pairs by affective cosine similarity. ECDR~ builds six-dimensional emotion profiles derived from Parrott's hierarchical model, applying symmetric KL-divergence to identify emotionally similar target-domain users for collaborative filtering . More recently, an affect-centric stance has been adopted by certain approaches~, by mapping music and visual art to a shared VA space, matching items by sentiment proximity using contrastive multimodal learning on audio, visual, and LLM-generated text features. Beyond sentiment, personality-aware RSs employ user traits to mitigate the cold start efect, while responsible RSs prioritize the users' well-being and explainability over engagement . SAGA-CDR shares both aims, using review sentiment instead of personality and explaining its predictions via SHAP/ICE and LLM justifications . The literature on sentiment-aware CDR demonstrates a clear technological evolution from early lexicon-based tools and topic modeling, to deterministic MLPs, and finally to modern transformer-based architectures and multimodal learning. By utilizing a RoBERTa model fine-tuned on 124M tweets, we produce contextual 768-dimensional per-review embeddings that capture linguistic nuances missed by static LDA topics. Our main technical contribution is the replacement of deterministic bridges with a mask-conditioned stochastic Conditional GAN. In emotion-driven recommendation , the recommendation process is enhanced through emotional alignment between books and music. Book summaries are analyzed using LLMs to determine their dominant emotional tone within a VA framework. Music tracks are also categorized according to their emotional characteristics. The system then filters predicted recommendations by selecting music items that share the same emotional quadrant as books the user has enjoyed, thereby producing recommendations that are not only personalized but also emotionally consistent with the user's reading experience. In this work, we presented SAGA-CDR, a two-phase emotionally guided CDR framework for the books-to-music setting. The 1 phase combines contextual transformer-based sentiment representations with a stochastic, mask-conditioned CGAN cross-domain mapping and a bias-aware rating prediction module. The 2 phase uses an LLM to classify books into VA emotional quadrants and filters personalized music candidates to match the book's VA quadrant. Experiments demonstrated that SAGA-CDR excels on prediction accuracy and remains competitive on ranking metrics, while also proving to be highly robust under complex cross-lingual transfer conditions. Also, SHAP and ICE analyses confirm interpretable, domain-consistent behavior. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first framework to address personal taste and emotional alignment together in a books-to-music CDR setting. Several promising directions for future work emerge from this study. Firstly, transitioning from discrete emotional quadrants to a continuous VA space could capture fine-grained affective nuances, potentially allowing for chapter-level granularity that produces a dynamic soundtrack evolving alongside the narrative arc. On the audio front, incorporating pre-trained music understanding models to extract embeddings directly from raw waveforms would expand coverage beyond metadata-linked catalogues and provide richer affective estimates. Furthermore, although CGAN training converged stably here, diffusion models, which avoid adversarial optimization altogether , could provide a more stable mapping conditioned on source-domain embeddings. For future work, we will also conduct live user studies with a larger population to measure emotional congruence during real reading sessions; the present work establishes only quadrant agreement, since the subjectivity of affective judgments makes perceived congruence measurable only through a controlled study.
