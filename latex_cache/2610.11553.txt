HAC: $K d$.} Retrieving evidence from visually rich documents is fundamental to document search and Retrieval-Augmented Generation . Financial reports, scientific articles, and scanned records convey information through both text and the arrangement of tables, figures, and page elements. Conventional OCR-based retrieval converts pages into text through recognition, layout parsing, and chunking, adding preprocessing overhead while potentially losing visual relationships . For example, identifying a region whose operating margin rises each quarter requires associating bar heights with time and the legend; region names and axis labels alone are insufficient . {figs/vector_redesign_v2/fig0_motivation.pdf} The same query and constructed chart are routed through OCR, text chunking, and text retrieval, or native visual encoding and token-level MaxSim. The example shows how flattened text may preserve words and axis ticks while omitting bar heights and colour-to-series binding; the page image retains the rising Asia series as visual evidence.} Native vision--language encoders instead represent page images directly. Single-vector models, such as DSE and Qwen3-VL-Embedding , map queries and pages into a shared embedding space, avoiding OCR and yielding compact indexes. However, summarizing many distinct page regions in one vector can obscure local differences between otherwise similar pages. Multi-vector late-interaction models, exemplified by ColPali , retain separate patch representations and match query tokens to them through MaxSim , enabling finer alignment with visual evidence. Fine-grained matching nevertheless leaves a coupled learning and storage problem. Visually similar pages may provide different answers, while mined hard negatives may contain complete or partial answer evidence; treating them uniformly as negatives can suppress useful matches. At EVIE's configured maximum of 1024 image tokens per page, a million pages of 256-dimensional BF16 image vectors require approximately 488 GiB of vector payload. Narrowing vectors and merging tokens reduce this cost, but both can alter the local similarities and best-matching regions used by MaxSim. A model trained at one width therefore need not preserve its rankings at narrower widths, and token compression must retain local distinctions across queries. Using a compact index only for candidate retrieval does not remove the storage burden when reranking still relies on stored full token representations, and missed candidates remain unrecoverable. Accurate, compact retrieval thus requires reliable evidence supervision, representations trained across widths, and compressed tokens that directly support final relevance scoring. To address these challenges, we introduce , a family of native visual document retrievers combining evidence-judged supervision with compact multi-vector indexing. Its uses a multimodal judge to promote candidates with complete answer evidence to positives and exclude those with partial evidence from the negative loss. In our audit, these two categories account for 14.4\ reduces token counts through offline hierarchical agglomerative clustering. Position-only pooling may merge heterogeneous content, while purely semantic grouping may conflate similar elements in distant regions. The main contributions of this paper are as follows: We introduce evidence-judged supervision that grades mined pages by answer completeness, expands positive supervision, and excludes partial-evidence candidates from the negative loss. We develop a bidirectional teacher--student framework with Prefix-MRL, training six nested widths with retrieval and ranking objectives in one checkpoint without re-encoding. We propose HAC, separating spatially regularized cluster assignment from semantic centroid storage to support single-stage MaxSim retrieval over compressed page tokens. EVIE builds on visual document representation learning, retrieval supervision, and compact multi-vector indexing. Native visual document retrievers encode page images directly to preserve information that may be lost during text extraction. DSE and Qwen3-VL-Embedding produce a single vector per page, offering compact indexes. ColPali applies ColBERT-style late interaction to patch representations, enabling query-token matching to individual page regions. Jina-embeddings-v4 supports both single-vector and multi-vector outputs. NeMoRetriever-ColEmbed and Nemotron-ColEmbed-V2 extend multimodal late interaction with larger vision--language backbones, while ModernVBERT studies efficient bidirectional document encoding. MoCa investigates modality-aware continual pretraining for bidirectional multimodal embeddings. Universal encoders such as E5-V, GME, and UniME address broader multimodal retrieval, while the VLM2Vec models and associated MMEB benchmarks cover diverse tasks involving images, videos, and visual documents. EVIE follows the bidirectional late-interaction architecture and focuses on the accuracy--storage trade-off in page retrieval. Hard-negative mining strengthens contrastive retrieval training, but retrieved candidates may include unlabeled positives or partially relevant documents. ColBERTv2 combines mined negatives with cross-encoder supervision and KL distillation to improve training signals, while Unveil distills visual--textual knowledge into a visual document retriever. ColMate enriches contrastive training with masked text supervision linked to document regions. mmE5 emphasizes high-quality synthetic training data, while Embed-RL and TRACE explore reasoning-driven multimodal embedding learning. EVIE makes answer completeness an explicit criterion for candidate relabeling: a multimodal judge promotes full-evidence candidates to positives, excludes partial-evidence candidates from the negative loss, and retains no-evidence candidates as negatives. This supervision trains a bidirectional teacher whose query-to-document and document-to-query ranking distributions are subsequently transferred to the student. Evidence grading determines how mined candidates contribute to retrieval training, while teacher distillation supplies ranking supervision across embedding widths. Matryoshka Representation Learning trains nested embedding prefixes so that one representation supports multiple output dimensions. Qwen3-VL-Embedding applies this principle to multimodal embeddings, and ColNetraEmbed extends dimension flexibility to multi-vector document retrieval. In late interaction, truncation changes the token similarities used by MaxSim, making retrieval quality at each width a training concern. EVIE's Prefix-MRL therefore applies both retrieval supervision and bidirectional listwise distillation at every trained prefix width. A shared projection head serves six nested dimensions from 64 to 2048 without rerunning the backbone, allowing index width to be selected from one student checkpoint. Multi-vector index size can be reduced by compressing individual vectors or storing fewer vectors per document. ColBERTv2 uses residual compression to reduce storage per vector, and ColPali evaluates hierarchical mean token pooling to merge redundant patch representations. PLAID accelerates late-interaction retrieval through centroid interaction and pruning, illustrating that serving efficiency also depends on the search engine. ColParse constructs compact representations from layout-informed regions; CausalEmbed generates autoregressive latent tokens; and MetaEmbed varies the number of learned meta-tokens. HAC follows the token-pooling approach and uses 2D spatial regularization in hierarchical clustering to account for page layout when grouping semantically similar patches. Cluster assignments use joint semantic--spatial features, whereas stored centroids average only the original semantic vectors. These centroids directly support MaxSim scoring. Combined with Prefix-MRL, this permits independent choices of embedding width and token count without retaining an uncompressed index for reranking.
