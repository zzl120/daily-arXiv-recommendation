However, TCBSA at $B=20$ achieves 3.64\ Semantic-ID-based generative recommendation represents items as sequences of discrete tokens and predicts the next item through autoregressive decoding~. Subsequent work improves identifier construction and sequence modeling through collaborative semantics and textual identifiers~. Recent frameworks such as MiniOneRec~ further advance generative recommendation through scalable model training and preference alignment. At training time, collaborative and behavioral information is incorporated through learned identifiers, dual-stream modeling, and behavioral prior heads~. TCA4Rec projects collaborative logits into token distributions for soft supervision during generator training~. APAO and BEAR~ address the training--inference gap through prefix-aware optimization and beam-aware regularization, respectively. Other architectures combine semantic generation with dense representations or item-level scoring, as exemplified by COBRA~ and Gryphon~. At inference, SpecGR~ verifies retrieved candidates with the generator, while LIGER~ combines generative retrieval with dense candidate reranking. Recent methods also employ trained process reward models or value-aligned components to influence prefix search~. MemGen~ instead adaptively combines completed discriminative and generative recommendation lists. TCBSA operates at a different interface: it projects retrieval-relative item-score margins onto shared prefix--token edges and adapts the beam scores of a frozen generator before pruning. Beyond recommendation, decoding-time control reweights a frozen generator using auxiliary attribute scores~, constrains beam search to valid outputs~, or uses a stronger model to select among a weaker generator's top-$K$ token candidates~. More broadly, test-time adaptation adjusts model predictions using information available during inference. Classical approaches often update model parameters or normalization statistics from unlabeled test inputs~, while retrieval-augmented methods incorporate external information into instance-level predictions~. In recommendation, DT3OR and T2ARec perform test-time parameter adaptation for distribution or interest shifts~, whereas ReAd~ retrieves collaborative items and refines item-level predictions using an augmented representation. TCBSA performs history-specific test-time adaptation within semantic decoding, incorporating collaborative scores from an independently trained discriminative recommender to modify beam selection while keeping both models frozen. It also provides a stability bound on low-confidence score perturbations and analyzes inference complexity. {figures/figure1_motivation.pdf} Generative recommendation has emerged as a promising paradigm for next-item prediction through autoregressive generation of item identifiers~. In semantic-ID-based approaches, shared semantic tokens capture commonalities across items and organize next-item prediction within a structured generative space~. At test time, however, beam search retains only the highest-scoring partial identifiers at each depth, making early pruning irreversible: once a prefix is discarded, every item beneath it becomes unreachable~. This limitation is particularly pronounced when an emerging interest is reflected by only a few recent interactions, while a longer and more coherent portion of the history favors an established preference. As illustrated in Figure~ , the frozen generative recommender may preserve prefixes aligned with the established interest while prematurely pruning the branch containing the emerging-interest target. A potentially relevant item can thus be lost before its complete semantic identifier is ever generated. The same user history can nonetheless yield a different ranking under discriminative recommendation, which scores complete catalog items directly~. Unlike generative beam search, discriminative scoring does not rely on successive decisions over semantic prefixes. As illustrated in Figure~ , the frozen discriminative recommender ranks the emerging-interest target second over the full catalog, even though the generative decoder has pruned its semantic path. Recent comparative findings further highlight their complementary strengths: discriminative models better capture observed item transitions, whereas generative models perform better on cases requiring generalization~. Crucially, an independently trained discriminative recommender can provide history-specific item scores at test time without retraining the generator. This creates an opportunity for : using complementary item-level information to adapt semantic-prefix selection while decoding is still underway. Existing approaches incorporate complementary signals and improve generative recommendation at different stages. Training-time methods incorporate collaborative signals or behavioral priors~, while prefix-aware objectives improve beam-search behavior~; both require modifying generator training. Post-hoc fusion combines completed discriminative and generative recommendation lists~, but cannot alter prefixes already pruned by the generator. Other inference-time methods employ retrieval-based verification~ or reward- and value-guided search~. In sequential recommendation, test-time adaptation has also been explored through parameter updates~ and retrieval-based item-level refinement~. Bringing collaborative scores from an independently trained discriminative recommender into the intermediate decisions of a frozen semantic-ID generator, however, presents a distinct technical difficulty. The two operate in different prediction spaces: the former scores complete items, whereas the latter selects prefix-conditioned token extensions that may be shared by multiple item paths. An item-level score therefore cannot be applied directly to a token-level decision. Under irreversible beam pruning, this mismatch raises a fundamental question: how can item-level collaborative scores be brought into the decoding decisions of a frozen generative recommender at test time, without updating either model? To address this question, we propose est-Time ollaborative eam earch daptation , a training-free method that adapts frozen generative decoding using item-level collaborative scores. Rather than combining completed recommendation lists, TCBSA transforms collaborative item scores into corrections to the semantic paths under exploration, allowing them to influence beam selection before pruning. For each user history, a frozen discriminative recommender scores the full catalog, and TCBSA computes score margins relative to the discriminative Top-$R$ cutoff, making them invariant to additive shifts in item scores. Using a precomputed item-to-path mapping, these margins are aggregated over shared prefix--token edges to construct history-specific collaborative potentials. Because multiple item paths can share semantic edges, the resulting potentials can also support items outside the discriminative Top-$R$ set. To regulate their influence, a confidence gate adjusts the intervention strength according to the maximum softmax-normalized discriminative score for the current history. During a single decoding pass, TCBSA incorporates these confidence-tempered potentials into cumulative path scores before each beam-pruning step, adapting the search while keeping both models frozen. Generative recommenders using semantic identifiers predict items through successive token decisions, but beam search may irreversibly prune promising item paths. This limitation is particularly consequential when a few recent interactions signal an emerging interest while earlier behavior favors established preferences. Yet an independently trained discriminative recommender may rank the same items highly from the same interaction history. Such complementary scores are available at test time, offering a way to adapt a frozen generator without retraining either model. The challenge is to use these scores before pruning: combining completed recommendation lists cannot alter earlier beam decisions, while scores over complete items cannot directly inform choices among semantic prefixes shared by multiple items. We propose est-Time ollaborative eam earch daptation , a training-free method that incorporates collaborative information into semantic decoding. TCBSA transforms discriminative score margins into potentials over shared semantic edges and uses a confidence gate to regulate their contributions to cumulative path scores before beam selection. We establish a sufficient condition for target prefix survival under global beam competition and bound score perturbations under low discriminative confidence. Full-catalog experiments show consistent improvements over strong baselines across datasets and different generative and discriminative recommenders. Analyses of emerging interests and decoding behavior demonstrate gains in recommendation accuracy and target path survival, while targeted interventions support the contribution of shared prefix corrections.
