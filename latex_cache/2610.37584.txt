Generative recommendation offers a promising paradigm by autoregressively generating discrete semantic IDs of items. However, randomly initialized SID token embeddings and GR models trained from scratch do not directly inherit the semantic relations inherent in items, while inter-item collaborative relations are learned largely implicitly through token-level attention and next-token supervision. To address this limitation, we propose , a unified framework that explicitly incorporates inter-item relations into semantic pretraining, history encoding, and SID generation. We pretrain the GR model through generative semantic retrieval, enabling it to inherit inter-item semantic similarity by learning to generate semantic neighbors’ SIDs. We combine global attention with local attention aligned with SID boundaries to capture historical context and directed transitions between adjacent items. We further develop a theoretical framework for hierarchical preference decomposition over the SID prefix tree, proving an exact factorization of collaborative preference distributions and a weighted decomposition of item-level KL divergence into prefix-conditional KL divergences. Building on this analysis, we distill a discriminative teacher’s full conditional distributions over all legal next tokens at ground-truth prefixes along the target SID path, jointly optimizing this objective with cross-entropy. EAGER~ uses separate behavioral and semantic generation streams with cross-stream information transfer. UNGER~ integrates semantic and collaborative information into unified item codes through alignment and knowledge distillation. ReSID~ constructs semantic IDs from structured item features through field-aware masked autoencoding and globally aligned orthogonal quantization. ETEGRec~ alternately optimizes its tokenizer and recommender through sequence--item and preference--semantic alignment. DIGER~ jointly learns semantic IDs and recommendation through differentiable quantization with decaying Gumbel noise. ActionPiece~ constructs contextual tokens by merging frequently co-occurring features within items and across adjacent interactions. TIGER-SST~ applies semantic subword tokenization to TIGER's history inputs while preserving fixed-length target SIDs. LIGER~ combines SID generation with dense retrieval to support candidate reranking and cold-start recommendation. MemGen-GR~ adaptively combines generative and item-ID-based recommenders using a memorization-aware indicator to determine ensemble weights for each instance. TCA and TCA-Aux~ maximize weighted token likelihood and augment CE with a full-vocabulary auxiliary KL loss, respectively, using token-level soft labels derived from collaborative item scores. APAO-pointwise~ optimizes ground-truth prefix likelihoods and adaptively emphasizes prefixes with higher losses. GFlowGR-DB and GFlowGR-TB~ impose flow-balance constraints on individual decoding transitions and complete generation trajectories, respectively, using collaborative signals to define terminal rewards. DECOR~ combines pretrained codebook embeddings with learned collaborative embeddings and adapts token representations to the interaction context. Latte~ introduces a latent token before each SID to create multiple decoding trees and alleviate probability coupling induced by a single tree. TopoTok~ preserves semantic relationships during quantization through inter-group, intra-group, and inter-item topology distillation. Unlike traditional discriminative methods that learn continuous collaborative embeddings for individual items, GR typically uses residual quantization to map pretrained item representations into compact semantic identifiers , where common prefixes reflect semantic similarity and shared tokens facilitate knowledge transfer across items~. Realizing this potential requires effective modeling of semantic and collaborative relations among items in discrete token sequences. Existing efforts have largely focused on improving item tokenization to better capture item relations. Some methods draw on subword tokenization to merge fine-grained tokens into larger units based on co-occurrence pattern~, while others incorporate collaborative information into SIDs by aligning semantic representations with collaborative item embeddings~. Recent work also combines GR with dense matching from traditional recommenders through hybrid retrieval, exploiting the complementary strengths of the two paradigms~. Furthermore, GFlowGR incorporates collaborative signals into item-level rewards to guide SID generation through flow balance objectives, while TopoTok~ preserves item neighborhood relations during tokenization through topology distillation at multiple granularities. Despite these advances, an inherent bottleneck remains in GR: Modeling inter-item relations is central to capturing user preferences in sequential recommendation~. Item text provides semantic cues for identifying potential substitutes and complements~, while co-occurrence and transition patterns in user interaction histories provide collaborative signals that characterize behavioral associations between items~. Moreover, differences in a user's preference strengths across candidate items give rise to relative preference relations among them, providing the basis for personalized ranking~. These relations are defined at the item level, whereas GR uses SID tokens as its basic modeling units, leaving inter-item relations to be learned implicitly. As shown in Fig.~, this bottleneck manifests as three challenges in a standard encoder-decoder GR framework. Although shared prefixes preserve some semantic structure, randomly initialized token embeddings and a GR model trained from scratch do not directly inherit the semantic similarity relations captured by continuous item representations. During history encoding, user interaction histories are flattened into SID token sequences, and the encoder integrates token information through bidirectional self-attention. However, its attention structure does not explicitly impose local constraints that respect complete SID boundaries and capture directed transitions between adjacent items. During generation, GR relies on next-token cross-entropy to maximize the likelihood of the target SID. However, this single-target supervision does not fully exploit the set of relevant candidate items or explicitly align generation with collaborative preference relations between users and candidate items, leaving inter-item competitive relations without explicit constraints. GR therefore needs to explicitly model inter-item relations when learning item semantics, encoding interaction histories, and generating recommendations. GR uses item tokenization to encode prior knowledge of inter-item relations into SIDs. TIGER~ pioneered the use of residual quantization in GR to learn semantic codes by reconstructing item content representations. Subsequent research has focused on incorporating collaborative signals into SID and aligning tokenization with recommendation objectives. LETTER~ introduces collaborative alignment and code diversity regularization, while ETEGRec~ alternates between tokenizer and recommender updates using alignment objectives. Other studies reconsider how item tokens are organized and represented. ActionPiece~ learns merge rules over unordered feature sets using co-occurrences within items and across adjacent interactions. SST~ compresses history-side item tokens into semantic subwords while retaining fixed-length SIDs as generation targets. DECOR~ preserves token assignments, combines frozen codebook embeddings with learnable collaborative embeddings, and refines token representations through contextual composition. However, the semantic organization of SIDs does not by itself ensure that the generator learns the semantics of complete items and the relations among them. Discriminative recommenders exploit inter-item relations through transition structures, representation geometry, and ranking supervision~. In LLM-based recommendation, explicit relation modeling is introduced through attention structures and collaborative supervision. IAM~ alternates between attention layers restricted to tokens within each item title and across items, separating title semantics from collaborative relations. TCA~ normalizes collaborative scores over prefix-compatible items and sums the probabilities of items sharing the same next token to construct soft labels for LLM-based item-title generation. Recent work also introduces relational supervision into GR. APAO~ optimizes prefix rankings with pointwise or pairwise objectives and adaptively emphasizes underperforming prefixes, while GFlowGR~ incorporates collaborative signals into item-level terminal rewards and guides SID generation through flow balance objectives. However, GR uses discrete SID tokens shared across items as its basic modeling units, rather than continuous representations of entire items or text tokens processed by pretrained LLMs. Relations among complete items must therefore be expressed through token composition and generation, yet the connection between item-level relations and token-level dependencies remains largely implicit in GR models.
