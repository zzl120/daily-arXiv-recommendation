Reasoning-enhanced recommenders introduce intermediate computation to infer user preferences and purchase intent before predicting the next item~. Latent reasoning methods internalize this intermediate process within hidden representations rather than decoding explicit text tokens; LASAR, for instance, aligns recurrent latent states with teacher Chain-of-Thought representations and applies reinforcement learning to determine the reasoning depth~. Avoiding rationale generation at test time leaves two practical costs: constructing training supervision and executing recurrent latent states. First, latent alignment remains tied to explicit teacher supervision: a large language model must still generate a reasoning trace for every training instance before the student can compress that trace into hidden states~. Second, Coconut-style recurrent execution feeds a final-layer hidden state back as the next step's input embedding~, requiring a feedback loop beyond the ordinary token-input prefill and decoding path used by serving engines such as vLLM~. We therefore examine the supervision source and the inference procedure . Which parts of a recommendation CoT trace are useful for supervision? A typical teacher trace interleaves factual evidence from observed interactions with extrapolated interpretations of user preferences and future intent, yet full-trace distillation treats all of this content as supervision. We develop a recommendation-specific diagnosis that measures each step's contribution to target-SID prediction and local trace coherence, then tests the resulting supervision choices through student retraining. Inspired by the step-utility perspective of SGP-CoT~, this analysis connects trace-level evidence to downstream recommendation quality. On Beauty, we observe that history analysis is the only reasoning step with positive mean contributions on both axes, whereas subsequent preference construction and intent inference yield negative mean answer contributions . Consistently, retaining only the history-analysis step on Instruments reduces supervision text by over 61\ We introduce to supervise latent computation directly from observed item histories without teacher generation. PHiT encodes item descriptions once using a frozen sparse encoder, caches the resulting lexical representations, and aggregates them across each user's interaction history into a multi-hot semantic target. During training, a lightweight linear head predicts this historical target from intermediate thought-token states jointly with the next-item SID objective. Because item representations are cached and reused across all user histories, PHiT replaces per-example autoregressive teacher generation with offline item encoding and fast history aggregation, relying exclusively on observed historical items without accessing the target next-item label. PHiT also changes how intermediate states are computed. We expand the model vocabulary with $N$ distinct thought tokens and insert them as a contiguous block between the interaction history and the target SID. Fixed thought-token IDs acquire user-specific contextual hidden states through causal self-attention over the preceding interaction history, retaining historical semantic evidence. Because both the sparse encoder and the auxiliary prediction head are discarded after training, inference simply appends the fixed thought-token IDs to the history prompt, computes their contextual states in a single causal prefill pass, and decodes the target SID through unmodified token and KV-cache interfaces. Evaluated across Beauty, Instruments, Sports, and Steam using supervised fine-tuning alone, PHiT achieves the best reported results on all 24 main-table metrics, with 22 statistically significant improvements over the strongest baselines. Controlled five-seed ablations on Beauty verify that supervising thought tokens with input-matched historical targets yields consistent gains beyond adding extra token positions or an unaligned auxiliary objective, while the complete target-construction pipeline processes 131,413 training examples in approximately 113 seconds on one GPU. Within generative recommendation, discrete semantic indexing and collaborative-semantic alignment map items into hierarchical token sequences suitable for autoregressive decoding~, complemented by streamlined training and inference architectures~.
