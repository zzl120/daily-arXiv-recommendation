While these advances brought substantial gains, two limitations persist: the vocabulary size required for items grows linearly with catalog size, and the sequential signal alone is not rich enough. Complementary directions have emerged to inject richer contextual information into generative recommendation. The first approach enriches the item representation by replacing the arbitrary, opaque, randomly-initialized item IDs with semantically rich discrete codes, known as Semantic IDs ~. Because each item is encoded with codes from a small shared codebook, SIDs also substantially reduce the vocabulary size. The second enriches the relational context by grounding recommendation in , which encode structured relationships between items, attributes, and external entities~. SIDs were popularized by TIGER~, which represents each item as a short sequence of content-derived codes from an RQ-VAE over pretrained embeddings~. Sharing code prefixes across semantically similar items enables parameter sharing and cold-start generalization~, and later work adds collaborative signals~ or end-to-end tokenization~. SPRIG adopts the TIGER formulation with the refined quantization of Generative Recommendation with Semantic IDs ~, but unlike these methods grounds recommendation in a structured relational context. A parallel line frames recommendation as over a KG, returning an item together with a human-interpretable multi-hop path. Early reinforcement-learning -based agents navigate from user to item~; the paradigm was later recast as , with constrained decoding guaranteeing valid edges~. KGGLM~ is the state of the art in this line, pretraining a generative model on generic KG paths and fine-tuning for path-reasoning recommendation. However, all of these represent items as opaque, arbitrary IDs, preventing parameter sharing and growing the vocabulary linearly with the catalog size. In this context, we propose , a generative recommender framework that unifies content-derived SIDs with knowledge-graph path reasoning in a single autoregressive model. SPRIG ingests hierarchical SIDs pre-computed from item content features via a residual quantizer, replacing the opaque atomic item tokens of prior KG-based recommenders with semantically informed, parameter-sharing codes. Afterwards, it leverages the rich contextual structure of a KG to pretrain a causal Transformer decoder on generic entity-relation paths, before fine-tuning on user-to-item recommendation paths whose last tokens consist of SID sequences. The path generator builds on KGGLM~, which we also adopt as our primary KG baseline. This design simultaneously inherits the expressiveness of KG path reasoning~ and the parameter efficiency and cross-item generalization of SIDs~, while decoupling the output vocabulary from the catalog size. To the best of our knowledge, SPRIG is the first model to integrate SIDs with KG path reasoning for generative recommendation. Within this context, we investigate whether KG-based generative recommenders benefit from representing item entities using semantically grounded identifiers , and whether the resulting shared code space reduces the output vocabulary without sacrificing recommendation quality . Consistent with GRID~, residual K-means attains the best accuracy, but the gap over RVQ and RQ-VAE is small ($ $1\ We introduced SPRIG, the first generative recommender to unify content-derived SIDs with KG path reasoning in a single autoregressive framework. By rewriting every item entity in the KG as a short sequence of hierarchical tokens and training a causal Transformer decoder over the resulting paths, SPRIG combines the relational reasoning of KG-based recommenders with the parameter efficiency and cross-item generalization of SIDs. Across two domains --- movies on and music on --- SPRIG outperforms prior generative recommenders spanning atomic-ID , SID-based , and KG-augmented families, surpassing its direct predecessor KGGLM on every metric, while remaining competitive with a strong sequential baseline at a lower parameter count and decoupling the output vocabulary from the catalog size. Several directions remain open. First, the use of SIDs offers as natural and promising application the use of SPRIG for item cold-start recommendation: a new item can be reached through the SID prefix it inherits from content-similar seen items, rather than requiring a dedicated embedding~. A faithful evaluation, however, requires cold-start–specific splits and a path-sampling strategy for items absent from the user–item training edges, which we leave to future work. Second, while KG path reasoning is inherently explanatory, SPRIG's paths terminate in SID codes that are less human-readable than named item entities; recovering faithful, interpretable justifications is a promising but non-trivial direction rather than an existing feature of the model. In addition, our finding that the optimal quantizer differs from that reported for purely sequential SID models~ motivates a deeper study of how tokenizer choice interacts with relational path modeling. We leave these research directions for future work.
