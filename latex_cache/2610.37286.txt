Generative recommendation has recently gained attention as a new paradigm that formulates item prediction as a sequence generation task ~. Under this paradigm, semantic ID-based approaches have become particularly prominent, which predict items by generating their semantic IDs, each comprising an ordered sequence of discrete semantic tokens obtained through residual quantization~. Within each item's semantic IDs, earlier tokens typically represent coarse-grained item semantics, while later tokens encode finer-grained information~. This structure enables semantically similar items to share coarse-level prefix tokens, capturing their common semantics while retaining finer-grained distinctions. Conventional semantic ID-based recommendation models adopt an encoder–decoder architecture~. The encoder contextualizes historical semantic tokens through self-attention, allowing tokens from different items and semantic levels to exchange information. The decoder can then attend to encoded tokens from all historical items and semantic levels, regardless of the level being predicted. This design brings together two distinct sequential structures: tokens at the same level across interactions capture behavioral patterns at a consistent semantic granularity, whereas tokens within an item describe its progressive semantic refinement. Given these different roles, it is unclear whether information from all levels is equally useful for next token prediction. Although the EOS state can aggregate information across semantic levels and is accessible to the decoder, its removal produces no consistent performance gain. These results suggest that a single shared EOS state, despite its access to all semantic levels, does not introduce cross-level interference strong enough to noticeably affect recommendation performance in this setting. Historical semantic tokens cannot attend to EOS under LIS, so their encoded states remain level-isolated even when EOS is retained as the sequence terminator.
