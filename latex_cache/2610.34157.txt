large-scale recommender systems~. Inspired by tokenization in LLMs and computer vision, recent methods represent each item as a short sequence of discrete semantic IDs and formulate next-item recommendation as autoregressive generation of the target SIDs~. Empirical studies further suggest that increasing model capacity and training computation can improve recommendation performance, suggesting that generative recommenders also benefit from predictable scaling laws~. However, unlike LLMs, recommender systems are deployed under much tighter computational and serving constraints. The key challenge is not simply to increase computation, but to effectively allocate a limited computational budget between interaction history modeling and target SID generation. Existing generative recommenders allocate computation differently between history modeling and target generation, as illustrated in Figure~. This architectural evolution progressively shifts computation from dedicated history modeling toward shared and ultimately target-centric computation~. The above architectural designs reveal a common conflict between expressive context modeling and efficient target generation. Rich user-item context is typically obtained by deeply encoding historical interactions before generation, whereas efficient decoding often treats the resulting context as fixed conditioning. Meanwhile, computational depth remains tightly coupled with the number of network layers in existing architectures, such that deeper context refinement requires additional parameters. These observations motivate a central question: Looped Transformers, which recursively apply a set of shared layers across recurrent steps, offer a promising way to decouple computational depth from parameter size or memory consumption~. Early studies combined recurrent depth with adaptive computation to iteratively refine token representations~, while recent work has revisited this paradigm to improve the reasoning and efficiency capabilities in modern language models~. Although recurrent depth improves model efficiency via parameter sharing, it is hard to differentiate two distinct computations over long behavioral histories and target SIDs in generative recommenders. Moreover, additional recurrent steps still incur inference latency, making unrestricted looping impractical under strict serving budgets. This motivates a recommendation-specific recurrent design that separately organizes history refinement and target generation while supporting efficient low-budget inference. {img/framework_comparison.pdf} Encoder-Decoder architectures allocate separate computation to history modeling and target generation. Decoder-Only architectures unify this computation within a shared stack. Lazy Decoder-Only architectures further reallocate the computational budget toward target generation.} In this paper, we propose , a parameter-efficient scaling framework for generative recommendation via hierarchical recurrent depth. LoopGR is part of the TGR series of generative recommendation research at Tencent . It comprises an encoder, a shared decoder and a lightweight shared context updater. After the encoder initializes the behavioral context, high-level decoder cycles and low-level updater cycles are recurrently interleaved. Specifically, the decoder predicts target SID representations from the current context while the updater feeds the emerging target state back to refine the context for subsequent decoding. This design allows user context to evolve during generation while scaling computational depth through shared layers. To support efficient inference under hard serving budgets, we propose a recurrent trajectory self-distillation to match predictive distributions and evolution directions from high-budget to low-budget recurrent trajectories. LoopGR reconciles dynamic context modeling with efficient target generation by decoupling computational depth from parameter count while retaining strong performance under limited recurrent budgets. Our contributions are summarized as follows: To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to introduce a recurrent-depth architecture for SID-based generative recommendation, which enables dynamic context refinement during target SID generation while decoupling computational depth from parameter count. We propose a recurrent trajectory self-distillation method that aligns both predictive distributions and their evolution directions. It avoids the entanglement between exit selection and trajectory formation and effectively transfers a high-budget teacher into a low-budget student for efficient inference. Extensive experiments show that LoopGR outperforms strong baselines with fewer parameters. Beyond overall comparisons, we systematically evaluate recurrent depth, model size and computation scaling. Trajectory-level analyses are conducted to characterize the evolution of predictive representations across recurrent steps. Generative recommendation has emerged as a new paradigm that directly generates target items from user-item interaction history~. With the rise of Large Language Models , one line of studies adapts pretrained LLMs to recommendation by aligning their rich linguistic priors with recommendation-specific objectives~. For example, OneRec-Think~ introduces itemic alignment, reasoning activation and reasoning enhancement to further equip recommenders with both explicit reasoning capabilities and personalized recommendation. A complementary line forgoes linguistic priors in LLMs and trains a recommendation-tailored architecture from scratch~. OneRec-V2~ proposes a lazy decoder-only architecture to reallocate computation toward the target SID generation and avoid repeatedly applying expensive sequence encoding. Besides, several studies have further explored latent reasoning for generative recommendation by introducing intermediate latent states~. Despite these advances, most existing methods retain fixed-depth Transformer backbones, where increasing representational capacity typically entails introducing additional layers and parameters. Such parameter-centric scaling is difficult to reconcile with the stringent memory and latency requirements of recommendation systems. Looped Transformers increase effective computational depth by repeatedly applying a set of shared layers to decouple the computational depth from model size~. As an earlier study, Universal Transformers~ proposes a self-attentive recurrent sequence model with Adaptive Computation Time ~ to iteratively refine the symbol representations. More recently, recurrent depth has been revisited in LLMs to improve parameter-efficient scaling and reasoning~. As an exemplary implementation, Ouro~ repeatedly reuses a Transformer stack and systematically studies the scaling behavior of looped language models. Huginn~ adopts a different architecture in which the iterative body is tied between prelude and coda stacks. In recommender systems, recurrent depth remains largely underexplored. LoopCTR~ uses a parameter-shared prefix-attention module as the recurrent block for CTR prediction. However, it tailors recurrence layers to discriminative CTR prediction rather than autoregressive item generation. Deeper recurrence introduces additional sequential computation, making the balance between recurrent depth and inference efficiency an important direction for recommender systems. We collect the intermediate representations at different SID positions from a 16-layer Encoder-Decoder, a 16-loop LoopGR teacher and distilled students with 8 and 4 loops. In the experiments, LoopGR exhibits clear position-dependent dynamics that are characterized by stronger oscillations at earlier SID positions and faster convergence at later SID positions. These non-monotonic patterns resemble the structured latent trajectories observed in previous Looped Transformers~. Unlike the progressive layer-wise transformations of the Encoder-Decoder, LoopGR repeatedly revises its latent state across cycles, yielding a more dynamic and position-dependent refinement process. Figure~ shows that reducing the loop budget through distillation does not simply truncate the teacher trajectory. The 8-loop student exhibits a much larger transition at the beginning of recurrent computation, followed by progressively smaller updates toward its terminal state. This tendency becomes even more pronounced for the 4-loop student, whose trajectory is dominated by the initial transition. Here, we propose an intuitive interpretation that the distillation objective encourages a low-budget model to approximate a longer refinement process with fewer recurrent transitions rather than reproducing every intermediate teacher state. Consequently, the teacher and student exhibit complementary computational behaviors. The long-budget teacher develops a richer refinement trajectory, whereas the distilled student learns a more direct trajectory toward the terminal prediction under a limited inference budget. We conduct ablation studies on two key design choices of LoopGR, namely the context updater and the training objective. For the updater, we replace the default GRU with three sequence modeling methods, including token mixing, linear attention, vanilla attention and an Identity variant that leaves the context representations unchanged. Furthermore, we compare per-step supervision, gated exit, Intra-Loop Self-Distillation {goyal2026elt} and Recurrent Trajectory Self-Distillation . At inference, the other alternative training objectives use the full budget and RTSD distills LoopGR into a student operating at half of the teacher’s budget.
