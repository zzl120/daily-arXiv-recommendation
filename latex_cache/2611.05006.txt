A bucket-wide level shift can produce a large absolute gap even when rankings are similar, whereas an operational top-$k$ decision depends on category shape. Restricting to common videos changes the estimand: it removes support entry and exit rather than explaining that part of a full dashboard gap. Within the retained support, we adapt the classical symmetric rate--composition decomposition~ to separate within-video residual differences from video-exposure mix, then use a stated Shapley convention~. We organize the evidence as an audit stack: a paired-support discrepancy, a complete-support error ledger, a frozen prospective calibration, and the resulting top-$k$ decision loss. A known-truth semi-synthetic benchmark then separates the conditions under which user demeaning helps from those it cannot repair. Our contributions are fourfold: a conditional audit protocol that reports the selection funnel and separates level, centered shape, rank, and decision discrepancies on paired user and video support; a complete-support, level--shape error ledger that combines support entry/exit with Kitagawa-style within-video and video-mix accounting; a leakage-resistant prospective protocol that fits on history and an early exploration window, freezes its parameters, and evaluates a later random-insertion reference; and decision-aware inference and a known-truth stress test exposing where level calibration, demeaning, and propensity weighting succeed or fail. Randomized recommendation logs support unbiased replay evaluation when the logging design and acceptance rule are known~; counterfactual learning-to-rank instead uses examination propensities to learn rankers from biased clicks~. Offline A/B studies connect counterfactual metrics to online outcomes and candidate decisions ~, while large action spaces and deficient support expose identification--variance tradeoffs ~. Serving policies reshape offline populations~. KuaiRec, KuaiRand, and Open Bandit provide dense, randomized, or multiple-policy logs~; short-video position models motivate our unobserved-rank boundary~. All real-data experiments use public, anonymized KuaiRand interactions; the remaining experiments are synthetic. We process no direct identifiers and collect no new human-subject data. Category rankings can concentrate exposure on engagement-maximizing content, reducing supply diversity and disadvantaging long-tail creators. Biased rankings can also misallocate exposure while appearing objective. We present the output as a measurement audit, not a standalone allocation rule. Deployment should combine uncertainty, diversity constraints, creator impact, and objectives beyond engagement.
