Controllable Multi-Objective Recommendation enables dynamic adjustment of objective trade-offs at inference time without retraining, adapting to shifting user or system requirements. Existing CMOR approaches typically rely on output-level reranking or parameter-generating modules, which either limit the scope of feasible trade-offs or incur substantial training and inference cost. Model merging provides a promising alternative by reusing multiple objective-specific models via parameter combination. However, its direct application to recommender systems is challenging due to the lack of a shared linear regime and cross-objective interference. To address these issues, we propose RecSoup, a lightweight two-stage approach for effective CMOR via specialized model merging. RecSoup first mitigates parameter interference across objectives through a pruning strategy that jointly considers objective-specific importance and cross-objective relevance. It then performs adaptive parameter merging via a learnable preference-to-coefficient mapping, allowing dynamic trade-off control at inference time without joint retraining or additional generative modules. Theoretically, we prove that RecSoup outperforms the parameter generation paradigm in effectiveness and efficiency, as it can fully navigate the Pareto front without smoothness constraints. Extensive experiments on multiple benchmarks demonstrate that RecSoup achieves improved Pareto front quality and effective controllability while delivers 165--228× faster inference-time latency than parameter generation approaches. While existing multi-objective recommender systems address these trade-offs during training, they typically enforce fixed preferences at inference time~. In practice, however, the preference weights on objectives are often dynamic, shifting across users' or systems' requirements. This motivates Controllable Multi-Objective Recommendation , which enables dynamic adjustment of objective trade-offs at inference time without retraining~. Research on CMOR can be categorized into two paradigms based on the stage of intervention: post-processing and in-processing approaches. Post-processing approaches, such as rule-based reranking like MMR~ intervene by adjusting the initially generated recommendation lists. While being model-agnostic, their performance is constrained by the fixed candidate set, often leading to a narrow scope of trade-off solutions. In-processing focuses on parameter-level modulation, primarily through parameter generation. These parameter generation approaches learn the mapping from user preferences over multiple objectives to the parameter space of recommendation model by employing generative architectures, such as hypernetworks~ or diffusion models~. Despite delivering superior performance, these in-processing approaches inflate the training cost with a massive number of additional parameters from the generative modules and incur non-negligible inference latency, while often suffering from optimization instability. This raises a critical open question: why not reuse existing recommendation models to efficiently achieve controllable trade-offs, rather than constructing new parameters from scratch? Recent advances in large language models offer a promising answer: , which adjusts model behavior through weighted merging of existing model parameters according to multi-objective preferences, eliminating the need for retraining or additional parameter-generating modules~. This paradigm is particularly well-suited for recommender systems, where multiple independently trained, objective-specialized sub-models commonly coexist in industrial training pipelines~. Rather than discarding these costly pre-trained checkpoints, model merging offers an efficient mechanism for reusing and combining their expertise. As illustrated in Figure~, in this paper, we realize controllable recommendation by dynamically merging objective-specialized sub-models in the parameter space. Given user-specified preference weights across objectives, a lightweight model merger $f_{ } $ maps these preferences to merging coefficients that govern the integration of parameters from each sub-model. The resulting merged model directly generates recommendations based on users' historical interactions. Notably, this model merging paradigm achieves both high efficiency and a high-quality Pareto front, while avoiding the costs associated with joint multi-objective retraining. However, directly applying existing model merging approaches to recommender systems is non-trivial. Unlike settings where task-specific updates reside in a shared linear regime and can be composed through task arithmetic~, objective-specialized recommendation models exhibit heterogeneous parameter updates driven by structurally conflicting objectives such as accuracy and diversity. As analyzed in Section~, recommendation task vectors substantially deviate from the linear regime hypothesis that underpins most task-vector merging approaches. The absence of a shared low-dimensional linear subspace renders naive linear interpolation ineffective and prone to cross-objective interference, motivating the need for a merging strategy tailored to the complex parameter geometry of recommendation models. To address these challenges, we propose RecSoup, a two-stage framework for controllable multi-objective recommendation. In the first stage, RecSoup employs a fine-grained pruning strategy to resolve parameter interference. Unlike magnitude-based approaches, we jointly consider objective-specific importance and cross-objective relevance, which establishes a harmonious parameter foundation for merging. In the second stage, RecSoup moves beyond linear interpolation. We integrate a learnable mechanism that captures the mapping from user preference vectors to merging coefficients, allowing the model to dynamically navigate the parameter space. The synergy between pruning-driven conflict mitigation and adaptive merging enables RecSoup to generate high-quality Pareto solutions with superior controllability, bypassing the optimization instability and architectural overhead of generative approaches. The key contributions are summarized as follows: We present , the first framework to integrate model merging into CMOR, adjusting objective trade-offs directly in the parameter space. We propose a pruning strategy that jointly considers objective-specific and cross-objective importance and can be integrated with existing dynamic merging mechanisms to substantially improve controllable multi-objective recommendation. We provide theoretical analysis showing that model merging is a more effective and efficient paradigm for CMOR.
