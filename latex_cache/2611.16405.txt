Generative embeddings, in contrast, use reasoning to organize query intent and relevance evidence, providing richer semantic representations for complex queries. To meet the demands of both large-scale low-latency retrieval and complex fine-grained matching, multimodal embedding model should not only support both representation capabilities, but also maintain their consistency within a unified representation space. To this end, we propose AntEmbed, a dual-mode multimodal embedding model that supports both efficient and fine-grained retrieval. First, we construct retrieval-oriented reasoning content that explicitly captures query intent and confusion-aware fine-grained semantic information. We treat the original and reasoning-enhanced forms of each candidate as semantically equivalent views of the same instance and include both in the positive set for the corresponding query. Through multi-positive contrastive learning, we establish cross-view matching constraints using a single embedding token, thereby aligning discriminative and generative embeddings within a unified embedding space. Second, we introduce cross-mode self-distillation, which transfers fine-grained semantic information from reasoning-enhanced representations to original representations through candidate similarity-distribution distillation and query representation alignment. This enables discriminative embeddings to achieve stronger semantic modeling without requiring additional reasoning. On the MMEB-v2 benchmark, AntEmbed-8B achieves a state-of-the-art overall score of 81.5 with its generative embeddings, with particularly strong performance on image retrieval and visual document retrieval. {figures/figure1.pdf} Overall and per-domain results on MMEB-v2. AntEmbed consistently outperforms competing generative and discriminative embedding models at comparable parameter scales. } Multimodal large language models , with their stronger vision–language understanding and unified modeling of multimodal compositional inputs, have increasingly become the backbone of general-purpose multimodal retrievers. Existing approaches typically employ large-scale contrastive learning to directly encode original multimodal inputs into vectors, using the final hidden state of a predefined embedding token as the embedding representation. Since they do not generate intermediate content, these are referred to as discriminative embeddings. Discriminative embeddings enable efficient encoding, but rely on direct input embedding, making it difficult to capture fine-grained attributes and the key semantic evidence underlying relevance judgments in complex inputs. {figures/figure2.pdf} UME-R1 optimizes the original and reasoning-enhanced inputs with separate within-view objectives, whereas AntEmbed treats them as semantically equivalent positive views and applies multi-positive contrastive learning to align their representations in a unified embedding space. This produces a smaller representation gap between the two views and improves retrieval performance} Recent studies have introduced Chain-of-Thought reasoning into the multimodal embedding, where intermediate reasoning processes explicitly organize query intent and relevance evidence to guide representation. The resulting representations are referred to as generative embedding. Existing studies show that generative embeddings can provide additional gains on complex and fine-grained multimodal tasks. However, they require an additional autoregressive reasoning process, which incurs substantial inference latency and computational overhead. This raises an important requirement: Methods such as UME-R1 address this requirement by introducing two independent embedding tokens, explicitly modeling discriminative and generative embeddings as separate embedding modes. As shown in Figure~ , the representations of the same instance under the two modes exhibit low similarity, indicating a clear separation between their representation spaces. Under this design, Consequently, retrieval systems must predefine the embedding mode and maintain separate corpus representations for different modes, introducing substantial index storage overhead. Notably, Figure~ also shows a substantial performance gap between the generative and discriminative embeddings, suggesting that the reasoning information can effectively enhance representation quality. Meanwhile, discriminative embeddings remain the primary representation for low-latency online retrieval; improving their semantic modeling capability could reduce the system’s reliance on generative embeddings. This motivates us to investigate: Based on the above observations, we propose AntEmbed, a dual-mode multimodal embedding model trained with a two-stage strategy. AntEmbed aims to accommodate both discriminative and generative embeddings within a unified embedding space and establish consistent retrieval representations across views. Specifically, for each retrieval query, we treat the original and reasoning-enhanced forms of its positive as semantically equivalent views of the same instance and jointly include them in the query’s positive set. The reasoning-enhanced view consists of the original input together with retrieval-oriented intent and confusion-aware fine-grained semantic information. In the first stage, the model uses a single predefined embedding token and multi-positive contrastive learning to jointly constrain the representations of original and reasoning-enhanced inputs, aligning discriminative and generative embeddings within a unified embedding space and establishing consistent retrieval representations across views. In the second stage, we further use the reasoning-enhanced branch as the teacher and perform cross-mode self-distillation at both the candidate similarity distribution and query representation levels. This transfers the fine-grained semantic information obtained through reasoning to the discriminative embeddings, enabling stronger semantic modeling without additional inference. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: We propose AntEmbed, a dual-mode multimodal embedding model that supports both efficient and fine-grained retrieval. AntEmbed treats original and reasoning-enhanced inputs as semantically equivalent views of the same instance and aligns them in a unified embedding space, enabling consistent retrieval across views. We first treat the original and reasoning-enhanced candidate forms simultaneously as positives for each query and align their representations through multi-positive contrastive learning. We then introduce cross-mode self-distillation to transfer fine-grained semantic information from reasoning-enhanced representations to original representations, enhancing discriminative embeddings while reducing reliance on additional reasoning. On the MMEB-v2 benchmark, AntEmbed-8B achieves state-of-the-art performance with its generative embeddings, while its discriminative embeddings remain competitive, outperforming some generative baselines. Multimodal embedding models aim to map heterogeneous modalities into a shared representation space for unified semantic matching and multimodal retrieval. Early approaches, exemplified by vision–language contrastive models such as CLIP and ALIGN, established cross-modal semantic alignment through large-scale image–text contrastive learning, laying the foundation for subsequent general-purpose multimodal embedding models. However, these models typically rely on relatively independent modality-specific encoders, limiting their ability to handle complex inputs with arbitrary combinations of modalities. With the emergence of multimodal large language models , their stronger cross-modal understanding and joint modeling capabilities have enabled a new paradigm for constructing general-purpose multimodal embedding models. GME, VLM2Vec, and Qwen3-VL-Embedding use MLLMs as backbones and adapt them through large-scale contrastive learning to support general-purpose multimodal representation and retrieval. Subsequent studies have further focused on complex inputs and fine-grained semantic matching. UniME and MM-Embed introduce high-quality hard negatives to improve the model’s ability to distinguish fine-grained semantic differences among candidates. However, such instance-level supervision does not explicitly model why two instances are relevant or which fine-grained evidence should be preserved in their representations. Chain-of-Thought reasoning can make implicit intent, fine-grained attributes, and relevance evidence explicit, providing multimodal embedding models with richer semantic context. This has motivated growing interest in reasoning-enhanced generative embeddings. Existing generative embedding approaches broadly follow two architectural paradigms. The first is the decoupled reasoning–embedding architecture, exemplified by TTE and Embed-RL, separate the reasoner from the embedding model, allowing both components to scale independently and enabling stronger external reasoners to enrich semantic understanding. Alternatively, another paradigm integrates reasoning and embedding into a unified multimodal model. While reducing communication overhead between separate modules, this design couples the two capabilities within a shared backbone, limiting their independent scalability. Regardless of the paradigm, generating explicit reasoning introduces additional computation compared with discriminative embedding. UME-R1 supports both discriminative and generative embeddings through two dedicated embedding tokens, allowing retrieval systems to select a representation mode according to task requirements and trade off efficiency against representational capacity. However, modeling the two modes separately can yield divergent representations and substantial retrieval performance gaps across modes. Maintaining separate candidate indexes for these modes further increases storage costs. Standard contrastive learning typically assigns a single positive sample to each anchor and treats the remaining samples in the same batch as negatives. Multi-Positive contrastive learning instead associates each anchor with a set of semantically related positives, leveraging richer supervision by pulling multiple positives closer while pushing negatives apart. Some studies incorporate multiple semantically related or similarly supervised samples into the positive set for contrastive learning. StableRep constructs positive sets from multiple images generated using the same text prompt, treating these images as mutual positives. For generative embeddings, the original and reasoning-enhanced forms of a candidate correspond to the same retrieval instance and can therefore be treated as semantically equivalent views under different information conditions. However, this instance-level correspondence remains underexplored as a means of connecting discriminative and generative embeddings. We include both candidate views in the positive set for each corresponding query and use Multi-Positive contrastive learning to align representations across the two modes. {figures/figure3.pdf} In Stage 1, we construct original and reasoning-enhanced multimodal views and optimize them with multi-positive contrastive learning using a sole embedding token. In Stage 2, cross-mode self-distillation transfers the fine-grained semantic information obtained through reasoning to the discriminative embedding through candidate similarity-distribution distillation and query representation alignment.} This section first outlines the basic paradigms and training objectives of discriminative and generative embeddings . We then describe the CoT data construction process for AntEmbed and its two-stage training strategy. In the first stage, we employ multi-positive contrastive learning to jointly optimize the original and reasoning-enhanced forms as positive views of the same instance. In the second stage, we introduce cross-mode self-distillation , using the reasoning-enhanced branch as the teacher and optimizing the original query branch through candidate similarity distribution distillation and query representation alignment. Unlike mathematical reasoning or code generation, multimodal embedding does not typically aim to derive a unique correct answer, but rather to determine the relevance between a query and a candidate. Therefore, the reasoning for retrieval should focus on relevance assessment, identifying the latent intent of the query, the semantic evidence supporting the relevance judgment, and the fine-grained attributes that distinguish semantically similar candidates. Motivated by this observation, we introduce Confusion-Aware CoT Construction. Similar to TTE, we employ Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct} as an external reasoner to generate reasoning trajectories for the training data. For a query $q$, we prompt the reasoner to focus on its retrieval intent and identify the key semantic evidence required to assess candidate relevance, rather than generating a specific answer. For a candidate $c$, semantic analysis based solely on its own content is insufficient to capture distinguishing information, as it lacks explicit comparisons with semantically similar candidates. To address this issue, we first use the open-source multimodal embedding model Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B} to retrieve the most similar candidates from the corpus, selecting the top-$K$ neighbors to build the confusion set: _K =_{c_j / \{ c_i \}} We then provide the candidate $c_i$ together with its confusion set to the reasoner, prompting it to identify the key attributes, relations, and visual or textual evidence that distinguish $c_i$ from these semantically similar candidates, thereby generating confusion-aware fine-grained semantic information. To avoid explicitly modeling discriminative and generative embeddings as two separate embedding modes, we extract both representations using a single predefined embedding token. If the original optimization objective is retained, the embedding space would establish the following matching relationships: q c^+;q^o c^{+,o} Although both representations are extracted from the same embedding token, the model would still learn two independent within-mode matching objectives in the shared vector space. Consequently, the training process does not inherently impose semantic consistency constraints across different reasoning conditions. To address this issue, we further treat different reasoning conditions of the same candidate as semantically equivalent positive views. Specifically, for an original query $q$ and its reasoning-enhanced counterpart $q^{o}$, we jointly include the original candidate $c$ and its reasoning-enhanced form $c^{+,o}$ as the positive set $ = \{ c^+,c^{+,o} \}$ for both queries. Although generative embeddings can capture richer semantic information through explicit reasoning, the additional autoregressive overhead makes them less suitable for routine encoding in online retrieval. To reduce reliance on computationally expensive generative embeddings, we further introduce cross-mode self-distillation, transferring semantic information from reasoning-enhanced representations to discriminative embeddings, thereby enhancing their semantic modeling capability. Specifically, we initialize both the teacher and student branches from the first-stage model, feeding them with reasoning-enhanced and original inputs, respectively, and update only the student branch. Specifically, we consider three external reasoners: a Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model fine-tuned with the training data, Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct used to construct our training data, and Gemini-3.1-Pro-Preview. Moreover, stronger reasoners consistently lead to better retrieval performance: Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct and Gemini-3.1-Pro-Preview further improve the average score to 79.3 and 80.2, respectively. These results demonstrate that AntEmbed can flexibly leverage external reasoners with different capabilities to further enhance retrieval performance. In this work, we proposed AntEmbed, a dual-mode multimodal embedding model that supports both efficient discriminative retrieval and reasoning-enhanced fine-grained retrieval. AntEmbed uses a two-stage training strategy to bring original and reasoning-enhanced inputs into a unified embedding space. Multi-positive contrastive learning aligns the two views of each instance, while cross-mode self-distillation transfers fine-grained relevance information from reasoning-enhanced representations to discriminative representations. On MMEB-v2, AntEmbed-8B achieves an overall score of 81.5 with reasoning-enhanced embeddings, outperforming the strongest baseline by 3.1 points. Its discriminative embeddings also provide a competitive alternative when low-latency retrieval is preferred. Further evaluation on MMEB-v3 suggests that AntEmbed generalizes to a broader range of retrieval settings, including tasks related to tool retrieval, GUI control, and agent memory.
