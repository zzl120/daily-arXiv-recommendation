However, evaluating many candidates with full-text inputs incurs substantial online computation and limits serving throughput. We introduce DuoLM, a framework for fast and effective personalized recommendation that integrates a compact recommendation model with an efficient serving runtime. An encoder LLM learns compact item representations for offline reuse, while a personalized ranker LLM combines them with user history and feedback for recommendation. We train both models end to end with recommendation supervision and teacher distillation. The runtime further accelerates inference through virtual token IDs for embedding access, shared history computation, and CUDA Graph replay. Benchmark results show that our framework achieves recommendation quality comparable to that of the expert teacher model. Each recommendation request typically involves multiple candidates, while the system must sustain a continuous stream of requests within a given computational budget. The practical value of language models for recommendation therefore depends on both recommendation quality and serving efficiency. Despite advances in language-model-based personalized recommendation, efficient online serving remains challenging. We identify two primary challenges. Traditional ID-based two-tower recommenders score items efficiently, but models trained solely on interactions do not directly incorporate textual semantics or pretrained world knowledge. This reliance on interaction data limits generalization to long-tail and cold-start items~. Full-text cross-encoders use item descriptions together with user history and feedback, but the resulting candidate representations depend on the user context and cannot be directly reused across users. Compressing item text can reduce online computation~, yet may discard details that matter to particular users. The challenge is to obtain compact, reusable item representations that still support high-quality personalized judgments. Inference engines such as vLLM~ improve latency and throughput via batching and prefix caching, but redundant work can remain in how a recommendation service prepares and submits its workload. A single recommendation request typically evaluates multiple candidates against the same user history. If candidates are submitted separately, the service may repeatedly construct and transmit the same history inputs and incur separate scheduling overhead for each candidate. Prefix caching can reuse history KV states~, but does not per se eliminate these repeated operations. Detailed results appear in Appendix~. In traditional recommendation pipelines, two-tower models encode users and items separately for efficient retrieval~. For models that rely on ID embeddings, sparse or missing interaction data limit representation learning for long-tail and cold-start items~. Language-based approaches use pretrained models to encode item content and model user preferences~, including hierarchical item and user modeling in HLLM~. Whereas HLLM scores candidates by user--item representation similarity, DuoLM lets each candidate attend directly to individual history items and their associated feedback. Prior work personalizes language models through history representations~, and decoding adjustments~. Continuous context compression replaces long text with compact representations consumed by downstream LLM~. KV-cache management and shared-prefix execution reduce redundant storage and computation~. Optimized attention kernels reduce memory traffic~, and runtimes support CUDA Graph capture and replay~. We presented DuoLM, a framework that jointly trains two LLMs for reusable item representations and personalized recommendation, paired with an efficient serving runtime. DuoLM achieves recommendation quality comparable to the teacher. For Books recommendation on a single H100, it delivers up to $60.1 $ the throughput of the teacher served by vLLM. *{Limitations} Our evaluation focuses on Amazon Books and Video Games with 0.6B language-model backbones. Results on these datasets may not generalize to other domains, larger models, or recommendation settings with rapidly changing user preferences. Recommendation quality is evaluated on fixed candidate sets containing randomly sampled unobserved items, which may be easier to distinguish than candidates produced by a strong retrieval system. Compressing each item into a single vector may discard details relevant to particular users. Offline representations also require regeneration when item content or encoder parameters change. Our throughput measurements cover online serving and exclude training and offline encoding costs. Finally, the serving results are measured on an H100 under controlled request loads. Performance under different hardware, traffic patterns, and latency constraints requires further evaluation.
