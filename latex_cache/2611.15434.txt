We propose , a listwise evaluator that jointly models user exposure and item value for re-ranking evaluation. We formalize the sequential browsing process as a discrete-time survival model, deriving monotonically decreasing reaching probabilities that capture the cascading structure of user browsing. We further introduce a multi-objective value estimation module with dedicated per-objective prediction heads and uncertainty-based loss weighting to adaptively balance objectives of varying scales and noise levels. The re-ranking stage must account not only for individual item relevance, but also for inter-item interactions and the sequential nature of user browsing. A reliable evaluator must capture two fundamental aspects: a user browses into the list, and the user derives from observed items. These two factors are tightly coupled: an item that is never reached contributes nothing to utility regardless of its intrinsic quality, making listwise utility estimation inherently non-trivial. Existing evaluators fall short on both dimensions. On the exposure side, most methods model list context without explicitly accounting for the probability that a user actually reaches each position~, ignoring the cascading, user-dependent nature of browsing. In reality, the probability of observing an item at position $t$ depends on the user's decision to continue past all preceding positions. On the value side, existing approaches aggregate multiple engagement signals into a fixed scalar combination~, conflating objectives of different scales and noise levels. We propose , a listwise evaluator that decomposes list utility into position-wise contributions by jointly modeling exposure and value. Central to our approach is the observation that a user can only derive value from an item they actually see: the expected utility of a ranked list is therefore naturally decomposed as a sum of per-position terms, each being the product of the and the item's given observation. This decomposition motivates two dedicated modeling components. For exposure, we formalize sequential browsing as a discrete-time survival process, yielding monotonically decreasing reaching probabilities without hand-crafted discount functions. For value, we adopt a multi-objective framework with per-objective prediction heads and uncertainty-based multi-task weighting. Both components share a unified transformer backbone. Reranking models broadly fall into one-stage and two-stage methods. One-stage methods model the target ranking in a single framework. Early works such as DLCM and PRM incorporate list context into deep models to refine item scores~, while SetRank uses permutation-invariant representations for set-level modeling~. Other approaches treat rankings as structured outputs emphasizing global optimization~, and more recent generative methods produce ranked lists directly~. Two-stage methods decompose re-ranking into a generation stage and an evaluation stage. Representative work includes PIER, which combines permutation-level generation with interest-aware evaluation end-to-end~, and subsequent approaches that improve generation diversity through multi-generator construction~. More recently, evaluator-focused work has explored list-level value estimation to better align re-ranking with user utility~, which directly motivates our work. Given a user and a candidate list from an upstream ranker, our listwise evaluator assigns a scalar utility to the entire list by jointly modeling per-position user exposure and item value. The evaluator is trained end-to-end on logged recommendation data with observed exposure and engagement signals.
