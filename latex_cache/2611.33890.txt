The architecture is particularly useful for knowledge-intensive question answering because the evidence store can be updated without retraining the generator. In many practical deployments, however, useful evidence is distributed across hospitals, laboratories, companies, or devices that cannot pool their complete document collections because of governance, ownership, confidentiality, or infrastructure constraints. Federated learning established a broader paradigm for collaboration without centralizing raw training data ; federated RAG extends the same decentralization principle to retrieval and generation. The Flower FedRAG example provides a reference pipeline for federated retrieval . It queries client-side FAISS indexes and combines returned candidates before generation. This architecture provides a useful basis for studying selective retrieval, but full broadcast requires retrieval work at every available source. Our implementation reuses Flower FedRAG components in a sequential simulation; it is not a deployment benchmark of independent organizations or a distributed Flower Message API experiment. Full broadcast increases the number of query recipients and the aggregate local retrieval work. It also enlarges the candidate pool processed by the coordinator; the effect on latency depends on concurrency, network conditions, fusion, and generation. A query sent to an irrelevant source is still disclosed to that source. We therefore distinguish the number of recipients, a data-minimization measure, from formal query confidentiality. The system problem is to allocate a limited retrieval budget while retaining useful evidence, not simply to reduce the number of contacted clients. Source selection is a classical problem in federated information retrieval . RAGRoute applies a lightweight neural classifier to source selection for retrieval-augmented generation . Client selection itself is therefore not the novelty claimed here. PACS-FedRAG investigates a complementary design: query-dependent semantic matching combined with online evidence and system feedback, without task-specific router training. RAGRoute is retained as closely related work, but is not implemented or evaluated in this study; no empirical superiority over it is claimed. PACS-FedRAG scores clients using a perturbed corpus centroid, historical evidence contribution, measured retrieval latency, estimated payload cost, and an exploration bonus. It selects a fixed number of available clients, combines their candidates using reciprocal rank fusion , and updates client statistics after retrieval. This design provides a lightweight routing policy for a coordinator that cannot inspect complete remote corpora in its intended deployment. Its current evaluation isolates the selection policy in a local simulation. The contributions are as follows: An online, fixed-budget selector that combines semantic relevance with historical evidence contribution, retrieval latency, payload cost, and exploration, without fitting a separate supervised router. A Gaussian-perturbed profile mechanism with an explicit replace-one adjacency model and sensitivity calculation, separating profile protection from passage disclosure and query-recipient reduction. A comparison against full broadcast, random selection, round-robin selection, and static perturbed-profile selection on two biomedical question sets, with fixed retrieval budgets and published per-query records. A retrieval-only privacy-budget sweep and component analysis with per-query records and calibration provenance, with explicit limitations on baseline compatibility and overlap-metric validity, and an assessment of deterministic rerun consistency. The paper does not claim end-to-end confidential inference, a new differential privacy mechanism, or an optimal routing policy. Selected passages are intentionally disclosed to the coordinator, and queries are visible to selected clients. The intended privacy scope concerns released profiles under the assumptions in Section 3.3. The publicly seeded simulation is not a production privacy guarantee. Lewis et al. introduced RAG as a sequence-to-sequence generation framework that combines a pretrained generator with retrieved passages . Subsequent RAG systems commonly use dense embeddings, approximate nearest-neighbor search, candidate reranking, and evidence-conditioned generation. FAISS provides efficient vector indexing and similarity search primitives used by many such pipelines . For medical RAG evaluation, MIRAGE/MedRAG unifies several QA datasets and four corpora and reports both retrieval and answer-level outcomes . These components motivate our use of the existing Flower pipeline instead of constructing an unrelated system. Federated RAG retains source documents at participating data providers while coordinating retrieval and generation. C-FedRAG places orchestration and inference inside a confidential-computing environment to protect intermediate results from an over-curious orchestrator . FRAG studies secure and efficient federated vector-database management and cryptographic protocols for collaborative retrieval . FedE4RAG focuses on federated embedding learning, knowledge distillation, and homomorphic protection of exchanged model information . Other work combines federated fine-tuning and RAG for domain-specific language models . A systematic mapping study identifies privacy-preserving retrieval, cross-client heterogeneity, and limited evaluation practice as central open concerns . These systems span different privacy goals. Keeping raw corpora local prevents wholesale data centralization but does not by itself guarantee that retrieved passages, semantic profiles, model updates, or queries reveal no sensitive information. C-FedRAG explicitly calls for threat modeling and considers differential privacy and trusted execution environments as complementary technologies . PACS-FedRAG therefore states a limited threat model and applies formal perturbation only to the profile used for routing. Federated learning uses client selection to manage statistical utility, system heterogeneity, and training speed. FedAvg samples clients and averages local model updates . Oort preferentially selects clients that offer high statistical utility while accounting for execution speed . Power-of-choice selection shows that non-uniform client choice can accelerate federated optimization under heterogeneous data . Our problem differs because clients do not train a shared model in each query round; they retrieve evidence. Nevertheless, the underlying idea---balancing utility, cost, and exploration---transfers naturally. In federated search, resource selection estimates which collections are useful for a query . RAGRoute is the closest related source-routing approach: it uses a trained neural classifier rather than the heuristic online score studied here . PACS-FedRAG combines profile similarity with observed evidence contribution and system costs, and perturbs routing profiles. These are design distinctions, not measured advantages over RAGRoute. The archived experiments include only non-neural selectors, and the validity limitations in Sections 4.6--4.10 constrain their interpretation. A controlled comparison with RAGRoute would require common corpus partitions, retrieval settings, evaluation queries, and a stated router-training protocol. Routing also introduces integrity risks. A client that advertises a misleading semantic profile can attract queries and return irrelevant or poisoned evidence . We assume honest clients and an honest-but-curious coordinator. Evidence feedback may reduce a client's score after unhelpful returns, but this is not a Byzantine-robust defense and has not been evaluated as a poisoning detector. Differential privacy limits changes in the output distribution between neighboring datasets . Here, neighboring corpora have the same public size and differ in one indexed record. The retrieval-only implementation uses the analytically calibrated Gaussian mechanism for a bounded-sensitivity centroid. This calibration provenance does not extend retrospectively to the earlier end-to-end run. Releasing a new independently randomized profile consumes privacy budget; reusing the same released profile for computations based only on that profile is post-processing. The analysis uses archived records; no new retrieval or generation experiment is reported. Independent reproduction requires the upstream data, indexes, models, and recorded environment. PACS-FedRAG implements a lightweight retrieval-budget decision using semantic profiles and four scalar statistics per client. The published records permit inspection of client selection, label predictions, calibration, and payload accounting. However, the principal evidence-overlap result is dominated by the interaction of disjoint partitions and deterministic RRF ties. The retrieval-only comparisons additionally reuse a Full reference from a different fusion protocol. Consequently, the archived results do not establish that adaptive feedback improves relevant evidence retrieval or preserves answer quality. Profile-noise and component changes demonstrably affect selected-client identities and participation concentration; the downstream value of those changes remains unresolved. Profile protection, query-recipient reduction, and passage confidentiality are separate properties. The retrieval-only analytic Gaussian calibration concerns one bounded indexed record's contribution to a released centroid under fixed-size replace-one adjacency. Publicly seeded simulation does not supply a production DP guarantee. Fresh independently randomized releases need secure randomness and composition accounting, and the transcript containing passages and evidence feedback lies outside profile-only protection. Selecting three of ten clients reduces direct query recipients by 70\ Feedback is sensitive to fusion semantics. With disjoint chunk identifiers, equal local ranks produce equal RRF scores. Insertion-order ties in the archived driver and document-ID ties in the retrieval-only driver select different subsets. Either deterministic convention can make a survival-based reward reflect an arbitrary ordering rather than relevance. Recomputing a same-protocol Full baseline repairs reference compatibility, but does not by itself repair the weak relevance interpretation of rank-only fusion in this partition. A relevance-sensitive fusion control and validated answer-level evaluation are needed. Malicious routing remains an untested integrity risk ; exploration and EWMA are not demonstrated poisoning defenses. The immediate next evaluation should use an identical, explicitly versioned fusion protocol for Full and selective methods, validate answer parsing with retained raw outputs, and include dataset-level and no-RAG controls. A changed fusion/reward policy requires new complete online sequences. Broader extensions include client-budget sweeps, topic-skewed partitions, query-order changes, availability changes, independently remeasured latency profiles, and distributed throughput and tail-latency measurements. Gold relevance judgments, dependence-aware uncertainty analysis, supervised-router comparisons, multi-centroid profiles, and malicious-client tests remain open work. The strongest limitation is measurement validity: archived-reference overlap is structurally capped and tied to arbitrarily favored clients, while the retrieval-only Full cache uses incompatible tie-breaking. Archived answer accuracy is below the fixed-label diagnostic, and absent raw responses prevent retrospective verification of parser behavior. Corrected same-protocol and answer-level results are not yet available. External validity is further limited by a sequential ten-client simulation, two restricted corpus subsets, the first 100 questions per dataset, one fixed budget, coarse hash partitions, fixed query order, and one latency calibration. The 600 question--seed records contain only 200 distinct questions. The scorer resets between datasets. Retrieval-only runs omit generation, and the earlier end-to-end run lacks equivalent analytic profile-calibration provenance. Neither three deterministic seeds nor a duplicate run demonstrates sensitivity to resampling or fresh production randomness. The study does not test malicious clients, profile poisoning, query privacy, passage confidentiality, dropouts, network contention, or composition across repeated releases. PACS-FedRAG is an online fixed-budget selector that combines perturbed semantic profiles with evidence and systems feedback. Its implementation and archived results make the policy inspectable, and the three-client budget reduces direct query recipients by 70\ The implementation and versioned result records are available in the project repository.
