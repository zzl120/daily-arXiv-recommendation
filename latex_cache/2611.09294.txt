We study how repeated updates can increase an item vector's norm and affect retrieval under an unnormalized dot product. A cosine-only loss makes the output gradient perpendicular to the current item vector---tangent to its constant-norm sphere. Training changes encoder parameters, however, so the realized item-vector movement need not remain tangent. We derive sufficient conditions under which it does; every nonzero update then increases squared norm by its squared length. When the tasks share an item encoder, sequential updates from both can accumulate in the same norm, which raw-inner-product serving can expose for either task. This conditional result is not a theorem about joint SGD. Controlled ablations test these conditions. On MovieLens~32M and Amazon Books, accumulated tangent movement accompanies frequency--norm association and additional head-item exposure under raw-dot rather than normalized-item ranking. In a nonlinear tower combining a per-item ID vector with a shared content encoding, active item movement remains near-tangent. We separate its norm change into the ID-update contribution and other output changes, then use a matched training intervention that removes immediate norm growth caused by the ID-row update. The intervention reduces norm-related exposure but also recommendation utility, so it is diagnostic rather than a proposed mitigation. Our logs are recommendation-only: the formulation supplies a shared-representation audit, while prevalence in joint traffic remains open. Search and recommendation are complementary routes to a shared catalog: search starts from an explicit query, whereas recommendation infers an information need from a user and context. Unified systems connect their behavior sequences, supervision, model parameters, or item representations . At candidate-generation scale, both tasks commonly use bi-encoder or two-tower retrieval: a request-side encoder produces a query or user vector, an item-side encoder produces a catalog vector, and a simple similarity enables precomputation and approximate nearest-neighbor search . Sharing the item tower is efficient, but it also lets training on one surface change the representation served on the other. Consider an item that receives repeated search-side updates. Under the conditions derived below, those updates add to the squared norm of the same catalog vector later used by recommendation. If recommendation retrieves with raw inner products, this search-derived magnitude enters its scores even if its request tower is unchanged; normalizing item vectors before serving removes this particular channel. This cross-surface dependency motivates measuring norm growth separately by task. We study the norm component of this cross-surface dependency. If item frequency becomes correlated with item-vector norm, raw-inner-product ranking contains a frequency-associated component: u,q_i = \|u\|\,\|q_i\|\, . That component may improve utility, amplify exposure, or do both; popularity is not intrinsically harmful . A common argument is that a cosine objective has a tangent output gradient, so the item vector itself must move tangentially and increase its norm . A deep encoder instead updates parameters; its Jacobian can rotate the output gradient and mix it with gradients from other examples. The theorem below gives narrow, testable sufficient conditions rather than a description of every modern tower. Outside those conditions, tangent output motion must be measured rather than assumed. We therefore also study a nonlinear item tower that sums a learned per-item ID vector and a shared content encoding. The optimizer rescales the active ID row's gradient by one scalar, so the output change due to that row remains exactly tangent; the shared content update supplies the residual that can break tangency. Tangency of the total item-vector movement is then a measurable property rather than a guarantee. Our contributions are: We derive the parameter-to-output update and prove sufficient conditions for exact tangent item motion, showing when a tangent output gradient does---and does not---imply tangent movement of a parameterized item vector. Under uniform interaction sampling and conditional on trajectory-dependent increments, sequential task-labeled search and recommendation updates contribute additively to one shared item norm. We state explicitly what this accounting does and does not prove about joint SGD or mixed-task minibatches. On MovieLens~32M and Amazon Books, condition tests, total-output diagnostics, exact path accounting, and a matched intervention connect tangent motion to frequency-associated norm growth and norm-induced head exposure, while reporting the accompanying utility cost. We translate the mechanism into task-separated diagnostics for shared item towers and serving indices. Joint search--recommendation research has integrated heterogeneous behavior sequences , introduced paired real-world logs , and shared scenario representations and supervision in industrial ranking systems . These works ask how information should transfer between tasks. We study a complementary question: what optimizer-induced state can be transferred unintentionally when an item representation or retrieval index is shared. The result is not a new unified architecture; it is an audit of a coupling channel that such architectures may contain. Two-tower models support large-scale web search and recommender candidate generation . A request tower may be deep and sequential , while an item side can remain a shallow ID matrix because such representations are inexpensive to precompute and can be effective . Our exact conditions apply per tower: complexity on the request side neither establishes nor invalidates the item-side guarantee. Normalized objectives are known to produce output gradients tangent to the hypersphere . Recent analyses connect this fact to norm growth . We refine the connection by separating the output gradient from the realized output displacement through the encoder Jacobian. In recommendation, popularity bias has multiple data-, model-, and evaluation-level causes ; representation collapse provides another model-side explanation . We isolate one frequency-associated magnitude path. Kim et al. report positive correlations between item popularity and embedding norm, decompose inner-product scores into angular and magnitude factors, and introduce partial or full item normalization at inference . We therefore do not claim the association, the algebraic score decomposition, or unit-item rescoring as novel. Our distinct contribution is upstream: we analyze the parameter-to-output map, derive sufficient conditions for tangent realized item motion, and account for that motion across task-labeled updates of a shared item representation. We do not claim this path is necessary, dominant, or harmful in every system. Contrastive objectives such as InfoNCE determine the output gradient, but the optimizer determines how that gradient changes a parameterized representation. Sparse recommendation systems consequently use specialized row-wise adaptive updates , while dense branches commonly use coordinate-wise adaptive optimizers. Our theorem covers direction-preserving row scalings; the hybrid study makes the residual from a dense shared branch explicit. Separately, training with normalized scores does not force serving to discard magnitude. Large-scale two-tower infrastructure may consume raw inner products for candidate retrieval or pass embeddings to a later scorer . We therefore treat raw-versus-unit ranking as a serving decomposition, not as an assumption that every deployed system deliberately mismatches its training score. Equation~ makes the unified-system implication specific. If search and recommendation update one direction-preserving item row, their tangent energies accumulate in one norm. If either surface then uses a shared raw-inner-product index or passes magnitude to a downstream scorer, state learned from one stream can affect candidates on the other. Neither joint training nor a shared catalog is sufficient by itself: the relevant item parameters must be shared, the update path must retain a tangent component, and serving must admit magnitude. The tangent-update and additive-accounting statements are agnostic to whether a realized event came from an explicit query or an implicit recommendation context. Consequently, a lookup-equivalent shared row satisfying the theorem has the same per-update norm law in either stream. This is the supported cross-task result. Our datasets contain recommendation interactions, not paired search and recommendation logs such as KuaiSAR . Query traffic can change item frequencies, negative construction, relevance supervision, request directions, and score calibration. We therefore do not know whether the tangent path is prevalent or material in a deployed joint model, whether search or recommendation contributes more energy, or whether the induced magnitude improves either task. The proposed task-separated experiment is future validation, not evidence reported here. The exact theorem is sufficient and covers a narrow direction-preserving row path; it is not a characterization of AdamW-trained shared MLPs. The hybrid experiment establishes existence beyond the exact regime for one construction, not prevalence across architectures. Frequency is observational in the data, and $G=D+H$ is an accounting identity rather than a causal mediation estimator. The retraction changes subsequent directions and lowers raw-dot utility, so it does not identify a free debiasing strategy. Finally, head-item exposure can encode useful priors as well as unwanted amplification; application-specific relevance, fairness, and business objectives are required to judge it. Cosine-gradient orthogonality is a property of an encoder output; tangent representation motion is a property of the parameter update. A lookup-equivalent item path with direction-preserving row updates bridges the two, making every step add its squared length to item squared norm. When search and recommendation share that path, their update opportunities conditionally accumulate in the same magnitude, which raw-inner-product serving can expose on both surfaces. Condition tests validate the predicted exact behavior, while a nonlinear tower that combines per-item ID vectors with a shared content encoder shows that a traceable tangent component can remain material outside the guarantee. Its serving effect varies sharply by dataset, and suppressing immediate norm growth reduces utility. The practical result is therefore an audit, not a universal bias or mitigation claim: measure realized motion, separate task-specific opportunity count from energy per update, and decompose direction from magnitude at serving. Joint search--recommendation logs are the necessary next test of cross-surface prevalence.
