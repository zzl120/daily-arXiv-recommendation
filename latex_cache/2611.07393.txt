In this paper, we argue that such cache-to-cache translation can provide utility beyond the original motivating application of real-time agent communication and can instead be utilized for retrieval-augmented generation . Building on the Cache-to-Cache fusion framework, our proposed system CacheGraft encodes each corpus passage offline into a fused KV cache representation between a larger sharer model and a smaller receiver model such that this cache can be injected into the receiver at query time with no need for the larger model. Across five QA benchmarks and two independent locally-run judges, CacheGraft improves answer quality over the text-only receiver baseline in 29 out of 30 comparisons, of which 17 of the 30 are statistically significant. Notably, these gains are zero-shot with respect to the fuser, which was never trained on retrieval data. Notably, this translation, typically accomplished by learning a separate adapter model, is feasible even if the two models have a differing number of weights and layers~. The overarching motivation driving this effort in KV cache translation is the vision of heterogeneous LLMs collaborating in a real-time multi-agent system by passing state via KV cache vectors as opposed to raw text. By operating in latent space, KV cache translation offers the promise of improving both the of multi-agent computation, since KV caches constitute richer conceptual representations of information in an LLM's native language, and the of such systems by eliminating the model's initial step of converting a textual input into KV vectors. The central observation behind this paper is that KV cache translation can be useful beyond the original motivating goal of real-time multi-agent LLM communication. To that end, we introduce the framework applying the KV cache translation paradigm for retrieval-augmented generation where an LLM marshals knowledge from an external corpus to enhance its context before generating a response. Applying KV cache translation to RAG is not immediately obvious because typical RAG deployments often consist of a single LLM; it is unclear where cross-model cache translation even comes into the picture. Our key insight is that we can leverage KV cache translation during the phase of RAG pipelines by enriching the representation of a document through fusing the KV cache of a larger model with the cache of a smaller model that does run online. Thus, the larger model serves as a de facto teacher and guides the smaller receiver model to better ``read" the contents of the documents. Unlike multi-agent communication, the offline phase of a RAG pipeline is not latency-critical, making the overhead of KV cache fusion more palatable than in the real-time communication setting. Moreover, this offline phase may enable using more powerful, but computationally expensive LLM models for indexing. These insights drive the development of our framework. In summary, we make the following contributions in this paper: To our knowledge, this work is the first to make the observation that KV cache translation can be leveraged in applications outside of the canonical motivating example of real-time agent-to-agent communication. In particular, we demonstrate that the Cache-to-Cache fusion technique~ can lead to improved generation quality in retrieval-augmented generation settings. We instantiate using an off-the-shelf pretrained KV cache fusion model published by the C2C team} that fuses the KV cache of Qwen3-4B-Base model with the cache from a Qwen3-0.6B model into the latter's KV space. We evaluate our proposed system} on five knowledge-intensive RAG question-answering benchmarks which differ from the original C2C evaluation benchmarks which largely focus on reasoning tasks. Interestingly, achieves these gains using a cache fusion model that was not explicitly trained for these retrieval applications. In addition to our proof-of-concept experiment results, we argue that KV cache fusion for RAG applications is a rich and understudied area and we highlight several open research problems for the community. Our work in this paper sits at the confluence of three research threads in machine learning and information retrieval that were previously disconnected. First is the aforementioned line of work on KV cache translation~ including the C2C framework~ we directly use in our experiments. We focus on using C2C in our evaluation of CacheGraft because, to our knowledge, it has demonstrated efficacy with the largest gap between the sharer and receiver models and thus constitutes the most scalable translation method to date. Secondly, on the RAG front, a number of works have proposed precomputing and storing a model's KV cache representation of documents and using the cache, not the raw text, at query time to reduce latency by eliminating the step of converting text into KV values~. However, these methods all use the KV cache of a single model and do not consider enriching the cache representation with signal from an additional, more powerful LLM, which differentiates our approach in this paper. Finally, multiple research efforts have also examined the problem of , namely preprocessing the text in a corpus to be more amenable to RAG applications~. can also be viewed as a document enrichment strategy, but in latent space as opposed to at the textual level. In short, C2C enables a LLM to pass contextual understanding to a LLM directly in latent space, by fusing the sharer's key-value cache into the receiver's. Both underlying models remain frozen; the only trained component is a lightweight per-layer network. Offline, for each passage, we prefill $ $ with fusion enabled and persist the receiver-space KV, keyed by a content hash. We also maintain an additional vector database index of document embeddings. Online, we identify the most relevant document to the query based on embedding similarity and load that document's fused KV cache into the receiver and decode the question. Crucially, we do not need anything from the sharer or the cache projection fuser model on the query path. We conduct all experiments on a single NVIDIA A100-40GB GPU and use standard greedy decoding at inference time. In all reported experiments, we use Qwen3-0.6B~ as our receiver and Qwen3-4B-Base~ as the sharer, with the released C2C projector for that pair. The projector is trained on general chat distillation and never sees retrieval data, so every result is zero-shot with respect to fuser training. The results in this paper present a proof of concept demonstration of KV cache fusion to enhance RAG applications and open many directions for future research in this domain. Below, we highlight two particularly interesting directions: As mentioned previously, we consider a single off-the-shelf C2C fuser model that was not targeted for retrieval tasks. Could we improve the performance of by training a fuser model with retrieval-specific training objectives and datasets? How far can we scale the gap between the sharer and receiver model before cache fusion fails? Could we even train the fuser to be more compressible to augment standard KV cache compression techniques? Prior work such as EnrichIndex~ and AutoIndex~ propose to modify document text to improve RAG performance. How does text-level document enrichment interplay with the latent space strategy of our method? Is there a text enrichment method that enhances KV cache translation beyond simply using the raw documents? Thus far, the idea of translating the KV cache from one LLM to another possibly very different model has been applied exclusively to communicating state in real-time multi-agent systems. The key insight at the heart of this paper is that the same idea can be repurposed for RAG by applying KV cache translation on documents offline between a larger sharer model and a smaller receiver to enhance the receiver's online answer quality. In fact, we argue that RAG might be an even fit for the Cache-to-Cache fusion paradigm because running forward passes through both the sharer and receiver model is a negligible cost for offline document indexing, but may be limiting in real-time inference settings. We operationalize this insight in the form of , a prototype RAG system that utilizes C2C fusion to enrich a small receiver model's KV cache representation with signal from a larger model that does not run at query time. Our initial evaluation extends C2C to zero-shot knowledge-intensive question answering tasks not present in fuser training where we find that consistently improves generation quality over the receiver-only text baseline on datasets where the larger sharer model has headroom to transfer. Perhaps surprisingly, these positive results on question-answering come from using an off-the-shelf pre-trained C2C fuser model that was not tuned for retrieval in any respect. Our findings also raise a number of interesting questions for future work, which we outline in Section~, that we hope will drive further work on latent-space document enrichment for RAG applications.
