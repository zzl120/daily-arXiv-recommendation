Knowledge Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Knowledge Graph Question Answering, Multi-Hop Reasoning, Latent Scoring Perspectives, Soft Prompting Large Language Models ~ have demonstrated impressive capabilities across a wide range of natural language processing tasks, including text comprehension~, open-domain question answering~, and natural language generation~. Despite these advances, LLMs remain limited due to the incompleteness of knowledge encoded during pretraining, which restricts their ability to provide factually accurate and up-to-date answers~. This limitation is particularly pronounced in knowledge-intensive scenarios that demand factual precision, reasoning over complex multi-hop relations, and adaptation to continually evolving information~. Consequently, LLM generations often contain hallucinated content, outdated facts, or unverifiable claims, undermining their reliability in real-world applications. To mitigate these issues, Retrieval-Augmented Generation ~ has been proposed to enhance LLM reliability by incorporating external knowledge during inference. RAG allows models to access up-to-date and factual information beyond what was memorized during pretraining. While early RAG systems primarily relied on unstructured text corpora, recent efforts have explored structured sources of knowledge for more precise and interpretable reasoning. Among these, Knowledge Graphs offer a highly structured and semantically rich format, representing facts as in the format of $ $. This format supports explicit relation modeling and multi-hop relational reasoning, making KGs especially suitable for question answering tasks that require factual consistency and transparency~. In the KG-RAG paradigm~, models typically follow a two-step process: , where a subgraph containing query-relevant triplets is constructed by expanding from entities appearing in the query; and , where triplets in the retrieved subgraph are presented as input to the LLM as evidence for answer generation. The performance of KG-RAG methods largely relies on the ability to retrieve and rank relevant triplets effectively, especially under multi-hop settings where irrelevant information can accumulate and mislead reasoning. Despite recent progress, current KG-RAG approaches still face limitations that hinder their effectiveness. First, most methods assess candidate triplet relevance through a single lens, such as semantic similarity or prompt-based scoring~. This rigid design fails to accommodate heterogeneous relevance signals across different candidate triplets, lacking the flexibility to account for diverse relational semantics~. A unified scoring strategy cannot effectively handle such diversity, leading to suboptimal triplet selection. Second, many candidate triplets may appear irrelevant when judged in isolation, especially those involving underspecified relations, but actually play a critical role in connecting earlier reasoning steps to the final answer. Existing methods typically treat each KG triplet independently and fail to model such multi-hop dependencies~. As a result, they struggle to maintain contextual continuity, often discarding useful evidence prematurely. Third, while most recent systems attempt to address these issues by leveraging large-scale language models such as ChatGPT to guide retrieval, which are computationally expensive and impractical for scalable or real-time applications~. We evaluate our method on four Question Answering datasets: WebQuestionsSP ~, WikiMovies~, and the datasets of MetaQA~ in both 1-hop and 2-hop settings. WebQSP contains 4,737 natural language questions annotated with corresponding SPARQL queries over Freebase~, a broad-coverage knowledge graph. This dataset requires both single- and multi-hop structured reasoning and is commonly used to evaluate complex KG-based QA. WikiMovies is a domain-specific dataset focused on the movie domain, constructed from WikiData and Wikipedia, with around 100k question-answer pairs over 75,000 movie-related entities targeting factual reasoning over a curated movie KG. MetaQA builds upon WikiMovies by introducing paraphrased and more compositional questions. In the 1-hop setting, each question is grounded on a single KG fact, while the 2-hop version requires 2-hop inference to connect two supporting facts. Compared to WikiMovies, MetaQA includes more natural language variation and higher reasoning complexity, making it suitable for evaluating generalization under compositionality. Together, these datasets span both broad-domain and specialized-domain KGs, and cover reasoning from simple fact retrieval to multi-hop compositional queries. In our framework, LLaMA and Flan-T5 are used as retriever models for scoring and selecting KG triplets, while ChatGPT-3.5 is used only during the final answer generation stage for some baselines. For triplet selection, the multi-perspective prompted scoring module is configured with $K=3$ latent scoring perspectives by default. The multi-hop expansion process is controlled by a hop budget $H=1$ for MetaQA-1hop and $H=2$ for other datasets, balancing retrieval depth and noise. We cap the number of retrieved triplets to 20 per query to maintain prompt length constraints. Perspective-specific and contextual soft prompts are jointly used to condition perspective scoring and improve relevance estimation under multi-hop contexts. All experiments are conducted with 5 random runs, and we report the average accuracy to mitigate variance. Our retrieval module operates on top of lightweight pre-trained language models, enabling efficient and scalable scoring without relying on large generative models during retrieval. We experiment with three representative retriever backbones: LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA3-8B, and Flan-T5-3B. LLaMA~ is a family of decoder-only transformer models pre-trained on large-scale public corpora. We adopt the 7B/8B variants of LLaMA2/3 as feature extractors for triplet scoring. In our setup, the LLaMA parameters are frozen, and only the soft prompts, projection layers, and lightweight multi-perspective scoring components are trained. As a decoder-only model, LLaMA encodes the concatenated prompt and triplet input into hidden representations, from which we use the latent output representation for downstream scoring. Flan-T5~ is an instruction-tuned extension of the T5 encoder-decoder architecture. The Flan variant is trained on a large collection of prompting tasks, making it effective under instruction-style input formats. We use the 3B variant in encoder-only mode to encode the concatenated soft prompts, question, and candidate triplet. Similar to LLaMA, the backbone parameters of Flan-T5 are frozen throughout training, with only the soft prompts, projection layers, and lightweight scoring components updated. For both model families, we prepend soft prompts as continuous embedding vectors at the input layer. This design does not modify the tokenizer or the internal architecture of the backbone language model. During training, gradients are propagated through the perspective-specific soft prompts, contextual soft prompts, projection layer, and adaptive aggregation module. This makes LSP-RAG compatible with different pre-trained language models while keeping the backbone frozen. We train all models using the Adam optimizer with learning rate $1 10^{-4}$, batch size 20, and linear learning rate decay over 5 epochs. Prompt parameters are initialized randomly and trained from scratch. Each latent scoring perspective uses two separate prompt embeddings: one perspective-specific and one contextual. The underlying language models are kept frozen throughout training, and only the soft prompts, projection layers, and lightweight scoring components are updated. We train all experiments on 4 NVIDIA A100 GPUs with 80GB memory using mixed-precision training enabled via PyTorch. During inference, retrieval is performed up to $H=1$ hop for the MetaQA-1hop dataset and $H=2$ for others, with $M=20$ triplets selected per hop. We set the number of latent scoring perspectives to $K=3$. The dimension of the latent embeddings in our method is set to the same dimension as the retrieval LLM, i.e., 2,048 for Flan-T5-3B and 4,096 for LLaMA2/3. All KG triplets are preprocessed into a bidirectional index, which maps each entity to its outgoing and incoming relations. This index is loaded into memory as a dictionary for efficient candidate expansion at each hop. We follow standard entity-level accuracy for all datasets, considering a prediction correct if it matches any of the gold answers. Each model is evaluated with 5 different random seeds, and we report the average accuracy and standard deviation across runs. For this reason, we set $K=3$ as the default configuration in our method, which achieves nearly the same accuracy as $K=4$ while being more cost-effective and requiring lower computing cost. Results in Table~ show that across all datasets and LLM settings, removing either consistently degrades performance. Without perspective-specific prompts, the model collapses multiple latent scoring perspectives into a less flexible scoring function, leading to weaker discrimination especially on multi-hop datasets such as WebQSP and MetaQA-2hop where heterogeneous relevance signals must be handled. Without contextual prompts, the model loses continuity across hops and struggles to select the right chain of evidence. This becomes severe in multi-hop reasoning QA settings. Notably, removing either type of prompt leads to consistent performance drops across both models. Removing perspective-specific soft prompts results in a larger degradation on both models, particularly on complex reasoning settings like WebQSP, indicating the importance of maintaining multiple prompt-conditioned scoring functions. In contrast, the removal of contextual soft prompts hurts both models on multi-hop datasets, highlighting the importance of preserving inter-hop coherence for complex reasoning. Both components play complementary and indispensable roles in enabling robust multi-hop retrieval. Retrieval-Augmented Generation ~ combines LLMs with external memory to address knowledge staleness and hallucination issues. The framework retrieves relevant passages from external corpora and conditions generation on both the input query and retrieved context. Early variants include end-to-end training approaches~, dense passage retrieval optimization~, and fusion-in-decoder architectures~. Recent advances have addressed key limitations through sophisticated retrieval strategies: Self-RAG~ enables adaptive retrieval decisions through reflection tokens, RAPTOR~ constructs hierarchical tree structures via recursive clustering, Anthropic's Contextual Retrieval~ reduces retrieval failures through contextual chunk embedding, GraphRAG~ leverages knowledge graphs for global reasoning, and CRAG~ introduces retrieval quality assessment mechanisms. Other developments include HyDE~ and agentic systems~. However, most methods are text-centric and do not fully leverage structured knowledge graphs, limiting the reasoning transparency and multi-hop capability. Mixture of Experts has emerged as a powerful architecture for scaling model capacity while maintaining computational efficiency through conditional computation~. Originally proposed to divide problem spaces into homogeneous regions handled by specialized sub-networks~, MoE has been successfully adapted to large language models where different experts are routed conditionally based on the input. Recent advances in MoE architectures include Mixtral 8x7B~, which demonstrated that sparse MoE models can outperform dense counterparts on knowledge-intensive tasks, and Switch Transformer~, which scales to trillion-parameter models by routing tokens to different expert networks through learned gating functions. However, MoE models have been shown to excel at memorization-intensive tasks but struggle with complex reasoning compared to dense models~, suggesting that conditional computation alone does not necessarily provide interpretable reasoning ability. In knowledge graph reasoning specifically, MoMoK~ introduces relation-guided modality knowledge experts for multi-modal knowledge graph completion to handle different relational contexts and modality-specific signals. Different from conventional MoE architectures that rely on specialized expert networks, our work uses lightweight latent scoring perspectives induced by perspective-specific soft prompts. These perspectives are not designed as human-defined semantic experts; instead, they provide multiple prompt-conditioned scoring functions that are adaptively aggregated for KG triplet relevance estimation. This design preserves the efficiency benefits of lightweight conditional scoring while keeping the reasoning trace explicit through retrieved KG evidence paths, offering an effective way for KG-RAG. Although LSP-RAG achieves strong retrieval and QA performance with lightweight language models, it still has several limitations. First, its computational cost increases with the number of latent scoring perspectives, since each candidate triplet is evaluated by multiple prompt-conditioned components. Second, LSP-RAG relies on hop-wise KG traversal with a fixed hop budget, which may miss evidence requiring long, indirect, or weakly connected reasoning paths. Third, our experiments mainly focus on relatively clean and well-structured KGs; extending the framework to incomplete, noisy, or large-scale heterogeneous graphs remains future work. Finally, latent scoring perspectives are not intended to correspond to human-defined semantic categories. The interpretability of LSP-RAG mainly comes from explicit KG triplets and hop-wise evidence paths, while controllable or semantically supervised scoring perspectives are left for future work. In this work, we proposed a novel knowledge graph-augmented retrieval framework for LLM-based question answering called LSP-RAG. Our approach introduces a multi-perspective prompted scoring module that leverages both perspective-specific and contextual soft prompts to evaluate candidate triplets, enabling robust multi-hop retrieval across knowledge graphs. Through the designed hop-aware retrieval and selection process, our method ensures contextual and adaptive evidence exploration while maintaining efficiency. Importantly, the design does not require fine-tuning large backbone language models; instead, it relies on a compact scoring architecture while still fully exploiting the capabilities of LLMs for final answer generation. Extensive experiments on the benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach. It consistently outperforms retrieval-based baselines, including ToG/RoG, even when the latter are powered by larger models such as ChatGPT. Our ablation studies further confirm the critical roles of perspective-specific and contextual prompts in improving multi-hop retrieval performance. Overall, our retrieval and scoring mechanisms enhance the reasoning ability of LLMs without relying on large models. We believe that LSP-RAG provides a general and cost-effective paradigm for future research in knowledge-enhanced LLM reasoning.
