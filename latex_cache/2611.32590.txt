Retrieval~ aims to efficiently identify a subset of items that users are likely to interact with from a item corpus, while ranking~ predicts multiple probabilities to characterize users' precise preferences for candidate items. This division has long been the dominant paradigm in the industry recommendation, as it effectively balances efficient search with accurate classification. However, these two stages are often encoded based on the same user sequence, while maintaining separate parameters and optimization objectives. Consequently, this cascaded design introduces inconsistent optimization objectives, redundant encoding of user sequences, and unnecessary consumption of computational resources. As industrial systems process longer user sequences and serve multiple recommendation scenarios, these costs become increasingly significant. At the same time, both retrieval and ranking are increasingly adopting Transformer~ architectures. On the one hand, retrieval leverage Transformers to encode historical interactions and extract user preferences for candidate generation. Some studies~ further adopt a generative paradigm, using semantic IDs to represent each item for autoregressive prediction. On the other hand, some ranking methods~ employ Transformer to jointly conduct user sequence modeling and feature interaction, which benefits from scaling depth, width, and sequence length. Although their outputs and prediction objectives are different, both stages rely on encoding the same user interaction sequences. This architectural convergence naturally motivates unifying retrieval and ranking within one Transformer backbone, which shares user sequence encoding and provides an opportunity for their supervision signals to jointly optimize the common backbone. Recent studies~ have explored unifying retrieval and ranking within one Transformer backbone. Specifically, UniPinRec~ employs masked action modeling to support retrieval and ranking with compatible input representations, and reuses user sequence KV caches across stages. UniR$^{2}$~ organizes user context, semantic ID generation, and ranking features into one decoder-only sequence, with ranking-side LoRA enabling task-specific adaptation under optimization isolation. OneTrans-V2~ further extends unification to retrieval, pre-rank, and fine-rank, combining shared user context with stage-specific computation. These studies demonstrate that unified modeling could reuse user-side encoding and reduce computational costs while preserving the distinct functions of retrieval and ranking. We find that the ordinary conflict rates in the Attention and FFN components are 45.85\ } In other words, a reliable gradient update direction~ can be understood as one that reflects the overall optimization trend for both retrieval and ranking, without being affected by gradient conflict. This issue is particularly relevant in multi-scenario recommendation, where interaction patterns and supervision vary across scenarios. Consequently, mitigating gradient conflict while identifying reliable update directions remains a major challenge for unified retrieval and ranking. To address the above limitations, we propose a , termed as . Specifically, we construct task-shared and task-private parameters on the Transformer backbone to alleviate the gradient conflict. Wherein, we introduce LoRA-modulated attention for the attention components, which jointly learns task-shared attention parameters and task-private low-rank adaptations for retrieval and ranking. For the FFN components, we propose task-adaptive sparse mixture-of-experts , which combines task-private with task-shared experts. Moreover, we present uncertainty estimation to evaluate gradient reliability of retrieval and ranking based on the historical directional stability, thereby determining a more reliable gradient update direction when gradient conflict occurs. Besides, knowledge distillation transfers candidate preference distributions from ranking to retrieval, establishing an explicit knowledge transfer path. For efficient online deployment, we further incorporate KV cache, kernel fusion, low-precision serving, and cross-scenario batching. In this paper, we propose LazUnirec, a unified retrieval and ranking approach for multi-scenario industrial recommendation. We first introduce LoRA-modulated attention and task-adaptive sparse MoE to alleviate gradient conflict on the Attention and FFN components by constructing task-shared and task-private parameters. Additionally, we present uncertainty estimation to evaluate gradient reliability of retrieval and ranking, thus guiding the model toward a more reliable gradient update direction when conflict occurs. Moreover, the knowledge distillation further establishes an explicit knowledge transfer path from ranking to retrieval. Extensive offline experiments and online A/B testing demonstrate the effectiveness of LazUnirec. LazUnirec has been deployed in a real-world multi-scenario recommendation system, delivering significant gains for the platform.
