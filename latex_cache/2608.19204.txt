CrossQ does not address the broader social risks of retrieval systems. It inherits the biases, omissions and failure modes of the underlying retriever and corpus. Making strong retrieval systems cheaper to deploy may also widen their use in harmful settings, including biased ranking or surveillance-oriented applications. This section provides extended discussion and additional experiments addressing generalization, indexing overhead, related work, failure cases, STE stability and hyperparameter sensitivity. CrossQ is designed for ColBERT-style max-sim scoring . We discuss potential extensions to other late-interaction variants: Some variants score documents by summing document-side maxima: $s = _j _i q_i, d_j $. CrossQ's document context $h $ could condition on which document tokens are likely to be selected, though the optimization signal would require corresponding adjustment to the listwise loss. For models using attention-weighted aggregation , the ``winner'' concept becomes soft attention weights. CrossQ could condition on attention entropy to allocate precision toward high-attention tokens, with the listwise loss naturally extending to attention-weighted scores. When compressing for cross-encoder reranking, CrossQ's ranking-aligned training transfers directly the teacher simply becomes the cross-encoder. However, cross-encoders lack explicit token-level winners, so document context would target tokens with high gradient attribution instead. The core CrossQ principle generalizes beyond max-sim: Identify which tokens disproportionately affect the scoring operator. Design conditioning signals that predict token importance without query access. Train with losses aligned to the downstream ranking metric. We leave empirical validation of these extensions to future work, as each requires operator-specific architectural choices and training signals. To test whether CrossQ's mechanism transfers across retriever backbones, we evaluate on a publicly available ColBERTv2-family encoder at $d=256$, keeping all other settings identical .
