Existing methods narrow this distance by changing how the two kinds of text are represented. Requests are rewritten toward skill documents~, documents toward requests~, generic directions are projected out~, and encoders are trained to cross the distance on their own~. Each of these is a representation choice. Prior work adopts one or two at a time, each evaluated on its own encoder and pool, so it is unclear how much of a choice's gain survives a fine-tuned encoder or the other choices. Nor does it measure the gap, so it cannot say which part a choice narrows. Together these choices form a representation design space for skill retrieval. We combine five of them in one retriever, SkillRep, which pairs a contrastively fine-tuned 0.6B encoder with four training-free modules, namely query-side and document-side LLM expansion, background projection and a BM25~ channel. On SRA-Bench, SkillRep improves MRR@10 over the strongest retriever of its size by $7.02$ points on the official pool and by $8.79$ on UniPool, a $111{,}247$-skill pool that we build from public registries, and it is best on both SkillsBench~ tiers. The value of each choice depends on the others. Fine-tuning, for instance, reverses the ranking of the four training-free modules, so that the most valuable changes from background projection to query-side expansion, and turns them from complementary into overlapping. To understand these phenomena, we measure the gap with three per-request quantities. The is a component shared by the embeddings of all skill documents, or of all requests, so it does not separate the needed skill from its competitors. The is the gold skill's content that a request leaves unsaid, which we measure by , the share of the gold document's content words that the request expresses. is the information lost when a skill is encoded into a single vector, which mixes the part that a request needs with all the other parts. Tracing each module through the three quantities, we first identify which part of the gap each one narrows . This explains how the modules interact. Those that narrow the same part of the gap overlap, and those that narrow different parts complement each other. Fine-tuning shrinks the documents' background, so projecting it out adds much less, and it makes the requests' background specific to requests, so that query-side expansion now narrows it as well and overlaps with the other modules that do. We also uncover the mechanism by which each module narrows its part of the gap. Query-side expansion helps through the gold document's words that the LLM supplies. Document-side expansion narrows the requests' background, and about half of its gain needs nothing specific to a skill. Our code is available at . As skill libraries outgrow what an agent can read in context, skill retrieval has emerged as a research problem in its own right. Several benchmarks evaluate the task. SRA-Bench~ pairs task requests from six source datasets with a skill library, SkillsBench~ measures how much skills help agents complete tasks, and SkillRet~ provides a large library with training and test splits. A survey catalogs these and other benchmarks~. Other studies analyze the setting itself, examining retrieval at registry scale~, the quality of published skills~, and whether retrieved skills are compatible with the request~. On the method side, existing retrievers each adopt one or two of the five representation choices . One group fine-tunes the encoder on request--skill pairs~. SkillReason~ additionally distills LLM reasoning about the request into it. A second group narrows the gap with LLM-written text. SkillDreamer~ expands the request by imagining how a capability would be realized, while other methods expand the documents offline into pseudo-queries~, capability pages~, enriched profiles~, typed pseudocode~ or evidence layers~. A third group changes how the existing documents are represented. SkillSight~ projects out a generic background subspace, field-aware retrieval~, SkillFlow~ and Skills in the Wild~ index different fields and add a lexical channel, and SkillFeed segments skills by section~. Beyond first-stage retrieval, further work reranks the retrieved skills~, routes from a frozen LLM's hidden states~, or compiles retrieved skills into compact context~. Several techniques from general information retrieval address the mismatch between how queries and documents are worded. Query expansion adds to a query the terms it lacks. With LLMs, HyDE~ retrieves with the embedding of a hypothetical answer document written for the query, query2doc~ appends a generated pseudo-document to the query, and query rewriting reformulates it~. Document expansion works from the other side, appending to each document the queries it could answer~, optionally filtered for quality~. Such generated queries also serve to train retrievers~. Hybrid retrieval combines dense retrievers~, which match by meaning, with sparse ones such as BM25~, which match exact terms~, and fuses their results by rank~ or by score~. In the two modules, an LLM writes text for one side in the style of the other, and that side's embedding is moved toward it. The query-side module follows HyDE~. Given the request, an LLM writes two short texts describing a skill that would solve it , which we call , and the request embedding moves toward their mean with weight $w$, which supplies words that the request does not contain. The document-side module is its mirror, following doc2query~. The LLM writes task requests for which the skill is the right tool (one stating a goal, one a concrete problem needing the skill's steps), and the document embedding moves toward their mean with weight $u$. Two skill documents can look similar merely because they share a domain or the library's template wording, and this similarity is the same for the right document and the wrong one. In the embedding it concentrates in a few directions shared across the library, which we call the documents' background $B_D$. Background projection subtracts each document's component along $B_D$ and rescales the result to unit length, $d $. The subtraction removes the score $ q, P_{B_D}d $ that the background adds and leaves the content part $P_{B_D^ }d$ unchanged, and the rescaling then enlarges the content part to unit length, most for documents with a large background. Requests need no projection because the stored documents lie in $B_D^ $, where $ P_{B_D^ }q,\, P_{B_D^ }d = q,\, P_{B_D^ }d $, so projecting a request leaves its ranking unchanged. $B_D$ is the top two right singular vectors of the document matrix after document-side expansion, which we do not center so that they include the direction all documents share. This is close to the standard approach of removing the mean and the top principal directions~. The next few singular vectors also hold background, but mixed with discriminative information, so removing them would hurt retrieval . Background projection therefore adds a second, narrower step. Following SkillSight~, it removes two directions taken from the library's most generic words, those with the lowest inverse document frequency , and estimated on the documents after the first step. This step removes little of each document's length, so it is subtracted without rescaling, which makes no difference to retrieval . Both steps are computed once over the library, so the projection adds no time at retrieval. The modules above operate on the embedding, so they can use only what the embedding has kept. We count a gold word as expressed in three ways. coverage requires the same word in the request. A dense encoder also matches words by meaning, so coverage counts a gold word when some request word lies within cosine $0.8$ of it, with each word embedded on its own by the untuned encoder. coverage drops the threshold and averages each gold word's highest cosine to any request word. Requests and skills are written for different purposes and in different words, and prior retrievers each narrow this gap with one or two representation choices. We combined encoder fine-tuning, query-side and document-side expansion, background projection and a lexical channel as modules of one retriever, SkillRep, which sets a new state of the art on SRA-Bench and SkillsBench with a 0.6B encoder. The value of each module depends on the others, and fine-tuning in particular reverses the ranking of the four training-free modules. We explained this by measuring the gap through three per-request quantities, namely the background, the unsaid vocabulary and dilution. Each module narrows a particular part of the gap, and modules that narrow the same part overlap while those that narrow different parts complement each other. The quantities also show how the modules work. Query-side expansion helps through the gold document's words that the LLM supplies, and the lexical channel recovers what dilution costs the single vector by counting the words a request shares with its skill. A module's gain is therefore best reported together with the encoder beneath it and the part of the gap it narrows. Each training pair comes with up to ten negatives, seven mined from the untuned encoder's top 50 and three random. Training uses an in-batch InfoNCE loss~ at temperature $0.05$, with negatives shared across eight GPUs, for one epoch at learning rate $2{ }10^{-5}$ and maximum length $1{,}024$. The training skills come from a registry snapshot that overlaps UniPool, so the encoder sees part of the library it later searches, but none of SRA-Bench's gold skills.
