They also routinely emit titles that do not exist in the target catalog. The dominant response in the literature is engineering-driven grounding: RecLM-cgen~, logit-space constrained fine-tuning~, bi-step grounding~, and retrieval rewriting~ all aim to drive an out-of-domain rate, OOD@$K$, toward zero. The residual failures these systems still produce are reported as a single binary quantity. A grounded recommender that is on its surviving hallucinations as on its correct items is indistinguishable from a calibrated one to any policy that gates on confidence. Recent calls for holistic generative-recommender evaluation flag exactly this gap~. Whether the model knew it was hallucinating is, empirically, a question about the joint distribution of the catalog-membership indicator and the model's verbalized confidence. We use ``know'' operationally throughout, referring to the model's verbalized confidence channel, without committing to a metaphysics of LLM knowledge. Two adjacent literatures have probed pieces of this joint distribution but not the joint itself. LLM-calibration work measures expected calibration error, Brier score, and reliability against open-ended QA oracles~. Recent theory connects miscalibration to hallucination as a population-level phenomenon~. The closest neighbor in recommendation, Kweon et al.~, decomposes uncertainty against . To our knowledge, no prior work reports probabilistic calibration metrics jointly with catalog-faithfulness rate for LLM recommenders. RecLM~ unifies three grounding strategies; LCFT~ constrains logits to the catalog at fine-tuning time; bi-step grounding~ retrieves candidates before generating; the Alipay search system~ rewrites generative retrieval queries to suppress hallucination at scale; ETEGRec~ learns end-to-end item tokens; and TIGER~ encodes items via semantic IDs. Across these systems, the dominant evaluation protocol reports a binary in-catalog rate, not a faithfulness distribution conditioned on confidence. Holistic-evaluation proposals~ argue this protocol is too coarse for generative recommenders, but do not themselves report calibration. MovieLens-25M~ contributes 62{,}423 movies with rich title and year metadata that recurs heavily in LLM pre-training data. Amazon Reviews 2023 Toys \& Games~ contributes 890{,}874 product titles, the large majority of which are obscure long-tail items. Yelp Open Dataset~ contributes 150{,}346 businesses keyed by name and city, with strong regional concentration. For each catalog we construct an audit set of 300 users with at least 30 prior interactions, plus a disjoint held-out set of 100 users for conformal calibration; per-cell audit counts are 285--300 users after dropping errored or unparseable responses . Items are stratified into popularity quartiles by interaction count; we report head and long-tail statistics in Section~. We evaluate four instruction-tuned LLMs from four independent vendors: Mistral Large , Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct and GPT-OSS-120B , and Claude Sonnet 4.6 . Each receives a zero-shot prompt containing the user's 30 most recent interactions and produces a top-10 list. Decoding is held fixed across models ; the full prompt templates are in Appendix~. For each generated title we test exact match against the catalog; on miss, we apply normalized fuzzy matching to absorb formatting variants. Items failing both tests are coded as out-of-domain. We report OOD@10 aggregate and per-quartile rates. We use verbalized self-rated confidence on a 0--100 scale, elicited via the Tian et al.~ ``Just Ask'' template, applied uniformly across all LLMs. Verbalized confidence is the natural elicitation for production deployment: it works uniformly across closed-source APIs and open-weight models, and Tian et al.\ document that token logprobs on RLHF'd LLMs are systematically over-confident relative to verbalized self-rating---verbalized confidence often logprobs as a calibration signal on instruction-tuned models. Cross-vendor replication across the LLMs in our audit serves as the cross-channel robustness control. We do not assume calibration is uniform across model families; that is precisely the audit's empirical question. The binding constraint on inference-time abstention is therefore the calibration quality of the confidence channel, not the conformal procedure on top of it. Conformal filtering composes on any frontier-LLM recommender without fine-tuning and is complementary to representation-level grounding ; lifting the bound requires either catalog-anchored elicitation or training-time calibration objectives that target catalog membership directly~. Four LLMs and three catalogs are a deliberate cross-section, not a comprehensive sweep. The subjects are zero-shot frontier LLMs used directly as recommenders; grounded, fine-tuned, and retrieval-constrained stacks are the more common production configuration and may hallucinate and calibrate differently, so the results characterize the zero-shot regime rather than deployed grounded systems. Public benchmark catalogs may also not reflect production catalog churn, regional inventory drift, or temporal staleness. Verbalized confidence is one elicitation among many and interacts with prompt phrasing~: the two catalog-anchored ablations of move the channel in opposite directions on the same task (on Llama the catalog-anchored prompt also cuts OOD@10 from 2.2\ The conformal guarantee is on the score quantile and across items; long-tail-stratum hallucination rates may rise above any chosen $ $ even when score-quantile coverage holds. Exchangeability between calibration and test items underwrites it and fails under temporal or popularity drift, so deployment needs periodic re-calibration.
