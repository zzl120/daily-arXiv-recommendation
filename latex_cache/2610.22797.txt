We compare supervised fine-tuning, rejection-sampled self-training, on-policy distillation, and verifier-based reinforcement learning as post-training methods and find that they elicit the missing behavior. The strongest post-trained model, the 8B model trained with a graded ranking reward, gives the best SID-only ranking and matches strong text-reading LLMs whose prompts are about $20 $ longer. Modern recommenders choose what to show through a cascade of stages, and the final stage reranks a given slate of candidates for the user. This stage depends on detailed comparison against the user's history and among the candidates, and reasoning rerankers make this comparison explicit: before the final ranking, they verbalize a rationale a reader can check . In parallel, generative recommenders increasingly represent items as compact Semantic IDs . Two properties of these identifiers suit reranking. First, they are short. A text rendering of a long history and slate runs to tens of thousands of tokens, which is costly and is where LLM ranking weakens ; the matched SID rendering stays in the hundreds. Second, their token embeddings are learned in part from behavior. In OpenOneRec, each item's code is assigned by quantizing a text embedding of the item, and the backbone then learns the embedding of each code token in a pretraining stage that aligns it with text and trains it on user behavior sequences, the stage OpenOneRec credits with encoding collaborative signal . The tokens can therefore carry preference signal that titles and captions do not contain. {overview.pdf} . Our contributions are threefold: We define pure-SID reasoning reranking and study it on a released testbed with paired item renderings, one-axis history and slate interventions, and interface-failure diagnostics. Generative recommenders index items as compact sequences of discrete Semantic ID tokens and generate the next item directly in this token space . OpenOneRec releases 1.7B and 8B models built on one SID tokenizer, together with training data and pipeline, which makes controlled downstream studies possible . One line examines the representation itself: attaching SID tokens to a language model does not by itself make their semantics usable , and the tokens' capacity and decoding structure limit what they express . Another aligns SID tokens with text in one backbone and adds verbalized or staged reasoning before item generation . Because the main decision in these systems remains which catalog item to generate, reasoning quality and decoding constraints cannot be separated. We instead fix one existing model family, remove the generation step, and test whether its pretrained SID tokens alone support reasoning over a supplied slate. Listwise rerankers prompt or train LLMs to order retrieved documents , and recent systems devote explicit test-time reasoning to this step , which REARANK trains with reinforcement learning from a listwise NDCG reward . In recommendation, LLMs can rerank candidate items without task-specific training, but their accuracy is sensitive to history length, candidate-set size, and presentation order ; ALRO trains the ranking directly with a differentiable listwise loss and a permutation-consistency term against position bias . The GR2 family is closest to our setting: a model generates a rationale and a ranking for a supplied slate , with industrial and diffusion variants training the same behavior at scale . Related designs reason pointwise before scoring each candidate or rerank generatively without a verbalized rationale . No system in this line verbalizes its reasoning from item identifiers alone. Wherever a rationale appears, readable item text is in the prompt. Such systems cannot show whether identifier tokens alone carry preference signal, or whether a rationale stays accurate once item text is withheld. In , SIDs are the sole item representation and matched text views make metadata access a controlled variable, so both questions can be measured. Standard recipes for eliciting reasoning include rejection self-training , on-policy distillation , and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards . Recommendation adaptations distill rationales into smaller recommenders and shape ranking behavior with rank-aware rewards ; Rec-R1 optimizes the LLM with GRPO against the downstream ranking metric itself, including product reranking, and compares this with SFT, rejection sampling, and DPO on text-based product search , while the industrial GR2 pipelines mix several recipes . We run SFT, RFT, OPD, and RLVR with matched settings on one pure-SID task, where items are never shown as text, so that each result, positive or null, can be judged against the supervision it receives. Testbed} We want a controlled study of two questions: whether the existing models can rerank candidates from SID tokens alone, and which post-training methods elicit that behavior. Answering them requires a resource that does not yet exist. The same users and slates must be rankable from SIDs, from item text, and from both, so that metadata access is a variable rather than a confound; every other factor must stay fixed while one intervention varies; and interface failures must be measured apart from ranking quality, since a model can break the output format while still holding useful ranking information. Given $X_{ }$, the model reasons about the user's interests in natural language and returns a ranking $ = $ of ten distinct candidate indices $ _j \{0, ,N-1\}$, most relevant first. Ranking quality is scored by the rank of $c^{+}$ in $ $. Because $ $ refers to candidates only by index, the same user, slate, and label can be scored under any rendering. The three renderings are item-matched views of the same example: SID-only , dense-caption text , and combined , which shows each item with both its SID and its caption. The primary setting is : inference uses $ _{}$ alone, so item titles, captions, categories, and derived user profiles are absent, while the task instruction and the generated rationale remain natural language. A gap between two views on the same users is then an item-rendering effect, not a model-family or population effect. We instantiate the testbed on Kuaishou short-video interactions and the OpenOneRec SID vocabulary . Its SID tokens quantize text embeddings of item descriptions, and their embeddings are aligned with text and then co-pretrained on user behavior sequences. Its matched public checkpoints, OpenOneRec-1.7B-pro and OpenOneRec-8B-pro, allow a scale comparison under one identifier system. The test set contains $400$ exact-one-positive users, balanced between explicit-engagement positives and long-view positives, so an end-to-end score never averages over examples with ambiguous credit. Each user contributes one $100$-item priority pool: the positive, clean exposure-hard negatives, and uniform-random catalog fill, with competing positives excluded rather than treated as negatives, so an item the user engaged with never enters a slate as a negative. Slates take the first $N$ pool items under a fixed deterministic shuffle, so they are nested and growing $N$ isolates the effect of added candidates. Histories use the same nested recent subsequence for every $k$, so the history curve measures added evidence for fixed users rather than a population shift. From the anchor setting, $k{=}64$ and $N{=}30$, we vary one axis at a time: history length $k \{0, ,256\}$, slate size $N \{10, ,100\}$, and the item rendering . Two further display orders are reserved for a display-order audit. Evaluation users are disjoint from every post-training pool, and each history temporally precedes its target. This blocks instance-level leakage from our own training but keeps representation-level exposure on purpose, since learning collaborative structure in the SID token space is the capability under study. Appendix~ gives the eligibility rules, label construction, negative recipe, and split manifests, and discusses pretraining exposure . The primary metric is end-to-end NDCG@5, with invalid outputs scored zero rather than dropped from a valid-only average, so interface failures count against a model; NDCG@10, Recall@5/10, and MRR are also reported. Because each user has exactly one observed positive, every reported metric is a function of that positive's returned rank. This is the standard offline protocol for implicit feedback : the score asks whether a model moves the observed item up, not whether the order among the remaining candidates is right, because no interaction log records that order. The audit in Section~ separately judges how rationales argue the comparisons among candidates. Alongside accuracy we log each failure mode the task makes possible. Identity\ counts rankings that return the candidate order exactly as shown, the literal ranking $ $: under shuffled candidates this output passes the format check but makes no ranking decision . Loop\ reasoning never terminates and instead runs to the token cap. We also record final validity and use of the fixed extraction fallback. Main comparisons are paired over the same $400$ users, summarized by a user-paired bootstrap interval for each method against its own initializer; we call a difference significant when its 95\ zero. Every system uses one fixed prompt and one display order rather than per-model tuning , and we audit both. Rescoring under the reserved display orders and under natural rewordings of the prompt shifts the anchor score by no more than rerunning an unchanged condition does, and the text reference's top pick is statistically indistinguishable from uniform over the $30$ display positions. SFT tests whether caption-grounded hindsight demonstrations can teach an SID-only student usable ranking behavior. Gemini-3.6-Flash receives the caption rendering and the known positive, then writes a concise rationale and a ranked list; the student imitates that response from the item-matched SID rendering with next-token likelihood , over $11{,}031$ training demonstrations from users disjoint from evaluation. Quality gates enforce the output format, positive-at-rank-one correctness, and candidate grounding, and answer-revealing phrasing is prohibited and audited. The teacher rationale is thus a hindsight training trace, and its ordering of the negatives is teacher-supplied. RFT amplifies successful responses that the SFT student already samples. For each SID-only prompt it draws eight responses, retains at most the first valid nonidentity trace that places the positive first, and continues likelihood training from the SFT checkpoint; this is offline verifier-filtered self-training in the style of STaR . OPD transfers reranking from a task-adapted model to the Base model through dense guidance on states the student itself visits . A fresh Base LoRA samples a response; the frozen SFT teacher supplies a next-token distribution on each student prefix; the update minimizes forward KL on the renormalized teacher top-32 support. Neither model sees captions, gold positives, or any reward. RLVR tests whether the observed positive and a strict output format provide enough direct learning signal. We presented a controlled study of reason-then-rerank with SIDs as the sole item representation, on , a testbed built for this setting. The existing SID-pretrained models fail at the interface: they often return the input order unchanged, gain nothing from longer histories, and do not improve when given readable captions. Post-training elicits the behavior. SFT teaches the reason-then-rank interface at both scales, and at 8B, RLVR with a graded ranking reward improves on SFT, matches text-reading LLMs from prompts about $20 $ shorter, ranks above random without any history, and keeps its gains at long histories where the text reference stops improving. The rationale audit shows the limit of these methods. 8B RLVR orders the slate well and argues its trade-offs better than the text reference, but it misstates the items it ranks. These results show what standard post-training already gets out of the SID tokens. The tokens carry item-level preference signal that a strong text reader does not recover from captions, since from the candidates alone 8B RLVR beats Random and the caption reader does not. They also make long histories usable, because each added history item costs a few SID tokens rather than a caption, and 8B RLVR keeps its gains at lengths where the text reader stops improving. The item descriptions are not yet faithful, however: 8B RLVR states interests the history supports but attaches them to items it misidentifies. SFT imitates a teacher that wrote from captions, and the verifiers score only the final ranking, so post-training that also grades the reasoning is a natural next step for reasoning recommenders. Until then, a practical design is to rank from SIDs, which keeps long histories cheap and uses behavioral signal, and then write the explanation from retrieved metadata for the few items the ranking places on top.
