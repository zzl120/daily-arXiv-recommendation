This formulation turns retrieval into sequence modeling, enables items to share token-level semantics, and avoids scoring every query against the full catalog . Recent industrial systems further show that Semantic ID generation can serve large-scale search and recommendation traffic . Despite this progress, most deployed generative search models remain closely coupled to the online scenario for which they were built. The coupling appears in three forms. First, occurs when training is dominated by interactions from an existing entry point. Such logs reflect the query format, exposed products, and intent distribution of that entry point, they provide little supervision for an intent that has not yet been launched. Second, occurs when one model is trained to fit one retrieval relation, such as visual similarity or recommendation. Supporting another relation then requires an intent classifier, a routing rule, or another expert model, which inflates system complexity and maintenance cost and lets routing errors propagate into the downstream retrieval result. Third, occurs when model size, decoding strategy, and inference budget are selected for one service-level objective. The resulting configuration is difficult to reuse across a high-throughput candidate-generation service, a latency-tolerant complex search application, and the many operating points between them. Open-ended multimodal product search exposes all three limitations at once, and in their most severe form. A user may upload a shirt and ask for ``sports shorts to match it,'' photograph a pair of sandals and request ``the women's version from this brand,'' or capture a household product and ask for ``a foldable one for children.'' These queries share the same image--text interface but express different retrieval relations. The desired result may preserve the pictured product, modify selected attributes, or belong to a complementary category. Query complexity also varies within an application: some requests can be answered from direct visual and attribute evidence, while others require multi-step comparison of category, style, function, and constraints. A practical system should understand these relations without requiring the user or an upstream router to name the intent. We formulate the resulting problem as : one model consumes a raw image and a free-form textual instruction, infers their implicit retrieval relation, and generates a set of product SIDs. We instantiate this formulation in , a native vision-language model system that scales along three connected dimensions: A reusable toolset performs candidate screening, relation verification, query generation or repair, reasoning annotation, and terminal quality control. The tools are composed into different workflows according to the target intent, source data, and lifecycle stage, from cold start to real-traffic refinement and continuous production. Continual pre-training first adapts the VLM to e-commerce products and the SID output space. Multi-intent, mixed-budget supervised fine-tuning then establishes similar, editing, and matching behaviors together with several reasoning budgets. Unified reinforcement learning then optimizes all intents jointly with Sample--Beam rollouts, which sample the reasoning trajectory but beam-decode the product SIDs, so that policy optimization matches the candidate-set generation used online. Model capacity provides coarse-grained adaptation to an application's resource envelope, while reasoning budget provides fine-grained adaptation to query complexity and latency requirements. These operating points expose the behaviors already learned during intent scaling, so no separate routing model is introduced. We evaluate the system through five questions: overall multi-intent retrieval quality; the effects of data volume, quality control, and real-traffic onboarding; the contributions of CPT, multi-intent transfer, reasoning-budget SFT, and RL; the quality--cost space induced by model and reasoning scale; and online business impact. The system has been deployed in multimodal search applications on Taobao Pailitao. Our contributions are: We formulate the transition from scenario-specific generative search to unified open-ended multimodal retrieval and implement a native-VLM system that maps free-form image--text queries directly to product SIDs without explicit intent input or intent-specific serving routes. More broadly, we provide a practical, industrial-scale blueprint for building open-ended multimodal search systems, spanning multimodal training-data synthesis and quality control, multi-stage model training, and production serving. We develop a three-dimensional scaling framework: a composable data toolset supports intent- and lifecycle-specific workflows; a multi-stage training recipe aligns heterogeneous retrieval relations in one model; and model capacity together with reasoning budget forms deployable quality--cost operating points. We introduce Sample--Beam RL rollouts and validate the complete system with offline and human evaluation, controlled scaling studies, and production measurements. This order separates how a behavior is learned from how it is used under production constraints. Open-ended image–text search cannot rely solely on mature interaction logs. Before a new query format or intent is launched, no direct traffic or interaction data are available, making it necessary to draw on proxy sources. These sources provide useful product-to-product relations, but vary in both structure and reliability. Such relations may be derived from product similarity, co-occurrence patterns, outfit compatibility, and other signals, while the underlying product data may include images, titles, categories, attributes, and other modalities. Once the application is launched and begins receiving traffic, interaction logs offer additional information. However, early user interactions are typically sparse and noisy, often containing misspellings, incomplete constraints, accidental clicks, and exploratory behavior. We therefore define data scaling over volume, query and intent coverage, lifecycle coverage, and quality. The goal is not one fixed data pipeline. It is to reuse a set of operations across diverse intents and stages, so a new workflow can be assembled without rebuilding the entire production process. The toolset contains five operations with different cost and precision profiles. They form a configurable processing graph: a workflow may invoke, skip, or revisit a tool according to its source and target supervision. cheaply removes implausible query--item or item--item pairs using category, attribute, behavioral, and lightweight multimodal signals. Its thresholds and retained distribution are intent-conditioned. verifies whether a retained pair expresses the target relation. It checks, for example, whether an editing pair preserves product identity while changing the requested attribute, or whether a matching pair is complementary rather than merely similar. converts proxy relations into free-form image--text requests. For real traffic, the same interface performs minimal repair of spelling, incomplete expressions, and conflicting constraints while preserving the inferred user intent. generates concise or detailed chain-of-thought supervision when required by the SFT configuration. It grounds the target relation in visible evidence, text constraints, and product knowledge rather than merely explaining the selected item after the fact. is the terminal gate. A multimodal evaluator applies an intent-specific rubric to the complete query--target instance. Its acceptance thresholds are calibrated against held-out human labels, and rejected or uncertain examples may be sent back to an earlier tool. The reusable interface is important because the same operation has different semantics across intents. Similarity filtering emphasizes preserved visual and category evidence, editing checks invariant and changed attributes, matching checks compatibility, function, and context. Adding an intent therefore requires a relation specification and quality rubric plus a workflow configuration, rather than a separate end-to-end annotation system. The tools are arranged as a cascade rather than applying the strongest multimodal model to every raw pair. Cheap structured and textual checks first reduce a large candidate pool, fine-grained visual-language verification and query generation are invoked only on retained examples. The orchestrator also uses the presence of source fields to determine the path. A product pair with no user text requires query synthesis, whereas a traffic record normally keeps the original instruction and invokes repair only when a quality check detects a recoverable defect. CoT generation is bypassed for No-think supervision and called with the requested budget for reasoning examples. This conditional execution reduces production cost and prevents unnecessary rewriting of real queries. Every completed instance passes the module. We manually annotated a calibration dataset independent of the training and test sets to adjust the rubric of the module. By scoring the calibration dataset, comparing the consistency with human annotations, and analyzing the patterns of scoring errors across different intents, the rubric settings were updated, enabling this module to serve as a reasonably effective substitute for human annotators in achieving rapid quality control aligned with human preferences. Generic vision–language models possess limited prior knowledge of platform-specific product taxonomies, attribute schemas, and product SID vocabularies. To bridge this gap, we perform continued pre-training on a mixture of e-commerce-specific and general-domain corpora. The training tasks encompass product-title understanding, product SID prediction, product-attribute prediction, e-commerce user-behavior prediction, and general natural-language modeling. This stage enables the model to acquire a unified understanding of e-commerce products across visual and textual modalities, while providing a stronger initialization for the SID vocabulary space used in generative retrieval. Through the data-construction workflow, we obtain multi-intent training data. Each training sample maps an open-ended query to the SID of a target product and may additionally include a chain-of-thought rationale. The training samples are organized into similarity, editing, and matching subsets. Nevertheless, all samples jointly update a single model and share a unified input–output interface. At serving time, the model is not provided with explicit intent labels. Instead, it must infer the appropriate retrieval relation from the evidence conveyed by the instruction and the accompanying images. Based on the form and granularity of the CoT rationales, we define three prompt-controlled reasoning modes. No-think directly emits the target SIDs. Low-think produces a concise analysis focusing on the decisive visual and textual constraints. High-think permits a more extensive reasoning trace for relational reasoning, constraint resolution, and candidate comparison. Mixed-budget supervised fine-tuning jointly optimizes these reasoning behaviors. This stage enables the model to address diverse retrieval intents under different reasoning budgets, although the reasoning mode is still specified through the system prompt. Every rollout updates the same policy $ _ $. Under the prompt-specified reasoning budget, the policy generates a reasoning trace and then the product SIDs. Group relative policy optimization compares outcomes for the same query. The following mechanisms address the training--serving alignment. Standard GRPO samples each completion autoregressively, whereas production generative retrieval typically beam-decodes the SID portion. To reduce this training--serving mismatch, we construct each rollout in two stages. Given a query $x$ and reasoning-budget prompt $p$, we first sample $N$ reasoning prefixes from the frozen old policy. For each prefix, we then run beam search with width $B$ to generate $B$ SID sequences: h_g & _{ _{}} , &&g=1, ,N,\\ \{y_{g,b}\}_{b=1}^{B} &=_{B}\! ( _{ _{}} ),\\ _{g,b}&=h_g y_{g,b}, &R_{g,b}&=r_t . The group contains $N$ sampled reasoning prefixes and $NB$ beam-branch trajectories. Each branch receives a candidate-level reward $R_{g,b}=r_t $, and rewards are normalized across the $NB$ branches before applying a GRPO-style clipped surrogate to both reasoning and SID tokens. The shared prefix $h_g$ therefore aggregates the advantages of its $B$ SID branches. Beam selection is not differentiated. Since the SID branches are selected by deterministic beam search rather than ancestral sampling, this is a beam-conditioned surrogate rather than an unbiased on-policy estimator. Its purpose is to align SID rollout generation, rather than the reward function, with serving-time beam decoding. Intent describes what a user wants, scenario describes where the model runs and under what resource envelope. We use one architecture and training framework to construct native VLMs at $0.8$B, $2$B, and $4$B parameters. Model size provides coarse-grained adaptation to an application's stable latency, memory, and throughput constraints. Within a selected model, No-think, Low-think, and High-think provide finer-grained operating points. Explicit budgets are useful when predictable cost is required. At serving time, the application passes the raw image and instruction to the selected model. The model emits an optional reasoning trace, fixes the trace when the block closes, and beam-decodes a set of product SIDs. Each valid SID maps deterministically to a catalog item, and the resulting candidate set enters the application's retrieval or exposure interface. No intent classifier selects an expert model. The application chooses model size, reasoning policy, and beam configuration according to its resource and latency envelope. These settings are held fixed when systems are compared online. Across these applications, PailitaoMMSearch2.0 operates as an end-to-end retrieval component: it consumes the user's raw image--text query and directly produces the final product list. For applications with an existing mature recall--ranking pipeline, products returned by PailitaoMMSearch2.0 are interleaved with those from the existing pipeline at the final presentation stage. Following the statistically significant gains observed in the online experiments, PailitaoMMSearch2.0 has been fully rolled out to $100\ traffic in the corresponding production scenarios. We presented PailitaoMMSearch2.0, a unified end-to-end multimodal generative retrieval system for free-form image--text queries and multiple shopping relations. Beyond a retrieval model, PailitaoMMSearch2.0 provides a practical, industrial-scale blueprint for open-ended multimodal search, spanning multimodal training-data synthesis and quality control, multi-stage model training, and production serving. A composable data-production toolset supports cold start and real-traffic refinement, while CPT, mixed-intent SFT, and unified RL train one model across similar, editing, and matching retrieval. Offline experiments show benefits from data scaling, domain-adaptive CPT, cross-intent supervision, and Sample--Beam RL. The reasoning-budget study further identifies a practical quality--latency frontier, and online A/B tests demonstrate system-level gains in production. Our study has several limitations. The term ``open-ended'' refers to the query interface rather than universal relation coverage, and our evaluation spans only three shopping relations in one e-commerce domain. Most ablations use the $2$B checkpoint, and the three model sizes are separate checkpoints rather than one elastic set of weights, so their interactions remain underexplored. Offline results also rely on an automatic judge that, despite calibration against disjoint human labels, may share correlated errors with the evaluators used for data filtering and reward modeling. Finally, the online A/B test measures the deployed system as a whole and cannot attribute gains to individual components. Future work will broaden the relation taxonomy, jointly optimize retrieval quality against latency and token cost, and study adaptive or distilled models that share parameters across serving budgets. We also plan to pair evaluator ensembles with periodic human audits and to examine stability under catalog and traffic drift. An editing query specifies a controlled transformation of the query product---for example, changing sleeve length while preserving its color. The evaluator must determine whether the target belongs to the intended fine-grained product category, satisfies the explicit editing and preservation requirements, and remains consistent with the requested editing direction. Early rubric versions emphasized visual and semantic similarity. Error analysis against held-out human annotations showed that this criterion was insufficient: a visually similar product could score highly without performing the requested edit, while a target that performed the main edit could still directly violate an explicit preservation requirement. Table~ summarizes the resulting revisions. The final rubric first identifies the intended fine-grained target category and extracts all explicit editing and preservation requirements from the instruction. It then evaluates the target using evidence from both its image and title and assigns one of the four scores in Table~. For example, given an image of a red short-sleeve shirt and the instruction ``find a long-sleeve one in the same color,'' a clearly red long-sleeve shirt in the intended fine-grained category receives a score of 3. A red shirt that appears to have long sleeves but for which the sleeve evidence is partially obscured may receive a score of 2, provided that no explicit requirement is directly contradicted. A clearly blue long-sleeve shirt receives a score of 1 because it directly violates the explicit color-preservation requirement. A long-sleeve jacket or an unrelated product receives a score of 0 because it does not belong to the intended fine-grained product category. During data production, targets with scores of 2 or 3 pass the relevance gate, score 3 is additionally used to compute the Excellent Rate reported in the experiments. The tables present the decision structure rather than the complete production prompt, which also includes intent-specific demonstrations and output-format instructions.
