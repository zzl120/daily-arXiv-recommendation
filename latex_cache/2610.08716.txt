<concept_significance>500</concept_significance> </concept> </ccs2012> {Information systems~Retrieval models and ranking} { } Generative retrieval trains a language model to generate the identifier of a relevant document. Recent work replaces the autoregressive decoder with diffusion, but changes identifiers, training recipe and decoding at once, so differences cannot be credited to the paradigm. On NQ320K and MS300K, we train autoregressive, masked-diffusion and block-diffusion models with residual-quantised, product-quantised and random identifiers. With identifier length and training budget fixed, we decode each model in several ways. Decoding alone moves a diffusion model's Hit@1 by 6.6 to 13.7 points. Our reference diffusion decoding, generate-and-match, generates an identifier, then retrieves the closest corpus identifiers. The generated identifier is right for 14--21\ reads a fully masked identifier once, and each document is scored by its codes' probabilities. It matches or beats generate-and-match in 11 of 12 settings. Autoregressive models still lead in Hit@1; on NQ320K, the lead comes from the model, not beam search. Starting from one sampled identifier, one-pass scoring removes 46--83\ deficit to beam search; from generate-and-match, at most a quarter. On NQ320K, every paradigm largely memorises which identifier answers which query: random identifiers keep 83--90\ residual-quantised ones by 3.4 points in the autoregressive model and by $-0.7$ to $+3.6$ in diffusion models; across decodings, AR's gap exceeds diffusion's by 1.5--2.3 points, around our $ 2$-point threshold. Paradigm comparisons must report each paradigm at its own recipe and best decoding. Four design choices bound our results. The first is the models. The paradigms do not share a backbone: AR's T5 is pretrained on C4, about twenty times more text than the diffusion models' OpenWebText, and since AR trained from random weights falls below MDLM , part of AR's lead may come from pretraining. Sizes are also only comparable, not matched , and we keep them below 500M parameters because a retriever over a few hundred thousand documents should stay small; as larger models may generate better, whether the ranking holds at scale stays open. The second is training. AR's recipe collapses diffusion , so each paradigm trains at its own, which leaves recipe and paradigm partly entangled. The third is the data and the docids. A docid is a short sequence of codes, and a language model learns to generate it. Most GR models generate the docid with an autoregressive decoder, one code at a time from left to right. Recent work uses diffusion instead, in document retrieval~ and in recommendation~. A diffusion decoder starts from a fully masked docid and fills in its codes over several steps.
