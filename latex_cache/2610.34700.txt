Across playlist and outfit benchmarks, improves recommendation performance by 63\ Bundle construction completes a partially observed bundle by generating a coherent set of missing items from a large product catalog . Widely adopted in e-commerce and online services such as music and video streaming , it has become a foundational recommendation task whose unordered output must capture bundle intent, cross-item compatibility, and set diversity while supporting efficient generation at catalog scale. Existing approaches broadly follow discriminative and generative paradigms. ID based sequential models capture user preferences from interaction sequences through recurrent, convolutional, or attention-based architectures. However, their reliance on sparse item identities limits semantic sharing across related items. Representation enhanced discriminative models alleviate this limitation by incorporating item features, multiple interaction views, or compositional representations. Nevertheless, they still retrieve or rank catalog candidates, implicitly modeling dependencies among jointly selected items only. Semantic ID based generative models instead represent each item with a sequence of discrete tokens and directly generate target identifiers. Autoregressive generation further captures dependencies among generated items through sequential conditioning. Yet its fixed left-to-right generation order imposes an artificial ordering on an inherently unordered set, resulting in an inductive bias poorly aligned with bundle construction. Diffusion language models offer a promising alternative by constructing bundle SIDs through iterative masked denoising . Their bidirectional attention provides an inductive bias better aligned with unordered bundle construction. Iterative denoising can reveal multiple tokens per step, potentially reducing inference latency. In confidence-guided decoding, the model ranks remaining masked positions by confidence and reveals one or more of the highest-confidence tokens per round. The ranking determines the reveal order, while the number of revealed tokens controls parallelism. Yet realizing these advantages presents two critical challenges. ~Despite its flexible reveal order, confidence-guided decoding tends to postpone semantic-fork decisions. The first SID token selects a coarse semantic region of the catalog and typically has higher uncertainty than later refinement tokens. Confidence ranking therefore prioritizes high-confidence refinement tokens, forcing the semantic fork to accommodate these earlier decisions rather than explore alternative semantic branches. Figure~ shows high pairwise cosine similarity among fifteen generated items, illustrating the resulting loss of bundle diversity. ~Revealing more tokens per step can compromise recommendation quality, stifling the potential for faster inference. Because simultaneously revealing dependent SID tokens relies on a bold conditional independence approximation, individually plausible predictions can combine into illegal SIDs. Figure~ shows that aggressive parallel decoding reduces latency but sacrifices SID validity, whereas conservative decoding requires many more denoising rounds. To overcome these limitations, we introduce , a diffusion framework for diversity preservation and efficient inference. Semantic-fork-aware Group Relative Policy Optimization preserves diversity through fork-first autoregressive rollouts and higher sampling temperatures at semantic forks. Pseudo-trajectory distillation improves efficiency by concentrating learning from the vast random corruption space onto the compact trajectory-induced subspace visited by high-quality decoding, unlocking reliable multi-token decoding. Together, these designs enable diverse and accurate bundle construction with substantially fewer denoising rounds. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: We identify two fundamental limitations of diffusion bundle recommenders. Confidence-guided arbitrary-order decoding prioritizes refinement tokens over semantic forks, restricting bundle diversity, while aggressive parallel decoding relies on a bold conditional independence approximation that compromises recommendation quality. We propose semantic-fork-aware GRPO to preserve bundle diversity by confronting semantic forks while their uncertainty remains intact. It combines fork-first autoregressive rollouts with level-specific sampling temperatures, without altering the bidirectional denoiser or its parallel decoding capability. We introduce pseudo-trajectory distillation to concentrate denoising learning on the compact subspace induced by high-quality, sequential, confidence-guided trajectories. This enables the model to preserve dependencies among SID tokens while resolving multiple tokens per denoising round, substantially improving inference efficiency without sacrificing recommendation quality. As illustrated in Figure~, conservative decoding preserves dependencies across SID levels and progressively constructs a valid catalog identifier, but requires more denoising steps. Aggressive decoding reduces latency by resolving multiple tokens together, yet their individually plausible predictions can form an incompatible SID outside the support of the item catalog. This tradeoff is rooted in the standard random masking objective, which trains the denoiser to reconstruct clean sequences from a vast combinatorial space of corrupted states. Each mask pattern defines a different conditional prediction problem, and the number of such patterns grows exponentially with sequence length. However, as shown in Figure~, a fixed inference policy visits only a compact trajectory-induced subspace along its denoising trajectories. Covering the much broader corruption space, therefore, imposes a largely unnecessary learning burden and disperses denoising capacity across states that are rarely or never encountered during inference. This diffuse supervision leaves the model insufficiently prepared to preserve token dependencies along the short trajectories required for efficient decoding.
