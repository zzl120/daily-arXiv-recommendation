JEV-27B-VL loses on the same three tasks. MM-Embed found that zero-shot pointwise reranking with a True/False readout helps multimodal queries and lowers accuracy on single-modal queries~. With the choice readout, single-modal text queries gain as well, and the losses are confined to image-only queries. The choice compares the 16 candidates in one context, and their probabilities sum to one. The yes/no readout scores each candidate in a separate prompt, and nothing places the 16 probabilities on a shared scale. We hypothesize that this is why the zero-shot yes/no readout falls below the first stage: ranking needs scores that are comparable across candidates, and supervised pointwise rerankers such as Qwen3-VL-Reranker are trained for reranking with this readout, while a general VLM is not. The same ordering of the two question types appears for short videos in a recommendation feed: the developers of JEV-27B-VL report that, on 200 cases built from public playlists, each with 20 candidates, one choice question per batch reached AUC 0.931 against 0.905 for one yes/no question per candidate~. Multimodal rerankers are usually dedicated models. We argued that a general VLM can serve as a multimodal reranker through System One decisions, and that decision tuning can improve its reranking. On MMEB-V2 image retrieval, one choice question over the candidates scores 5.4 to 7.3 hit@1 above a yes/no question per candidate, and the yes/no question falls below the first stage. Qwen3-VL-Reranker is trained from Qwen3-VL, and a stronger VLM is also a stronger backbone for a dedicated reranker. Our comparison sets a zero-shot 27B model against supervised 2B and 8B models of a different generation, so it changes supervision together with scale and generation. It shows that a zero-shot VLM can match a dedicated 8B reranker on MMEB-V2 visual documents today, not that it would match a dedicated reranker trained from the same backbone; JEV-27B-VL, the best reranker on both visual-document benchmarks, inherits the same confound. This view limits the practical value of the general route; describes the test. This view qualifies Finding 1. Qwen3.8-27B has more than three times the parameters of Reranker-8B and needs more GPU memory, and its latency per query is 1.1 times that of Reranker-8B with the released code and 1.5 times with batched scoring . In favor of the position, as argued in , a system that already serves a VLM could rerank with the same model. This view also qualifies Finding 1. Small general VLMs lower the first stage , so Finding 1 holds only for sufficiently large models, and at small sizes a dedicated model is ahead today: Reranker-2B improves both visual-document benchmarks, where Gemma~4 E2B and E4B lower them. This view questions what is new in the position. ZipRerank~ trains for reranking with the readout we use . Our position is that decision tuning can also produce a good reranker, and the two routes can coexist. Under ours, a model tuned on many kinds of multimodal decisions, of which reranking is one, should approach a dedicated listwise reranker on reranking while keeping its other decisions. This view concerns the choice readout. One prompt holds at most 16 candidates under the labels A--P, while the published Qwen3-VL-Reranker numbers rerank the top 100. Deeper lists could be reranked with sliding windows~ or with repeated choices over small sets, as in setwise sorting~, at one prefill per window or set; the decision endpoint of JEV-27B-VL also extends its labels beyond P with no retraining~. Listwise readouts depend on option order~ and prefer some labels whatever the input~, and we present the candidates in first-stage order only. These limits matter for deployment, but they are properties of the readout rather than arguments for a dedicated model: listwise LLM rerankers in recommendation show position bias as well~. The Gemma~4 results of , per task and per dataset: {figures/how_it_works} Multimodal retrieval usually ranks in two stages. A first-stage retriever encodes the query and each item of the corpus, such as an image or a page, independently into dense vectors and returns the most similar items as candidates. A reranker then reorders the top candidates. Multimodal rerankers are usually dedicated models: a vision-language model is trained on retrieval data to read the query and one candidate together and output a relevance score, for example the probability of ``yes''~, or to rank several candidates at once~. In this view, better reranking means training a better reranker, and a retrieval system serves the reranker as a separate model. Reading a score from the model's output, rather than generating text, is not specific to reranking. System One decision models such as Jev~ map a state and a typed question to one probability per option, and are used for decisions of many kinds. General models can make these decisions: open language models serve as Jev-style decision models with little or no training, by reading the probabilities of option labels from the next-token distribution~, and VLMs make the same kind of decision when the state contains an image~. Decision tuning, as in JEV-27B-VL~, trains a general model to make such decisions across tasks. Reranking is such a decision: the state holds the query and its candidates, the question asks which candidate best matches the query, and the options are the candidates. We therefore take the position that . The position matters for two reasons. First, for deployment: retrieval-augmented systems often already serve a large VLM to generate answers. If that model can rerank through one decision over the candidates, the system needs no separate reranker to train, serve and keep up to date. Second, for research: if reranking is one decision among many, it can improve with decision tuning across tasks, without reranking-specific training. The readout itself is not new: setwise prompting~, FIRST~ and ZipRerank~ all rank candidates by the probabilities of their labels. What is open is whether a general VLM can rerank this way without training for it. ZipRerank reports that zero-shot VLMs of 8B and 11B parameters, prompted as in RankGPT, do not consistently improve over the first stage~, and zero-shot pointwise MLLM rerankers have helped only some query types~ or not at all~. In our experiments the answer depends on the question type and on scale. One choice over all candidates improves the first stage on image retrieval, where a yes/no question per candidate falls below it. Among Gemma~4 models, those of 12B parameters and more improve page retrieval zero-shot, and the two smaller ones lower it . We test the position on MMEB-V2 image and visual-document retrieval~ and on ViDoRe~V3~, reranking the top 16 candidates of a first-stage retriever. We first confirm on MMEB-V2 image retrieval that the ``choice'' format outperforms the ``noul'' format asked zero-shot , and use it in all following experiments. Two findings support the position. First, through one zero-shot decision, Qwen3.8-27B~ beats a dedicated 2B reranker on all three benchmarks, comes within 1.6 points of a dedicated 8B reranker on each, and matches it on MMEB-V2 visual documents. Second, the same VLM tuned on text decisions alone reranks pages slightly better on most tasks, and natural images no better. These findings are encouraging but confounded. The VLM we test is larger than the dedicated rerankers and from a newer model generation, so whether a dedicated reranker built on the same backbone would do better remains open. discuss this and other alternative views, and the experiments that would test the position. TypeSafe introduced System One models with Jev, describing Jev as ``unstructured state in, typed probabilistic decisions out'' and training it with reinforcement learning for calibrated decisions~; its weights and training method are not public~. Independent evaluations measured Jev on public text benchmarks~ and in applications~. Open reproductions read the same kind of decision from frozen language models: LLM-as-Jev scores numbered options with a prefilled answer prefix~, Releasing the Inner Jev reads letter logits at the last position and adds a light LoRA recipe~, and AnyJev divides out a label prior and averages over cyclic option rotations~. Deußer et al.\ scored our backbone, Qwen3.8-27B, with this readout on text tasks~. In these works the options are mostly text. PixelJev~ answers questions about an image with text options. We rerank the images and pages returned by a retrieval system, on retrieval benchmarks, and compare with supervised multimodal rerankers at the same depth. Zero-shot listwise reranking with LLMs started by generating permutations of passage identifiers~. Setwise prompting showed that a zero-shot model can rank a small window of passages by the likelihood of their labels~, and FIRST trained a listwise reranker to rank by the logits of the first identifier token~. In-context reranking reads attention weights~. Pointwise rerankers read the probability of a relevance label~. In recommendation, listwise rerankers that read identifier log-probabilities show position bias~. Our readout is that of setwise prompting and FIRST; we apply it zero-shot to images and pages. Supervised multimodal rerankers score each pair with a yes/no or relevance head~ or rank several candidates jointly~. Zero-shot MLLM rerankers have been used pointwise, where they help multimodal queries and hurt single-modal ones~ or do not help at all~, and listwise by generating a permutation~. Closest to us, ZipRerank trains a VLM to rank pages by single-token letter logits in one forward pass~; its smaller zero-shot VLM baselines ``do not consistently improve over the first-stage retriever''.
