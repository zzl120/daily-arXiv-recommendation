These candidates differ in their features, feedback signals, and value objectives. Organic content is primarily ranked according to user preferences, while advertisements additionally involve the advertising value associated with bids~. An item’s value also varies with its surrounding candidates and display position ~. Industrial ranking must therefore model candidate interactions while balancing user utility and commercial value~. Prior studies progressed from modeling candidate interactions in discriminative item-level scoring~ to constructing lists through sequential candidate selection~. Generative ranking~ further shifts ranking from candidate scoring toward list generation, strengthening the modeling of list context and dependencies among candidates through feedback-based learning~ and generation mechanisms~. However, these methods typically operate on truncated, single-business candidate sets~. In industrial settings requiring cross-business blending, the generated ranking list is subsequently combined with items from other businesses through mixrank to produce the final list. This blending changes item positions and list context, creating a discrepancy between the generated ranking list and the final list, as illustrated in Fig.~ . This discrepancy motivates a unified generation over the full request-level heterogeneous candidate set, which jointly determines the selected items, their ranking, and the composition of the final list, without subsequent cross-business blending ). We presented UGR, a framework that directly generates ranking lists over the full request-level heterogeneous, multi-business candidate set received from the upstream stage. UGR extends exposure-sequence imitation with complementary feedback supervision at the item and list levels. By separating historical ad exposure modeling during training from current ad value integration during serving, UGR enables generative ranking to operate within the existing ad allocation and GSP pricing rules. Experiments on two industrial datasets and large-scale online A/B tests consistently validate the effectiveness of UGR. The results demonstrate the benefits of unified generative ranking for improving recommendation quality and business outcomes, as well as its practicality for industrial deployment. For CVR, an item is assigned a label of 1 if a transaction is observed. Higher values of all four metrics indicate better performance. Fig.~ reports the complete scaling results for NDCG, MAP, and AUC for both IPV and CVR. For IPV, MAP improves consistently with the token dimension, while AUC shows an overall upward trend with minor intermediate variation. For CVR, MAP follows a pattern similar to NDCG, reaching its highest value at a dimension of 512 and decreasing slightly at 1024. In contrast, CVR AUC improves across the evaluated dimensions. Overall, the effect of increasing representation capacity depends on both the ranking target and the evaluation metric. Larger token dimensions generally improve IPV performance, while the improvements in CVR ranking quality become less pronounced at larger dimensions. We use a token dimension of 256 in the main experiments to balance ranking quality and serving cost.
