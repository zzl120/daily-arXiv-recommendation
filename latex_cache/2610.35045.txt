Dense retrieval models are typically trained by contrasting a relevant document with one or more documents treated as negatives. The choice of negatives has a direct effect on the training signal: random negatives are often too easy, whereas lexical or dense retrieval can provide candidates that are close to the query and therefore more informative~. Existing hard-negative strategies consequently define hardness mainly through lexical score, embedding similarity, or retrieval rank. These signals are useful for locating difficult candidates, but they do not determine whether an unjudged candidate is a valid negative. Incomplete relevance judgments can leave missing positives or ambiguous cases among highly ranked documents~. Structured domains provide another source of information. Concepts may be linked through prerequisite, hierarchical, related-concept, scope, or level relations. A document can therefore be retrieval-hard while still conflicting with the target represented by the query. For example, a resource about advanced SQL optimization may be close to a query for ``beginner SQL joins'' even though its target concept and level differ. Such structure can be used during training-data construction without changing the retriever architecture. We introduce , a constraint-aware hard-negative mining framework. Starting from lexical and dense retrieval results, removes known positives, constructs a local typed context for the query target, evaluates the remaining candidates with deterministic compatibility rules, and records the rule evidence associated with each selected negative. The structured model is used only during offline negative construction; training and inference use a standard dual encoder. To evaluate the proposed selection mechanism, the empirical study addresses three research questions: - How does constraint-aware negative selection compare with retrieval-rank-matched selection? - What is the contribution of constraint-aware negatives within a mixed negative pool? - How do changes in the structured information available to the classifier affect negative selection and retrieval performance? The evaluation covers MOOCCubeX and Course-Skill Atlas, BGE and E5 encoders~, triplet loss and cached multiple-negatives ranking loss ~, and ten training seeds. The contributions are threefold. First, we formulate hard-negative mining by separating retrieval hardness, positive annotation, and operational incompatibility under a structured domain model. Second, we define a mining pipeline that associates selected negatives with explicit relation, scope, target, or level evidence while leaving the inference-time retriever unchanged. Third, we provide a multi-seed evaluation aligned with these research questions through rank-matched, matched-mixed, and structural controls across multiple encoders, losses, and datasets. Prior work relevant to this study spans three areas: hard-negative mining for dense retrieval, synthetic retrieval supervision, and the use of structured knowledge in information retrieval. We focus on how these approaches construct or select training signals for retrieval models. Dense Passage Retrieval and Sentence-BERT use bi-encoder representations for efficient semantic retrieval~, while ColBERT and SPLADE use late interaction and learned sparse representations~. BEIR highlights performance variation across domains and tasks~. For negative selection, ANCE mines approximate nearest neighbors~, RocketQA combines large candidate pools with denoising~, ADORE selects highly ranked dense negatives~, and BM25 supplies lexically similar candidates~. Negative effectiveness depends on difficulty and sampling strategy~. However, unjudged high-ranking documents may be relevant when judgments are incomplete~, motivating selection signals beyond retrieval proximity. A complementary line of work creates additional supervision for retriever training. Promptagator generates task-specific queries from a small number of examples~, InPars generates synthetic query--document pairs~, and GPL combines query generation with pseudo-labeling for domain adaptation ~. SyNeg more directly generates semantic hard negatives using large language models~. These approaches modify or generate training examples from text or model predictions. They demonstrate that retriever supervision can be improved by controlling the composition of the training data, but they do not generally exploit explicit domain relations to determine whether an already retrieved candidate is compatible with the query target. Ontologies, taxonomies, and knowledge graphs have been used for semantic search, query expansion, entity-oriented retrieval, and explanation ~. Explicit constraints over RDF knowledge graphs have also been used to identify recommendations compatible with user preferences~. Structured knowledge is also used in knowledge-intensive retrieval and retrieval-augmented generation, where retrieved evidence supports downstream reasoning or generation ~. In these settings, structured information is typically incorporated during retrieval, reranking, reasoning, or generation. Educational retrieval provides a suitable setting for studying structured signals because learning resources are often associated with explicit concept relations. Competency graphs have also been used to guide learning-resource annotation with supporting textual evidence~. MOOCCubeX provides learning concepts and prerequisite relations ~, while Course-Skill Atlas provides hierarchical and related-skill structure over higher-education curricula ~. Taken together, prior work has studied retrieval-based hardness, generated supervision, and structured knowledge mainly as separate mechanisms. The gap addressed in this paper is the use of structured-domain incompatibility specifically during offline hard-negative selection. follows this direction while leaving the underlying dual-encoder architecture and inference procedure unchanged. The present experiment therefore characterizes end-to-end sensitivity of the mining procedure. The rank-matched comparison distinguishes the effect of negative source from differences in retrieval position. The results indicate that the difference between constraint-aware and rank-matched selection depends on the training configuration, with a more consistent positive pattern under triplet loss than under cached MNRL. This pattern may reflect how the two objectives use explicit negatives. Triplet loss contrasts each query--positive pair with the selected negative, whereas cached MNRL distributes competition across explicit, cached, and in-batch examples. The present experiments do not estimate the interaction between loss function and negative selection, so this interpretation remains to be tested directly. The mixed-pool experiment separates the performance of the complete negative pool from the contribution of its constraint-aware component. The mean differences between and matched-mixed are small on both collections under BGE with cached MNRL. These results indicate that the effect of replacing one negative source can depend on the composition of the remaining training pool. The structural variants show that retrieval performance is sensitive to changes in the information available to the constraint classifier. On Course-Skill Atlas, the shuffled-graph and no-structure variants obtain higher mean scores than the full structured version, whereas the differences are smaller on MOOCCubeX. These comparisons cannot be attributed solely to relation semantics because modifying the structure can also change candidate eligibility and negative-pool composition. A relation-level analysis would require the candidate identities, retrieval positions, and pool sizes to remain fixed while only the structural relations are modified. The rule-based classifier also records the basis of each negative-selection decision. Selected candidates are associated with a violation category and the textual or structured evidence that triggered the corresponding rule. These records provide a means to inspect the composition of the mined negative pool and the relative frequency of different rule types. They should be interpreted as operational mining annotations rather than independently validated relevance judgments. The scope of the study is limited in several respects. Both datasets are from educational domains and contain explicit structured metadata. All reported results use development partitions, and Course-Skill Atlas contains only 32 development queries. The constraint classifier relies on source labels, aliases, lexical evidence, and manually specified rules. Rank matching approximates retrieval position but does not control all candidate characteristics, and the mixed-pool and structural experiments are restricted to BGE with cached MNRL. Further evaluation should therefore include structured domains, and structural controls that preserve candidate identities and pool composition. We presented , a hard-negative mining framework that combines retrieval-based candidate generation with structured incompatibility rules while retaining a standard dual encoder at inference time.
