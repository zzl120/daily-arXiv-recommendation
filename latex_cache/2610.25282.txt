Retrieval systems are increasingly expected to handle queries that demand genuine reasoning rather than surface-level matching. This challenge is compounded in real-world settings, where users combine natural language with images --- attaching circuit diagrams to technical questions, screenshots of error logs to debugging requests, or scientific figures to research queries. In these settings, identifying the relevant document requires understanding what the image depicts, how it relates to the textual question, and which documents in the corpus collectively address both dimensions. Yet most retrieval systems are designed for text-only queries, and those that support multimodal input lack the reasoning capacity to handle this level of cross-modal inference. This gap is starkly exposed by MM-BRIGHT~, the first benchmark for reasoning-intensive multimodal retrieval. Despite significant advances in vision-language models, the best multimodal encoder achieves only 27.6 nDCG@10 on the multimodal-to-text task --- than the best text-only retriever , meaning that adding visual information actively performance. This counterintuitive result reveals three compounding failure modes that existing approaches have not addressed jointly: ~: existing rerankers apply a single, uniform reasoning pass over all candidates, regardless of how heterogeneous the query's underlying information needs are; ~: systems treat every query independently, discarding accumulated knowledge about which retrieval strategies succeed or fail within a domain; and ~: standard dense encoders are not trained to handle the abstract, cross-modal reasoning that multimodal queries demand, producing embeddings where visual signals add noise rather than signal. Prior work addresses at most one of these failures in isolation~. We introduce (emory-nhanced ultimodal rchestrated eranking gent), a unified agentic framework that addresses all three failure modes jointly. As illustrated in Figure~, MEMORA operates through four tightly integrated stages: ~a encodes queries enriched with chain-of-thought traces to produce a high-quality initial candidate pool; ~an retrieves similar past queries from domain memory and uses accumulated factor outcome experience to decompose the current query into prioritized intent factors; ~a issues factor-specific subqueries, extracts targeted evidence per candidate, and organizes judgments into a bipartite that a memory-conditioned meta-verdict model consults to produce the final ranking; and ~an dynamically decides whether to expand retrieval, invoke the judge, or stop based on ranking margin and evidence support, making MEMORA responsive to query difficulty rather than fixed in its computation. BRIGHT~ and RAR-b~ made reasoning a core requirement and exposed sharp performance drops in existing systems. MM-BRIGHT~ extends this setting to multimodal queries, and the concurrent benchmarks MRMR~ and ARK~ confirm that visual and spatial reasoning remain persistent bottlenecks. Dense retrievers such as E5~, GritLM~, and SFR-Embedding~ perform well on standard benchmarks but degrade when relevance depends on reasoning. ReasonIR~ and DiVeR~ partially close this gap through reasoning-aware training. Multimodal retrievers, including VLM2Vec~, GME~, Argus~, and Nomic-Vision~, extend dense retrieval to vision-language inputs. However, their best result on MM-BRIGHT Task~2 still falls below the strongest text-only baseline, indicating that visual signals introduce noise unless they are accompanied by explicit reasoning. A recent line of work addresses this gap by inserting explicit reasoning between the multimodal query and the retriever. BRIDGE~ reformulates multimodal queries into text queries aligned with text retrievers, learning this alignment through reinforcement learning. HIVE~ adopts a query--hypothesize--verify loop in which an LLM proposes candidate hypotheses and checks them against retrieved evidence. MARVEL~ combines adaptive query expansion with reranking in a unified expand--rerank--retrieve pipeline, while VISA-Agent~ converts visual content into symbolic representations that an agent can reason over during retrieval. Together, these methods show that translating visual content into reasoning-friendly forms is more effective than embedding it directly. LLM-based rerankers~ substantially outperform dense retrieval alone. RankGPT~ pioneered listwise reranking, and Rank1~ and RankR1~ further incorporate chain-of-thought reasoning and reinforcement learning. Query expansion methods~ and agentic systems~ improve recall through LLM-generated subqueries and interleaved reasoning. Memory-augmented approaches draw on episodic experience for LLMs~, case-based RAG~, and multimodal episode indexing~.
