Flow-based collaborative filtering instead learns a discrete path from a sampled source to the user's observed training interactions , enabling inference in a few steps. Existing flow-based CF methods sample sources using global item frequencies to reflect the sparsity and popularity of observed interactions. Each source is sampled independently of its target history, leaving the predictor without user-specific source cues for learning to reconstruct that history. We propose SourceFlow, which brings collaborative structure into the source distribution itself rather than adding further conditioning to the predictor. Like inference from observed histories, training uses collaborative evidence to predict missing interactions. Specifically, Neighborhood Coupling constructs displaced binary sources by replacing historical items with unobserved collaborative alternatives. Collaborative Consensus further refines replacement probabilities using evidence from other interactions of users who share the observed item. Stretched Restoration retains these replacements at late flow times during training, providing more opportunities to recover the original interactions. Experiments on five recommendation datasets show consistent improvements over competitive baselines, including a mean relative Recall@10 gain of 15.7\ strongest flow-based baseline. Controlled experiments confirm that these gains depend on correctly pairing collaborative sources with their target histories, as reassigning the same source vectors across users reverses the gains. On e-commerce and review platforms, recommendation systems use recorded user--item interactions to rank products and services according to individual preferences~. Collaborative filtering supports this task by inferring user preferences from collective interaction patterns . Learning such preferences remains challenging when implicit feedback is sparse and noisy. Generative CF addresses this problem by modeling user interaction distributions, with methods ranging from variational autoencoders to diffusion models . DiffRec, for example, reconstructs user histories through iterative denoising~. Gaussian corruption converts binary interaction vectors into continuous states. Such continuous values cannot directly signal the occurrence of an interaction, as binary implicit feedback does~. Flow matching offers an alternative by learning a time-dependent vector field that transports an explicit source distribution toward the target distribution. FlowCF brings this framework to collaborative filtering through a behavior-guided Bernoulli prior and discrete interpolation, which preserves binary interaction states while enabling few-step inference~. CoFlowCF further introduces contrastive supervision over hidden representations to improve user-level discrimination while capturing shared interests~. FlowCF and CoFlowCF sample training sources from global item frequencies to reflect interaction sparsity and popularity~. At inference, however, these models start from a user's observed history and rely on its preference cues to rank unobserved items. During training, sources are sampled independently of the target history (the paired user's observed training history to be reconstructed). These sources therefore provide no user-specific cues for reconstruction. This raises a central question: how can source design help flow-based CF learn user preferences more effectively? Figure~ illustrates the requirements for this source design. Copying the target history leaves no reconstruction task. Gaussian perturbations create a reconstruction task by adding noise to binary histories. The continuous states can obscure the preference signals~. A suitable source should be binary, make reconstruction non-trivial, and retain user-specific collaborative cues for reconstruction. We propose to reshape the reconstruction task through source design rather than compensate for missing user-specific source cues with additional predictor conditioning. SourceFlow replaces observed items in the paired user's history with unobserved collaborative alternatives from the training interaction graph. The resulting sources share no items with their target histories, yet retain the paired user's collaborative preference structure for reconstruction . Just as inference starts from a user's observed history to rank unobserved items, training starts from user-specific collaborative cues to reconstruct the target history. CDAE reconstructs histories from corrupted inputs~, while variational models such as Mult-VAE, MacridVAE, and RecVAE learn latent preference representations for reconstruction~. Diffusion-based CF trains models to reconstruct histories from states at different perturbation levels. The perturbation creates a reconstruction task, but excessive noise can erase personalized information. DiffRec therefore limits Gaussian corruption to retain preference signals in the perturbed history~. Collaborative structure can also inform the reconstruction process. GiffCF constructs perturbed states by smoothing interaction histories over an item--item graph, yielding continuous preference signals~. CF-Diff supplies multi-hop collaborative connectivity as a condition for denoising, providing information beyond the user's direct interactions~. These diffusion models reconstruct histories from continuous states, with user information carried by the perturbed input or supplied separately as a denoising condition. Flow matching offers deterministic transport through a learned velocity field along an explicit source--target path. FlowCF adapts this formulation to collaborative filtering with a Bernoulli source parameterized by global item frequencies and a mask-based interpolation whose states remain binary, enabling few-step reconstruction of user histories~. CoFlowCF retains the same source and discrete path, but augments the flow encoder with dropout-based contrastive views to improve inter-user discrimination without directly separating scores for shared items~. Related flow models have also appeared in sequential recommendation. FMRec uses straight trajectories and deterministic Euler sampling for target-item generation~. FAVE replaces Gaussian noise in its consolidation stage with a semantic anchor derived from the masked interaction history, then learns an average velocity with a straightness constraint for one-step prediction~. Beyond recommendation, flow matching commonly trains on independently paired samples from a simple source and the data distribution~. Changing this joint coupling can simplify the regression target without changing either endpoint marginal . OT-CFM constructs minibatch optimal-transport pairings to obtain simpler flows, while Multisample Flow Matching generalizes such pairings and shows lower gradient variance, straighter flows, and stronger few-step generation~. Stochastic interpolants with data-dependent couplings directly sample the base conditional on a target observation, enabling conditional transport under the same squared-error objective~. For conditional generation, C$^2$OT adds condition-aware transport costs to avoid the train--test mismatch induced by condition-agnostic pairings~. A complementary line changes the source distribution itself. Conditional-prior flow matching centers a source distribution near a target-space point predicted from the condition~, whereas CSFM learns a condition-dependent source and stabilizes it through variance regularization and directional alignment~.
