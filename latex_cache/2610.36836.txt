When incorporating multimodal information, existing multi-scenario recommendation methods typically treat textual and visual representations as generic input features and directly feed them into their original parameter-sharing architectures. However, this extension may not fully accommodate the different modeling requirements of collaborative and multimodal information and may not be the best modeling paradigm. Our controlled experiments show that directly adopting these architectures is not the optimal modeling approach. To this end, we propose Dual-Branch Modeling with Triadic Factorization , a framework for multimodal multi-scenario recommendation comprising a collaborative branch and a multimodal branch. Specifically, the collaborative branch learns structured collaborative preference representations through CANDECOMP/PARAFAC decomposition of user–scenario–item interactions and residual quantization, thereby capturing cross-scenario commonalities and scenario-specific differences. The multimodal branch leverages the learned user and scenario preferences to guide adaptive modality fusion; additionally, item-level contrastive learning aligns collaborative item preference representations with multimodal representations to facilitate effective collaboration between the two branches. Extensive experiments on two real-world datasets, AntM$^2$C and Amazon, demonstrate that DuTri outperforms existing multimodal and multi-scenario recommendation baselines in overall recommendation performance, validating the effectiveness of the proposed framework. Deep Recommender Systems have been widely deployed on online platforms, where they alleviate information overload and improve user experience. As internet services evolve into increasingly diversified commercial ecosystems, industry has a growing demand for a unified recommender that can serve multiple scenarios with different user behaviors and data distributions~. This trend has motivated research on multi-scenario recommendation , which aims to jointly model cross-scenario commonalities and scenario-specific characteristics within a unified framework~. Meanwhile, multimodal information, such as item titles and images, provides rich semantic signals for modeling item characteristics and user preferences, and has demonstrated effectiveness in click-through rate prediction~. By capturing content-level semantics beyond sparse ID features, such information can further facilitate knowledge transfer and improve generalization~. Therefore, incorporating multimodal information into multi-scenario recommender systems is important for improving industrial recommendation performance. Integrating multimodal information into multi-scenario recommendation requires capturing cross-scenario shared preferences and scenario-specific variations while effectively leveraging multimodal semantics. When incorporating multimodal information, existing multi-scenario recommendation methods often treat it as generic input features and directly reuse their original parameter-sharing architectures to model cross-scenario commonalities and scenario-specific characteristics~. However, multimodal and collaborative information differ in their representation spaces and semantic content, and directly reusing the original parameter-sharing architectures may not fully accommodate their different modeling requirements, making this extension potentially suboptimal. To examine the effectiveness of this extension, we conduct controlled experiments comparing conventional MSR backbones with scenario-specific prediction networks under ID-only and multimodal settings. As illustrated in Fig.~, we extend three representative MSR models with multimodal features while preserving their original parameter-sharing structures ). For comparison, we replace their MSR backbones with scenario-specific MLPs ), while retaining shared ID embeddings and feature-fusion layers. Both strategies are evaluated under ID-only and multimodal input settings, with identical input features, fusion methods, and training and evaluation configurations within each setting. As shown in Fig.~, the evaluated MSR models outperform Scenario MLP under ID-only inputs on AntM$^2$C, indicating that their cross-scenario sharing mechanisms offer advantages in this setting. However, after introducing multimodal information, Scenario MLP achieves higher mean AUC than all three MSR models across every scenario. A similar trend is observed on Amazon, as reported in Appendix~. These results indicate that the advantages of conventional cross-scenario sharing mechanisms under ID-only inputs do not necessarily carry over to multimodal settings, suggesting that directly reusing the original parameter-sharing architectures may not be the best modeling strategy. This motivates us to explore a dual-branch architecture in which the collaborative branch learns collaborative preferences that capture cross-scenario commonalities and scenario-specific variations, while the multimodal branch uses these preferences to guide adaptive multimodal fusion ). To this end, we propose Dual-Branch Modeling with Triadic Factorization . This framework comprises interconnected collaborative and multimodal branches responsible for collaborative preference learning and multimodal fusion, respectively ). Unlike approaches that directly reuse existing parameter-sharing architectures, DuTri learns collaborative preferences from user--scenario--item interactions while modeling multimodal information through a complementary multimodal branch. Specifically, the collaborative branch employs CANDECOMP/PARAFAC decomposition and residual quantization to learn structured collaborative preference representations. By sharing user and item factors across scenarios and using scenario factors to modulate their interactions, this branch captures cross-scenario commonalities and scenario-specific variations within collaborative preference learning. Meanwhile, the multimodal branch leverages the learned user and scenario preferences to guide adaptive fusion of item ID, textual, and visual features, allowing modality contributions to vary across users and scenarios. Furthermore, item-level contrastive learning aligns collaborative item preference representations with multimodal representations, facilitating information exchange between the two branches. The resulting representations are used for scenario-specific prediction. Our main contributions are as follows: We identify a limitation of directly reusing existing parameter-sharing architectures in multimodal multi-scenario recommendation: conventional cross-scenario sharing mechanisms that are effective under ID-only inputs may lose their advantages when applied to fused multimodal representations, motivating a reconsideration of how collaborative preference learning and multimodal fusion should be organized. We propose DuTri, an interconnected dual-branch framework that learns structured collaborative preference representations directly from user--scenario--item interactions through CP-based triadic factorization and residual quantization, while capturing cross-scenario commonalities and scenario-specific variations. The learned preferences further guide adaptive multimodal fusion and facilitate item-level cross-branch alignment. Experiments on AntM$^2$C and Amazon demonstrate that DuTri outperforms the evaluated multimodal and multi-scenario recommendation baselines, with ablation studies further validating the effectiveness of its key components. We review two lines of research most relevant to our work: multi-scenario recommendation and multimodal recommender systems. Multi-scenario recommendation jointly models multiple business scenarios that differ in user groups and data distributions~. Early studies mainly focus on balancing scenario commonality and specificity. For example, HMoE~ models task relationships implicitly in the feature space and explicitly in the label space, while STAR~ pairs a global center module with scenario-specific branches. More recent work explores finer-grained scenario relationships. M2M~ models inter-scenario dependencies with meta-attention, AdaptDHM~ dynamically assigns samples to latent domains, DFFM~ models scenario-aware feature interactions, and HierRec~ captures hierarchical correlations among scenarios. Other studies investigate scenario commonality from the data and feature perspectives, such as adaptive selection of shared instances in SSIM~ and instance-level embedding optimization in MultiEmb~. Most of these methods are designed around ID-based features and do not explicitly address multimodal fusion. Related context-aware recommendation methods, such as iTALS~, use tensor factorization to model user--item--context interactions. AMS~ generates sample-level modality activation masks conditioned on scenario and modality information, and decouples its modality fusion module from the MSR backbone. However, iTALS does not address multimodal fusion, while AMS focuses on modality scheduling without explicitly aligning modality representations with separately modeled collaborative preferences. DuTri organizes cross-scenario sharing through shared user and item preference factors and scenario factors that modulate their interactions, and uses this preference structure to connect separate collaborative and multimodal branches. Multimodal recommendation methods draw on textual, visual, and other heterogeneous signals to improve user-interest modeling. These methods largely center on cross-modal interaction and alignment, efficient modality fusion, and the transfer of semantic knowledge~. MARN~ separates modality-shared representations from modality-specific ones, whereas GMMF~ derives user-specific fusion weights. MAKE~ and EASE~ enhance representation generalization through semantic-aware pre-training and LLM-guided adapters, respectively. DMF~ models fine-grained interactions between ID-based collaborative representations and multimodal representations, while Diff-MSIN~ explores cross-modal synergy and interactions. SOLID~ and PSRQ~ adopt semantic codebooks or residual quantization to learn multimodal representations. However, these studies primarily focus on multimodal representation learning and fusion. DuTri explicitly incorporates cross-scenario collaborative preferences into multimodal modeling: user and scenario factors jointly condition the fusion of ID, textual, and visual representations, while item factors support alignment between collaborative preferences and modality representations. For Amazon, we construct three category-based scenarios using Sports and Outdoors, Movies and TV, and Books. The Amazon protocol enforces temporal ordering within each pair and does not impose a common temporal cutoff across scenarios. Overall performance is summarized using sample-weighted AUC , with weights determined by the number of test instances in each scenario. Although existing multimodal recommendation methods effectively model cross-modal interactions and can capture user-level modality preferences to some extent, they are primarily designed for single-scenario recommendation and lack explicit joint modeling of cross-scenario shared preferences and scenario-specific preferences. In contrast, DuTri learns collaborative preferences from user--scenario--item interactions and jointly uses user and scenario preferences to guide multimodal fusion. This allows the contributions of different modalities to adapt to the current user and scenario, incorporating preferences learned from cross-scenario interactions into multimodal modeling. The experimental results show that DuTri achieves higher mean WAUC than the evaluated multi-scenario baselines on both datasets, also outperforming Scenario MLP and AMS, which explicitly models modality scheduling. Existing multi-scenario recommendation methods effectively capture cross-scenario commonality and scenario differences through parameter sharing. However, under the early-fusion setting adopted in our evaluation, they incorporate multimodal information as additional inputs while retaining their original sharing architectures, without dedicated mechanisms for connecting collaborative preference learning with multimodal fusion. The competitive performance of Scenario MLP further indicates that sharing the prediction backbone after fusion does not necessarily outperform scenario-specific prediction networks under multimodal inputs. In contrast, DuTri shares user and item preference factors in the collaborative branch and uses scenario factors to modulate their interactions. It further uses user and scenario preferences to guide fusion in the multimodal branch and item preferences for cross-branch alignment, connecting cross-scenario collaborative modeling with multimodal content through preference representations. To examine alternative ways of incorporating multimodal information into multi-scenario recommendation, we construct four strategies using HierRec as a common backbone on AntM$^2$C: ``Direct Input'' feeds fused item ID, textual, and visual representations into the backbone; ``Quantized Input'' discretizes these representations and incorporates the corresponding codes as inputs, inspired by the semantic-ID strategy in RQ-GMM~; ``Shared-Specific Quant'' applies a shared quantization stage followed by a scenario-specific one, following the shared-bottom organization of shared and specialized components; and ``Shared-Expert Quant'' combines shared codebooks with dynamically selected expert codebooks, inspired by the shared-expert tokenization in UniTok~. These strategies adapt the above ideas to our setting. All strategies and DuTri use the same input features, data splits, and evaluation protocol. As shown in Table~, ``Quantized Input'' achieves lower mean WAUC than ``Direct Input'', suggesting that introducing quantized multimodal representations does not necessarily improve recommendation performance in this setting. Both ``Shared-Specific Quant'' and ``Shared-Expert Quant'' achieve higher mean WAUC than ``Direct Input'', supporting the consideration of cross-scenario sharing when organizing quantized representations. DuTri achieves the highest mean WAUC among the evaluated strategies. While the alternatives incorporate multimodal information through input representations and codebook-sharing mechanisms, DuTri learns a triadic preference structure from interaction feedback and uses it to connect collaborative and multimodal modeling through alignment and conditional fusion. Its overall advantage supports using cross-scenario collaborative preferences to guide multimodal modeling in the evaluated setting.
