We introduce LLM-IRA, the first extension of the quantum-inspired interactive ranking aggregation framework~ to document reranking. By leveraging large language models solely for pairwise judgments on high-disagreement document pairs, our method learns fusion weights in a principled, interactive way. The final ranking is a simple, ultra-fast pointwise linear fusion of scores, no full pairwise matrix or expensive listwise prompting is required. Experiments on the BEIR benchmark and TREC Deep Learning tracks with an open-weight instruction-tuned judge show that LLM-IRA matches or numerically exceeds strong static fusion baselines on 5 of 6 evaluated benchmarks. We report two weight-update variants: momentum blending and a full accumulated-history refit, which together reach statistical significance over the best unsupervised aggregator on 4 of 6 benchmarks . On the benchmarks we profiled , it already clears the best unsupervised baseline within as few as 5 LLM calls per query, well before exhausting its typical $ $25--30 call budget. LLM-IRA also achieves cost-effectiveness comparable to state-of-the-art zero-shot LLM rerankers at an order of magnitude fewer LLM calls, and requires no training data or model fine-tuning. Ranking Aggregation is a crucial technique in information retrieval, designed to fuse multiple search strategies to improve robustness and generalization, as no single base ranker performs optimally across all domains. Existing RA methods broadly divide into two categories: unsupervised techniques such as Reciprocal Rank Fusion and CombSUM, which are simple and cost-effective but lack external guidance for optimization; and fully-supervised approaches, including learning-to-rank models or zero-shot listwise LLM rerankers . While the latter deliver superior performance, they demand either extensive labeled data for training or prohibitive inference costs through massive prompting. Recently, a Quantum-Inspired Interactive Ranking Aggregation framework was proposed for image retrieval , demonstrating that RA can be modeled as fusing multiple ``incomplete observations" into a ``complete observation" using quantum theory. QI-IRA achieved state-of-the-art performance with remarkably low labeling costs . However, QI-IRA's pointwise relevance feedback is inadequate for document reranking, where strict relative ordering is required. In this paper, we propose LLM-IRA, a novel framework that extends the quantum RA analogy from point-wise absolute relevance to pairwise relative relevance. We model document pairs as entangled quantum systems and replace costly human feedback with an efficient interactive LLM loop that dynamically samples only the highest-disagreement pairs. This enables our method to learn optimal fusion weights for heterogeneous base rankers while retaining the simplicity and speed of linear pointwise fusion. We report two weight-update rules for turning LLM preferences into fusion weights: and a , and find the latter the stronger configuration overall. Experiments across two independent LLM judges demonstrate that LLM-IRA matches or outperforms static fusion baselines on the majority of tested benchmarks under either rule, and matches zero-shot LLM rerankers at a fraction of the cost. RA combines outputs from multiple base rankers to improve overall performance. Unsupervised methods, such as RRF and CombSum/CombMNZ~, are lightweight and widely used in hybrid retrieval pipelines, requiring no training or labels. These approaches fuse ranks or scores heuristically and perform well in diverse settings but lack adaptability to query-specific nuances. Supervised RA treats fusion as a learning-to-rank problem. Classic models like RankNet~ use pairwise losses to learn preferences, while LambdaMART~ optimizes listwise metrics via gradient boosting. These methods achieve high accuracy with sufficient labeled data but incur heavy annotation and training costs, limiting their use in zero-shot or dynamic scenarios. The rise of LLMs has produced a parallel and highly practical family of zero-shot rerankers. These fall into three main paradigms: pointwise scoring , pairwise comparison , and listwise reranking . While effective on benchmarks like BEIR and MS MARCO, these methods scale poorly: listwise prompting requires linear or quadratic LLM calls relative to candidate size, leading to high token costs. LLM-IRA uses LLMs more sparingly, only for targeted pairwise judgments on disagreed pairs, to guide fusion weights, achieving comparable performance at a fraction of the expense. has evolved from classical Rocchio-style pseudo-relevance feedback and its neural variants toward LLM-as-Judge techniques . Recent studies explore pairwise and listwise LLM judgments for query rewriting or preference alignment , yet they typically treat the LLM as a full reranker or query expander rather than as a lightweight supervisor that learns fusion weights for cheaper base retrievers. Most closely related to our work, LLM-RankFusion~ uses in-context learning with calibration to estimate pairwise preference probabilities and aggregate multiple rankers. While it achieves strong effectiveness, it requires comparing pairs in the candidate pool , making it impractical for large pools. In contrast, LLM-IRA queries the LLM only on the small subset of pairs where base rankers disagree, reducing cost by an order of magnitude while maintaining competitive effectiveness. While unsupervised RA is efficient but static, supervised LTR and LLM rerankers are powerful but costly, and interactive feedback lacks principled fusion mechanisms. Starting with uniform weights, we compute an initial fused ranking. From the top-$r$ documents, we sample $K$ pairs with high ranker disagreement . These pairs are queried to the LLM: ``Given the query, which document is more relevant: A or B?'' The LLM's judgment acts as a definitive measurement, collapsing the pair's state. LLM-IRA enables efficient fusion of heterogeneous retrievers with minimal LLM feedback. While our framework shows strong potential, it presents several limitations for future research: Even with capable 8B--14B instruction-tuned judges, pairwise preferences on contentious, high-disagreement document pairs remain noisy, and Covid shows this can narrow or flip the outcome against LLM-IRA relative to a strong static aggregator . Because the system directly translates the LLM's absolute judgment into mathematical weight adjustments, noisy or incorrect feedback can heavily penalize superior base rankers; a confidence-gated update mitigates but does not eliminate this risk, and the appropriate confidence threshold is judge-specific and currently set empirically rather than calibrated automatically. The current 2D quantum state space strictly models relative preference . In reality, both documents might be entirely irrelevant to the query. Future work must mathematically integrate a ``both irrelevant" dimension into the model without violating the fundamental quantum rule that the sum of squared probability amplitudes must equal one. To maintain computational efficiency across large datasets, the final global inference step abandons the pairwise logic and reverts to standard 1D pointwise linear weighting. Future work could explore scalable methods for executing a strictly pairwise final aggregation. The authors have no competing interests to declare that are relevant to the content of this article.
