Similar wording may miss complementary rules or amendments. Vietnamese statutes expose structure through , , , instrument identifiers, and references. DRiLL includes questions requiring multiple Articles~, making it suitable for examining relationships beyond individual similarity scores. Our research question is: Structure includes legal-unit form, instrument membership, and reference neighborhoods. Lineage-related cues connect base provisions with amendments, guidance, or introduced units. We examine these cues through a retrieval method. We follow EDM's DRiLL retrieval setting, conventional features, and staged design~, simplifying the LLM-dependent stages to reduce LLM calls. We then add legal evidence to the internal controls. Final selectors share candidates, listwise judgments, and learner settings. Five-run results, split-gain importance, and cases characterize DRiLL evidence through LightGBM and explicit features. Future work can explore other learners, representations, and datasets. DRiLL systems combine lexical and semantic matching with richer Article context. Hieu and Do use hybrid retrieval and a cross-encoder, with reconstructed titles and hard-negative training~. Their design enriches individual candidates with document context. Hoai et al. use LLM augmentation and hierarchical chunking to form coherent retrieval units~. This addresses the internal organization of long legal texts. ViDRILL combines sparse and dense retrieval with semantic reranking, dual-level chunking, and hard-negative mining~. These pipelines improve matching and candidate quality. We examine additional evidence connecting candidates with other provisions. HyDE embeds a generated hypothetical document to retrieve real corpus passages~. This enriches the query view, while retrieved legal texts remain the evidence for answering. EDM combines hybrid retrieval, learned ranking, and LLM relevance judgments~. It also uses citation expansion and combines matching scores with document-form cues. Our internal controls reuse this feature design while simplifying the LLM stages. The comparison examines added legal evidence under shared final-selection inputs. We distinguish logical LLM evaluations implied by these designs from measured latency or monetary cost. G-DSR incorporates legislative structure into dense retrieval through a graph neural network~. This captures dependencies beyond an isolated Article. LegisSearch combines a legislative graph, contextual embeddings, and LLM query expansion for Italian legislation~. Its typed citation and amendment relations provide a precedent for representing lineage. These approaches motivate examining the direction and type of legal connections. Extracted DRiLL links remain potential relevance cues. A relation may identify a missing companion or a topical distractor, so its value depends on the query and other candidates available for selection.
