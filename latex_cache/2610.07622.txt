Large language models have revolutionized natural language processing with their advanced text comprehension and reasoning capabilities. However, they are not designed to handle domain-specific knowledge, rapidly changing information, private data, or long-tail entities. These limitations result in issues such as hallucinations~, spurious explanations~, and inconsistencies~. Recent techniques have improved reasoning through prompting methods, such as Chain-of-Thought ~ for step-by-step reasoning, and Self-Consistency~ for selecting the most consistent answer across sampled reasoning paths. To overcome these challenges, recent research has focused on injecting domain-specific knowledge into language models by integrating external sources and leveraging domain corpora. Retrieval-augmented generation has proven effective thanks to a two-stage retrieve-and-read framework, in which the retriever quickly extracts relevant data from large corpora~. By incorporating accurate, up-to-date information, RAG can enhance the LLM's response pertinence, reduce hallucinations, and produce more reliable, contextually relevant outputs. Numerous studies have focused on pretraining table-specific language models for downstream tasks. Building on the success of masked language modeling, TAPAS~ introduces a BERT-like architecture enhanced with positional embeddings to encode table-specific information. During pretraining, the model reconstructs masked table elements. In contrast to TAPAS’s reconstruction approach, TAPEX~ pretrains a language model to execute SQL operations on tables, motivated by the intuition that accurate SQL execution reflects robust table comprehension. To advance LM capabilities in table retrieval, develop a dense retriever leveraging contrastive learning, pretraining, and negative sample mining. By employing TAPAS as the base encoder, they demonstrate that dense retrieval outperforms traditional methods like BM25~ in open-domain single-table retrieval. develop a method that enables an LLM to construct sub-evidences and sub-questions to answer natural language questions over large tables. Their approach employs in-context learning by providing a small set of manually crafted examples to guide table deconstruction and reasoning. introduce a RAG framework for single-table QA, retrieving relevant schema and cells to address scalability and context length limitations when feeding large tables to the LLM. Inspired by the effectiveness of reasoning chains in textual contexts, propose using an LLM agent to dynamically plan table operations based on prior steps, forming an iterative reasoning process. They demonstrate that enabling LLMs to generate a reasoning chain improves performance in single-table QA. While single-table QA has garnered research attention, studies on multi-table QA remain limited. make a pioneering contribution by pretraining the TAPEX model on a multi-table dataset with SQL queries and tabular answers, followed by fine-tuning on natural language questions. This approach adapts to databases lacking key-foreign-key constraints. It uses flattened table representations for coarse-grained retrieval, while downstream question answering relies on SQL generation and execution. Although significant progress has been made in single-table QA scenarios~, the more challenging and practical task of multi-table QA remains relatively underexplored. Multi-table QA involves answering questions that require the integration of information from multiple tables, necessitating sophisticated reasoning and advanced data fusion techniques~. This challenge is further compounded by the complex relationships between tables, such as identifying joinable keys or overlapping attributes, particularly when such relationships lack a well-structured explicit relational schema, as is often the case in web tables and data lakes~. Many existing studies assume that the relevant tables are already provided~. In an open-domain table QA setting, the system must instead retrieve them from a larger corpus. This requires considering both query-table relevance and table-table relationships: a table with limited direct relevance may still be needed to connect complementary information~. Large language models have demonstrated remarkable proficiency in various natural language processing tasks~. Their adaptation to QA via retrieval-augmented generation has led to substantial performance gains~. In the RAG framework, relevant information is first efficiently retrieved from a pre-indexed large corpus and used to augment the query provided to the LLM, thereby mitigating issues such as hallucinations and outdated knowledge. Despite the effectiveness of the RAG framework in single-table QA contexts~, its application to multi-table QA remains underexplored. The challenges of multi-table QA stem from several factors. First, multi-table QA requires the system to reason across multiple tables, often necessitating different types of joins and aggregations. Second, the sheer volume of tables in a typical database complicates the retrieval process, as the system must identify not only the main tables relevant to a query but also any auxiliary tables needed to connect and augment the data. Third, each table can vary significantly in size, both in the number of columns and rows. For instance, data warehouses often contain wide tables with billions of rows. These challenges, combined with the limitations of current LLM context windows, make the direct application of LLMs to multi-table QA non-trivial, thereby necessitating novel approaches. To tackle the aforementioned challenges, we propose a novel methodology that extends the RAG framework to encompass the entire database. Our approach decomposes the problem of multi-table QA into two critical subtasks, and . ~ In the retrieval step, we adopt a two-pronged approach to identify the relevant tables in a scalable manner. First, table search quickly selects a subset of tables relevant to the query from the entire database using a table index. Next, we enrich the tables of interest by augmenting them with query-relevant rows. The collected context is then fed into an LLM to select the most promising tables. ~ In , we propose a novel reasoning protocol designed to answer the natural language question given the retrieved tables. Specifically, we design a multi-table-aware Chain-of-Thought prompting technique combined with a program-aided reasoner that executes operations over the retrieved tables and returns the final answer. Our experiments on multi-table QA datasets demonstrate that not only improves the retrieval of relevant tables but also enhances the reasoning task, resulting in higher overall QA performance in the evaluated settings.
