 {acl} {fontenc} {inputenc} {Property} {OpticalRec } Recent progress in vision-language modeling and multimodal learning has opened new opportunities for recommender systems~. These improvements mainly stem from two aspects: First, textual and visual representations offer rich semantic signals that help characterize user interests and item properties. Second, the above representations learned during large-scale vision-language pretraining are transferrable to recommendation tasks~. As a result, large vision-language model representations have become a powerful basis for improving recommendation quality, contextual understanding, and personalization in modern recommender systems~. Despite above mentioned benefits of large vision-language models, injecting rich multimodal features into recommender systems continues to pose significant challenges. A fundamental bottleneck lies in the widely-adopted decoupled/independent encoding of each modality followed by the lossy late fusion~. Two major disadvantages exist here: 1. independent encoding inherently omits vision-language cross-interactions such as cross attention and yields embeddings in heterogeneous vector space per-modality, 2. the lossy late fusion such as summation, concatenation and graph fusion, is applied to integrate the heterogeneous embedding in 1., which distorts the cross-modal semantics in resulting encodings. Specifically, summation or concatenation-based late fusion omits vision-language cross interactions with fusion-entailed information loss~; Graph-based methods exploit multimodal fusion by similarity graphs, but initial graph construction still falls back to late-fused separate encoding~. Recent works continue to refine downstream graph architectures while overlooking image-text interactions and the disruptive late fusion~. Hence, we analyze that a unified-space encoding paradigm is optimal for multimodal recommendation that satisfies: Multimodal content should be presented in a unified input space, allowing rich vision-language interactions instead of isolation. The unified input should be encoded once into a homogeneous embedding space, without additional disruptive late fusion. Motivated by these observations~, we propose , a lightweight, plug-and-play unified encoding framework for multimodal recommendation. Through an efficient, one-time rendering operation that transforms textual metadata into visual text/glyph, enables rich images and text cross-interactions within a unified pixel space during vision encoding. This also naturally aligns with a “see-as-users-see” principle: users shift attention back-and-forth between item image and text when browsing online shopping content. Further processed by the language decoder, together forms a mechanism and outputs homogeneous embeddings without late fusion. Pretrained language and vision-language models are widely adopted in multimodal collaborative filtering for transfer learning~. A major line of work adopts pretrained multimodal embeddings as frozen item features for collaborative filtering~. Early methods such as VBPR~ leverage CNN-based image features to model visually driven user preferences. Later methods further introduce textual features and graph neural networks for high-order multimodal interaction modeling~. For example, MMGCN~ and GRCN~ extend user-item graph with modality-aware fusion. Later developments such as LATTICE~ and FREEDOM~ introduce item-item content similarity graphs for augmentation. Recent methods continue to explore tailored graph structures and better training supervision~. In contrast, our work shifts the focus from increasingly specialized graph networks to unified encoding for more advanced image-text interactions. Beyond feature-based MMCF, recent and past studies also explore multimodal sequential/generative recommendation and model behavior study~. We focus on MMCF for a cleaner encoding setup, where item representations directly interact with collaborative signals. Feature-based multimodal recommendation predominantly follows a separate-encoding paradigm plus late fusion~. This paradigm aligns with early vision-language representation learning such as CLIP, which learns shared image-text embeddings but still encodes each modality separately. Later models such as Flamingo and LLaVA move toward joint, interleaved vision-language modeling. More recent models such as Qwen-VL~, InternVL~, and LLaVA model series~ further strengthen visual grounding and layout-aware understanding. However, how such unified vision-language perception can benefit MMCF remains unexplored, as user-item collaborative signal is inherently multifaceted. Yet for multimodal recommendation, encoding rich item vision-language semantic interactions remains a long-standing bottleneck, which hampers accurate item representation learning and user-item matching. Mainstream approaches primarily adopt independent encoding of vision and language modality followed by rigid late fusion such as concatenation, inherently omitting native vision-language interactions and introducing cross-modal semantic distortion. To address this challenge, we propose , the first visual-space unified encoding paradigm for multimodal collaborative filtering, a fundamental recommendation setting. Instead of isolated modality-specific encoding, renders item textual metadata as visual glyphs, enabling native image–text interaction within visual encoder---the level.
