Figure~ illustrates the complete runtime execution alongside a sample trace. First, the propose stage partitions the 20 candidates into distinct evidence groups under fixed precedence rules: query alignment, lyric resonance, taste continuity, pool coherence, and discovery. In the sample trace, tokens matching ``driving bassline'' and ``dark and intense'' yield a 0.60 similarity score for ``Camel'' by Flying Lotus, satisfying the query-alignment threshold. A single structured LLM call processes the proposed candidate groups together with one routed demonstration. This demonstration provides response selection guidance rather than introducing additional recommendation candidates. Second, within this single call, the assign step identifies usable personalization anchors and supported themes. Third, the select step chooses a core track, optional supplementary tracks for requests seeking multiple tracks, and optional historical references. The same joint call generates the raw response text. Lastly, deterministic rules validate and repair title formatting errors or citation violations. Tailoring input text to specific scoring mechanisms allows the hybrid interface to leverage exact catalog cues while the conversational interface captures evolving language constraints, as illustrated in Figure~. These findings outline trajectories for architectural extension, particularly regarding the LightGBM ranker that currently relies on two upstream pools and compact features. Integrating stronger retrieval components with diverse behaviors will provide more informative signals to the calibrator. Several limitations remain in the current implementation. Conversational music recommendation must balance precise catalog entity retrieval with fluid dialogue tracking~. Traditional architectures rely on a single text interface for both tasks, creating a bottleneck that forces a severe trade-off between tracking evolving intent and preserving exact entities, which compromises explanation credibility~. To resolve this, we propose an operationally decoupled architecture that separates retrieval and response. As shown in Figure~, these pipelines connect strictly via ranked candidates and metadata evidence, directly aligning with the ACM RecSys Challenge 2026} objectives of top-20 ranking and evidence-grounded response generation. Figure~b illustrates this single-interface vulnerability. At turn 4, a listener requests ``melancholic darkwave'' citing ; by turn 6, they add ``gothic rock'' and a ``heavier beat'' without repeating the artist. A keyword baseline drops the ground-truth track---``Dollhouse ''---to 20th place due to this entity shift. To fix this inefficiency, Figure~a shows how our system pipelines a hybrid lexical-dense pool for exact matching alongside a conversational pool driven by fine-tuned Qwen 8B adapters. Merging these independent views advances the target track's rank from 20th to fourth in the 8B pool, second after reciprocal rank fusion , and first after LightGBM calibration. After ranking, generating explanations requires strict factual grounding. Instead of relying on free-form generation, Figure~ illustrates our PAS framework for evidence-guided reasoning and deterministic validation. PAS converts response generation into a joint reasoning step conditioned on candidate-level evidence and a matched demonstration, followed by deterministic validation to anchor outputs to verified catalog attributes. This framework secured third place for team in the Blind-B industry track, and we open-source our code}.
