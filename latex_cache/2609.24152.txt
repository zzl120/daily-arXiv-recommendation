</concept> </ccs2012> {Information systems~Multimedia and multimodal retrieval} We presented GradCIR, a graded-relevance methodology for composed multimodal retrieval in e-commerce visual search. The central methodological contribution is the use of 4-level ordinal relevance labels, assigned at scale by a VLM judge and exploited by a hierarchy-aware angular objective. The graded-vs-binary ablation isolates this single design choice and shows that finer relevance granularity is what closes the ranking-quality gap on graded NDCG metrics. The full pipeline curates 3.5M labeled pairs without manual annotation through an iterative relevance-feedback loop. On the internal benchmarks, GradCIR outperforms all baselines on almost every metric, with the largest gains on the more fine-grained graded NDCG metrics. On public benchmarks, GradCIR is competitive with the strongest baselines. The system is deployed in production at Walmart, where it's serving live visual-search user traffic. Future work includes extending the graded formulation to multi-item compositional queries and exploring attribute-level conditioning for more fine-grained relevance modeling. Visual search on large e-commerce catalogs must serve both ``similarity'' queries that ask for items resembling an uploaded image and ``modifier'' queries that comprise an image and text describing a desired modification . The latter is the setting known as . Existing CIR methods, however, treat relevance as binary and train on triplets with a single positive target---a poor fit for real catalogs where many candidates partially satisfy a user query and ranking across that partial-match spectrum drives the customer experience. We propose a methodology for training CIR retrievers on graded relevance, consisting of: a VLM to curate training data, generating both queries and 4-level relevance labels without manual annotation, an iterative relevance-feedback loop that expands the training set by mining hard negatives from the in-training retriever, and a hierarchy-aware angular objective to train the retriever directly on the graded labels rather than collapsing them to a binary split. We call this methodology GradCIR and instantiate it on a PaliGemma2 bi-encoder trained on 3.5M graded pairs curated from raw Walmart catalog data. A controlled graded-vs-binary ablation isolates the supervision granularity and shows lift of 4.9\ GradCIR targets composed image retrieval where every candidate is a single sellable item. Two consequences shape our evaluation choices and scope: The CIRR and CIRCO benchmarks draw query and candidate images from NLVR2 and COCO respectively, consisting of natural scenes with multiple objects, and free-form composition. A retriever trained on catalog images is fundamentally out-of-distribution on these benchmarks, and the resulting numbers measure adaptation effort more than retrieval quality. FashionIQ and Street2Shop, by contrast, draw from a fashion catalog and align with our training distribution, which is why we report these as our public benchmarks. We do not address multi-item compositional queries , which require a more expressive query representation than a single image--text pair; or explicit attribute-level controllability at inference time, which would benefit from explicit attribute conditioning. Extending the graded-relevance formulation to these settings is left to future work. All architecture choices, hyperparameters, prompts, augmentation details, and recipe details required to reproduce the methodology on any comparable proprietary catalog are provided in the paper. The FashionIQ and Street2Shop results use public data and standard public evaluation protocols. Production visual-search systems at e-commerce scale~ typically formulate retrieval as image-similarity matching, learning an embedding space over pairs labelled as same-product or not. Two limitations carry over to all of them: relevance is binary, and the query is image-only. Text-aware variants such as VL-CLIP~ and the four-tower image--text formulation of Zhu et~al.~ loosen the latter by also accepting text input in the query, but still treat relevance as binary. The underlying embedding models split into two families that we use as baselines and compare in our architecture ablation . encoders such as CLIP~, ALIGN~, and SigLIP~, VL-CLIP~ align image and text in a shared space via a contrastive loss and combine them at inference by fusing unimodal embeddings. This two-tower design is fast to serve but cannot represent ``image is X but text says change attribute Y'' interactions that modifier queries require. VLMs such as PaliGemma2~ , Qwen-VL~, LLaVa~, Gemini~, and ModernVBERT~ jointly process image and text tokens through a transformer, giving richer cross-modal interactions at a higher serving cost. Recent early-fusion retrievers -- GME~, MM-Embed~, mmE5~, Qwen3-VL-Embedding~ are trained on synthetic pairs from web image--caption corpora but, like the industrial systems above, supervise with binary positive/negative labels. GradCIR is, to our knowledge, the first to train an early-fusion VLM retriever on graded relevance for composed multimodal retrieval. Composed image retrieval ~, also referred to as composed multimodal retrieval in recent surveys, formulates the query as an image plus a textual modifier, and asks the model to retrieve an item that satisfies both. approaches train an image--text fusion module on top of a frozen vision--language encoder. ARTEMIS~, PL4CIR~, TG-CIR~, CLIP4CIR~, BLIP4CIR , and SPRC~ are some of the representative methods in this family. SPN4CIR~ extends SPRC by mining additional positive and negative triplets, and is the current strongest peer-reviewed supervised baseline on FashionIQ that we compare against. A second line of work scales CIR with : MagicLens~ mines 36.7M open-ended instruction triplets from the web; CompoDiff~ generates triplets with latent diffusion; CoVR~ mines triplets from web video captions; CoLLM~ couples an LLM with CLIP/BLIP backbones and trains on a 3.4M-triplet synthetic dataset . These approaches produce strong zero-shot and supervised CIR models but treat the target as a single binary positive and require an explicit triplet-construction step. A third line targets by mapping the visual modality into the text space of a frozen CLIP encoder: Pic2Word~, SEARLE~, LinCIR~, Context-I2W~, CIReVL~ , and Slerp-TAT~ are representative methods in this family. These methods avoid CIR-triplet training but inherit the binary-target evaluation protocol. None of the above CIR works, to our knowledge, trains on ordinal graded relevance. Standard CIR datasets themselves encode a single positive target per query. In production retrieval over a real catalog this is a poor fit: many candidates partially satisfy a query, and the ordering of those partial matches is what determines the customer experience. A typical visual search system consists of a that takes the user query and returns a set of candidate items from the catalog, and a that orders the retrieved candidates by relevance. This paper focuses on the retrieval component, which is typically realised as deep metric learning over high-dimensional embeddings: relevant pairs are placed closer in the embedding space than irrelevant ones, and approximate nearest-neighbour search retrieves the top-$K$ candidates at inference time. Many user queries, especially for categories like Fashion, have a visual aspect which can be difficult to express in words. Most existing systems, however, tackle only the limited use-case of ``shop similar items''~. Building a robust visual search system at production scale poses several unique technical challenges. The first is : query images and product images come from different distributions, as catalog images are usually clean and high-resolution, whereas user-uploaded images contain noise, clutter, and irrelevant background. The second is : Walmart's catalog contains hundreds of millions of items, and retrieving tens of highly relevant items with low latency is critical to a good customer experience. The third is : in a real catalog, candidates are rarely cleanly ``relevant or irrelevant''. For a query of a grey velvet 3-seater sofa, an identical product is an exact match; the same sofa in beige is a strong near match; a generic grey 3-seater is a partial match; a velvet armchair is irrelevant. Collapsing these four cases into a binary labeling scheme discards the information that makes a retrieval ranking useful. The dominant line of work on composed image retrieval ~ optimizes for the binary case: triplets of where exactly one target is correct. This works well for the academic CIR benchmarks but is a poor fit for production retrieval over a real catalog, where the ranking quality across a partial-match spectrum is what drives customer engagement. This paper presents , a methodology for training composed-multimodal retrievers on graded ordinal relevance. The methodology is backbone-agnostic: in the experiments we instantiate it on PaliGemma2 and additionally apply the same recipe to four other contemporary multimodal encoders to confirm that the gains are properties of the methodology rather than of any particular backbone. PaliGemma2 combines a SigLIP-400M vision encoder with a Gemma~2 2.6B language model, connected via a linear projection that maps patch embeddings into the language model's token space. Images are encoded at $224{ }224$ resolution into $256$ patch tokens, text is tokenized with SentencePiece and capped at $128$ tokens. The interleaved image-patch and text tokens are processed jointly by the Gemma~2 decoder stack, yielding a contextualized early-fusion representation at every position. Since PaliGemma2 is a generative VLM with a prefix-LM attention pattern and does not natively expose fixed-length embeddings, we adapt it for retrieval in three ways: We remove the language-modelling head entirely; the model is used purely as an encoder. The hidden state at the last non-padding position serves as the sequence embedding. Because we treat the entire input as the bidirectional prefix , every token, including the final one, attends to all image and text tokens, so its hidden state aggregates the full multimodal context without requiring an additional pooling layer or a dedicated token. A short prefix token --- or --- is prepended to the input text, allowing the shared encoder to learn asymmetric representations for the two roles. Bi-encoder retrievers are commonly trained with multiple-negatives ranking loss ~ or InfoNCE, which both rely on in-batch negatives and treat relevance as binary: every non-positive item in the batch is an equally bad negative. This is a poor fit for graded relevance, where a item and an item carry very different penalties for being ranked equally with the positive. We instead use AngleLoss~, which transforms graded labels into angular constraints in the embedding space. GradCIR-PG2 is deployed as the retrieval backbone of Walmart's visual-search system, currently serving a partial traffic slice. This is the first production visual-search system at Walmart. At inference time, every catalog item is pre-indexed by its GradCIR-PG2 embedding truncated to 256 dimensions as described in . User queries are encoded online by the same model and retrieved via approximate nearest-neighbour search. The existing text-based reranker is applied on top of the retrieved candidates to produce the final ranking. The full pipeline operates within the production latency SLA.
