Instead of scoring items from a predefined candidate pool, semantic-ID-based GR models directly predict target items by autoregressively generating their discrete Semantic IDs ~. This formulation unifies item representation with generation, showing strong capabilities in modeling complex user behavior sequences. Many representative GR methods formulate recommendation as predicting the next item from user history~, a setting we refer to as . In practical search, advertising, and recommendation platforms, another important scenario is ~. As illustrated in Figure~, when a user interacts with a specific product in a general feed, the platform immediately opens a follow-up feed organized around that item. In this context, the clicked item is no longer an ordinary historical interaction. Instead, it serves as an explicit trigger that conveys the user's immediate shopping intent, which should actively steer the direction of follow-up recommendations. Although Trigger-Induced Recommendation represents a cornerstone scenario across industrial digital platforms, trigger modeling remains largely unexplored in generative recommendation . Existing GR frameworks inherently presume behavioral continuity, lacking dedicated mechanisms to accommodate sudden shifts in user intent. Meanwhile, representative TIR solutions are developed for discriminative CTR prediction using trigger-aware representations~. Because these methods produce only scalar ranking scores or static representations, they cannot steer discrete, token-by-token autoregressive generation. While extending GR to TIR marks a fundamental transition from discriminative scoring to condition-controlled generation, it inevitably exposes an essential structural tension between historical preference continuity and trigger-driven intent redirection. An overemphasis on continuity reduces the model to a conventional history-based system that fails to capture immediate user impulses. Conversely, an exclusive reliance on the trigger risks confining candidate generation to an overly narrow semantic space, disregarding persistent user preferences and causing severe overspecialization. Reconciling this structural tension within an autoregressive framework introduces three challenges across encoding, representation, and decoding. First, during sequence encoding, standard self-attention allows extensive interaction histories to easily dilute a single trigger token, preventing it from effectively contextualizing recent user actions without an explicit mechanism to calibrate its influence. Second, the interplay between persistent preferences and immediate intent varies dynamically across requests. A user action may reflect either casual exploration where historical taste should prevail, or a decisive shift toward a new interest requiring strict trigger alignment, making static fusion schemes inadequate. Third, during autoregressive decoding, semantic IDs unfold sequentially from broad categories to fine-grained attributes. Conditioning only at the initial step inevitably weakens over successive generation steps and cannot accommodate the varying semantic granularity of each token, underscoring the need for persistent, position-aware guidance throughout the generation trajectory. To address these challenges, we propose , an end-to-end framework that establishes a tailored generative paradigm for trigger-induced recommendation. As illustrated in Figure~, TriGen systematically reconciles preference continuity and intent redirection across the entire autoregressive pipeline through three synergistic designs. At the sequence encoding stage, places repeated trigger representations at the history tail, using RoPE to model relative positions and the repetition count to regulate trigger influence. At the representation stage, decouples persistent historical preferences from immediate trigger impulses, employing an input-dependent gating module to dynamically arbitrate their balance on a per-request basis. Finally, at the autoregressive decoding stage, incorporates level-aware transformations directly into the decoder layers, sustaining persistent trigger guidance that adapts to the evolving semantic granularity of each decoding step. Across all runs, TriGen sustains consistent gains over both discriminative and generative baselines. No public dataset with Semantic-ID-ready item text exposes a real trigger field, so on Amazon the trigger is the last history item rather than an observed click. Baselines keep the full sequence, so this does not favour TriGen; it instead caps the trigger signal available to it, and the public gap should be read as conservative. Only the industrial dataset, where the trigger is an independent field, measures the mechanism at full strength. TriGen assumes exactly one trigger per request, which matches the detail-page scenario but not surfaces where several recent signals arrive at once. Extending both injection points to a set of triggers, and how the fusion arbitrates among them, is left to future work.
