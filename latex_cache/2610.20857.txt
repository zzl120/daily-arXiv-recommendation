We present , an auditable literature-search system built on PaperArena. It combines multi-source discovery, identity verification, facet-based abstract assessment, semantic citation expansion, and joint Expert ranking. A retrieval-only mode ranks up to five papers from a shortlist of at most 60, using complete available abstracts and locally selected PDF evidence from five fixed acquisition attempts, without generating a long-form report. In a historical comparison covering ten queries from each of EASY, MEDIUM, and HARD, aggregate reported Hit@5 is 53.3\ Researchers often remember an idea before they remember a title: a neighborhood with uncertain boundaries, a circuit transformation with a particular constraint, or an assistant that recognizes when a complex task changes. Recovering relevant prior work from such descriptions is difficult because vocabulary, application setting, and the level of technical detail can differ substantially between a query and a paper. A useful assistant must retrieve candidates, establish their bibliographic identities, and distinguish a close conceptual match from work that merely shares a topic. LLM-based research systems provide useful ways to explore and synthesize sources. STORM organizes research into multi-perspective information gathering and article outlining~, Co-STORM supports interactive exploration~, and Agent Laboratory connects literature review with experimentation and reporting~. Our focus is narrower: given an idea, which five identifiable papers are most likely to match it, and what evidence explains their selection? This objective is not equivalent to the quality of a general research report. We introduce , an extension of PaperArena and its implementation~. The contribution is an engineering and evaluation framework for inspectable idea-to-paper retrieval: it preserves search queries, candidate identities, assessments, filtering reasons, PDF availability, and the final ranking. This makes it possible to distinguish a discovery miss from a candidate that was retrieved but lost during filtering, or from a task that failed operationally. A high-level result alone cannot make those distinctions. The central design separates inexpensive assessment of a broad candidate pool from a bounded examination of document evidence. After abstract assessment and citation expansion, an Expert model jointly considers up to 60 candidates. Five papers are selected for PDF acquisition; unavailable PDFs are not replaced by additional papers in retrieval-only mode. Complete available abstracts remain in the Expert input, along with evidence availability and up to six locally selected PDF passages per acquired paper. A missing PDF does not make a paper irrelevant and does not prevent it from ranking first. The optional full-report mode retains downstream synthesis for users who need a written literature analysis. The revised evaluation covers 30 query cases: ten from each of three prompt tiers, with shared targets across tiers. Thus the present evidence shows a recovery advantage in two tiers and a low observed API cost, with longer latency than the supplied browser averages. Our contributions are: An auditable retrieval-and-ranking workflow with identity-aware candidate records, semantic citation filtering, and a joint Top 5 decision over a bounded shortlist. A retrieval-only execution mode that preserves abstract and PDF evidence assessment while omitting report-specific processing, exposed through local web, Python, CLI, and Notebook interfaces. PaperArena supplies a foundation for evaluating tool-augmented reasoning over scientific literature~. builds on that foundation and studies a specific retrieval endpoint rather than claiming to introduce the general agentic-research setting. Scholarly indexes such as OpenAlex~ provide useful metadata and links, while paper identity must still be checked across publication and preprint records. Our public retrieval profile uses Semantic Scholar, Crossref, and arXiv-related resolution; OpenAlex remains an optional legacy backend. SciFact explicitly associates scientific claims with supporting or refuting evidence~. Our task instead ranks works by how well they match an idea; facet scores and selected passages are decision evidence, not formal proof of novelty or claim truth. Implementation analysis similarly surfaces code, pseudocode, dependencies, and missing details without establishing that an entire published experiment is reproducible. We therefore use external graphs as exploratory references, not as ground truth for causal research lineages. Model-recalled seeds and web-derived paper candidates are discovery hypotheses, requiring bibliographic resolution before they can be treated as established identities. The current profile combines recalled seeds, web search, and scholarly metadata. Semantic Scholar supplies paper records and citation neighborhoods. Crossref supplies bibliographic verification and metadata; merely receiving a reference in a Crossref response does not insert it into the candidate pool. Explicit Crossref-reference expansion is disabled in the default profile. arXiv identifier resolution and PDF access remain available even when arXiv keyword search is disabled. OpenAlex is disabled in this profile but retained for compatible older workflows. Consequently, provider availability and a paper's route into the candidate registry are recorded separately. Local selection bounds the candidate workload. The protected relevance policy uses a BM25-like score over title and complete available abstract text, complemented by recorded retriever ranks. It reserves limited capacity for corroborated seeds, strong retrieval ranks, and missing-abstract candidates, and handles publication-year eligibility and duplicate entities explicitly. This is still a budgeted selection algorithm: a relevant candidate can be dropped below a capacity boundary. It is not a guarantee of recall, and it is distinct from the semantic citation-neighbor gate. The merged abstract prompt produces an ordinal overlap label, covered facet IDs, task and method similarities, a short reason, and the paper's high-level facets. The labels are $3$ for high overlap, $2$ for partial overlap, $1$ for adjacent work, and $0$ for no overlap. Missing evidence and invalid model outputs retain explicit unassessed or failed states; they are not converted into valid zero scores. In the staged profile, the older lexical-zero and Boolean LLM prefilters are bypassed before abstract scoring. Local budget selection still applies. After initial assessment and metadata enrichment, up to five papers seed citation expansion through Semantic Scholar. Unique neighbors with abstracts are assessed in batches of up to 20. The Light-role model receives the original research query, complete idea, generated search queries, organized keywords, facets, and each neighbor's title and abstract. Its rubric recognizes synonyms and mechanism-level matches, and explicitly forbids assigning zero merely because a keyword is absent. The complete prompt and hypothetical calibration examples appear in Appendix~. Valid zero-score neighbors are excluded; positive neighbors are ordered $3>2>1$, with up to 15 retained per seed under the default setting. Missing abstracts or scoring failures remain unassessed and bypass that positive-only cap, while still being subject to the global candidate budget. The default global budget admits at most 50 new citation candidates. Identity checks, deduplication, and abstract assessment then update the pool. Neither semantic filtering nor a citation relationship grants automatic access to the final shortlist. The current profile selects up to 50 assessed works plus a buffer of up to ten boundary or uncertain candidates. Thus at most 60 distinct eligible works reach the Expert, and the actual count can be smaller. Assessment ordering considers overlap grade, task and method similarity, covered facets, and related structural signals; the staged policy does not use citation count as a relevance tiebreaker. Conflicting identities and known out-of-range publication years are excluded and logged. Exactly five candidate slots, or fewer when fewer eligible candidates exist, are selected for PDF acquisition. These are fixed paper-level attempts, not a promise of five successful PDFs or a cap of five HTTP requests. If a PDF is unavailable, the workflow does not substitute another paper. The attempted paper remains available through its abstract or metadata, and the acquisition outcome is included in the Expert input. For each available PDF, the default local extractor scans native text from all pages using windows of at most 1,800 characters, with a half-window stride. It orders windows by distinct idea/facet terms, capped term frequency, section cues, and deterministic location tiebreakers. It first takes a high-scoring window, then seeks nonoverlapping query-relevant coverage of method, experiment, and conclusion cues before filling the remaining slots. At most six windows are sent per paper, in reading order. This selector is a lexical window heuristic with section cues: it is not a guaranteed Introduction/Related Work/Method/Conclusion parser, and it makes no extra model call. Full extracted text stays in local artifacts. Sent windows record page locations, offsets, and hashes, and are explicitly marked partial. The Expert receives the entire eligible shortlist, complete available abstracts, existing assessments, and only the PDF material actually acquired and selected. Earlier grades are evidence rather than a mandatory final ordering. Papers with unavailable or unattempted PDFs may rank first. The model returns exactly $ $ candidate IDs in consecutive rank order, a one- or two-sentence explanation for each, and an evidence-source label. Reasons are qualitative; the system does not claim calibrated probabilities. Validation checks candidate membership, duplicate identities, rank order, and consistency between claimed evidence sources and supplied material. A failed Expert output is recorded as a ranking failure rather than replaced silently with a rule-based Top 5. Operational completion and scientific target recovery are separate outcomes. A partial run may contain a valid ranking while recording unavailable documents or incomplete checks; a successful ranking alone does not prove a target hit. Retrieval-only optimizations include concurrent independent seed branches, reuse of valid unchanged model responses within a run, batched scholarly requests, shared HTTP connections, compact Expert payloads, and skipping report-only evidence processing. Reuse keys include the prompt, evidence, model, and configuration dependencies; changed evidence must be reassessed. Compact serialization preserves the abstract and selected evidence while removing duplicated or audit-only material. These choices reduce redundant work by design, but this paper does not report a controlled ablation establishing a latency or accuracy effect for each optimization. The Expert has a separate maximum output budget of 16,384 tokens. Its prompt still requests only up to five concise entries; this ceiling is not observed consumption or a request for a long reasoning transcript. Request budgets, deadlines, status records, cancellation, and provider-specific configuration are available through all entry points. The public runner does not automatically restart a whole task. In full-report mode, additional artifacts include method/experiment snippets, representative-paper selections, synthesis input, and a rendered report; these costs are outside the reported retrieval-only comparison. Code Extractor takes a supplied PDF, Markdown, or text document and emits structured implementation diagnostics and a Markdown report. It combines rule-based code/pseudocode extraction with optional LLM analysis of completeness, frameworks, dependencies, model structure, and missing components. Native PDF text extraction is the default; structured MinerU parsing is explicitly optional. Model-recovered descriptions must be distinguished from verbatim executable source. The public interface separates analysis from execution. With model assistance disabled, scores are labeled heuristic; with code execution disabled, execution outcomes remain unrun even if syntax checks succeed. Missing packages, parser failures, and requested model-analysis failures are exposed as diagnostics. This tool can identify potentially missing details, but successful parsing, syntax checks, or high model scores do not establish paper-level reproducibility. Evolution Mapper retrieves a topic's papers and citation neighbors, builds a directed graph, groups papers by year, and exports Mermaid diagrams, key-paper summaries, and path analyses. Citation edges and chronological views are descriptive evidence. They must not be interpreted as proof of technical influence, and edge orientation must be distinguished from chronological traversal. The paper limit bounds seed discovery; citation neighbors can enlarge the graph.
