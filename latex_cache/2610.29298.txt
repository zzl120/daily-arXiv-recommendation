 Generative recommenders replace the retrieve-then-rank cascade with a single model: beam search over semantic item identifiers performs retrieval, and the same likelihood performs ranking. We show that this shared score makes them forget long-tail items twice. In retrieval, beam search keeps identifier prefixes by the probability mass of their subtrees, so tail items are pruned early, and a wider beam admits few more of them. In ranking, the likelihood places tail items below head items even when they are admitted, so expanding retrieval alone brings little or no gain. Both failures reflect missing item-level memorization , which collaborative filtering provides and shared-prefix generation lacks. To this end, we propose CAPE , a training-free plug-in that restores memorization at both stages: a memorization channel retrieves co-occurrence-supported tail items outside the beam and scores them with the frozen generator, and memorization-calibrated ranking fuses the generator likelihood with co-occurrence evidence while keeping the generator's first item. These failures share one cause. In the memorization--generalization view of Wide \& Deep~, a recommender either recalls which items co-occur in the data or generalizes through shared representations. Recent evidence shows that generative recommenders lean toward generalization and trail ID-based models on test cases whose item transitions were seen in training~. We locate where this deficit costs the long tail: each retrieval decision is made on shared prefixes, and each ranking decision on a likelihood learned mostly from frequent items, so neither consults item-level co-occurrence, the signal that item-based collaborative filtering memorizes~. Existing remedies, however, act on one stage only. Popularity correction~ and diversity re-ranking~ reorder the retrieved candidates, which cannot recover targets the beam never retrieved . Generative retrieval decodes identifiers instead of scoring an embedding table~, and TIGER brought it to recommendation with residual-quantized semantic identifiers~. Later work improves identifier construction and collaborative alignment~ and scales the paradigm into industrial and open systems that unify retrieval and ranking~. LIGER~ trains a hybrid of generative and dense retrieval for cold-start items; CAPE instead keeps the generator frozen, uses training co-occurrence, and also repairs the ranking stage, which Section~ shows to be necessary. Popularity bias lowers the accuracy and exposure of tail items~, and remedies include re-ranked objectives~, propensity weighting~ and causal adjustment~. For LLM-based recommenders, CoRAL retrieves collaborative evidence into the prompt for long-tail items~, and D3 shows that decoding amplifies popular and homogeneous items~; these methods correct one stage or retrain the model. Wide \& Deep~ argues that recommenders need both memorization of co-occurrence and generalization through embeddings, and item-based collaborative filtering is the classic form of the former~. For generative recommenders, Ding et al.~ find that TIGER trails an ID-based model on test cases whose item transitions occur in training, and blend the scores of the two models with a per-user weight. CAPE starts from the same deficit but shows where it arises , that it concentrates on the long tail, and that fixing either stage alone is ineffective; it also adds items the beam never produces, which no blending of existing scores can recover , and needs no second trained model. Rank fusion~ and guided decoding~ combine model scores with auxiliary ones, and beam search is known to return candidates that differ from the model's most probable outputs~; Proposition~ makes this concrete for identifier tries.
