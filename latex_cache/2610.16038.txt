While existing multimodal sequential recommendation methods leverage global attention to compress historical interactions, they inadequately capture the concurrent nature of multi-interest behaviors and fail to model the underlying multi-centric distribution of user intent. To tackle this issue, we propose OT-FlowRec, an optimal transport-guided multi-bridge flow matching framework that explicitly models multi-interest distributions to facilitate multimodal sequential recommendation. Unlike single-path generative models, OT-FlowRec constructs a mixture of parallel, interest-specific transport flows to model the multi-centric distribution characteristics, where each flow preserves a distinct user interest cluster via an optimal transport plan. Then, a geometric transport mechanism is designed to establish minimum-cost paths from the latent multi-interest distribution to target preference prototypes to provide multi-interest awareness. Moreover, a progressive time-sampling warmup strategy dynamically biases optimization weights toward the final states of the generation process, bridging the gap between continuous global distribution fitting and practical top-$k$ ranking metrics. This approach achieves explicit interest disentanglement, ensuring the preservation of a multi-peaked preference representation instead of collapsing it into a unimodal average. Extensive experiments on three public datasets demonstrate that OT-FlowRec outperforms existing state-of-the-art baselines. Multimodal sequential recommendation has emerged as a pivotal paradigm for delivering personalized services in e-commerce, driven by its capacity to model the temporal dynamics of user preferences. By synergistically integrating interaction sequences with complementary signals derived from text and images, MSR is advantageous in disambiguating items that share similar behavioral footprints yet differ semantically, meanwhile enriching preference inference from sparse or inherently noisy user interactions. This enables MSR to capture users' nuanced, often concurrent interests—a capability that pure sequential models lack, and has drawn growing research attention. . A substantial body of research has been devoted to MSR, which can be categorized into three technical lines: traditional sequential methods, diffusion-based methods, and flow-based methods. Specifically, traditional sequential methods use recurrent networks or self-attention to capture ordered dependencies through discriminative learning . Diffusion-based methods learn stochastic denoising processes in continuous spaces, improving robustness to noisy interactions and supporting flexible generation of target-item representations . Flow-based methods employ vector-field learning and deterministic ODE evolution, reducing the stochasticity and iterative cost of diffusion sampling while directly modeling continuous preference trajectories . {R}{0.55 } {Figures/figure1.pdf} Although promising success has been achieved by previous works, two primary limitations remain. Existing global attention-based approaches compress multi-peaked interactions into single deterministic embeddings, which fail to describe the intrinsic multi-centric distribution characteristics. Moreover, most existing diffusion-based and flow-based methods construct single-peaked distribution representation, which is insufficient to model the multi-centric distribution of user intent.~ . Prevailing flow-based generative recommenders construct time-dependent conditional paths based on independently sampled prior distributions, without accounting for the underlying optimal transport geometry between users' historical interactions and their target preferences. Consequently, the learned trajectories are directionally unconstrained and lack semantic grounding to the multi-interest prototypes . To address these limitations, we propose OT-FlowRec, an optimal transport-guided multi-bridge flow matching framework. Rather than collapsing a complex interaction sequence into a single representation, a Multi-Prototype Guided Source Distribution module is constructed to represent concurrent user preferences with multi-interest source distribution. Its interest-specific components initialize parallel transport flows, preserving the underlying multi-peaked structure and avoiding unimodal collapse. Considering the advantage of interest prototypes~ in capturing diverse user semantics, an OT-Guided Multi-Bridge Flow Matching module is further built, where multiple preference prototypes are first constructed. Accordingly, an interest distribution-to-prototype transformation learning process is then designed to map the multiple interest distribution to their matched prototypes through those parallel transport flows. To facilitate the transformation, minimum-cost paths are computed to connect the latent source distribution to target preference prototypes, ensuring explicit awareness of diverse user interests during generation. Furthermore, a mismatch remains between continuous distribution fitting and discrete ranking evaluation during the sampling process. We therefore develop a Progressive Time-sampling Warmup that progressively shifts optimization toward the terminal states of the generation trajectory. By focusing training on these later states, PTW narrows the gap between continuous trajectory fitting and top-$k$ ranking. Traditional sequential recommendation methods primarily learn behavior transition patterns from user historical interaction sequences . Early approaches utilized Markov chains, RNNs , and CNNs , which were later broadly superseded by Transformer-based architectures like SASRec and BERT4Rec to better capture sequence dependencies. To alleviate interaction sparsity, subsequent research incorporated auxiliary multimodal information , utilizing techniques like multi-sequence attention and modality experts . However, these methods typically remain within the discriminative modeling paradigm, compressing complex user behaviors into deterministic, single-point representations. This limitation prevents them from fully capturing the multi-peaked distributional nature and generative evolution of dynamic user interests. Diffusion-based methods shift recommendation from discriminative matching to generative distribution modeling . Early works like DiffuRec and DreamRec reconstruct target item representations via stochastic noise-addition and reverse denoising processes, thereby capturing diverse user interests as continuous distributions rather than fixed vectors. Subsequent research has enhanced this paradigm by integrating multimodal user-item graphs , aligning generation with personalized ranking objectives , and employing token-level diffusion to prevent embedding collapse . Despite these advancements, the inherent reliance on curved probability paths and multi-step stochastic sampling limits their inference efficiency and often leads to error accumulation during the generative process. Flow-based models replace the stochastic denoising of diffusion models with deterministic continuous generation, transporting prior distributions to target distributions via time-dependent vector fields . Recent works have adapted flow matching for recommendation by designing discrete flows for implicit feedback , utilizing straight trajectories to reduce preference perturbations , and employing Stein-guided entropic flows to capture multi-peaked sequential preferences . Despite improving inference efficiency and stability over diffusion models, existing flow matching methods typically construct simple, global straight paths. This ignores the explicit geometric transport costs between historical distributions and latent multi-interest prototypes. Furthermore, their continuous distribution fitting objectives often misalign with discrete top-$k$ ranking metrics. To bridge these gaps, our OT-FlowRec proposes OT-MBFM for explicit interest disentanglement, alongside the PTW strategy to directly optimize late-stage generation for practical ranking accuracy. In this paper, we proposed OT-FlowRec to jointly model concurrent user interests in multimodal sequential recommendation. To overcome unimodal collapse, our framework constructed parallel transport flows that disentangled diverse interests and preserved multi-peaked preference distributions. Integrating minimum-cost geometric transport paths with the progressive time-sampling warmup strategy achieved explicit multi-interest awareness and bridged continuous distribution fitting and discrete top-$k$ ranking metrics.
