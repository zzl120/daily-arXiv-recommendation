Driven by growing model complexity and the high cost of training and maintenance, modern recommender systems increasingly adopt the Machine Learning as a Service architecture~. In this setting, the model provider trains and hosts the recommendation model, while the service operator serves end users by querying the model through APIs~. This separation means that the SO can use the model but cannot access or modify its internal parameters. As the direct interface with users, the SO is responsible for maintaining the quality of recommendation results. However, user preferences change over time. Users may lose interest in previously preferred items or accidentally click irrelevant ones. {0.4 } {introduction.pdf} {0.48 } Such noisy or outdated interactions can cause the system to repeatedly recommend unwanted content~. To maintain user engagement, the SO should allow users to revoke specific historical interactions and remove their influence on future recommendations. This practical need motivates the task of recommendation unlearning~, which aims to remove the influence of selected historical interactions from a trained recommendation model. Achieving recommendation unlearning in the MLaaS setting introduces two critical. First, a strict asymmetric access constraint: the SO has no access to the recommendation model's internal parameters or gradients. Since the model is controlled by the MP and exposed only through APIs, the SO cannot directly modify model weights to remove the effect of specific training data. Second, unlearning requests may arrive continuously as users interact with the system~. Repeated approximate updates across sequential requests may accumulate errors, gradually degrading overall recommendation quality~. These two constraints make existing unlearning methods difficult to apply, because most of them require access to model internals or control over the training process. Existing methods can be broadly grouped into three categories: that retrain affected model partitions; that apply lightweight representation updates; and that adjust weights based on estimated data influence. Despite their different techniques, these approaches share a common limitation: they require access to model internals, training procedures, or updatable model components. As a result, they are difficult to apply in MLaaS systems where the model can only be accessed through APIs. Given the strict constraints of the MLaaS paradigm, recommendation unlearning should shift away from parameter updates, as the Service Operator can only observe outputs via APIs. This suggests a different unlearning strategy: instead of updating model parameters, the system should revise recommendation outputs to remove the effect of forgotten interactions. However, executing output-level unlearning is non-trivial and presents two major challenges: To revise outputs correctly, the system must first identify which candidate items in the recommendation list were actually influenced by the forgotten interaction. Once the affected items are identified, the system must carefully penalize their scores to erase the forgotten data's influence. This adjustment must be highly precise. If the score reduction is too aggressive, it will distort the user's normal preferences; if it is too weak, the model will fail to truly the revoked interaction, leaving unwanted data traces behind. To address these challenges, we propose Causal Explanation-based Unlearning , a novel framework for recommendation unlearning through output-level score intervention in MLaaS environments, without modifying internal model parameters. To address the first challenge, our framework employs a Causal Contribution Identifier . This module identifies the historical interactions that causally support a recommendation and estimates their contribution strengths. Instead of exposing internal model parameters, the MP only needs to provide these attribution scores to the SO through the API interface. This design remains compatible with the MLaaS access setting without exposing model internals. To address the second challenge, we introduce the Unlearning-driven Rank Adjuster . Guided by the causal contributions provided by the CCI, the SO uses this module to adjust the scores of affected items and remove the influence of the forgotten interaction. Because CEU does not modify model parameters, it is naturally suited to continuous unlearning requests and avoids the error accumulation caused by repeated parameter updates. Recommendation unlearning seeks to eliminate the influence of selected interactions from trained recommender models, e.g., removing outdated or noisy behaviors. Existing approaches can be broadly grouped into three categories. First, retraining-based methods aim for complete removal by partitioning data and retraining affected sub-models . While exact, this paradigm severely limits efficiency and scalability. Second, fine-tuning-based methods avoid full retraining by applying lightweight representation updates to correct the influence of the forgotten data. Third, influence-based methods efficiently approximate the gradient or loss impact of deleted data to adjust model weights. Furthermore, prompt-tuning-based recommendation unlearning has also been explored, where lightweight prompts and a teacher--student objective are used to forget target interactions while preserving useful retained knowledge . Despite their efficiency, both fine-tuning and influence-based approximate methods often suffer from incomplete forgetting, unintended performance drift, and accumulated errors during continuous sequential requests. Crucially, because all three paradigms require accessing model internals to update parameters, they are incompatible with the strict asymmetric access constraints of MLaaS. This motivates our parameter-free framework. Recent studies use causal and counterfactual perspectives to identify what truly drives a recommendation, generally falling into two directions. The first builds causality-aware models incorporating explicit reasoning structures. For example, using reinforcement learning on knowledge graphs~ or neural symbolic reasoning to trace underlying user intents. The second focuses on post-hoc counterfactual explanations, analyzing trained black-box recommenders by asking ``what-if'' questions. CountER~ searches for minimal input changes reversing a decision, while CF$^2$~ combines factual and counterfactual reasoning to capture necessity and sufficiency. While these methods quantify causal influence for interpretation and transparency, they do not leverage these attributions to execute recommendation unlearning, especially under the strict constraints of MLaaS architectures. Higher values indicate better recommendation quality on the retained data after unlearning. We evaluate unlearning completeness using Membership Inference Attack accuracy~. This metric measures how well an attacker can distinguish between forgotten and retained interactions. An MIA accuracy closer to $0.5$ means the forgotten interactions are harder to identify, indicating better forgetting. We evaluate unlearning efficiency by recording the end-to-end latency of unlearning requests. Lower latency indicates stronger efficiency. All experiments are conducted on a server with one NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. We fix the random seed to 2020 for all runs. Unless otherwise stated, all models are trained with Adam for 300 epochs, using a learning rate of 0.001 and a training batch size of 2048. We evaluate the model on the validation set after each epoch and apply early stopping with a patience of 10, where Recall@10 is used as the validation metric. For evaluation, we adopt the full-ranking setting and report Recall, NDCG, and Hit at $K {10,20}$, with an evaluation batch size of 4096. For CEU, the CCI module pre-computes causal explanations for the top 50 candidate items of each user. For each candidate item, we retain at most 5 causal interactions as its explanatory support set. These cached explanations are then used by URA during the unlearning stage to perform score adjustment without updating model parameters.
