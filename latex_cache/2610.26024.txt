This provides a more expressive and semantically grounded representation of items. However, most existing methods remain centered on item identity embeddings, treating multimodal information as an auxiliary signal through concatenation , addition , or related fusion operations. Under this paradigm, multimodal content mainly enhances ID-driven representations, rather than being exploited as a primary source of semantic signals. In principle, directly modeling items from multimodal content is particularly appealing for diversity-oriented recommendation. Unlike ID embeddings, which are largely shaped by historical user--item interactions and thus easily dominated by collaborative patterns such as popularity and exposure bias, multimodal information is grounded in the intrinsic characteristics of items themselves. It therefore provides richer semantic cues for distinguishing items from different perspectives, offering a more natural basis for diversified recommendation. Nevertheless, directly exploiting multimodal features remains highly nontrivial. Recent studies attempt to learn item representations directly from multimodal features , yet prior works show that using pre-trained multimodal representations does not necessarily improve recommendation performance and may even degrade it , as further analyzed in Section~. To understand the limitations of existing methods in diversity-oriented recommendation, we analyze the problem from two complementary perspectives: semantic utilization and structural representation. The former focuses on how multimodal semantics are extracted and organized, while the latter examines how representations are distributed and evolved during training. We begin by analyzing the role of multimodal semantics in recommendation. Specifically, we analyze the distribution of item embeddings in the latent space . As illustrated in Fig.~, item embeddings exhibit a clear dimensional collapse phenomenon, where representations are highly concentrated within each category with limited intra-category variation. While some degree of clustering is expected, the observed distributions reveal an excessive concentration tendency. Such structural compression reduces the discriminability among items and restricts the model’s ability to retrieve diverse candidates. Consequently, recommendation results become increasingly homogeneous. For instance, users located near a dense region in the latent space are likely to receive recommendations from a narrow subset of items within the same category, while other categories remain underexplored. From this perspective, the accuracy--diversity trade-off can be understood as a consequence of structural bias. Dimensional collapse reduces representation diversity, while accuracy-oriented objectives further reinforce concentration on dominant items and categories. Together, these effects lead to improved accuracy at the cost of diminished diversity. This analysis highlights the necessity of } to alleviate dimensional collapse while preserving meaningful semantic relationships, thereby enabling a better balance between accuracy and diversity.
