Using both today means two models, two indexes and a score-fusion step. We present , a 179M-parameter English encoder that produces both representations in one pass and scores them with one unmodified MaxSim. The dense embedding is written into the list of token vectors as three extra vectors, and a one-dimensional tag keeps the two kinds of vectors from matching each other, so that plain MaxSim equals the token-level MaxSim plus a weighted dense cosine. The model therefore runs in a standard late-interaction index without changes; we test it with PLAID. A shared 18-layer trunk with a separate 4-layer top per head, and a cross-encoder distilled into the score, turn the two heads into one retriever. On BEIR, reaches 59.79 nDCG@10. This is 2.57 points above LateOn, the strongest model of its size, and 2.10 points above it when both models read documents of the same length; is ahead on all 15 datasets. On a decontaminated version of BEIR it stays ahead on average and on 8 of 12 datasets. Among the 156 models with complete BEIR results in the MTEB results repository, only models with at least 1.5B parameters score higher. The dense model is better where a long query has to be matched as a whole, as in argument retrieval, and the late-interaction model is better where a single entity or phrase decides, as in the open-domain question answering. Their scores are complementary. Systems exploit this complementarity today through retrieval: two models, two indexes, two searches, and a fusion step such as reciprocal rank fusion . Models that return several representations at once save the second encoder, but every representation still has its own index and its own scoring function. { /paradigms.pdf} computes both scores with one encoder, one index and one MaxSim.} In this work we introduce }, a retriever that holds a dense head and a late-interaction head in one encoder and adds their scores inside a standard late-interaction index . Specifically, we make the following contributions: For the representation, we store the dense sentence vector as three extra vectors, the , in the list of token vectors, and give every vector a one-dimensional with opposite signs for tokens and slots. Inside MaxSim a token then never selects a slot and a slot never selects a token, so the unmodified MaxSim returns the token-level score plus a weighted dense cosine . The index and the scorer need no change: the model is saved as a ColBERT model and runs in PLAID through PyLate . For the architecture, the two heads share the first 18 layers of ModernBERT-base and each has its own copy of the last four, which raises the score of each head on its own by about one point. The dense head uses the latent-attention pooling of NV-Embed , here on an encoder-only model . For the training, we distil a cross-encoder into the score, the score that is used at inference, in addition to the losses of each head .
