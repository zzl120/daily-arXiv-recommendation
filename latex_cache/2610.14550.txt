Conventional offline engagement metrics are limited by sparse, biased feedback, while online evaluation is costly and time-consuming. In this work, we propose a user--product relevance metric for offline evaluation of DPA retrieval systems. The metric first compresses the engagement history into a compact set of intent representatives via Semantic-ID-grouped, event-stratified segmentation, then assesses the relevance of a recommended product to each individual intent and aggregates them as a user-level relevance score, yielding a stable and interpretable measure of retrieval quality. Recent work uses LLMs as semantic relevance judges for search and recommendation, scoring content alignment directly rather than relying on engagement signals~. Sun et al.~ show that prompted LLMs are effective zero-shot re-rankers and can outperform strong supervised baselines; Choi et al.~ report substantial agreement between LLM evaluators and human raters; and large-scale benchmarks find that LLM-generated relevance labels broadly track human judgment at scale~. These works target search-style relevance with an explicit query. In contrast, DPA retrieval system do not have search queries and typically require inferring user-level relevance signals from heterogeneous engagement histories. We propose an offline evaluation framework that uses LLM judges to score user--product relevance for DPA retrieval. Two operational problems shape the design: the engagement history is heterogeneous and long, and frontier LLMs are too expensive to call on every event--candidate pair at production volume. We address both with an SID -grouped, event-stratified compression of engagement history into a small set of weighted intent representatives, and an agentic guideline-discovery loop together with instruction-guided teacher-student finetune for production serving. Our contributions are 3-folds as follows: We define a user-level offline evaluation metric for DPA retrieval that aggregates LLM-judged intent--candidate relevance under a threshold-$k$ aggregation rule, without requiring an explicit query. We propose an efficient LLM judge pipeline with 1) SID-grouped intent decomposition that reduces LLM calls by $30 $, and 2) teacher-student distillation from an agentic, human-vetted guideline that replaces the frontier teacher with an efficient student at production cost. The metric ranks six retrieval sources consistently with conversion precision/recall - how well the retrieved candidates aligns with user's subsequence purchases - and is directionally aligned with online CTR/CVR deltas across four A/B tests; It thus provides a reliable view of retrieval system health and a low-cost signal for guiding offline model iteration. We address the first with an agentic guideline-discovery loop followed by a human-alignment pass , and the second with knowledge distillation into a small student . We build the evaluation guideline in two phases. Phase A is an where an LLM judge iteratively refines its own decision rules from labeled examples; Phase B is a pass where editors steer the resulting rule set toward DPA business priorities. We start from a minimal seed prompt -- a single instruction, ``'' -- with no per-class definitions, examples, or attribute hints. A frontier LLM labels a stratified calibration split using this seed prompt, scored by per-class F1 against the human labels. The refinement loop iterates clustering mis-classified pairs by the confusion and the judge's free-form rationale; prompting a separate reasoner to propose up to three new sub-rules targeting the true-label class for the largest cluster; and provisionally appending each sub-rule, re-labeling the calibration set, and accepting iff the paired-bootstrap 95\ The agentic phase optimizes calibration-set agreement but is silent on downstream business goals. Human reviewers audit the Phase-A output against three criteria: coverage of business-critical mismatches , editorial-standard compatibility, and reproducibility on a $200$-pair business-stratified sample. Vetoed rules return to Phase A with an explanatory note. The most consequential intervention in our deployment promoted demographic mismatch from a -leaning signal to a hard override -- a rule the loop repeatedly deprioritized because demographic-mismatch pairs are statistically rare in the calibration set. Phase B is load-bearing: demographic-override rules emerge from editorial review rather than from the agentic loop, because demographic-mismatch pairs are too rare in the calibration set to drive the loop's statistical gate. The resulting guideline -- the seed taxonomy refined by Phase-A sub-rules under the Phase-B business overrides -- is what the rest of the paper refers to as ``the guideline''. It instructs the LLM judge to assign each pair to one of five tiers and to emit a structured rationale by selecting from a controlled attribute vocabulary . GPT-5 runs in parallel under the same guideline as a verification agent; pairs on which the two agents agree at high self-reported confidence are accepted directly, and the disagreed cases are sent to human auditors for relabeling. At serving time, the student replaces both teacher LLMs in the inference path; the teacher is invoked only on guideline-update or distillation-refresh cycles. In this section, we benchmark the proposed LLM-based user-product relevance metric against conventional DPA retrieval offline metrics and evaluate their consistency across various retrieval sources. A key advantage of the LLM-based approach is its independence from online traffic data. While conventional metrics often rely on future engagement signals to accurately measure precision/recall, our metric operates strictly offline, providing a high-fidelity relevance signal without the need for real-time interaction logs. Utilizing online traffic, we employ a look-back evaluation strategy that links observed conversions to their contributing ad requests ~. We define relevance using a strict exact-match criterion, where a retrieved product must be identical to the converted item within a one-day post-impression attribution window. This focus assesses the system's ability to surface the precise product driving a transaction. Accordingly, Conversion Precision is the proportion of retrieved products that match the conversion, while Conversion Recall measures the fraction of ultimately converted products successfully surfaced by the system. Metrics are limited to users with both an eligible ad request and a subsequent confirmed conversion. A production DPA retrieval system blends several heterogeneous sources, each with distinct algorithmic and signal-coverage profiles, so a single offline metric that ranks them consistently must generalize across signal types. Classical offline metrics for recommendation retrieval -- Recall@$K$, NDCG@$K$ over future-engagement labels, and conversion-attributed precision/recall~ -- have well-known weaknesses in ads: severe selection bias from the exposure policy, heavy tail of low-engagement requests, and a lookback window that conflates retrieval quality with downstream ranker and budget effects. The offline-online evaluation gap in e-commerce recommendation has been documented at multiple scales~. Counterfactual estimators reduce some of this bias~ but assume access to logged propensities that are rarely available at the retrieval stage of a multi-tower ad system. Our work is complementary: rather than debiasing engagement labels, we replace them with a content-grounded relevance label produced by an LLM judge, and report alignment with engagement-based offline metrics. LLM-as-judge has moved from open-domain summarization~ to e-commerce and recommender relevance labeling: LREF~ fine-tunes an LLM as a query-product relevance scorer with chain-of-thought, BloomIntent~ performs intent-conditioned evaluation of SERP satisfaction, Wang et al.~ progressively train an LLM-based semantic-ads retrieval encoder at Walmart, Penha et al.~ examine LLM-judge ranking agreement with humans in Cranfield-style movie and podcast recommendation, and large-scale studies show LLM labels broadly track human judgment in IR settings~.
